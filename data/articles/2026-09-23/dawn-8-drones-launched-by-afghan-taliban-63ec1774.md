# 8 drones launched by Afghan Taliban shot down near Torkham border, Kohat: security sources

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Wed, 23 Sep 2026 15:03:58 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2032066/8-drones-launched-by-afghan-taliban-shot-down-near-torkham-border-kohat-security-sources
- Collected: 2026-09-23 12:31:03 UTC

---

The armed forces on Wednesday shot down eight drones launched by the Afghan Taliban after they entered Pakistani airspace near the Torkham border and Kohat, according to security sources.
The sources said the drones were intercepted by the air defence systems, which they described as “active and vigilant”.
“The armed forces and the ‘Shaheens’ of the Pakistan Air Force professionally thwarted the Afghan Taliban regime’s cowardly attempt to target public areas,” security sources said.
According to the sources, four of the eight drones were shot down in areas adjacent to the Torkham border, while the remaining four were downed near Kohat.
Earlier, security sources said two “rudimentary” drones had been brought down using “soft skill” methods about seven kilometres from Kohat city. They said the armed forces’ air defence systems remained on round-the-clock alert and were maintaining vigilance against “all threats from Kharjistan”.
“This incursion serves as yet another proof of the Afghan Taliban regime’s direct support and patronage of terrorists,” security sources said.
Later, security sources said the wreckage of the destroyed drones was being recovered. Last night’s exchange of fire in Kurram sector and Wednesday’s drone incursion “demonstrated the state of the Afghan Taliban’s panic and disarray,” security sources added.
The development comes two days after Pakistan, in response to recent terrorist attacks in KP, conducted “calibrated” airstrikes in the border region with Afghanistan against terrorist hideouts and safe havens of terrorist organisations.
A statement from the information ministry on Monday said that over 28 terrorists were eliminated in the strikes. “It is a matter of grave concern that Afghanistan continues to remain a hub of terrorism,” the ministry said at the time, specifying that the “threat to Pakistan emanates from Afghanistan”.
Before the airstrikes, security forces conducted a “well-planned intelligence-based ground operation” on September 20 against a group of terrorists in KP’s Kohat and Hangu districts, the ministry said. Nine terrorists were eliminated in the engagement.
The airstrikes came after a major attack in the old Police Lines in KP’s Kohat claimed 23 lives and injured about 100 others. Eight terrorists were killed during an overnight clearance operation launched to clear the area.
It should also be mentioned that on Sept 20, two officers and four others were martyred while eight terrorists were eliminated during a fire exchange between a security forces convoy and armed combatants in Hangu, the military’s media wing said.
In July, the Afghan Taliban regime launched four rudimentary drones across the border into Balochistan, which were shot down by security forces. Two drones were also shot down in June in Qila Abdullah and Chagai districts.
Pak-Afghan ties
Tensions between Islamabad and Kabul have remained high, punctuated by cross-border clashes over the past year.
Islamabad has repeatedly urged the Taliban administration in Kabul to dismantle terrorist sanctuaries on Afghan soil, particularly those linked to the banned TTP, that are used for attacks in Pakistan. Officials say those appeals have gone unheeded.
A day earlier, Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar addressed a meeting of the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation (OIC) and called for steps towards concrete and verifiable action to prevent the use of Afghan soil for terrorism against any neighbouring country, particularly Pakistan.
Dar said Pakistan was “seriously concerned” about the presence of over two dozen terrorist groups in Afghanistan that threatened regional and international peace and security.
Last week, Pakistan also told the UN Security Council that it would respond to terrorist threats emanating from Afghanistan in self-defence as and when needed and in accordance with international law.
Earlier this month, the Foreign Office (FO) declared that meaningful progress in relations with Kabul would depend on the Taliban taking concrete and verifiable action against Tehreek-i-Taliban Pakistan (TTP) and other terrorist groups operating from Afghan territory.
It maintained that while Islamabad welcomed China’s continuing engagement, the external facilitation could not substitute for action by Kabul against terrorist infrastructure.
According to a UN Security Council-mandated monitoring report — covering the period from December 16, 2025 to June 9, 2026 — Afghan Taliban’s continued support for TTP has contributed to a rise in the frequency and lethality of terrorist attacks in Pakistan, even as Kabul has taken some initial steps to rein in the group.
The report also pointed to growing operational and facilitation linkages among the TTP, Al Qaeda, Islamic State-Khorasan (ISIL-K) and the Baloch Liberation Army (BLA).
