# After hours of negotiations, second round of govt-opposition talks ends in a 'deadlock'

ISLAMABAD: Hours-long negotiations between the government and the opposition during a second round of talks ended in a “deadlock” early on Sunday.
The meeting at Parliament House began after 4pm on Saturday, with discussions focusing on the PTI’s planned Oct 4 march to Islamabad. Details on the negotiating process were sparse as both sides seemingly agreed not to speak to the media before matters concluded.
However, shortly before 3am on Sunday, Senate Opposition Leader Allama Raja Nasir Abbas, who was part of the opposition delegation, told the media that the talks had not succeeded in a breakthrough.
“Deadlock,” he told reporters waiting at Parliament House in footage aired on Geo News. Asked what the points of contention were, he said they were “small things”. He added that the “issue” of meetings with PTI founder Imran Khan could not be solved.
He went on to say that the deadlock was over Imran’s meeting with his personal physicians, and how to address terrorism in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. He said that talks had now ended, adding that a detailed press conference would be held later.
Minister of State for Interior Tallal Chaudhry, who was part of the government’s delegation in the talks, said the talks failed primarily because the “KP government could not assure whether they would cooperate in the fight against terrorism in the future”.
He alleged that the PTI’s provincial government in KP did not want to implement the National Action Plan and that the PTI delegation that participated in the talks had also left the venue “disappointed because the KP government did not cooperate on terrorism”.
An agreement had been reached on all other matters, he said in response to a question.
Asked about the possible future course of action, he said the “Constitution has several options”, seemingly a referring to the possibility of imposing governor’s rule or emergency in KP.
Separately, PPP’s Sherry Rehman, who also participated in the talks, said the government delegation debated all points with the opposition.
“Unfortunately, the draft [agreement] was changed four times after it was agreed upon,” she told Geo News, adding that she would not go into the details.
Speaking to reporters, PTI interim chairman Barrister Gohar Khan said that the party’s long march was “intact” and would go ahead. At the same time, he asserted that imposing an emergency or governor rule was not the answer to the protest.
He further said that the participants had also not reached an agreement on when the next round of talks would take place.
He said that the opposition’s demands presented during the second round of talks were “simple” and included meetings with Imran and his spouse, Bushra Bibi.
He said that there were a few issues pertaining to terrorism, where the negotiating process became “stuck”.
Hours-long talks
Visuals broadcast by DawnNewsTV after 9:30pm showed Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi — part of the government delegation — departing Parliament House with a document in his hand, with media reports suggesting he was likely to consult the president.
More than an hour later, footage showed Naqvi returning to Parliament House, with the interior minister maintaining his silence as reporters swarmed him for queries.
The meeting, chaired by National Assembly Speaker Ayaz Sadiq, was aimed at defusing the country’s ongoing political crisis. The PTI’s planned Oct 4 protest march, aimed at demanding Imran’s release, has emerged as the latest point of contention between the party and the federal government.
Even as extended talks kept hopes for an agreement alive, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Chief Minister Sohail Afridi remained adamant on proceeding with PTI’s march to Islamabad.
After voicing his determination on Saturday afternoon — which irked government representatives — the KP CM again took to X at 12:46am to detail the itinerary for the protest march.
Laying out the journey plan, Afridi said PTI supporters from the districts of Tank, Dera Ismail Khan, South Waziristan, North Waziristan, Bannu, and Lakki Marwat will join the long march from Lakki Marwat’s Gandi Chowk Lakki Marwat at 1pm on Oct 4.
The caravan will halt for the night near Karak, from where people will depart at 12:30pm the next day and stay overnight near Kohat.
Then, at 12pm on Oct 6, protesters from Kohat, Hangu, Kurram, and Orakzai will join the convoy, which will move towards Peshawar and spend the night near Mattani.
The KP CM’s post went on to say that the caravan will depart from Budh Bair in Peshawar on Oct 7 afternoon. “The remaining constituencies of Peshawar will join us at Kohat Road Bridge and the Motorway Toll Plaza. And we will spend the night near Charsadda Interchange.”
They would then depart the Charsadda Interchange at 12:30pm on Oct 8 after people from Charsadda and Mohmand join them.
Refraining from detailing the final leg of the trip to the federal capital, Afridi said the people “of entire KP” would gather at Khairabad on Oct 8 and spend the night there. “God willing, the plan for the next phase will be announced there.”
NA speaker hopes for ‘cordial’ talks
When the huddle began, the NA speaker expressed his hope that the talks would proceed in the same “cordial atmosphere” as Friday’s, according to a post on the NA’s X account.
The government side included Naqvi, Tallal Minister for States and Frontier Regions Amir Muqam, Parliamentary Affairs Minister Tariq Fazal Chaudhry and Prime Minister’s Adviser on Political Affairs Rana Sanaullah.
Rehman, along with MNAs Syed Naveed Qamar and Aijaz Hussain Jakhrani, represented the PPP.
The opposition delegation comprised the Senate opposition leader, PTI interim chairman Gohar Ali Khan, Opposition Chief Whip Amir Dogar, MNA Asad Qaiser, PTI Secretary General Salman Akram Raja and PTI Sindh President Haleem Adil Sheikh.
Prior to the meeting with the government, the opposition held a meeting in which standard operating procedures (SOPs) for the talks were finalised.
It was decided that it would be proposed during the meeting that no one from either side would speak to the media during the talks and that a joint statement would be issued afterwards to avoid any misunderstanding.
Speaking to Dawn, TTAP spokesperson Akhunzada Hussain Ahmed Yousufzai said comments made by PPP’s Rehman on Friday had triggered a controversy, leaving the situation unpleasant until late at night.
“The opposition alliance held a meeting before the start of the talks, during which the government side’s views regarding the law and order situation in KP were discussed. After consultation with the KP chief minister, a response was prepared, which will be presented to the government side during the meeting today,” he said.
For the first time in almost three years, representatives of the federal government and the opposition PTI formally came together on Friday for talks aimed at easing the political tension in the country, and apprised each other of their positions.
Though neither side had shared its specific demands with the media, both were hopeful of a positive outcome from the meeting.
Sources on both sides told Dawn that the PTI wants Imran to be shifted from jail to a hospital for medical treatment, while the government has insisted that the opposition call off its protest march on Islamabad.
In August, PTI announced a long march to Islamabad on Sept 27 against what it called the “illegal imprisonment” of its party founder. However, a couple of days before its scheduled long march, the party postponed the march until Oct 4.
Tariq Fazal objects to KP CM’s remarks
CM Afridi’s Saturday statement vowed to hold the long march on Oct 4 and to prevent a repeat of the events of May 9, 2023.
He said PTI was the largest and most popular political party in Pakistan. “Pakistan is a democratic country whose Constitution grants us the right to peaceful protest.”
Detailing his long march plan, Afridi said an “ocean of people” would begin its journey from Gandi Chowk in Lakki Marwat at 1pm on Oct 4.
The party would continue its struggle until the supremacy of the Constitution and the law was ensured in Pakistan and an independent judiciary was restored, the PTI leader added.
“All demands of the PTI are in accordance with the constitution and the law.”
He stressed that the party would “neither allow May 9 to happen again nor allow anyone else to repeat it”.
Reacting to the KP CM’s remarks, Chaudhry said Afridi’s statements could affect the ongoing negotiation process even though the government wanted them to be “meaningful and fruitful”.
He urged PTI leaders to refrain from making such statements until the final stage of the negotiations, so that the dialogue process was not “harmed”.
“The language that Sohail Afridi is using against his opponents and our leadership does not seem like that of a chief minister; his statements are extremely indecent and reprehensible,” Chaudhry said.
Speaking to reporters ahead of the meeting, PPP’s Rehman stressed that her party was making efforts to solve issues in a democratic manner.
Asked about KP CM being adamant on the PTI march, the PPP leader said it was up to the PTI, but added that she would refrain from making comments before the talks conclude.
While acknowledging that each party had its positions, she noted that the objective of holding negotiations was to “discuss those positions”.
‘Political solutions to political problems’
Separately, PPP Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari, an ally of the PML-N government, reiterated his call for PTI to call off its long march.
Speaking at an event in Karachi on Saturday, Bilawal said it was his party that urged the Centre to engage in dialogue with PTI whereas the federal government had “made up its mind” on imposing emergency or governor’s rule in KP.
In a post on X on Friday, the PPP chairman had pressed PTI to unconditionally call off the long march, which he said would allow the country to come together to address the threat of terrorism and threats emanating from neighbouring countries.
Bilawal also welcomed the government’s decision to work with his party and jointly engage with the opposition “to find political solutions to political problems”.
Also on Friday, the army’s top brass warned that any attempt at creating agitation or a “May 9-like situation” would be dealt with “decisive force”.
Ahead of the talks, federal ministers had warned that the Centre would have no option but to impose governor’s rule or an emergency in KP, citing the province’s security situation and the protest call, while PTI had warned of dire consequences if such a step was taken.
The decision to hold talks was taken on Thursday at a presidency meeting attended by President Asif Ali Zardari, Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, Naqvi, Attorney General for Pakistan Mansoor Awan and other PPP and PML-N leaders.
A source privy to the huddle told Dawn the emergency option was discussed, but participants agreed to first give the opposition a chance to settle matters peacefully through dialogue, and to seriously consider an emergency only if the PTI did not back down. The coalition also believes the march will remain confined to KP and not enter Punjab or Islamabad.
On Thursday, Sanaullah said the government and PTI were engaged in “off-the-record talks” over the protest, and that the decision on the march rested with the PTI’s senior leadership, “with whom off-the-record consultation is ongoing”. “So let us see, even if they do not postpone it, we have arrangements in place to stop them,” he warned.
Separately, PM Shehbaz has invited NA Opposition Leader Mehmood Khan Achakzai for dialogue, and the two are expected to meet in the coming days.
