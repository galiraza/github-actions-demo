# PTI cannot force govt to accept its demands by holding state 'hostage', says Sanaullah

- Published: Wed, 07 Oct 2026 15:26:44 +0500

- Collected: 2026-10-07 14:09:57 UTC

---

The PTI cannot pressure the government into accepting its demands by holding the state “hostage” and creating political instability, Adviser to the Prime Minister on Political Affairs Rana Sanaullah said on Wednesday.
Speaking to the media in Islamabad, Sanaullah said that the government would not allow the opposition to use talks as a “tool” for making political demands.
“The doors for talks are open,” Sanaullah said, recalling Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif’s remarks yesterday calling on the PTI leadership to sit down with the government for dialogue and abandon their protest plans.
However, Sanaullah said, negotiations could not be used to strong-arm the government.
The PML-N leader described the PTI as having been “successful” in obstructing peace. He lamented that politics had been transformed into a culture of “below the belt” jabs at one another and a “culture of intolerance”.
On Sunday, hours-long negotiations between the government and the opposition during a second round of talks ended in a deadlock. The meeting at Parliament House began on Saturday afternoon and continued into the wee hours of Sunday, with discussions focusing on the PTI’s planned Oct 4 march to Islamabad.
After the talks collapsed, Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi accused Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Chief Minister Sohail Afridi of “sabotaging” the negotiations, saying that he had changed the draft of the agreement despite what had been agreed to earlier.
However, PTI leaders, presenting the opposition’s stance on the failed talks, declared that reconciliation with the government was not possible without including incarcerated party founder Imran Khan in the dialogue process. They said that another reason for the failure of the talks was that the government was not willing to pursue a collective strategy against terrorism.
The prime minister’s adviser on Wednesday contended that the PTI’s stance that it was being left out of decisions concerning the Centre’s counterterrorism policy was “completely wrong” and “misleading”.
He added that the reasons for the deadlock in talks had been misinterpreted.
“The policy on countering terrorism is already being acted upon,” Sanaullah noted, referring to intelligence-based operations (IBOs) being carried out on a daily basis with “huge success”.
He went on to claim that the PTI was against such IBOs.
In June 2024, the government announced a reinvigorated “Operation Azm-i-Istehkam” but, following criticism, clarified soon after that it would only entail intensifying ongoing IBOs and rebranded it as “Vision Azm-i-Istehkam”.
Sanaullah pointed out that the armed forces and law enforcement agencies have been sacrificing their lives to keep the operations going.
He also said it was being falsely claimed that new military operations would be undertaken and more people would be displaced. He clarified that the ongoing IBOs were supposed to continue under the existing counterterrorism policy.
Sanaullah further said that Chief of Defence Forces and Chief of the Army Staff Field Marshal Asim Munir had previously clarified that no one would be internally displaced as a result of the operations.
‘Politics over national interest’
Speaking on the matter of the ongoing rift between Pakistan and Afghanistan, Sanaullah said that Islamabad had been “fair” in demanding that the Afghan government not allow its land to be used for terrorist activities in Pakistan.
He also elaborated on past talks with the Afghan authorities, saying that in the multiple rounds of dialogue, the Afghan interim government had accepted evidence presented by Pakistan for terrorist attacks emanating from Afghanistan as true.
However, he said, the Afghan Taliban had refused to guarantee that their land would not be used for terror attacks against Pakistan.
“Pakistan’s policy is that it has the right to neutralise training camps or any activity on Afghan land that could cause terrorism in Pakistan later on,” Sanaullah stated.
The policy was based on experiences from the past 40 years and more, he added.
The PML-N leader contended that the PTI was the only party opposed to the policy because it wanted to prioritise its politics over national interest.
During his press conference, he also recalled PTI’s past protests in Islamabad, including in 2022 and 2024.
Sanaullah highlighted the recent rise in terrorism incidents, stressing that martyrs included children, civilians and soldiers from KP.
“On one hand, the martyrs’ bodies were lying there, and on the other hand, the entire provincial government was playing the dhol and chanting slogans, urging people to join the march on Islamabad.”
The attitude of the KP government was “unacceptable”, the PML-N leader declared, adding that the people in the province had also condemned it.
“Prioritising personal and political agendas over national interest is not permissible by law, the Constitution or political ethics,” Sanaullah remarked.
He criticised the KP government’s performance, claiming governance and working for the betterment of the people was not on the PTI’s agenda.
