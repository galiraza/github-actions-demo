# Pakistan looking to raise more than $1bn under govt's push for privatisation, says finance minister

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Mon, 12 Oct 2026 11:51:47 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2036589/pakistan-looking-to-raise-more-than-1bn-under-govts-push-for-privatisation-says-finance-minister
- Collected: 2026-10-12 06:55:48 UTC

---

The government is looking to push for the privatisation of five to six entities that could raise upwards of $1 billion, Finance Minister Muhammad Aurangzeb told Reuters on Monday, stating that he expected the economy to grow by more than four per cent this fiscal year despite elevated oil prices.
Pakistan’s economy is considered to be highly vulnerable to a prolonged Middle East conflict given its dependence on Gulf energy imports, remittances and financing support from the region, even as it remains reliant on external financing to bolster foreign exchange reserves and meet debt repayments.
Pakistan has been able to procure adequate quantities of fuel, successfully avoiding any shortages, and critical sectors such as agriculture were holding up, Aurangzeb said.
“So I’m still quite clear that we should clock north of 4pc,” he said in an interview on the sidelines of the annual meetings of the International Monetary Fund and World Bank in Bangkok.
“And on the inflation side, obviously, we are keeping a very close eye because it does have a knock-on impact in terms of the commodity prices.”
Following a period of severe economic turmoil exacerbated by the Covid-19 pandemic, the cash-strapped economy has recovered some of its footing, supported by a $7bn, 37-month IMF loan program known as the Extended Fund Facility signed in 2024 that includes some tough measures such as raising tax revenue on farm income.
Following the privatisation of flag carrier Pakistan International Airlines last year, Aurangzeb said the government has around another dozen entities in the pipeline, including state-owned power distribution companies and the outsourcing of airport operations.
“I expect about five or six of these to go through during this fiscal year,” he said, adding that they could raise more than $1bn.
Privatisation has been seen as a key test of Pakistan’s reform credibility with the IMF, with officials previously underlining that failure to offload loss-making state firms risked renewed pressure on public finances.
“We are quite clear that we want the private sector to lead the country,” said Aurangzeb. “So our role is to help support, assist any way we can in terms of the ecosystem.”
Exchange stabilisation facility
The US-Israeli-led war with Iran, now in its eighth month, and the inflation and hardship it has caused, will dominate the agenda and sideline conversations during the meetings that are being held outside Washington for the first time in three years.
Pakistan, which reached a staff-level agreement with the IMF last week, potentially unlocking about $1.21bn in financing under the Extended Fund Facility and a climate program, is continuing discussions with the United States for a proposed $10bn exchange stabilisation facility, Aurangzeb said.
But he declined to provide a time frame for the conclusion of negotiations.
“If it comes through, this will be a great help for us,” he said, referring to the facility, which is in addition to ongoing talks with the Export-Import Bank of the United States (EXIM) and the US International Development Finance Corporation (DFC).
“If it doesn’t come through, this does not, you know, create any problem for us in terms of access to market.”
