# Prime suspect in Gujranwala bride's murder killed in alleged exchange of fire, claims CCD

- Collected: 2026-10-31 13:16:03 UTC

---

LAHORE: The Crime Control Department (CCD) claimed on Saturday that the prime suspect in the murder of a bride on her wedding day in Gujranwala district was killed during an alleged exchange of fire.
According to a CCD spokesperson, the prime suspect, Sarfraz, had allegedly been hiding in Karachi with the other suspects before being arrested.
The spokesperson claimed that while he was being transported back by the CCD, the convoy came under attack. Sarfraz was allegedly killed by gunfire from his own accomplices during the exchange of fire.
The spokesperson further claimed that the suspects, including a woman, had been constantly changing locations before their arrest the previous night.
The bride was shot dead on the day of her reception in Gujranwala city, allegedly by the prime suspect whose marriage proposal was declined by her family.
According to the CCD Gujranwala, the suspect opened fire on her after stopping her vehicle as she returned from her wedding reception.
A department spokesperson said the case was registered at a CCD police station on the complaint of the deceased’s father. The Gujranwala CCD booked three suspects — Sarfraz, his female cousin and an unidentified accomplice — under sections 302 (premeditated murder), 311 (Ta’zir after waiver or compounding of right of qisas in qatl-i-amd), 109 (punishment for abetment if the act abetted is committed in consequence and where no express provision is made for its punishment) and 34 (common intention) of the Pakistan Penal Code.
The CCD, established last year, has been criticised by human rights advocates and civil society members for a high number of casualties.
The Human Rights Commission of Pakistan (HRCP) alleged earlier this year that the CCD has adopted a “deliberate policy of staged police encounters leading to extrajudicial killings”. In response, the department termed the claims as unfounded and contrary to the department’s operational framework.
A day earlier, the CCD claimed that two alleged robbers were “killed during an encounter” in Punjab’s Kasur. Police claimed the suspects were wanted by Kasur, Lahore and Sheikhupura police in dozens of cases of robbery, house dacoities and murders.
On October 29, the department claimed that a man involved in a case of a murder of a 10-year-old boy was also allegedly killed in an encounter.
Earlier this week, the CCD released a comparison of crime figures of September 2023-24 and September 2025-26, claiming 63 per cent reduction in overall crime, including robbery and motorcycle theft, across Punjab.
According to the CCD spokesperson, Lahore recorded the steepest decline in overall crime, at 78pc, in September 2025-26 compared to September 2023-24. Faisalabad followed with a 68pc decrease and Sahiwal with 66pc. Multan recorded a 59pc decline, while Gujranwala and Bahawalpur each saw a 58pc decrease. Crime rates also fell by 57pc in Sheikhupura, 56pc in Sargodha, 52pc in Rawalpindi and 43pc in Dera Ghazi Khan.
