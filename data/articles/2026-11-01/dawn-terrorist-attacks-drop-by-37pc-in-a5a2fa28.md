# Terrorist attacks drop by 37pc in October: PICSS

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Sun, 01 Nov 2026 20:06:44 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2041388/terrorist-attacks-drop-by-37pc-in-october-picss
- Collected: 2026-11-01 18:21:14 UTC

---

ISLAMABAD: Pakistan witnessed a significant improvement in several key security indicators during October, with terrorist attacks declining by 37 per cent compared with September, it emerged on Sunday.
According to statistics released by the Islamabad-based Pakistan Institute for Conflict and Security Studies (PICSS), the country experienced 143 terrorist attacks during October, compared with 226 in September. The decline reversed two consecutive months of increasing terrorist violence.
The improvement was particularly pronounced in Balochistan and mainland Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, while suicide attacks also declined substantially. However, an increase in kidnappings, particularly in Balochistan, remained a major area of concern.
Data gathered by PICSS showed that terrorist attacks claimed 111 lives in October, compared with 260 in September, representing a reduction of 57pc. The number of people injured in these attacks dropped even more sharply, from 242 to just 69, a decline of 71pc.
The improvement in the security situation was also reflected in the overall human cost of violence.
According to PICSS, terrorist attacks and security forces’ counterterrorism operations collectively resulted in deaths of 53 security forces personnel, 45 civilians and five peace committee members, while 169 terrorists were killed.
The 71 injured included 29 security forces personnel, 11 peace committee members, and 31 civilians.
A rolling 12-month comparison conducted by PICSS revealed several encouraging developments. Injuries resulting from terrorist attacks fell to their lowest monthly level in the past year. The combined number of injuries from terrorist attacks and security operations also reached a 12-month low, as did injuries among security forces personnel.
The number of suicide attacks was jointly the lowest recorded during the same period. However, the number of terrorists killed, at 169, was also the lowest monthly figure in the past 12 months, reflecting a decline in terrorist fatalities, the report noted.
Suicide attacks declined sharply during October, with only one such incident, compared with three in September. The October incident involved a vehicle-borne suicide attack but caused no fatalities or injuries.
In contrast, the three suicide attacks recorded in September, including two vehicle-borne attacks, resulted in 45 deaths and 138 injuries. Consequently, fatalities and injuries from suicide attacks both declined by 100pc during October.
According to PICSS, continued counterterrorism operations across the country by security forces resulted in the killing of 156 terrorists and the arrest of seven suspected terrorists during October. Two security forces personnel and three civilians lost their lives during these operations, while two security forces personnel were injured.
Balochistan
Balochistan recorded one of the most significant improvements, with terrorist attacks declining by 46pc, from 132 in September to 71 in October. Fatalities from terrorist attacks dropped from 136 to 44, representing a reduction of approximately 68pc, while injuries declined from 63 to 37, a decrease of 41pc.
Separately, security forces’ operations in the province resulted in the killing of 79 terrorists.
Three civilians lost their lives, and two security forces personnel were injured during these operations.
In October, the Inter-Services Public Relations (ISPR) had confirmed that an army major was martyred in an improvised explosive device (IED) attack in Balochistan.
Khyber Pakhtunkhwa
Mainland KP, excluding the formerly federally administered tribal areas, also witnessed a substantial improvement. PICSS reported 40 terrorist attacks in October, compared with 58 in September, representing a decline of 31pc. Fatalities from these attacks decreased from 90 to 38, a reduction of approximately 58pc, while injuries fell from 165 to 19, an improvement of 88pc.
Counterterrorism operations in the region resulted in the killing of 42 terrorists, while two security forces personnel lost their lives.
The erstwhile FATA region experienced a comparatively modest decline in terrorist violence, the report said. The think tank recorded 30 attacks during October, compared with 34 in September, a reduction of 12pc. Deaths resulting from terrorist attacks declined from 33 to 25, while injuries increased slightly from 11 to 13. Security forces’ operations in these districts resulted in the killing of 35 terrorists.
Sindh and Punjab
Punjab witnessed two terrorist attacks during October, compared with none in September. These incidents resulted in four deaths and no injuries.
Sindh recorded no terrorist attacks during the month. However, counterterrorism operations in the province resulted in the arrest of seven suspected terrorists. Islamabad, Azad Jammu and Kashmir, and Gilgit-Baltistan also remained free of recorded terrorist attacks during October.
Kidnappings ‘serious security concern’
Despite the overall improvement, kidnappings emerged as a serious security concern, particularly in Balochistan.
PICSS recorded 88 people kidnapped in terrorist incidents nationwide during October, compared with 72 in September, an increase of 22pc. This was the highest monthly kidnapping figure recorded during the preceding 12 months. Balochistan accounted for 70 of these kidnappings, representing nearly 80pc of the nationwide total.
The increase suggests that, despite the reduction in attacks and casualties, kidnapping remained a significant tactic employed by terrorist groups, the report said.
While October’s figures indicate a significant month-on-month improvement, the longer-term security picture remains challenging.
According to the report, Pakistan experienced 1,376 terrorist attacks during the first 10 months of 2026. These attacks, alongside security forces’ counterterrorism operations, collectively resulted in 3,902 deaths, including 645 security forces personnel, 65 peace committee members, 595 civilians, and 2,597 terrorists.
Another 1,953 people were injured during the January–October period, including 639 security forces personnel, 62 peace committee members, 1,055 civilians, and 197 terrorists.
Compared with the corresponding 10-month period of 2025, the number of terrorist attacks increased by 53pc, while the combined death toll rose by 36pc.
Despite the monthly decline in terrorist attacks, violence-linked fatalities rose sharply by 83pc in the third quarter of 2026, according to a recent report from the Centre for Research and Security Studies, reversing a declining trend reported in the first and second quarters as terrorism and counterterrorism deaths surged nationwide.
