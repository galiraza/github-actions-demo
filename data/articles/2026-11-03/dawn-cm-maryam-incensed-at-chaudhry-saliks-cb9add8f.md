# CM Maryam incensed at Chaudhry Salik's claims of corruption in Punjab

LAHORE: Punjab Chief Minister Maryam Nawaz on Tuesday said she would be sending a defamation notice to Minister for Overseas Pakistanis Chaudhry Salik Hussain for levelling allegations of corruption against her government.
During a private television channel talk show on Monday, the Pakistan Muslim League-Quaid (PML-Q) leader lavished praise on former Punjab chief minister Chaudhry Pervez Elahi.
However, he also took a swipe at the present provincial administration, alleging that political considerations were increasingly shaping development priorities and influencing the allocation of public funds. He said he regularly heard complaints of corruption in Punjab, alleging that a disproportionate share of the province’s development budget was being channelled towards Lahore.
Addressing a ceremony in Attock on Tuesday, CM Maryam said: “There is a federal minister … somebody sent me his statement about corruption in Punjab. Today, I am sending him a defamation notice.”
She said that she would not hear “false allegations of corruption”.
“If you have evidence, bring it forth — otherwise I will drag you to court,” she said. She also asked him why he remained a minister in a government that he considered corrupt.
The chief minister went on to say that the reason behind the anger of the PML-Q leader was neither a matter of corruption nor progress. The issue, she said, was that she changed the deputy commissioner (DC) of Gujrat a few days ago whereas Salik had wanted a DC of his choice to be appointed.
“I will not allow anyone to interfere in the will of the people,” she said.
Maryam also took exception to Salik’s comments calling Elahi the “best-ever chief minister” in Punjab.
“The Gujrat that Pervez Elahi handed me — I went there about two years ago and it was raining. Believe me … the water was up to here,” she said, gesturing to her neck.
“My Land Cruiser was completely submerged,” she said.
“It looked less like Gujrat and more like a village … now, go and look at Gujrat. Now, when it rains, the rainwater doesn’t accumulate. That work was not done by the Chaudhrys,” she said.
Speaking to the media in the wake of CM Maryam’s comments, Salik said he was sticking to his statement. In remarks aired by Geo News, he said: “God save us from pride and arrogance”.
Asked about providing evidence for the corruption allegations, he said: “Please go and listen to the interview again.”
He also refuted the claim that the row had to do with distracting from the matter of new provinces.
“It was an interview, someone asked a question and I responded honestly,” he said.
“Yes, there definitely is [corruption],” he added.
Responding to the remarks, CM Maryam said: “So, levelling false accusations is considered humility, while offering a response is deemed arrogance? Bravo!”
During his interview, Salik had also questioned lavish government spending, particularly the use of official aircraft, arguing that public representatives should remain mindful of the country’s economic difficulties. Officials using government aircraft, he said, should be required to demonstrate the tangible outcomes and public benefits of their trips.
He further called for greater ministerial accountability, proposing that the federal as well as provincial ministers should be empowered to perform their duties, but also be held answerable for their performance.
His comments had also prompted a sharp rebuttal from Punjab Information Minister Azma Bokhari, who advised the PML-Q leader not to “bite the hand that feeds him”.
Bokhari had also alleged that his displeasure stemmed from a request for appointment of a DC of his choice being turned down.
The remarks carry particular political significance given the fractured relationship between the Chaudhry family and the PTI leadership. Salik, the younger son of PML-Q president Chaudhry Shujaat Hussain, belongs to a family whose political loyalties have diverged sharply since the ouster of Imran Khan’s government in 2022 through a no-confidence vote.
While Elahi and his son, former federal minister Moonis Elahi, aligned themselves with the PTI, the Shujaat family chose to stand with the Sharifs. Salik’s elder brother, Shafay Hussain, is also part of CM Maryam’s cabinet in Punjab.
