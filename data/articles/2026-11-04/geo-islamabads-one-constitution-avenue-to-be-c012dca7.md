# Islamabad's One Constitution Avenue to be converted into five-star hotel: Naqvi

Published November 04, 2026
ISLAMABAD: Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi on Wednesday announced that the government would convert the controversial One Constitution Avenue project in Islamabad into a five-star hotel within months, following the federal cabinet’s approval of a joint investigation team’s (JIT) report.
Addressing a press conference in Islamabad, Naqvi said the government would approach a major international hotel chain to operate the building, which was originally approved for a five-star hotel.
“We will make every effort to convert this building into a five-star hotel within the next few months, which was its original purpose,” the interior minister said.
He noted that Islamabad faced a severe shortage of hotels and expressed hope that an agreement with a major hotel chain would be finalised soon.
Naqvi said the federal cabinet had approved the JIT’s findings, paving the way for the government to take legal possession of the property.
He maintained that the Capital Development Authority (CDA) already held possession of the building in official records, but the cabinet’s decision had now provided the necessary legal basis.
The minister said the CDA’s member administration would immediately assume responsibility for managing the building’s affairs, with the government’s priority being to make the hotel operational as soon as possible.
He also assured that individuals entitled to compensation under the JIT’s recommendations would receive their payments.
Naqvi thanked Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif for supporting the government’s position despite criticism following its earlier move to take possession of the property.
He maintained that the government was determined to apply the law equally, regardless of an individual’s financial or social standing.
Speaking at a separate press conference alongside Information Minister Attaullah Tarar and Minister of State Talal Chaudhry, Planning Minister Ahsan Iqbal said the federal cabinet had approved the JIT report investigating irregularities in the project.
He said the inquiry found that the project had been executed through manipulation of the bidding process, unauthorised relaxation of rules and suspicious documentation.
According to the JIT’s findings, the first auction was cancelled without approval from the CDA Board, while a subsequent auction was conducted under revised terms.
The report also found that restrictions concerning building height, covered area and parking requirements were relaxed without the board’s approval.
Iqbal said the project had originally been approved as a five-star hotel but apartments were subsequently sold in violation of the approved purpose.
The JIT report states that 254 apartments were sold through 99-year sub-leases despite the project’s approval as a hotel with serviced apartments.
The inquiry also found that the lease was awarded to a private company, although the successful bidder was a four-member consortium and the company was not among its members.
It further identified irregularities involving the removal of a bank guarantee requirement shortly before the lease agreement was signed.
The report also raised questions about the authenticity of the project’s documentation, including allegedly forged signatures and irregular registration of the lease.
Iqbal said the JIT identified Abdul Hafeez Sheikh as the alleged mastermind behind the irregularities, along with several former CDA officials and other individuals.
The report also named Kamran Lashari, Dr Faisal Awan, Azhar Ali Chaudhry, Brigadier Nusrat Ullah and the late Rai Nawaz Kharal as having acted in collusion.
The planning minister said Sheikh’s company had been blacklisted from future public-sector projects.
He maintained that the irregularities had caused losses to the national exchequer and resulted in individuals being deprived of their money.
Iqbal said the JIT had recommended a fresh, transparent auction of the land and buildings and restoration of the project’s original purpose.
He added that the project should also be renamed because the Ministry of Foreign Affairs had objected to its use of the One Constitution Avenue address, which it maintained belonged to its own premises.
The minister said the government had directed the National Accountability Bureau (NAB) to investigate the roles of those involved, including individuals who allegedly facilitated the irregularities.
He also highlighted recommendations to digitise CDA records and introduce institutional reforms to prevent similar cases.
Information Minister Attaullah Tarar said the government would ensure the protection of people who had invested in the project.
He said the JIT had identified serious shortcomings and that NAB would conduct its investigation on merit.
Tarar also confirmed that Abdul Hafeez Sheikh’s name was on the Exit Control List (ECL).
Minister of State Talal Chaudhry said the project had remained controversial for nearly two decades and that action had been taken against it twice during PML-N governments.
He said all concerned individuals had been given opportunities to substantiate their claims, but seven to eight people had failed to appear despite repeated summons.
According to Chaudhry, former prime minister and PTI founder Imran Khan was among those who did not appear.
The minister said NAB would be required to file a reference against those found responsible within three months.
He also assured affected buyers that their principal investments would be refunded along with interest calculated using the Karachi Interbank Offered Rate (KIBOR).
The JIT report recommends that the CDA compensate genuine buyers through refunds of their principal amounts, together with KIBOR-based interest or an inflation adjustment, noting that the authority had collected fees before disclaiming responsibility.
The report further recommends immediate installation of appropriate fire-safety equipment and a comprehensive overhaul of CDA procedures to prevent irregularities in future development projects.
