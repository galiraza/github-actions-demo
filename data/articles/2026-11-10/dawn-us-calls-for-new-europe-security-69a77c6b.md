# US calls for new Europe security framework as Russia, Ukraine hold first peace talks in 9 months

- Published: Wed, 11 Nov 2026 00:47:28 +0500

- Collected: 2026-11-10 20:24:15 UTC

---

A first day of US-led talks between Ukraine and Russia in Abu Dhabi ended on Tuesday, with Washington calling for a “new” security framework, as Europe fears US President Donald Trump plans a deal with Moscow on the continent’s future.
Trump’s envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff brought together the warring sides for several hours of negotiations — the first since February — that are set to continue on Wednesday.
Kushner told delegates the United States had “narrowed the gaps” between the sides — without providing details — and hoped to move towards a “solution” to Europe’s worst conflict since World War II.
In Paris, US Secretary of State Marco Rubio hailed the talks as “very productive” and called for Europeans to be eventually included at the negotiating table “because they are part of solving this problem”.
Ukraine’s Volodymyr Zelenskyy later said the talks were “constructive and quite substantive”, saying his team reported to him that humanitarian issues and security guarantees were among what was discussed.
Speaking in Copenhagen, the Ukrainian president also said it was “time” for Europe to choose a representative for the talks.
Europe has been rattled as Trump has floated a deal with Russia on rewriting Europe’s post-war security, also worrying that Washington will force Kyiv to accept a de facto defeat.
At the Abu Dhabi talks, Trump’s son-in-law Kushner said the US hoped to “create a new framework” of security between Ukraine, Russia and Europe that will secure “decades, if not centuries, of peace”.
Moscow, which several weeks before invading Ukraine issued an ultimatum to Western countries to roll back troops from eastern Europe, had also said this was a priority.
Ukraine has warned against a “Yalta 2.0”, referring to Western leaders dividing Europe with the Soviet Union after World War II, and the EU has said security arrangements can only be discussed after a ceasefire.
“Putin wants the USSR back,” Zelensky told Danish media.
Russia, which occupies swathes of its smaller neighbour, pummelled Ukraine with more attacks overnight, cutting power to thousands as temperatures plummet.
Putin has repeatedly turned down broader ceasefire offers and vowed his troops will press on in Ukraine.
Moscow pushes security review
In a sign it is serious about reviving its demand to review Europe’s decades-old security arrangement, Russia included a diplomat — Sergei Ryabkov — who logged Moscow’s pre-invasion demand for Nato to pull back from eastern Europe.
A Russian delegation source had said that settling the war was “merely one item on the agenda” and that Moscow’s main objective was reviewing security “for the entire continent”.
Kyiv’s chief negotiator Rustem Umerov met the US envoys and European, EU and Nato officials before the negotiations.
Nato’s general in Europe, Alexus Grynkewich, is also taking part.
In a joint statement, the Europeans, Ukraine, US and Nato reaffirmed their “shared commitment to a strong post-war European security architecture”.
Ukraine pushes for truces
Umerov said Kyiv’s priorities remained securing a ceasefire and security guarantees.
“Our people are waiting to end this war,” he said.
As talks continued, Ukrainian and French foreign ministers in Paris commended US efforts to push for “partial truces”, which could serve as a “first step toward a complete and unconditional ceasefire”.
Last month, a local truce in Ukraine’s Kherson region brokered by US First Lady Melania Trump became the longest pause in fighting during the war.
Kyiv is also pushing post-war support for its army.
A senior Ukrainian source told AFP that Ukraine wanted at least 10 years of financial support to sustain an 800,000-strong army able to defend any future Russian attack.
Land issue
There is huge distrust in Ukraine towards Trump’s aims.
The US leader hinted before the talks that Kyiv might have to give up land, saying: “They’re going to end up with a little bit different shape.”
Russia’s troops have advanced westward but have not been able to seize the whole of the coveted Donetsk region, and Moscow demands Kyiv surrender the parts of the embattled area it controls.
“Allowing Russia to decide the borders of Europe will be a huge mistake,” Denmark’s Mette Frederiksen said, as she hosted Zelensky on Tuesday.
The Ukrainian leader is due to meet other Nordic leaders in Denmark and earlier spoke to Finland’s Alexander Stubb, telling him Kyiv’s negotiators are doing their “best” to ensure talks “deliver results”.
Stubb is rumoured to be one of the contenders who could represent Europe at negotiations to end the war.
The Finnish leader and Norway’s Jonas Gahr Støre held a rare phone call with Putin last month, which they called “frank and substantive”.
