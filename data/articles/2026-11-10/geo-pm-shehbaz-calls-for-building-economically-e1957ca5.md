# PM Shehbaz calls for building economically stronger, energy-secure Pakistan

Published November 10, 2026
MIANWALI: Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif on Tuesday called for building an economically dominant, energy-independent and environmentally resilient Pakistan, declaring that the country will harness its resources, technology and human capital to secure a prosperous future.
Addressing the dome placement ceremony of the Chashma-5 Nuclear Power Plant (C-5), the prime minister described the milestone as a reflection of Pakistan’s determination to shape its own future through hard work, technological advancement and strategic partnerships.
The premier, while emphasising the importance of energy security, said Chashma-5 represented much more than steel and concrete — rather, it was a promise of clean, reliable, and affordable electricity that would strengthen the national economy and improve people’s lives.
He said that nuclear power would reduce Pakistan’s dependence on imported fuels and international price fluctuations while ensuring a stable electricity supply for industries, hospitals, educational institutions and households.
“Pakistan will not wait for anyone, but will build its own future through its hard work and untiring efforts,” the prime minister recalled, referring to his commitment at the project’s groundbreaking ceremony in July 2023.
He noted with satisfaction that the dome had been installed less than three and a half years after the groundbreaking, ahead of schedule, and expressed confidence that the project would also be commissioned earlier than planned.
The prime minister stressed that Pakistan, as a climate-vulnerable country, had a particular responsibility to pursue energy solutions that simultaneously advance economic development and protect the environment.
Highlighting the strategic importance of national security for economic progress, he said no country could build a prosperous future without first safeguarding its present.
He acknowledged the role of the armed forces under the leadership of Chief of Army Staff and Chief of Defence Forces Field Marshal Syed Asim Munir, in ensuring security and stability.
Referring to the country’s experience during Marka-e-Haq, the prime minister praised the professionalism, courage and resolve of the armed forces, saying a secure environment was essential for attracting investment, reviving the economy and accelerating socioeconomic development.
He also appreciated CDF Munir’s support for the government’s economic revival and development initiatives.
Underscoring Pakistan-China strategic cooperation, PM described Chashma-5 as another enduring symbol of the two countries’ all-weather friendship and their shared commitment to economic progress.
He said the project marked the seventh nuclear power unit China had helped Pakistan construct, reflecting the consistency and strength of bilateral cooperation across challenging times.
The prime minister conveyed his gratitude to Chinese President Xi Jinping, the Chinese government and the China National Nuclear Corporation for their continued support, acknowledging the contributions of Chinese engineers and their Pakistani counterparts.
He further stated that the second phase of the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC) would open new economic opportunities, strengthen strategic ties and contribute to a brighter future for both countries.
The prime minister, while congratulating the Pakistan Atomic Energy Commission (PAEC), China Atomic Energy Authority, China National Nuclear Corporation, contractors, engineers, financial institutions and other stakeholders, also acknowledged the people of Mianwali for hosting the landmark project.
The PM expressed hope that Chashma-5 would remain a lasting symbol of Pakistan-China friendship and shared aspirations for peace, stability and prosperity.
Earlier, the PAEC Chairman highlighted nuclear energy’s growing contribution to Pakistan’s electricity mix and national energy security. He said the country’s nuclear power plants were safely and efficiently generating 3,530 megawatts of electricity, helping avoid approximately 15 million tonnes of carbon dioxide emissions annually.
Nuclear energy currently accounts for around 16.6% of Pakistan’s electricity generation despite representing only 8.6% of installed power capacity. Its contribution rises to approximately 26% during winter.
The Chashma-5 project will have a generation capacity of 1,200 megawatts and is expected to begin electricity production in September 2030.
