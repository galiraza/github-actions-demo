Published November 12, 2026
Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif on Thursday expressed grief over the martyrdom of 10 Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP) police personnel, including Deputy Superintendent of Police (DSP) Haidar Zaman, in a terrorist attack in Kohat, paying tribute to their sacrifices in the fight against terrorism.
In a late-night official statement, the prime minister strongly condemned the attack in the Darabukach area of Kohat and extended his heartfelt condolences to the families of the martyred personnel.
According to police, militants belonging to Fitna al-Khawarij, acting at the instigation of criminal elements, attacked a police party returning from a joint operation with the local administration against illegal gold mining.
The police personnel fought back against the attackers, engaging them in an exchange of fire that left 18 militants dead, while 10 officers and personnel embraced martyrdom in the line of duty.
Paying tribute to the officers and personnel who lost their lives while bravely confronting the attackers, Shehbaz said the KP police had set an outstanding example of dedication to duty, courage and bravery by making the ultimate sacrifice.
He described the police personnel who laid down their lives for the country's peace and security as heroes of the nation, saying their sacrifices would always be remembered.
The prime minister also prayed for the elevation of the ranks of the deceased officers and strength and patience for their bereaved families.
Commending the police for their response to the attack, Shehbaz said the killing of 18 militants during the operation demonstrated the professional capabilities, courage and unwavering commitment of the KP police in the fight against terrorism.
He said the nexus between criminal elements and Fitna al-Khawarij was a malicious attempt to spread unrest in the country, vowing that such elements would not be allowed to achieve their nefarious objectives.
The prime minister said the KP Police had rendered unparalleled sacrifices in the war against terrorism, adding that the entire nation held its brave officers and personnel in high regard.
He also commended police personnel and officers of other law enforcement agencies for carrying out their duties with courage and steadfastness, saying the nation stood shoulder to shoulder with the security forces and law enforcement institutions in the fight against terrorism.
Reaffirming the government's resolve, Shehbaz said there would be no compromise on Pakistan's peace and stability and that the government was determined to eliminate the menace of terrorism from the country.
The martyred personnel included DSP Haidar Zaman. Twelve injured policemen were also shifted to the hospital following the attack, according to reports.
Pakistan has witnessed a sharp rise in cross-border terrorist incidents, particularly in KP and Balochistan, since the Afghan Taliban returned to power in 2021. The country recorded its highest monthly number of militant attacks in 12 years during September 2026.
According to data compiled by the Pakistan Institute for Conflict and Security Studies (PICSS), an Islamabad-based independent think tank monitoring anti-state violence in the region, 225 militant attacks were recorded across the country in September, compared with 199 in August.
This represented a 13% increase, marking the third consecutive month of rising militant attacks.
Islamabad has repeatedly urged Kabul to prevent its territory from being used by militant organisations to carry out attacks inside Pakistan.
Pakistan maintains that the Afghan Taliban regime has failed to take effective action against groups responsible for attacks on Pakistani security forces and civilians.
Despite multiple rounds of talks, Islamabad and Kabul have yet to resolve their differences, with the issue of militant groups operating from Afghan soil remaining a major point of contention between the two sides.
