# Microsoft 'deeply sorry' as outage triggers global chaos

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Fri, 13 Nov 2026 23:38:11 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2044258/microsoft-deeply-sorry-as-outage-triggers-global-chaos
- Collected: 2026-11-13 20:25:19 UTC

---

Microsoft on Friday apologized for a global outage that disrupted public services and businesses around the world, as it reported a “broad recovery” for Outlook email and other services.
The company ruled out any malicious “cyber or AI” activity as the cause of the outage, which affected several Microsoft products and disrupted health care services, delayed corporate earnings reports and left schools struggling to communicate.
“We are deeply sorry for the disruption this incident has caused our customers and partners,” Microsoft said on X, adding full services were still being restored after the outage started on Thursday.
“We are seeing broad recovery and stability across our services. This was a technical issue not related to cyber or AI / agents.”
The disruption knocked out Microsoft products in 74 countries and territories, affecting users across the Americas and parts of Europe, Asia and Africa, according to the outage tracker Downdetector.
A top UK trade union said the disruptions had a “serious impact” on the country’s health care service.
National Health Service staff receiving emergency calls were “unable to refer patient details in the normal way,” creating a massive backlog, said Rachel Harrison, public services head at the GMB union.
As the outage dragged on for hours, airlines in India warned that customer queries may not be getting through.
IndiGo and Akasa Air both said on X that their email support services had been affected and that they may not be able to receive customers’ emails.
Immigration New Zealand said the disruption had forced a halt in the processing of all visa applications.
‘Significant impact’
Japan’s economy, trade and industry minister Ryosei Akazawa warned of a “significant impact” of the outage as the Microsoft products were used by a “vast number of people”.
In London’s financial district, several people interviewed by AFP said the outage exposed their dependence on Copilot, Microsoft’s AI assistant.
“I think one of the biggest things is Copilot not working,” said Yashmit Sharma, 26, an auditor. “I think this was the main bummer. And Outlook as well, of course.”
Sheera, 40, a risk manager at a mid-sized bank, said businesses needed to be better prepared for unexpected technology failures.
“I think we need to be a little bit more careful,” said Sheera, declining to give her last name. “Anything can happen: you wake up and Outlook is not working and it’s the main communication we use for our global team.”
The Australian Computer Society (ACS) called for a national review of sovereign IT risk, with CEO Prins Ralston saying that small businesses may not have a workaround when Outlook fails.
“We need to know the systems that keep this country running won’t simply fall over without notice,” Ralston said, according to ACS publication Information Age.
On Thursday, Microsoft attributed the disruption to “elevated load” on its infrastructure.
In an updated statement on Friday, it said the “incident was introduced by an issue during an earlier network capacity upgrade that surfaced following a recent network change.”
“We have addressed the underlying issue and continue to restore service to remaining customers and regions.”
Reports of problems on Downdetector had begun to decline by 0900 GMT (2pm PKT).
Microsoft initially said it was investigating outage reports “in the Americas”, but later confirmed that the issue apparently affected “Microsoft 365 service connectivity for users in any region”.
Microsoft 365 is the company’s cloud-based subscription service that includes popular applications such as Outlook and Teams.
‘Improvements’ in North America, Europe
Microsoft said earlier that it was seeing “improvements” for Outlook email and other services. In an update, it noted “broader improvements in North America, sustained improvements across much of Europe, and encouraging recovery signals in Asia-Pacific”.
Microsoft said server issues were behind the incident and that a broader roll-out of software fixes was starting to stabilise the system.
Outlook and Teams are two of the most widely used workplace applications with hundreds of millions of users. The outage prevented people from performing essential functions such as sending emails, managing calendars and hosting virtual meetings.
It affected government agencies from Australia to Japan and the United States, caused delays at trading firms, hindered some companies from publishing earnings and prompted airlines to warn customers of communication lags, according to public statements.
Organisations that host Microsoft services on their own servers, rather than in the cloud, were somewhat spared.
In its latest update, Microsoft said efforts to improve serviceability were not progressing as expected.
“We’re identifying alternate workstreams that can be applied to increase capacity in the face of sustained traffic management imbalances,” it said.
Downdetector showed more than 25,000 reports of Outlook problems in the US at 9:30pm ET (7:30am PKT). It also showed outages for several Microsoft services in the Asia-Pacific region, including India, Australia and Japan, early Friday local time.
At its peak, Downdetector showed more than 130,000 reports of Outlook problems in the US around 1pm ET on Thursday.
In a service health notice, Microsoft said features of Microsoft 365 services that depended on Exchange Online and email were affected, while elements that did not rely on Exchange Online remained functional. Exchange Online is the company’s cloud-based messaging platform that delivers email, calendar, contacts, and tasks.
Another outage for Microsoft
The outage is one of the longest in Microsoft’s history and the second disruption to its services this year.
Microsoft said the outage specifically hit features tied to Exchange Online — its cloud-based email and scheduling hub — leaving unconnected applications operational.
The incident deepens questions about the global reliance on a handful of Big Tech companies that supply both the essential software and the cloud infrastructure that underpins it.
Microsoft shares fell 1.7 per cent before the bell, lagging the broader market. They have gained roughly 8pc so far this year.
“The bigger worry is that this is not the first Microsoft 365 outage this year,” said Josh Gilbert, a market analyst at trading platform eToro. “If reliability starts to look like a recurring problem, it hands every IT department a reason to think twice before putting even more of their business in one provider’s hands.”
Still, some analysts have questioned whether such incidents can push customers on to rivals due to the steep switching costs of replacing tools embedded so deeply in daily operations.
Microsoft faced an outage with its 365 services in January this year and also in October last year. Last month, it reported an outage of its Azure cloud platform that impacted its suite of productivity software, and a range of industries worldwide.
Trades had to be confirmed by fax
A spokesperson for the UK government said it was working with Microsoft to find a solution for the outage that hit organisations such as the NHS and London’s Metropolitan Police.
Lack of access to emails and other services was impacting the Metropolitan Police’s ability to report some crimes online, though its ability to respond to calls was not affected. NHS said its local services were turning to alternate measures such as using IT systems or paper records.
For businesses, the fallout was widespread.
European exchange operator Euronext reported email disruptions, British insurer Aviva activated backups for its earnings calls and Danish shipping giant Maersk warned customers of delayed updates.
American AI giants OpenAI and Anthropic also faced issues with Microsoft connectors, while Indian airlines IndiGo, Akasa Air and Air India said their email support services were impacted.
The outage also disrupted fund management at Taiwanese securities firms, life insurers, and investment trust companies. One of the companies went as far as cancelling a bond repurchase transaction, a source familiar with the situation told Reuters.
Another Taiwanese financial company resorted to using fax to confirm trades, a second source said.
In Australia, where the spy agency and federal police were among organisations caught in the outage, Cyber Security Minister Tony Burke said Microsoft does not suspect a cyber attack.
“They believe they are dealing in some ways similar to what we saw with respect to CrowdStrike where there is an update that has gone awry,” Burke said at a press conference.
“The impact that they are dealing with … is very much on email systems,” the minister added.
Impact on email systems
Australia’s spy agency and federal police were among organisations caught up in the outage on Friday.
The CrowdStrike malfunction of 2024 was one of the largest internet disruptions, hobbling technology systems in hospitals, banks, and airports, highlighting the vulnerability of the world’s interconnected technologies.
The US House of Representatives said the outage impacted its users and its engineers were working with Microsoft.
The company had earlier in the day pointed to a number of servers being unexpectedly unavailable and said it was not driven by an increase in traffic.
Microsoft had then said the restored servers remained stable so far and that it was shifting traffic to alternate routes wherever possible while streamlining the process to bring the remaining impacted servers back online.
“As capacity is restored, users may experience a gradual recovery as their clients reconnect,” Microsoft said in a previous statement.
Businesses feel the impact
OpenAI said it was investigating an issue with Microsoft connectors in which new connections may be broken and tools are not working as expected.
Anthropic said it had identified an issue with an upstream provider affecting its Microsoft 365 connector.
Microsoft last month reported an outage of its Azure cloud platform that impacted the tech giant’s suite of productivity software, and a range of industries worldwide.
Electric truck maker Workhorse Group, which was expected to report third-quarter results on November 12, rescheduled it to November 16 citing business disruptions caused by the global Microsoft outage, while Switzerland’s Richemont briefly delayed its first-half results announcement on Friday.
