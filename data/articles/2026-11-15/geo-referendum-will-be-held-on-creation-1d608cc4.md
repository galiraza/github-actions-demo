# Referendum will be held on creation of new provinces, says Mustafa Kamal

Published November 15, 2026
KARACHI/ISLAMABAD: Muttahida Qaumi Movement-Pakistan (MQM-P) leader Mustafa Kamal has said a referendum will be held on the creation of new provinces, describing December as a crucial month for the proposed move.
Addressing party office-bearers and workers in Karachi’s District Central, Federal Health Minister Kamal said Pakistan was standing at a decisive historical juncture and that “dreams of the past are now turning into reality”.
He alleged that opponents of new provinces wanted to pit the city’s and province’s linguistic communities against one another.
Kamal said the time had come to end the feudal, landlord and capitalist systems in the country. He also claimed that the Pakistan Peoples Party (PPP) did not represent the entirety of Sindh and that Sindhi nationalists were enemies of the Sindhi people.
Meanwhile, government sources say preparations are complete to grant Islamabad a special administrative status, with a new governance model proposed for the federal capital.
Under the proposal, Islamabad could be made a special-status territory or a model administrative unit, with a mayor serving as its administrative head. The elected body of the proposed unit could comprise 25 to 30 members.
The sources said the plan also envisaged expanding Islamabad’s geographical boundaries. Proposals under consideration include incorporating parts of Haripur district in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, some areas of Rawalpindi and parts of NA-51 in Murree into the federal capital.
However, a proposal to include Gwadar or other ports in Islamabad’s administrative structure has reportedly been dropped.
The remarks come amid a renewed debate over the creation of new provinces, with Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi recently describing a referendum as the “best solution” to the issue.
Naqvi said public opinion should determine whether new provinces should be created, adding that all political parties, including the PPP and Pakistan Muslim League-Nawaz (PML-N), needed to be taken on board.
Defence Minister Khawaja Asif has also backed the creation of new provinces on administrative grounds, saying even 12 could be considered.
The discussion follows reports that a proposal to reorganise Pakistan into 12 provinces is under consideration, with Punjab potentially divided into four provinces, while Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Balochistan could each have three and Sindh two.
However, no final decision has been taken on the proposed boundaries, administrative structures or constitutional process.
