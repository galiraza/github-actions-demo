# CDA finalises revised PC-I for Islamabad cricket stadium at Rs16.7bn

- Published: Mon, 16 Nov 2026 07:11:50 +0500

- Collected: 2026-11-16 07:36:50 UTC

ISLAMABAD: The Capital Development Authority (CDA) has finalised the modified PC-I of the Islamabad Cricket Stadium, increasing the estimated cost of the project from Rs11.9 billion to Rs16.7bn for both packages, covering the grey structure, finishing and electrical works.
“Modified PC-Is for both phases have been completed and will soon be presented before the upcoming CDA Development Working Party (DWP) for approval,” a CDA officer told Dawn.
He said the cost of the project, which was previously estimated at Rs11.9bn in an unapproved PC-I, had increased to Rs16.7bn after the inclusion of additional components.
These included the grey structure, finishing, lighting, heating, ventilation and air-conditioning (HVAC) systems, preparation of the ground and pitches, and a security camera system.
The officer said the earlier PC-I envisaged a seating capacity of 25,000. However, when the CDA invited requests for proposals earlier this year, it was decided to increase the capacity. Several other components had also been added to the modified PC-I, he added.
Stadium to be developed in two phases; CDA aims to complete entire project within a year
Asked about the delay in the groundbreaking, the officer said the modification of the PC-I had nothing to do with the commencement of construction.
“The bidding process for the grey structure and road network has already been completed, and groundbreaking of Phase-I (grey structure) will be held soon. It will be completed within six months,” he said.
Another CDA officer said that after approval of the PC-I, the authority would initiate the tendering process for Phase-II as well.
“We will try to complete both phases, covering the entire project, within one year,” he said.
Recently, Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi had announced in a press
talk that the groundbreaking of the stadium would take place within 10 days.
A Pakistan Cricket Board (PCB) official indicated that Pakistan was likely to host some matches of the ICC Women’s T20 World Cup 2028 at the proposed Islamabad stadium.
“If the Islamabad stadium project is completed on time, we will organise some matches of the Women’s World Cup in 2028 in the capital,” the official said.
According to the CDA, the stadium will have a seating capacity of 35,000, making it the largest cricket stadium in Pakistan. It will be a replica of the Dubai Cricket Stadium, the official said.
The CDA is constructing the stadium at the foothills of the Margalla Hills near Sector D-12.
Some months ago, the authority received a bid of Rs8.9bn from a joint venture of Habib-ZKB-EA.
Of the total amount, Rs5.8bn was allocated for the three-tier stadium structure and Rs3.1bn for external works, including three kilometres of three-plus-three-lane roads and associated infrastructure.
Later, the CDA secured a rebate of Rs400 million from the construction firm, bringing the contract amount down to Rs8.5bn.
It is relevant to note that the site selected for the stadium falls in Zone III, where construction activity is generally banned and local landowners are not allowed to build houses on their land.
However, the CDA maintains that sports and recreational activities are permissible in the zone and that the construction of a cricket stadium falls within the permitted activities.
In the past, after receiving land from the CDA, the PCB had initiated construction of a stadium at Shakarparian. However, the project was abandoned following directions from the Supreme Court.
This time, the CDA itself is undertaking the construction of the cricket stadium.
