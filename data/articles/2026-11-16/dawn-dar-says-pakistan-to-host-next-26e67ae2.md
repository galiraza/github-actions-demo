# Dar says Pakistan to host next Makkah alliance summit later this month

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Mon, 16 Nov 2026 21:18:15 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2044957/dar-says-pakistan-to-host-next-makkah-alliance-summit-later-this-month
- Collected: 2026-11-16 16:42:20 UTC

---

Pakistan will host the next meeting of the Makkah Defence Alliance’s apex committee, likely later this month, Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar said on Monday.
He made the remarks while answering a query during a press briefing regarding a ministerial meeting of the Economic Cooperation Organisation (ECO) held in Islamabad.
Asked about regional security architecture, Dar recalled the Aug 7 signing of the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement between Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and Turkiye. The pact empowers the three states to treat an attack on any one of them as an attack on all.
Providing details on the alliance’s progress, Dar noted that its apex committee, called the Strategic Political and Defence Committee (SPDC), “will actually be meeting, hopefully [at the] end of this month, in Islamabad”.
“We offered in our inaugural meeting that our next formal meeting will be held in Islamabad and everybody accepted,” he said, noting that an emergency meeting of the SPDC was held in Riyadh last month amid Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia.
The SPDC comprises the foreign and defence ministers of the three countries, as well as their top military officials.
In response to the question about a possible new security architecture for the region, Dar said: “Well, let me be very honest with you. I think time and again, different regions, including this region, have witnessed that the need and the best way to have regional security is to have some sort of security at the local or domestic architecture.
“In other words, this region should have its own security architecture. Maybe this is a beginning.”
He remarked that the “global institutions who claim to be masters of having the security arrangement globally were not created in one day”, adding that the Makkah pact was signed for self-defence.
The foreign minister highlighted that the pact does not violate Article 51 of the United Nations Charter, which allows individual and collective defence.
“Now, it is not restricted that these three countries will have a monopoly and will not have, will not entertain ever [any] other membership,” he said.
DPM Dar said there was a “transition time of setting up the infrastructure” of the alliance, as part of which various decisions were made, including related to the secretary general’s office and the permanent secretariat in Riyadh.
He added that a working committee has also been formed, comprising one person each from the foreign, defence and military offices of the three countries.
“That committee has been working; it has had a few sessions. And the last meeting, hopefully, will be in November, where they will work out all the nitty-gritty regarding detailed SOPs etc,” he said.
“I’m very hopeful that, God willing, this is not going to benefit just these three countries. New membership will be entertained after we are established in about one year,” Dar further said, adding that membership requests of those who will agree to the alliance’s principles will be entertained.
ECO Secretary General Asad Majeed Khan and Information Minister Attaullah Tarar were also present at the media briefing.
Islamabad has ‘not given up’ on US-Iran peace efforts
DPM Dar also reaffirmed that Islamabad has not given up on its diplomatic efforts for a peaceful resolution to the US-Iran war.
Replying to a query, Dar said he had “very candid, very frank, very brotherly, very comprehensive discussions” with Iran’s FM Abbas Araghchi on the sidelines of the ECO meeting, which they both attended.
Noting that the two discussed the latest regional situation, the deputy premier said Iran had “fast-tracked” its seven-day proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz to a two-day plan.
“So, the response from the United States is pending. We have not given up hope or efforts. We are still doing our best [so] that the situation gets sorted out,” DPM Dar stated.
He also expressed hope that, now that Washington was no longer busy with its midterm elections, it would respond to the proposal “very soon”.
Asked how ECO planned to navigate the US sanctions on Iran, considering that the organisation’s secretariat was based in Tehran, Dar said, “Sanctions are not going to be there forever.
“And once sanctions are over, the whole world will just land there; I can guarantee,” he maintained, recalling that a British prime minister was the first person to visit Tripoli when sanctions against Libya were withdrawn.
“I think the legal side, the sanctions, international abiding of the sanctions … Pakistan has always, you know, followed international law, UN Charter and followed whatever resolutions have been decided and adopted in the UN Security Council,” Dar affirmed.
He expressed his hope that the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding signed between Iran and the US on June 18 was implemented.
Dar remarked that the seven-day and two-day Hormuz proposals were “actually extracted” from the Islamabad MoU.
“There’s nothing new. It’s only fast-tracking things here and there, showing confidence-building measures, taking actions by one side and opening of the Hormuz by the other.
“So I think let’s hope for the best. We haven’t given up hope,” the foreign minister reiterated.
In their meeting on the sidelines, Dar and Araghchi reviewed bilateral relations and exchanged views on regional and international developments of mutual interest, the Foreign Office (FO) said.
“They reaffirmed the strong and brotherly ties between Pakistan and Iran and reiterated their commitment to further strengthening bilateral cooperation through sustained high-level engagement.”
According to FO, Araghchi congratulated Pakistan on the successful hosting of the ECO ministers’ meeting and “appreciated the productive deliberations, warm hospitality and excellent arrangements”.
Both sides reaffirmed their commitment to advancing ECO’s priorities, particularly regional cooperation and connectivity, the statement added.
On his part, Dar emphasised that “dialogue and diplomacy remain the only viable means of addressing outstanding issues, preventing escalation and achieving lasting peace and stability”.
Stressing the value of continued engagement and consultations, both sides agreed to maintain close contact on matters of mutual interest.
Months of diplomacy have failed to resolve the war, which began with surprise US-Israeli strikes on Tehran that assassinated Iran’s supreme leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei.
During more than eight months of war, Iran has restricted shipping through the Strait of Hormuz — a key oil trade route — and struck infrastructure and US assets across the Middle East.
On Sunday, Bahrain was rattled by the second Iranian drone attack in three days, ending a long lull in strikes during the war.
On Friday, following this month’s US strikes on Qeshm island in Hormuz, Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said their forces had targeted a US early warning radar system in Bahrain.
