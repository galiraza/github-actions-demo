# 'I may not return alive': In open letter to top judges, Imran pleads for medical treatment for him and Bushra Bibi

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Mon, 16 Nov 2026 19:15:48 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2044922/i-may-not-return-alive-in-open-letter-to-top-judges-imran-pleads-for-medical-treatment-for-him-and-bushra-bibi

ISLAMABAD: Incarcerated PTI founder and former prime minister Imran Khan on Monday appealed for medical treatment for himself and his wife Bushra Bibi in an open letter to the chief justices of the Federal Constitutional Court (FCC), the Supreme Court (SC) and the Islamabad High Court (IHC).
The letter was shared on the social media platform X by Imran’s lawyer Barrister Salman Safdar, who had met the ex-premier at Adiala jail on Nov 2 and Nov 11. The letter said that during the Nov 11 meeting, Imran had directed Safdar to “carry his words to your lordships”.
“I have conveyed this letter faithfully and solely on these instructions. The words that follow are his [Imran’s],” the letter said, which also bore Safdar’s signature.
In the letter, Imran detailed his own deteriorating health condition as well as his wife’s, pleading with the courts to grant them access to medical treatment.
“I spent my life building hospitals so that those who could not afford treatment would receive it free of cost. Today, I ask only that my wife and I be treated,” he said.
Imran said that he was writing from a “solitary cell”, as that was now “the only way my voice can leave these walls”.
“I do not write as a former prime minister, nor as the head of a political party. I have given my life to Pakistan. I served this nation on the cricket field, in social work and philanthropy and as its 22nd prime minister. For seventy years, not a single criminal case stood against my name,” he said.
“Then, after the age of 70, came an unprecedented and ruthless campaign of political victimisation: countless prosecutions, more than a hundred appearances before the courts, pre-arrest bail sought again and again, and an assassination attempt which, by the grace of Allah, I survived. Through all of it, I chose the path of law. I decided that I would fight every one of these politically motivated cases in the courts and I never stopped,” he said.
He said that from “time to time” he was granted relief from the courts that “renewed my faith that justice would prevail”.
“After the constitutional amendments, however, that relief became narrow, and then it closed off altogether. It grieves me deeply to say that, when I needed it most, the system I had placed my trust in did not come to my aid. Within the walls of Adiala, my wife and I have faced criminal trials one after the other, and our appeals still await decision. Yet I have never once sought relief on medical grounds, nor used illness as a tactic. I fought on the merits, with confidence, and I still do,” he said.
Imran went on to say that “everything changed” after the judgement in the Toshakhana-2 case.
The ex-premier and his wife were sentenced to 17 years in jail in the case, which pertained to the purchase of an expensive Bulgari jewellery set, gifted to Imran by the Saudi crown prince during an official visit in May 2021, at a throwaway price.
“I was totally isolated, disconnected and held incommunicado from the outside world, in torturous solitary confinement for up to twenty-two hours a day, in disregard of every constitutional right and every entitlement the Prison Rules guarantee a prisoner: no family, no lawyers, no call to my sons, no books and no television,” Imran said in his letter.
He said that after months without access to his lawyer, Safdar was allowed to meet him on Feb 10 after the SC appointed him amicus curiae.
“He found that I had lost 85 per cent of the vision in my right eye and that neither my family nor my counsel had been told. After that visit, the disconnect returned, and the jail administration, acting vindictively, isolated me once again,” he said.
Imran said that he had only been allowed to meet his lawyer four times during the year, while Bushra Bibi had only been allowed to meet her counsel once. He asserted that this was a violation of the rules and the basic rights of every prisoner, adding that it had left them “unable to defend ourselves in the very cases we are fighting”.
“Prolonged solitary confinement and the absence of any meaningful human interaction have taken a heavy toll on my mental health. This is not my claim alone: the jail’s own medical board has recorded my anxiety and advised more frequent contact with my wife and family, reading material and television. That advice has not been followed,” he said.
Imran alleged that his weekly meeting with his wife was skipped by jail authorities “without reason or explanation”. He said that he had also learnt that his sisters were in custody, adding that he was not allowed to meet them. He said his sons, Kasim and Sulaiman, were also not allowed to call in contradiction of court orders.
“No one in the jail is permitted to respond to me or speak to me, not when I offer them salaam and not when I recite a verse of the Holy Quran. The purpose, I can only conclude, is to make me ill and to break my will,” he said.
Imran said that despite this, he was more concerned about Bushra Bibi’s conditions. He said that in April, she suffered a retinal detachment and underwent a “complicated eye surgery in secrecy” and was sent back to solitary confinement.
“When I saw her this month, she could neither bear the light nor open her eyes, and without another surgery she may lose her sight permanently. Her records are withheld, her doctors denied, her suspension of sentence unheard, despite the law’s compassion for women prisoners,” he said.
Imran said that on Aug 18, the SC directed that he be shifted to Shifa International Hospital within two days, a medical board be constituted to examine him and that he be allowed interactions with his family.
“That order has not been implemented. My case now rests on the docket of the FCC, awaiting a hearing,” he said. He said that his appeal in the Al-Qadir trust case was being taken up ahead of older ones concerning the health of the couple and unimplemented court orders.
“It is my wife’s failing sight, and that unimplemented order of the honourable SC, that compel me to write. In solitary confinement, my blood pressure has become uncontrolled: 130, then 150, then 170, and last week 192 and 193 on Nov 10. The doctors now tell me that I am highly vulnerable to a brain haemorrhage and cardiac arrest and that my heart has already suffered damage. At 74, this unrelenting state is taking me to a place from which I may not return alive,” he said.
“I have fought impossible legal battles in countless cases, and I have not stopped. But the system has stopped giving me justice,” he lamented.
“I do not ask your Lordships for any favour. All I request as justice is the implementation of the orders of the honourable SC and proper treatment for our medical conditions. I will only remain able to prove my innocence if I stay alive. I appeal to your Lordships, on the most basic grounds of human compassion: give me justice before it is too late,” he concluded.
‘Incredibly harrowing’
Meanwhile, Imran’s son Kasim said in a post on X that it was “devastating to read this letter from my father”.
His other son, Sulaiman, described the letter as “incredibly harrowing”.
“My father’s appeal to receive proper medical care is in no way a request for special treatment. It is the absolute bare minimum required to comply with internationally recognised standards for human rights,” he said. He called on authorities to shift Imran to Shifa International hospital for treatment “before it is too late”.
The leaders of the opposition in the National Assembly and the Senate, Mehmood Khan Achakzai and Allama Raja Nasir Abbas expressed “grave concern, sorrow and regret” over the conditions and treatment described in Imran’s letter, calling it a “test for our entire justice system”.
“It is deeply regrettable that a former prime minister who has consistently approached the courts to face the cases against him and fight his legal battles is now compelled to appeal to the higher judiciary through an open letter merely to secure his basic human needs, medical care and the right to meet his family,” they said in a joint statement.
“The fundamental responsibility of the state and the relevant authorities is to ensure the protection of every person in custody’s life, health and human dignity, not to add to their difficulties and suffering,” they said.
The joint statement also termed the concerns regarding Bushra Bibi “profoundly regrettable”.
“We strongly urge the higher judiciary to take immediate notice of all the matters raised in Imran’s letter and take effective judicial action to protect his life, health, liberty and fundamental rights,” the opposition leaders said.
They clarified that their demand was “not for any special concession or extraordinary facility, but for compliance with the Constitution, the law and court orders”.
Speaking to Dawn, PTI Information Secretary Sheikh Waqas Akram called on the courts to take notice of Imran’s letter.
“His case should also be seen on humanitarian grounds and medical treatment for him and Bushra Bibi should commence immediately,” he said.
We urge the court to take notice and provide legal rights to Imran without further delay,” he said.
‘Claims contrary to facts’
Meanwhile, Parliamentary Affairs Minister Tariq Fazal Chaudhry said that claims regarding the “denial of medical facilities, treatment and other basic amenities in prison are contrary to the facts”.
“Imran being provided with the necessary facilities, including meals of his choice, books and television. In fact, he is currently among the most privileged prisoners in Pakistan,” the minister said.
“His health is reviewed and monitored weekly by qualified medical specialists. Whenever required, he is also examined at leading medical facilities outside the prison,” he said.
Chaudhry added that Imran had been taken to the Pakistan Institute of Medical Sciences “eight times for medical examinations, where doctors declared his health satisfactory”.
“A doctor from London, who also practises in Pakistan, was specifically called to examine his blood pressure. According to the medical assessments, his blood pressure is satisfactory,” he said.
“It is the government’s responsibility to ensure proper healthcare, medical supervision and protection of the legal rights of Imran, just as it is for every other prisoner. The government has neither neglected his medical care in the past nor will it allow any negligence in the future,” he said.
The minister said that the PTI should refrain from turning a “sensitive matter like Imran’s health into a political propaganda campaign and should instead present facts responsibly”.
“Political narratives cannot overturn legal proceedings or provide anyone with an escape from legal accountability,” he said.
Imran and Bushra Bibi’s health
Imran has been in prison since August 5, 2023, while his wife was arrested on January 17, 2025, in a separate case in which the couple were sentenced.
The IHC has allowed Imran twice-a-week meetings — on Tuesdays and Thursdays — with his family, lawyers and other associates. Despite multiple court directives, Imran’s meetings with his family and party leaders have been largely restricted.
Imran’s eye ailment — right central retinal vein occlusion — came to light in late January, following which he has been taken to hospital multiple times.
On Aug 18, the SS directed the government to shift the jailed ex-premier to Shifa International Hospital in Islamabad, a day after the superintendent of the Adiala jail — where Imran is imprisoned — submitted a report to the court on the ex-premier’s health condition.
However, the government took the PTI founder to Pims and subsequently transported him back to prison. At the time, Information Minister Attaullah Tarar had blamed “the security situation created by PTI workers on the way to and outside” the hospital for the diversion. The PTI has denied the allegations and accused the government of deliberately flouting the court’s orders.
Meanwhile, Bushra Bibi suffered a retinal detachment in April and was taken to the hospital for surgery. She has since approached the courts seeking an immediate medical examination by an ophthalmologist and permission to receive treatment from doctors of her choice at her own expense amid concerns over her deteriorating eyesight.
According to the former first lady, doctors who examined her had diagnosed her condition as a “mature cataract” and advised that another eye surgery would be required.
