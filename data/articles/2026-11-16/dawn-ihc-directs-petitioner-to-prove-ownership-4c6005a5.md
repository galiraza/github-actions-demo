# IHC directs petitioner to prove ownership of Bari Imam House to CDA

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Mon, 16 Nov 2026 18:48:33 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2044945/ihc-directs-petitioner-to-prove-ownership-of-bari-imam-house-to-cda
- Collected: 2026-11-16 16:42:20 UTC

---

ISLAMABAD: The Islamabad High Court (IHC) on Monday disposed of a writ petition challenging the sealing of Bari Imam House in Nurpur Shahan, directing the petitioner to approach the Capital Development Authority (CDA) with documented proof of ownership, while leaving the authority free to proceed in accordance with law if he failed to do so.
Justice Ayyaz Shaukat, in a judgment authored on the petition filed by Muhammad Akmal, caretaker of the premises located opposite the shrine of Bari Imam Sarkar, declined to grant the declaratory relief sought.
He observed that the core dispute — whether the structure stands on exempted shrine land or on land acquired by the CDA in 1963 — turned on contested questions of fact that could not be resolved in constitutional jurisdiction on bare assertions.
The court noted that the petitioner had produced no ownership documents, and that his counsel had candidly conceded that the relevant record lay with the CDA and the Islamabad Capital Territory (ICT) administration.
The CDA had sealed the premises on Oct 30 in a joint operation with the district administration, maintaining that the property is an unauthorised encroachment on government land acquired under Award No. 4/DC CDA/1963, with compensation paid to the landowners.
According to CDA, the land comprising Khasras No. 435, 582, 591, 592 and 593 formed part of that acquisition and contains no building by the name of “Bari Imam House” in the official record. Meanwhile, the shrine’s land is recorded on entirely different Khasra numbers — 277, 665, 677, 678, 679 and 680 — across the road.
The petitioner, by contrast, had claimed that the premises comprise a mosque, a langar khana and wuzu (ablution) facilities, and that the land was exempted shrine property never acquired by the CDA.
He also alleged that the sealing took place without any notice or opportunity of hearing, in violation of several constitutional provisions.
The court, however, refrained from a pronouncement on the merits of either claim.
Noting that the petitioner’s grievance regarding the absence of notice and the CDA’s objection to his locus standi could both be addressed through a fair and expeditious determination based on documents, the court directed the petitioner to submit his ownership documents, if any, before the CDA within five days.
The CDA was then required to afford him a hearing, consider the documents alongside its own record, and decide the matter by a speaking order within two days thereafter.
The court clarified that if the petitioner does not submit the documents or does not wish to do so, the CDA will be at liberty to proceed in accordance with the law.
During the hearing, the petitioner’s counsel conceded that the property was not his own and that he was merely a caretaker of the shrine, while also stating that he did not possess the relevant documents and that they were held by the CDA.
The CDA’s counsel, Kashif Ali Malik, argued that the petition was not maintainable and that the occupation amounted to encroachment on CDA land.
The interim order dated Nov 3, which had directed the de-sealing of the premises, stands merged in the final order. The court had restrained the officials from carrying out any “demolition” until the next hearing.
Any person aggrieved by the CDA’s decision may avail the remedy available under the law, and the court expressly stated that nothing in its order shall be construed as an expression of opinion on the merits of title or possession.
