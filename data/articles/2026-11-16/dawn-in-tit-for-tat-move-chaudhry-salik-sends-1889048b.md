# In tit-for-tat move, Chaudhry Salik sends CM Maryam, Azma Bokhari defamation notices

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Mon, 16 Nov 2026 20:38:01 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2044932/in-tit-for-tat-move-chaudhry-salik-sends-cm-maryam-azma-bokhari-defamation-notices
- Collected: 2026-11-16 16:42:20 UTC

---

In a tit-for-tat move, PML-Q leader and Overseas Pakistanis Minister Chaudhry Salik Hussain on Monday also sent a defamation notice to Punjab Chief Minister Maryam Nawaz.
After the PML-Q leader levelled allegations of corruption against the incumbent Punjab government, CM Maryam sent a Rs5 billion defamation notice to him, demanding he retract his remarks.
Alongside furnishing a reply to that notice, Salik said on Monday that he had also sent two legal notices for defamation to Maryam and Punjab Information Minister Azma Bokhari.
“A formal reply to the legal notice has been duly sent,” Salik said in a post on X.
“Attempts to intimidate through legal notices will neither silence nor deter me from raising concerns on matters of public interest and their well-being,” he reaffirmed.
The minister went on to reiterate the allegations: “The province continues to suffer from gross mismanagement and rampant corruption, issues that demand a ‘little’ self-reflection and accountability, not intimidation.”
The PML-Q leader also reiterated the “commitment to transparency in the interest of Punjab and Pakistan”.
In a separate X post, Salik announced, “Further, I have also sent through my legal counsel two legal notices for defamation. One to Mrs Maryam Nawaz Sharif and the other one to Mrs Azma Zahid Bokhari.”
In an interview last week, Salik stated that he stood by his earlier remarks regarding corruption in Punjab and also termed Maryam’s statements in response to him as “below the belt”.
The PML-Q minister also regretted that fiery statements by Bokhari had worsened the row.
Reply to CM’s defamation notice
Salik’s response to CM Maryam’s notice, a copy of which is available with Dawn, stated that the minister’s remarks were “an expression of opinion on a matter that is fair and in the public interest”.
“Even otherwise, and without prejudice to the foregoing, the remarks would not have fallen within the scope of ‘defamation’ at all,” it contended. It said that the notice from CM Maryam wrongly portrayed Salik’s remarks as “assertions of fact”.
“Even assuming, for the sake of argument, that the remarks were statements of fact (which they were not), they would not amount to defamation because, by definition, defamation can only occur by a ‘false or untrue statement’, whereas the remarks, even if they were assertions of facts, would not have been false or untrue,” it said.
The response also cited several public statements by CM Maryam in which she acknowledged the existence of corruption in the province. It argued that the chief minister’s own statements supported Salik’s position that corruption existed in Punjab and that discussing the issue was a matter of legitimate public interest.
In his response, the PML-Q leader termed CM Maryam’s notice “an attempt to weaponise the law of defamation to stifle legitimate discussion and criticism on matters of public interest and importance”.
“It is well established across common law jurisdictions that governments, government bodies and public authorities, particularly those that are democratically elected, cannot maintain an action for defamation,” it stated.
“The demand for five billion rupees is, in any event, wholly baseless. It bears no relation to any genuine claim for compensation,” the response said, adding that the objective was to “intimidate public representatives and to warn others, including the press, against speaking about corruption in Punjab”.
“The notice is legally misconceived, untenable and, in several respects, verges on the absurd,” the response went on to say, adding that it appeared to be a calculated attempt to “intimidate and exert undue pressure” on Salik with the “ulterior objective of stifling legitimate discourse on the serious and important issue of corruption”.
Rejecting the demands made in the notice, the PML-Q leader declared that he would not withdraw the statements made in the interview, apologise, publish any retraction or pay any sum.
Notice to Bokhari
Meanwhile, the minister also sent a defamation notice to Bokhari over “the false, baseless and defamatory” remarks by the Punjab information minister on her X account on Nov 2, wherein she had termed Salik’s criticism as the consequence of not having his choice be appointed the deputy commissioner (DC) of Gujrat.
The said statements constitute an “unwarranted attack” on reputation and had been made and propagated without any lawful justification, the defamation notice said.
It said that Bokhari’s statement, which insinuated that the remarks made in the interview were a consequence of the change of the Gujrat DC, was “clearly false and untrue”.
The notice called upon Bokhari to withdraw the allegation that Salik sought the appointment of a DC and tender an unconditional apology within seven days.
It also demanded the removal of the alleged defamatory statements within seven days from the accounts and channels within Bokhari’s control.
The notice said that if the Punjab information minister failed to provide a satisfactory response within the stated period, Salik reserved the right to institute appropriate proceedings and to seek the relief available under the law, including damages and costs.
What did Salik say?
During a talk show on a private television channel, the PML-Q leader praised the performance of his uncle Chaudhry Pervez Elahi as a former Punjab chief minister.
He further criticised the incumbent provincial administration headed by CM Maryam, alleging that he regularly heard complaints of corruption.
He also questioned lavish government spending, particularly the use of an official aircraft, arguing that public representatives should remain mindful of the country’s economic difficulties.
Incensed by Salik’s allegations of corruption, Maryam sent a Rs5bn defamation notice to him.
She challenged him to provide evidence for his corruption claims, threatening legal action if he failed to do so. She also questioned why he remained a minister in a government he considered corrupt.
Both CM Maryam and the Punjab information minister alleged that Salik’s displeasure stemmed from his request to appoint a deputy commissioner of his choice in Gujrat being turned down.
Salik later clarified that his interview was recorded on Sept 25, before the new DC was appointed, suggesting CM Maryam’s information was incorrect.
He also regretted the withdrawal of his security by the provincial government after the incident.
