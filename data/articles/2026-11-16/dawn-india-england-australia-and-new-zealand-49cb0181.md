# India, England, Australia and New Zealand to host all ICC events from 2028-31

LAHORE: Pakistan failed to get any event of the International Cricket Council (ICC) at least till 2031, as the game’s world governing body announced its activity programme for the 2028-2035 cycle on Sunday.
According to a press release issued by the ICC, India, England, Australia and New Zealand swept all the events to be held from 2028 to 2031. From 2032 to 2035, the venues of the remaining events will be decided later.
India, having a major influence in the ICC’s affairs, will host the 2029 ICC Champions Trophy and the 2031 ICC World Cup jointly with Bangladesh.
Australia and New Zealand will organise the 2028 T20 World Cup. England will continue to host the ICC Test Championship final in 2029 and 2030, while it will also hold the 2030 T20 World Cup along with Ireland and Scotland.
Surprisingly, Pakistan, an important stakeholder of the cricketing world which successfully hosted the eight-team Champions Trophy last year, was not given any ICC event.
“The decisions were made with increasing opportunities for Full and Associate Members in mind, strengthening qualification pathways and enhancing competitive standards across international cricket,” the ICC said in a press release.
Commenting on the announcement of the activity programme, Sanjog Gupta, the ICC chief executive, said: “The ICC events portfolio and schedule are critical components of cricket’s global architecture and deliver elevated context, competitiveness and consequence to the ecosystem. These tournaments serve as pinnacle moments for fans and players alike, with the associated pathways driving aspiration and access for the broader cricketing landscape.”
MARQUEE MEN’S EVENTS SCHEDULE
The ICC Board approved the windows for established ICC marquee men’s events until 2035. The confirmed schedule runs from the 2028 Men’s T20 World Cup in Australia and New Zealand up to the 2035 Men’s World Cup.
The 2027 Men’s World Cup Qualifier, scheduled for February-March, will feature 10 teams and 23 matches. The tournament will comprise two groups of five, followed by semi-finals and a final, with the top four teams qualifying for the World Cup.
The winners will progress directly to the 2027 Men’s World Cup Group Stage, while the three remaining teams will compete in the Super Series to determine the final Group Stage spot.
The 16-team Global Qualifier, scheduled to be played during the first quarter of 2028, will feature 31 matches, with four groups of four competing across the group stage, quarter-finals, semi-finals and the final. A total of eight teams will qualify directly to the 2028 Men’s T20 World Cup from the event. The highest-placed team from each region will qualify, subject to minimum performance criteria, with the remaining places going to the next highest-placed teams overall.
2028 WOMEN’S T20 WORLD CUP QUALIFIER
With the qualification framework for the 2028 Women’s T20 World Cup approved in May 2026, regional allocations for the Global Qualifier have now been confirmed.
The Netherlands and Scotland, having participated in the 2026 tournament without securing automatic qualification for 2028, will be joined at the Global Qualifier by eight teams from regional qualifying.
Those eight teams will advance through five regional qualification events scheduled for 2027, with two spots each up for grabs across Africa, Asia and the East Asia-Pacific, and one more from both the Americas and Europe. The top two teams from the Global Qualifier will secure the final places at the ICC Women’s T20 World Cup 2028.
Host nations and dates have been confirmed for the final qualification events for the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics. The men’s qualifier for LA28 will take place from Feb 5-13, 2028 in Bangladesh, prior to the ICC Women’s Qualifier, which will run from Feb 26 to March 5, 2028 in the United States.
The events constitute the final stage of the qualification pathway for cricket’s return to the Olympic programme at LA28. They will each feature eight teams — spread across two groups of four — and 13 matches. Each team will play the other teams in their group once, with the group winners progressing to the final. The winners of the final will secure the final qualification berth for the LA28 Olympic Games.
