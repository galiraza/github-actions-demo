# SBP says inflation to ease to 5-7pc target range during FY2027

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Mon, 16 Nov 2026 17:25:02 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2044936/sbp-says-inflation-to-ease-to-5-7pc-target-range-during-fy2027
- Collected: 2026-11-16 16:42:20 UTC

---

The State Bank of Pakistan (SBP) said on Monday that consumer price inflation was expected to gradually ease towards the medium-term target range of five to seven per cent during financial year 2027.
Real GDP growth would remain in the range of 3.5-4.5pc, the bank said in a yearly report, which added the country’s current account deficit was likely to be in the range of 0-1pc of GDP in the financial year.
It projected the fiscal deficit to range between 3-4pc of GDP.
In its Annual Report on the State of Pakistan’s Economy for FY26, the central bank said Pakistan’s economy demonstrated resilience against domestic and global shocks, with improvements across key macroeconomic indicators during the outgoing fiscal year.
According to a statement, the report attributed the gains to a prudent monetary policy stance, continued fiscal consolidation and economic reforms under the International Monetary Fund (IMF) programme, which helped preserve macroeconomic stability despite external and domestic challenges, including shifting US trade tariff policies, monsoon flooding and the outbreak of conflict in the Middle East in February 2026.
The statement said real GDP growth increased during FY26, with a major impetus coming from a rebound in large-scale manufacturing. It said the recovery in economic activity led to an increase in imports, while unfavourable global economic trends constrained exports.
However, strong growth in workers’ remittances kept the current account deficit at 0.1pc of GDP in FY26. Foreign exchange purchases by the SBP, a reduction in forward liabilities and sustained official inflows also contributed to an improvement in the central bank’s foreign exchange reserves, it added.
The statement said continued fiscal consolidation and a substantial decline in interest payments contributed to improved fiscal outcomes during FY26. Prudent monetary and fiscal policies also helped keep average national consumer price inflation close to the upper limit of the medium-term target range of 5-7pc, despite an energy price shock that pushed year-on-year inflation into double digits from April 2026 onwards.
However, the statement stressed that deep-rooted structural reforms were necessary to place the economy on a sustainable high-growth path and create employment opportunities for the expanding working-age population.
A chapter in the report, titled ‘Structural Challenges to Job Creation in Pakistan’, identified weak and volatile economic growth, slow structural transformation and signs of premature deindustrialisation as major obstacles to employment generation.
Low female labour force participation, high youth unemployment and widespread informal employment further compound these challenges, the statement said.
It added that the report also highlighted emerging pressures from technological developments, the green transition and geoeconomic fragmentation.
It called for measures to encourage private sector investment, address infrastructure gaps, improve technology and skills development, rationalise taxes and simplify tax administration. Reducing economic policy uncertainty through rules-based fiscal and regulatory frameworks is also identified as essential for sustained growth.
On the macroeconomic outlook, the SBP said Pakistan entered FY27 on a relatively stronger footing, with improvements in key economic indicators also contributing to upgrades in the country’s sovereign credit ratings.
A gradual recovery in domestic demand and recent fiscal measures were expected to support economic activity. While worsening El Niño conditions posed risks to the agricultural outlook, preliminary information indicated higher output of key kharif crops, including sugarcane, rice and maize, compared to last year, it said.
The report expected economic expansion, tariff rationalisation and higher global commodity prices to drive import growth, while exports were projected to recover moderately on the back of policy incentives and a slight increase in global demand.
Continued growth in workers’ remittances was expected to keep the current account deficit within 0-1pc of GDP in FY27. Meanwhile, foreign exchange purchases, official inflows and $3 billion raised through Eurobond issuance in September 2026 were expected to support further reserve accumulation towards four months of import cover.
The impact of the global energy price shock was expected to put upward pressure on inflation, which was projected to gradually ease towards the medium-term target range of 5-7pc during FY27.
