# Separate legislative assembly in Islamabad on the cards

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Mon, 16 Nov 2026 09:51:17 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2044836/separate-legislative-assembly-in-islamabad-on-the-cards
- Collected: 2026-11-16 07:36:50 UTC

---

ISLAMABAD: The government has intensified its efforts to set up a separate legislative assembly in Islamabad.
If all goes as planned, a legislative assembly will be set up in the capital city, which will be headed by an elected mayor. Some months ago, a high-profile committee headed by Planning Minister Ahsan Iqbal had finalised recommendations, which were presented to Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif. The recommendations proposed the devolution of powers from the federation to Islamabad Capital Territory as a federating unit, with a provincial-style government. It was stated that the proposed Islamabad Assembly would have 27 members and would be headed by an elected mayor/chief minister.
However, new proposals are now emerging for setting up a separate legislative assembly. Some TV channels reported on Sunday that the boundaries of Islamabad would also be expanded and that there were chances that Haripur, Murree and parts of Rawalpindi would be made part of Islamabad. When contacted, a senior government officer said that it was clear that a new, separate legislative assembly would be set up in Islamabad.
“As far as making Haripur, Rawalpindi and Murree part of Islamabad is concerned, nothing is final yet. They are part of separate provinces, and we will see if they can be made part of Islamabad or how laws passed by Islamabad will be implemented in these areas. So, again, nothing is final; these are discussions. But yes, Islamabad will have a separate legislative assembly,” he said.
It is relevant to note here that local government elections in Islamabad have been facing inordinate delays, as the elections have been postponed at least five times during the last four years, with the government always stating that new legislation is being made for a “vibrant system”. As per the latest schedule, elections were supposed to be held on Dec 20, but the Islamabad High Court suspended the schedule on a petition which pleaded that polls could not be held until the legal status of the existing constituency delimitation was conclusively determined.
