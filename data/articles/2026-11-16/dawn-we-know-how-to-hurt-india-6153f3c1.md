# We know how to hurt India, says Pakistan coach Solano ahead of SAFF Championship final

Pakistan head coach Nolberto Solano believes his side has what it takes to trouble India in the SAFF Championship final, insisting that his players must focus on their own strengths rather than become preoccupied with their opponents as the two arch-rivals prepare for a historic showdown on Tuesday.
Pakistan will face defending champions India at the National Stadium in Dhaka, Bangladesh, with the Green Shirts chasing their maiden SAFF Championship title and a landmark first appearance in the tournament’s final.
Speaking at the pre-match press conference on Monday, Solano acknowledged India’s quality but maintained that Pakistan had studied their opponents and understood where they could make an impact.
“We all prepare. It’s a final match, and we respect India. We are always checking and looking, but in football there are no more secrets,” Solano said.
“You know how they play. The most important thing is to focus on ourselves, on what we want to do, because we know our strengths and how we can hurt India.”
The Peruvian coach described the encounter as a special occasion and a derby, recognising the significance of a Pakistan-India contest, particularly with silverware at stake.
However, he stressed that his team’s attention must remain on executing its own game plan rather than allowing the occasion or the opposition to dictate its approach.
‘Football history’
For Pakistan, Tuesday’s final represents an opportunity to turn an encouraging tournament campaign into one of the most significant achievements in the country’s football history.
The Green Shirts booked their place in the showpiece match with a hard-fought 1-0 victory over hosts Bangladesh in Saturday’s semi-final. Abdul Samad Arshad scored the decisive goal in the closing stages to send Pakistan into their first-ever SAFF Championship final.
Their progress has been particularly noteworthy given the limited preparation time and the difficulties surrounding the availability of several overseas-based players during the group stage.
Pakistan’s final opponents, meanwhile, are pursuing a historic achievement of their own. India have won the SAFF Championship nine times and will be looking to secure an unprecedented third consecutive title, having lifted the trophy in the previous two editions.
The final will be a rematch of the Group ‘B’ encounter between the two sides on Nov 5, when India secured a 3-2 victory after establishing a commanding three-goal advantage.
Suhail Ahmad Bhat scored twice, while Manglenthang Kipgen added India’s third as Pakistan struggled to find their rhythm during the opening half.
The Green Shirts responded with two goals in stoppage time, including a spectacular overhead kick from Alamgir Ghazi, but their late rally proved insufficient to prevent defeat.
Solano admitted that Pakistan’s slow start and lack of concentration had contributed to the result, while also pointing to the difficult journey and inadequate preparation time before their opening fixture.
“I don’t like to make excuses, but we had a very bad journey before we arrived, and we faced them without two days of training. In the first 45 minutes, we weren’t focused. We were quite slow, so they took their chances and scored,” he said.
The coach, however, was encouraged by his side’s response after the interval, believing Pakistan had shown enough improvement to suggest they could compete more effectively in the rematch.
“In the second half, we improved and played much better. We could have come back into the match, but we did it a little bit late. That’s why we lost 3-2. But it’s another game, a different game,” Solano added.
Pakistan’s squad has since received a significant boost with the arrival of several overseas-based players who were unavailable during the group stage because the tournament fell outside the FIFA international window.
Captain Abdullah Iqbal, Easah Suliman, Adil Nabi, Ali Akbar Khan and Abdul Samad Arshad were among the players who joined the squad ahead of the knockout stages, giving Solano additional options and greater depth.
The impact of their arrival has been evident in Pakistan’s subsequent performances, particularly their defensive organisation and ability to manage difficult moments against Bangladesh.
India coach Naushad Moosa has also acknowledged that Pakistan present a different challenge from the team his side defeated in the group stage, pointing to the arrival of additional players and the tactical adjustments expected for the final.
‘Biggest achievement’
Abdullah acknowledged the importance of the occasion but insisted that the players would not approach the final any differently from their other matches.
Rather than allowing the pressure of a historic opportunity to affect their performance, the defender wants his teammates to remain disciplined, support one another and follow the instructions of the coaching staff.
“It’s a final, a big game, but we have to take it like any other game. Focus on the plan the coach has set for us, help each other out on the pitch and perform as usual. Nothing extra,” Abdullah said.
He admitted that the pressure of competing for a major regional title was unavoidable, particularly given what victory would mean for Pakistan football, but insisted that it would serve as additional motivation.
“Of course, there is pressure to win a final. It’s a big thing for our country, creating history, and it only motivates us to do better,” he added.
Abdullah also described Pakistan’s qualification for the final as a historic achievement, saying a tournament triumph would take the country’s footballing progress to another level.
“On the footballing side, it is the biggest achievement that we’re here in the final, and if we can go all the way, it will be even bigger,” the captain said.
With Pakistan having played a demanding schedule during the tournament, Solano was also asked whether fatigue could become a concern ahead of the final.
The coach dismissed the suggestion, insisting that his players were mentally prepared for the challenge and would not use the schedule as an excuse.
“Nobody is tired, my friend. We all focus. We all have a strong mentality,” he said. “When you play a final, my friends, the last thing you can be is tired. We are ready for fighting. No excuse. We want to give you everything.”
