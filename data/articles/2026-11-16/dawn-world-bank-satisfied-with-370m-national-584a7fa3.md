# World Bank ‘satisfied’ with $370m National Health Support Programme

ISLAMABAD: The World Bank has rated the implementation progress of its $370 million National Health Support Programme (NHSP) in Punjab, Sindh and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa as “satisfactory”, with a “moderate” overall risk rating, and expects the programme to meet its targets before the Dec 31, 2027 deadline.
Effective since October 2022, the NHSP aims to advance universal health coverage by strengthening equitable access to and the quality of essential healthcare services at the primary level.
The World Bank provided approximately $259m in financing, while the remaining funds came through grants and co-financing from the Global Financing Facility, the Gates Foundation, the Global Fund and Gavi.
In its public disclosure, the Washington-based multilateral lender rated both, progress towards achieving the programme’s development objectives and overall implementation progress, as “satisfactory” while describing the overall risk as “moderate”.
The NHSP “has sustained strong implementation momentum and continues to deliver tangible results across provinces”, the World Bank said, adding that approximately 76 per cent of total programme funding had been disbursed.

“Provincial governments have shown strong ownership, and the reforms introduced through the Disbursement Linked Results (DLRs) are strengthening primary health care systems. The remaining DLRs are on track, and all are expected to be achieved well ahead of the programme’s closing on Dec 31, 2027,” the bank noted.
The World Bank has already disbursed $205m (79.3pc) of its $259m financing, while a $36.8m co-financing facility has been completely disbursed. Two other financing components of $35m and $29m have each recorded disbursements of 50pc, according to the lender.
The programme uses a Program-for-Results (PforR) financing instrument, the first of its kind in Pakistan’s health sector, under which funding is linked to the achievement of agreed results.
The approach builds on Pakistan’s universal health coverage reforms and improvements in implementation capacity demonstrated through the earlier National Immunisation Support Project, which included the federal health ministry and provincial governments.
According to the World Bank, the programme’s improved health facility readiness score has reached 75pc in KP, 80pc in Punjab and 77.5pc in Sindh.
However, the use of modern contraceptives among women of reproductive age remains relatively low at 15pc in KP, 30pc in Punjab and 20pc in Sindh.
The NHSP complements existing investments in human capital and builds on reforms aimed at improving the quality of and equitable access to healthcare services.
It focuses on three areas, including coverage and quality of essential healthcare services, governance and accountability and healthcare financing.
Under the first component, the programme seeks to improve the availability of healthcare staff, supplies and essential medicines, while strengthening patient referral systems for emergency and higher-level care.
Goods exports hit $11.4bn
Separately, the federal government said the country’s goods exports had reached “an all-time high” of $11.4bn during July-October of the current fiscal year, registering year-on-year growth of 8.8pc from $10.4bn in the corresponding period last year, the state-run Associated Press of Pakistan reported.
In a statement, the government attributed the performance to measures introduced in the federal budget and coordinated efforts by the finance team and relevant institutions.
