# FCC allows PHC to hear Article 199 cases except those concerning emergency measures

Published November 16, 2026
ISLAMABAD: The Federal Constitutional Court (FCC) has amended its November 5 stay order restraining the Peshawar High Court from hearing cases concerning arrests under Section 3 of the Maintenance of Public Order (MPO) in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.
The clarification was issued on Monday as the FCC bench comprising Chief Justice Aminuddin Khan and Justice Aamer Farooq approved the PTI's petition.
The top court clarified that its November 5 stay order was related to matters concerning 3-MPO and governor’s rule, adding that the PHC could continue hearing other cases under Article 199 — except those concerning the imposition of governor's rule or a state of emergency in KP.
Ahead of the FCC’s Nov 5 stay order, the PHC had restrained authorities from making arrests under 3-MPO. It had also ordered the release of 28 Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI) workers detained in various districts of KP.
During the hearing of the case, PTI chief Barrister Gohar told the court that its November 5 stay order had affected proceedings of the PHC and brought lawyers' professional activities to a halt.
Justice Aamer Farooq observed that a simple reading of Article 245 suggested that cases under Article 199 could not be heard.
However, a literal interpretation of the provision would bring the entire judicial system to a standstill, the judge observed.
The court also granted the Peshawar Bar Council permission to become a party to the proceedings.
Barrister Gohar also sought relief concerning arrests made under Section 3 of the MPO, pointing out that a review board had not yet been constituted in KP.
Responding to the observation, the KP advocate general informed the court that the review board would be formed after three months.
Chief Justice Aminuddin Khan told Gohar that the miscellaneous petitions had been filed at the Peshawar Registry and would be examined once they were brought before the bench.
Speaking to the media after the hearing in Islamabad, PTI chairman said cases relating to governor's rule and a state of emergency would not be heard by the PHC.
He added that the court had accepted the Peshawar Bar Council's application to become a party to the case.
Gohar further clarified that proceedings in other cases under Article 199 would continue at the PHC, excluding those concerning governor's rule or a state of emergency.
