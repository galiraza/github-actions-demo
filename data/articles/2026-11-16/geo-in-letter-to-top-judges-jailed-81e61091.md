ISLAMABAD: Jailed Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI) founding chairman Imran Khan has reportedly written a letter to the chief justices of the Supreme Court (SC), Federal Constitutional Court (FCC) and Islamabad High Court (IHC), expressing concern over his health and urging them to ensure the implementation of court orders.
In the letter authored by Barrister Salman Sadar on behalf of the ex-premier, the former prime minister said doctors had warned him of the risk of a brain haemorrhage and a heart attack, adding that his blood pressure had reached 193.
"Doctors have told me that I am at risk of a brain haemorrhage and a heart attack. My blood pressure has reached 193," he wrote in the letter.
Khan, 73, has remained in prison since August 2023 following convictions in several cases that he and his party describe as politically motivated. He has faced multiple legal proceedings since his ouster as prime minister in April 2022 following a no-confidence vote.
In the letter, Imran urged the chief justices to ensure that court decisions were implemented, saying he wanted to prove his innocence if he remained alive.
"I will prove my innocence if I remain alive," he wrote. The letter was addressed to the chief justices through his counsel Barrister Salman Safdar.
“I do not ask Your Lordships for any favour. All I request as justice is the implementation of the orders of the Honourable Supreme Court and proper treatment for our medical conditions. I will only remain able to prove my innocence if I stay alive. I appeal to Your Lordships, on the most basic grounds of human compassion, give me justice before it is too late,” Khan wrote.
The development came days after IHC division bench comprising Chief Justice Sardar Muhammad Sarfraz Dogar and Justice Muhammad Asif had directed jail authorities to allow lawyers Khosa and Salman Safdar to meet Imran and his wife, Bushra, at Adiala jail while hearing their appeals against convictions in the £190 million case.
Lawyers representing former prime minister Imran and his wife, Bushra Bibi, have submitted affidavits to the IHC, detailing their meeting with the incarcerated couple at Adiala jail on November 11.
Meanwhile, Bushra Bibi has approached the IHC through lawyers Latif Khosa and Barrister Salman, seeking her immediate transfer to a specialised hospital for medical treatment and possible eye surgery.
In her petition, she requested the court to order authorities to produce her complete medical record and ensure that all necessary facilities were provided for her surgery.
The petition stated that Imran Khan had raised concerns about his wife's health during a meeting with lawyers on November 10. It added that Bushra Bibi had informed them about a possible eye surgery scheduled for November 15.
According to the petition, she was experiencing eye pain and difficulty seeing. A doctor examined her in jail seven to eight days after she first complained of the problem and informed her that she would require surgery.
The petition also raised concerns about the conditions in which former first lady was being held, claiming that she was confined in a large hall-like room with poor hygiene.
It further alleged that seven small snakes had emerged from her cell and were killed by jail staff. Rats, cockroaches and insects were also said to be present in the room.
The petitioner's counsel argued that inadequate cleanliness in the jail could increase the risk of infection following eye surgery and urged the court to arrange her transfer to a specialised hospital for proper care.
