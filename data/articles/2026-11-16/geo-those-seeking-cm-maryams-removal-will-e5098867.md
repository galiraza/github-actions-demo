# Those seeking CM Maryam's removal will get a 'shock', warns Punjab minister

Published November 16, 2026
Punjab Information Minister Azma Bokhari on Monday ruled out the possibility of a change in the province's chief minister, asserting that CM Maryam Nawaz would complete her term.
"We will go to the polls after completing our term. Those hoping to see someone else replace chief minister [Maryam] will receive a shock," she said during a press conference in Lahore.
Bokhari asserted that CM Maryam would head into the next election based on her performance during her term.
The Punjab minister criticised Federal Minister for Overseas Pakistanis Chaudhry Salik Hussain response to a notice, saying it failed to adequately address the corruption allegations.
Her remarks refer to the public dispute that erupted after Hussain criticised the Punjab government's governance and alleged widespread corruption in the province during an interview with a private television channel.
The federal minister had alleged that political considerations were influencing development priorities and the allocation of public funds, claiming that a disproportionate share of Punjab's development budget was being channelled towards Lahore while other districts were being overlooked.
However, Hussain acknowledged that he did not possess documentary evidence to substantiate the claims but maintained that corruption was taking place.
CM Maryam subsequently announced legal action against the federal minister, challenging him to produce evidence or face proceedings.
During today's presser, Bokhari said that the federal minister's reply demonstrated that he had no substantive answer to the allegations.
The provincial minister questioned the claims that cupboards were filled with evidence, saying an audit report had instead been presented in response to the notice.
She maintained that those making baseless and incorrect allegations would be given a response.
Commenting on corruption, Bokhari said it was taking place across the country but action against corruption was being taken only in Punjab.
Addressing reports about the transfer of the provincial government's Gulfstream G-500 aircraft to the Pakistan Air Force (PAF), the minister said that the air force looked after state-owned assets, particularly aircraft.
"There is nothing unusual about the Pakistan Air Force maintaining the aircraft," she said, adding that aircraft required maintenance after operating for a year.
Official documents had revealed that the Punjab cabinet had approved to transfer the operational control and maintenance of the provincial government's Gulfstream G-500 jet to the PAF.
The documents stated that the Punjab government's VIP Flight Wing had been facing technical difficulties in managing the modern aircraft because of its limited technical capabilities.
