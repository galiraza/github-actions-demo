# Autopsy reveals multiple injuries on teenage domestic worker found dead at FIA official’s residence in Islamabad

ISLAMABAD: The autopsy report of a 13-year-old domestic worker, who was found dead at the official residence of a senior Federal Investigation Agency (FIA) official on Sunday night, suggests that she had been subjected to repeated physical assaults.
According to the first information report (FIR), the girl, identified as Fatima Asghar, allegedly died due to physical torture and violence. The suspects are the FIA official’s dual-national wife and a Qari who worked as a servant at his Islamabad residence.
The autopsy report stated that the deceased had died from repeated physical assaults that caused grievous injuries and severe inflammation, leading to thromboembolism (blockage of a blood vessel by a blood clot) and cardiopulmonary arrest (cessation of heart and breathing functions).
“All injuries were inflicted before death and were sufficient to cause death in the ordinary course of life,” it added
The autopsy report documented 20 injuries on the girl’s body, all of which were suspected to have been caused by physical assault. These included swelling on the left side of the head near the temple, a blood clot on the right side of the head, a bald patch on the right side of the scalp, two old, healed scars on the right cheek, swelling and skin loss on the left cheek, a bruise on the left shoulder and a wound on the left arm.
Other injuries included swelling and bruising on the left wrist, the back of the left hand and the second and third fingers; a scar between the thumb and index finger of the right hand; a scar on the outer side of the right arm; and swelling and bruising on the right hand, including the second and third fingers.
The report also noted multiple old, linear dark marks on the right side of the chest and lower abdomen, as well as an old, unhealed burn mark on the left side of the back near the shoulder blade.
Old burn scars on the back, a thickened scar on the right upper thigh, darkened and swollen skin around both knees and the left thigh, swelling in the knee joint and a non-healing wound on the outer side of the left knee were also found.
The examination also revealed extensive swelling across the head, extending from the left temple to the back of the head, along with internal bleeding attributed to physical assault. A separate blood clot was also found on the right side of the head.
The report said samples of the heart, lungs, liver, blood and stomach contents, along with other specimens, whole-body X-rays and a brain CT scan, had been handed over to the police for examination and analysis.
Meanwhile, First Lady Bibi Asifa Bhutto Zardari expressed sorrow over Fatima’s death, saying that no child should be subjected to violence, abuse or exploitation.
In a statement, she pointed out that Islamabad’s child employment law, enacted four years ago, prohibited the employment of children of Fatima’s age as domestic workers, even with parental consent. She stressed that the law must be enforced in letter and spirit to prevent the exploitation of children.
Asifa called for a thorough, impartial and transparent investigation into the circumstances surrounding Fatima’s death and urged that those found responsible be brought to justice in accordance with the law.
She also emphasised the need for collective efforts to protect vulnerable children, particularly those working in domestic settings, from abuse and exploitation.
The suspects were arrested on Sunday after an FIR was registered against them under Section 302 (intentional murder) of the Pakistan Penal Code at Shams Colony police station.
In the FIR, Fatima’s father, a resident of Rahim Yar Khan, said his daughter was a seminary student and was residing with close relatives at Police Lines Headquarters in Islamabad’s H-11 sector. He said he was informed in the morning that she had suddenly fallen ill and was taken to a hospital, where she died.
An Islamabad police officer said Fatima had been working as a domestic help at the official’s residence for a couple of years, dating back to when he was serving in the capital police.
