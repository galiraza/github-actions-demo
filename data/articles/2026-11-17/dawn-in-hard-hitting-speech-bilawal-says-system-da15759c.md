# In hard-hitting speech, Bilawal says system was ‘made to collapse’

- Published: Tue, 17 Nov 2026 21:33:28 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2045176/in-hard-hitting-speech-bilawal-says-system-was-made-to-collapse

Key takeaways:
- Bilawal opposed creating new provinces ‘unconstitutionally’, such as via referendum; urged PML-N to decide on provinces issue, claimed ‘pressure’ on PPP
- Called for releasing “political prisoners”, including Imran; noted “injustices” to opposition regarding reserved seats, PTI symbol
- Said 27th Amendment passed without consensus; supported Fazl’s “right to criticise” govt 
Amid debate on a “system reset” in the country, PPP Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari said on Tuesday that the governance system had not collapsed, but was rather “made to collapse”.
Over the past few months, Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi has whipped up a political storm since he called for the formation of new administrative units in the country, saying the current “system” of governance had “collapsed” and needed a “reset”.
During his speech at the Karachi Press Club, Bilawal recalled Naqvi’s remarks and said, “Nobody objects that the system has collapsed, but there is no discussion and debate on why it collapsed.”
He noted that the proposed solution “was not an actual remedy to the illness due to which we suffered a system collapse”.
“If we want to focus on resolving the issues of the public, if we want to fight poverty, unemployment, inflation, then we absolutely need to decide why the system collapsed,” the Bhutto scion said.
’Reset must be political’
Bilawal asserted a reset was “definitely needed”; however, he also stressed that it should be political.
“Why political? Because politicians are the representatives of the people, and if politicians do not play their role, then these fundamental issues won’t be resolved,” Bilawal said.
“I believe the system has not collapsed, but it has been made to collapse,” he stated, while acknowledging his own role in the matter.
“Everyone makes mistakes, but there comes a time when you must fix those mistakes. How do we fix that?” Bilawal asked rhetorically.
He contended that state institutions had “lost their legitimacy”, calling for it to be restored.
During his speech at the ‘Meet the Press’ event and responding to queries later, Bilawal firmly declared that the PPP would oppose any “unconstitutional” moves regarding new provinces.
He also voiced his strong opposition to a referendum on creating new provinces, questioning the “intent” behind it, and said his party lawmakers were facing “pressure” but would not succumb to it.
The PPP chairman’s remarks went beyond his oft-repeated calls for electoral reforms and political consensus, as he called for the opposition PTI to be given reserved seats as a “confidence-building measure”.
He also questioned the current status of the judiciary, particularly in the wake of the 27th Amendment, which he had strongly advocated for at the time of its passage just a year ago.
‘Injustices’ to PTI of reserved seats, symbol
The PPP chairman further observed that “some such injustices” had been done that, if corrected, could quickly restore the opposition’s trust.
In an indirect reference to the PTI, Bilawal recalled the revocation of the PPP’s party symbol in 1988 and said, “Revoking the symbol from a party is akin to you joking with the public, and the public denounced this tradition and action and responded to you in those elections.”
Then, in a stance deviating from the ruling coalition’s, Bilawal said that “what happened with the reserved seats was a joke”. He said the opposition was not only deprived of those seats, but “those in the treasury benches also put them in their pockets”.
The PTI — deprived of its reserved seats in the 2024 elections — was declared eligible for the seats by the Supreme Court. However, a constitutional bench formed later via the 26th Amendment overturned that ruling while hearing review pleas of the coalition parties.
“And from those same reserved seats, which are not yours, you brought constitutional amendments not once but twice, and are now thinking to bring a third one,” Bilawal said.
He called for resolving reservations regarding rigging and returning the party symbol “so they have their own identity”.
“It is a confidence-building measure that we, without any preconditions, return the reserved seats to the opposition, which is their right,” said Bilawal, whose PPP was also among the beneficiaries of the reserved seats case.
‘Imran Khan a political prisoner’
Reiterating his call for electoral reforms, Bilawal also stressed the need to restore the PTI’s and the opposition’s trust in the Parliament.
“If we want the system to be reset and the Parliament to start functioning, vengeful actions will need to be stopped. How much revenge will you take after all?” he said.
The PPP chairman said not only should vengeful actions be halted, but the “tradition of revenge inserted in Pakistan’s politics” should be ended forever.
He even recalled a speech by PTI founder Imran Khan, who questioned if “we were ants”.
Bilawal called for efforts to ensure that there is no political prisoner in Pakistan, terming it necessary for the “system to function”.
“What a weird country this is; since 1947, whoever becomes the prime minister goes to jail, whoever gets out of jail goes to sit in the Prime Minister House,” he remarked.
“You broke [our] back, then ask ‘why did you fall’,” he quipped, emphasising that political prisoners would need to be released.
When asked whether he considered Imran a political prisoner, Bilawal responded in the affirmative.
“A person becomes the prime minister and is later imprisoned. So I can either accept that every prime minister in the country’s history has been a thief, or I can accept that all prime ministers in our history have been victims of political revenge,” he added.
Noting that the PTI had made mistakes as well, Bilawal said, “One day, I hope no prime minister should have to go to jail, and for that, we need to develop consensus.”
‘Federal govt must decide on reset’
Bilawal, in response to a question, also called on the federal government to present its stance on the governance reset.
Presenting his own approach, he said, “The political reset that I spoke of, I will try to develop that consensus, and I would like to create a consensus for it in my own party so that we can engage other political parties”.
As for the federal government, Bilawal said, “I don’t know what they are thinking.”
In an apparent reference to the interior minister’s remarks about being on the “same page” as Bilawal, the PPP chairman said, “I had heard we were on the same page, and if this is the page we are on, then nothing could be better.”
Bilawal said there had been a debate that had been “forced” in the country for the past few months.
“Even the cabinet had not discussed the matter, let alone the parliament [..] So, how could it be that federal ministers are talking about it of their own accord,” he asked.
At this, the PPP chairman said he would like to believe that Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif has “enough authority over his own ministers”.
“You are sabotaging your own prime minister, and then you complain that the system has collapsed [..] I believe that is unfair.”
In recent days, PML-N’s Khawaja Asif, who serves as the defence minister, has been advocating for new administrative units, suggesting the formation of up to 12 provinces.
Bilawal maintained that his party would welcome any debate and consensus on the matter; however, he hinted that deliberation and consensus “may not be on the agenda”.
Bilawal said that “suggestions” were coming from outside of the Parliament, which he described as showing “clear indication [..] that the opinion of Parliament, public, and cabinet did not hold any weight and you just want to use a backdoor channel”.
“Up until today, neither the prime minister nor a single member of the provinces has brought up the issue with the president of Pakistan or me,” he added.
On a possible rollback of the 18th Amendment, Bilawal pointed out that the Sindh government was giving the Centre Rs585 billion surplus in financial support.
“Donations worth Rs 260bn were given in security and national interest,” he said.
“I am not even counting the rightful share of the province — of petroleum levy and gas levy — it is about Rs1.7 trillion from the petroleum levy.”
He remarked, “So, you will pickpocket the province, loot our resources and then come and ask us, ‘why the roads are broken and why no schools or hospitals have been built’.”
Bilawal assailed the Centre, asking if a province was paying a Rs585bn surplus, Rs260bn for national security and Rs1.7tr on the petroleum levy, then “the federal government should be held responsible for the lack of public spending, not the provincial government”.
He claimed that the “easiest way to do corruption in Pakistan was to be on the state’s right side”.
‘Referendum has never been democratic’
On the interior minister’s recommendation of holding a referendum to decide on the creation of administrative units, the PPP chairman pointed to the “intention” behind the proposed action.
“In our history, a referendum has always been carried out for an undemocratic act and never a democratic one,” the Bhutto scion remarked.
He argued that if the Constitution had relevant provisions on the creation of administrative units, then the mere suggestion of holding a referendum “meant that you were looking for an unconstitutional method”.
The PPP chairman vowed that his party would stand firmly against any such “unconstitutional” action.
Bilawal also spoke against “any forced division” in Sindh, warning that while PPP remained in favour of creating provinces, it was against a “forced separation”.
He cautioned, “We talk about the garbage on Karachi’s streets, but if Sindh is divided, we will see blood flow through its streets.”
On being asked whether a compromise could be reached on the creation of new provinces, Bilawal recalled that several resolutions had been passed by the Sindh Assembly against the province’s division.
“Any more discussion on this issue is a slap to the people of Sindh,” he declared.
“I do not understand what Mohsin bhai is trying to do,” Bilawal said.
Replying to a question about new provinces and a “reset”, Bilawal said the PML-N-led government had not discussed the matter with the PPP, adding that this raised the question of why this was “being made to discuss by the media”.
Bilawal said he has invited PM Shehbaz to hold talks with the Sindh government on public issues, and also suggested that he convene his cabinet meetings in each province.
‘Pressure on PPP lawmakers’
Reiterating the PPP’s stance on new provinces, Bilawal stressed that as a generational politician, he would want to take steps as per the public’s wishes.
“Those from whom these suggestions are coming, they have not won even a single election in their lives. And neither do they have chances in the next election … they would become a minister, prime minister or chief minister whether they get a single vote or not in the next elections,” he said, adding that each side was taking a stance as per their situation.
Replying to another query, Bilawal said, “On the one hand, there are no discussions ongoing; on the other hand, there definitely is pressure on the party’s MNAs and MPAs, but PPP’s representatives do not function on someone else’s pressure, but the public’s.”
He further remarked, “If tomorrow, my software is updated and I direct my party to vote for Sindh’s division, there will be a revolt against me. They will not cast a majority vote even on my directives.”
He quipped that “some people were dreaming of becoming the chief minister of their area, but they would not even be able to remain their house’s chief minister”.
The Bhutto scion further affirmed that his PPP would not “back any conspiracy against Punjab”, adding that he would support only those decisions endorsed by the Punjab Assembly.
He pointed out that there was a lack of discussion on declaring Gilgit-Baltistan a province even though there was a consensus in the region on this demand.
‘27th Amendment passed with brute majority’
Speaking on the judiciary, Bilawal noted that “our internal wish of a constitutional court” as per the Charter of Democracy had been fulfilled, but questioned whether the court had the public’s confidence.
“If the lawyers and judges themselves do not have confidence in the courts, then the system did not collapse, but was made to collapse.”
The PPP chairman said he was not calling for the revocation of such moves, but for greater consensus. He asserted that the 26th Amendment was passed with “bilateral agreement, if not complete consensus”, as the opposition’s input was taken through Fazl.
Bilawal said the 27th Amendment was “passed neither through political consensus nor bilateral agreement; it was passed with brute majority”.
He called for engaging with the opposition on judicial reforms and termed it a requirement for the “political, public and democratic reset”, adding that without such a reset, the public’s key issues of inflation and unemployment cannot be addressed.
Bilawal also noted that rigging allegations often surfaced during elections.
“I have been in politics for the past 20 years, and usually, I have seen that it is the opposition that accuses the other side of rigging and the government denies it. For the first time, I am seeing that people from within the government are claiming that rigging has been done.”
‘Fazl has right to criticise govt’
Bilawal also came to Maulana Fazlur Rehman’s defence as tensions between the Jamiat Ulema-i-Islam-Fazl (JUI-F) chief and the government have flared in recent weeks.
The past month has witnessed exchanges of harsh remarks between Fazl and federal ministers, after the JUI-F chief accused the Centre of depriving the provinces of their rights.
These developments came after Fazl joined the opposition in criticising the Centre’s imposition of governor’s rule and emergency in the PTI-ruled KP. He also accused the Centre of reneging on its commitments amid his efforts to mediate between the federal and KP governments.
In his address, Bilawal stressed that it was the opposition’s role in the Parliament to criticise the government.
“It is the opposition’s right to criticise you, and however he (Fazl) does it, it is their right,” he said.
Bilawal remarked that the opposition had the “most patriotic” role in the Parliament.
“For any government to question the patriotism of an opposition member [..] it is not only an injustice to that parliament or to the system, but the biggest injustice to the country,” he added.
The PPP chairman termed a difference of opinion within the Parliament the “beauty and strength of democracy”.
Bilawal reiterated, “If we want to reset the system, a political reset has to happen; it should begin from the parliament”.
He noted that the opposition had lost trust in the system.
“They genuinely have reasons to have lost confidence in the working of the Parliament, and we all have to regain this trust,” he said.
“The government cannot expect the opposition to clap along with every one of the government’s decisions,” he remarked.
He claimed that the “easiest way to do corruption in Pakistan was to be on the state’s right side”.
Bilawal said: “When someone from the opposition makes a speech [..] then all of a sudden, you set the National Accountability Bureau (NAB) on them, arrest their sons; there was no such realisation before.
“Now, all of a sudden, there is encroachment (around their house),” he said, in an apparent reference to the Capital Development Authority’s (CDA) recent notice on “unauthorised construction” at the JUI-F chief’s residence.
“This is why the system has collapsed […] you are making it collapse,” he said, calling on the Centre to engage with Fazl.
On governor’s rule in KP, Bilawal noted that PPP had the governor in the province, but stressed his stance that it could not remain forever.
“If you are yourself saying that the majority of them (PTI) were ready but one or two people sabotaged the talks, then what is stopping the government from approaching the opposition again?” he said, recalling remarks by “minister for everything”.
“If the PTI is ready to fight against terrorism together with you, then the governor’s rule can also be ended,” Bilawal said, terming it another way to regain the opposition’s trust.
