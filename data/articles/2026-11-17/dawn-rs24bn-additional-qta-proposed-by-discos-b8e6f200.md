# Rs24bn additional QTA proposed by Discos draws protest from industrial consumers

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Tue, 17 Nov 2026 21:37:34 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2045200/rs24bn-additional-qta-proposed-by-discos-draws-protest-from-industrial-consumers
- Collected: 2026-11-17 16:38:58 UTC

---

ISLAMABAD: Industrial consumers on Tuesday protested over a Rs24 billion additional quarterly tariff burden proposed by power companies due to higher capacity charges, variable operation and maintenance (O&M) costs and the impact of system losses, resulting in an estimated tariff impact of around Rs1.30 per unit for three months.
A brief intervention from the power division, represented by the chief financial officer (CFO) of the Power Planning and Monitoring Company (PPMC), Naveed Qaisar, was the request at a public hearing that the additional quarterly tariff adjustment (QTA) be applied beginning in the Dec 1, 2026 billing and ending in February 2027.
The hearing was presided over by National Electric Power Regulatory Authority (Nepra) members Amina Ahmed, Maqsood Anwar Khan and Ghulamullah Shaikh, on the request of 11 distribution companies (Discos) to seek approval for charging the additional QTA for the July-September 2026 period. Once approved, the additional QTA would also be applicable to K-Electric under a government decision in place since 2023.
Two industrial interveners — Amir Shaikh and Rehan Javed — expressed serious concern over Rs39bn additional capacity payment claims for the quarter against the pre-approved references built into the base tariff, besides the Rs5.6bn impact on variable O&M and Rs7.8bn impact of system losses in monthly fuel charges.
Amir Shaikh, from Lahore, said QTAs normally stay negative when fuel cost adjustments (FCAs) are higher than charges to consumers in respective months. This time, however, the industry faced a double shock — positive FCAs throughout the quarter followed by positive QTAs — while the industrial output had gone out on the premise of lower QTAs.
He said the major reason appeared to be units sold to selective consumers under the incremental consumption package (ICP) at an unrealistic cost of Rs10 cheaper than normal tariffs, which translated into a higher QTA for all consumers — despite the government’s promise that the impact of the ICP would not be charged to consumers except for ICP beneficiaries.
“This is like rubbing salt into our wounds. It is shocking that the QTA has gone up,” he said.
Javed, from Karachi, noted the Discos claimed Rs29.25bn of negative impact of the ICP on industrial consumers, saying it was actually an additional burden from all consumers. A series of questions he raised over the data provided by Discos remained unanswered and Nepra did not press for responses either.
Javed alleged that the Discos had counted the wrong units and measured against FY2023-24, instead of references set for this year under the notified tariff.
“The ‘relief’ shown (the ICP of Rs29.25bn) is not relief received; it hides a cost other consumers pay,” he said, adding that this was not legally covered by any Nepra decision as all consumers were being forced to fill a Rs15 per unit delta.
He contended that when every Disco was claiming to have sold more units than the reference, the capacity cost should have reduced. “Why, then, is the capacity claim going up? Is the benefit of the higher sales being lost because of ICP units?”
He demanded to defer the incremental units amount in the QTA claim until the full data, as demanded in March and September 2026, had been filed.
Cumulatively, all 11 Discos reported that capacity charges during the July-September period had increased by Rs39bn, which also included the impact of system losses on the monthly fuel cost and variable operation and maintenance costs.
Seven out of the 11 Discos demanded additional recovery of about Rs37bn on account of capacity charges, variable O&M charges and the impact of T&D losses on monthly FCA. However, a significant portion was offset by negative adjustments of Rs13bn from four other Discos.
The highest additional claims came from Islamabad Electric (Rs10.833bn), followed by Rs10.46bn from Faisalabad and Rs7bn from Peshawar Electric. Tribal Electric led with a negative adjustment of Rs5.8bn, followed by Rs4.1bn by Quetta and Rs1.95bn by Hyderabad Electric.
In line with policy guidelines issued by the federal government for uniform quarterly adjustments, the QTA determined for Discos for the third quarter of CY2026 will also apply to K-Electric consumers once Nepra issues its judgment.
Under the tariff mechanism, changes in fuel cost are passed on to consumers only on a monthly basis through an automatic mechanism, while QTA, on account of variation in power purchase price, capacity charges, variable operation and maintenance costs, use of system charges and including the impact of transmission and distribution losses, is built into the base tariff by the federal government.
