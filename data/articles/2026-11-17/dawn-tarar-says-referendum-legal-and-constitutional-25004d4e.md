# Tarar says referendum 'legal and constitutional' method of gauging public opinion on new provinces

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Tue, 17 Nov 2026 21:31:52 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2045201/tarar-says-referendum-legal-and-constitutional-method-of-gauging-public-opinion-on-new-provinces
- Collected: 2026-11-17 16:38:58 UTC

---

Information Minister Attaullah Tarar said on Tuesday that a referendum was a legal and constitutional method of gauging public opinion on new provinces, adding that no political party should have reservations regarding it.
He made the comments in televised remarks following a hard-hitting speech by PPP Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari. The minister said that there was an ongoing debate in the country regarding “good governance”.
“The public wants a good government that is accountable … You and all of us have been part of this system for decades. Today, we cannot separate ourselves from the weaknesses of the system,” he said.
He said that to “enjoy the perks” being in power and also do opposition amounted to having a “double standard”. He said that the reason behind the PPP chairman’s remarks was the ongoing debate regarding provinces.
“And this debate is raging among the masses. Will you silence the people? Will you stop them from talking about improving governance? This debate will happen in Parliament and outside it,” he said.
The minister added that the people could not be stopped from talking about something that was for their benefit.
“Democratic and political processes demand that this debate be done by the people … What is the problem with going to the public? You and I are not going to decide what the people want. They are the ones who will decide,” he said.
“No political party should have reservations in going to the public. When public opinion comes forward … referendum is part of the Constitution. It is a legal and constitutional process. The people are the final decision-makers and good governance is the charter of every political party,” he said.
The minister said that the people appointed public representatives, not the other way round.
“The people are the ultimate decision-makers. If the people of Pakistan and Sindh was more provinces, then make them; otherwise, don’t,” he said. Tarar added that instead of making the issue of smaller administrative units smaller, they should be viewed through the lens of development.
“Will you deprive the people of their rights just because referendums were previously used in a non-democratic manner?” he asked.
“Look, we are part of the same government. Come and sit down [with us] so we can make decision for the betterment of the people and the country. [Let] this decision be based on public opinion. And a referendum is an excellent way of gauging public opinion, so what’s the harm?”
JUI warns of potential ‘misuse’ of referendum
The Jamiat-Ulema-i-Islam (JUI) also responded to Bilawal’s statement on social media, which they termed “realistic” and viewed in high regard, while warning that a referendum could also be “misused”.
“By exposing the government’s mandate, Bilawal Bhutto has supported the JUI’s position,” party spokesperson Aslam Ghauri said. Stating that the PPP had made its position on the division of provinces clear, he expressed the JUI’s alignment with this position.
“The experience of holding referendums has always been tainted,” Ghauri added. “If the Constitution provides a procedure for resolving an issue, there is no need for a referendum.”
He also claimed that referendums, “like emergencies and governor’s rule”, would also be “misused” by invoking the Constitution.
Over the past few months, Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi has whipped up a political storm since he called for the formation of new administrative units in the country, saying the current “system” of governance had “collapsed” and needed a “reset”.
Last week, Naqvi also floated the idea of holding a referendum to gauge public opinion on the issue, saying the exercise could settle the contentious debate once and for all.
PPP Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari, Sindh Chief Minister Murad Ali Shah and other senior party leaders have repeatedly declared that the division of Sindh was neither legally nor politically acceptable to the party.
In September, the Sindh Assembly adopted a resolution rejecting all attempts to divide the province or create new administrative units.
President Asif Ali Zardari also met Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif over the weekend. Official sources said the two agreed to “strictly adhere to constitutional provisions” governing the creation of new provinces or administrative units, with any proposed referendum on the contentious issue also requiring parliamentary approval.
However, Bilawal on Tuesday termed the referendum an “unconstitutional move”, vowing that his party would stand against any such measure.
