# Pakistan, Turkmenistan explore ways to boost bilateral ties, regional connectivity

Published November 17, 2026
Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Senator Ishaq Dar on Tuesday held a tête-à-tête with Deputy Chairman of the Cabinet of Ministers and Minister of Foreign Affairs of Turkmenistan Rashid Ovezgeldiyevich Meredov in Islamabad.
The two officials reviewed the longstanding and fraternal relations between the two countries, according to a statement issued by the Foreign Office.
During the meeting, both leaders discussed ways to further strengthen cooperation in trade, investment, energy, regional connectivity and people-to-people exchanges.
The two ministers also exchanged views on regional developments.
Deputy Prime Minister Dar underscored that dialogue and diplomacy remained the only viable means of resolving outstanding issues and achieving lasting peace and stability in the region.
Foreign Minister Rashid Meredov appreciated Pakistan’s constructive role and continued diplomatic efforts to promote regional peace and stability.
Following the one-on-one meeting, the 6th Session of the Pakistan–Turkmenistan Joint Intergovernmental Commission was held, during which the two sides reviewed the entire spectrum of bilateral co-operation and discussed the opportunities for power co-operation, transit trade and regional connectivity.
During the meeting, co-chaired by DPM Dar and Turkmen FM Meredov, the two sides underscored the importance of stronger business-to-business engagement, institutional partnerships, and people-to-people exchanges.
The two sides discussed ways to deepen collaboration across trade and investment, energy, agriculture and food security, transport and communications, industry, IT and telecommunications, education, health, banking and finance, tourism, sports and culture.
DPM Dar stressed the need to implement the agreed outcomes through Joint Working Groups, close institutional co-ordination, and regular follow-up to translate the longstanding friendship into tangible benefits.
The session was attended by the federal ministers for power, commerce, and petroleum, the minister of state for federal education and professional training, and the minister of state for national heritage and culture, along with senior officials from the relevant ministries and departments.
Separately, Federal Minister for Commerce Jam Kamal Khan held a bilateral meeting with Turkmenistan’s Minister of Trade and Foreign Economic Relations, Dortguly Suleymanov, discussing measures to expand bilateral trade, strengthen regional connectivity, improve market access and promote greater business cooperation between the two countries.
Jam Kamal emphasised the importance of translating strong bilateral relations into practical commercial partnerships, particularly in textiles, agriculture, food processing, manufacturing and logistics.
He appreciated the exchange of business delegations between the two countries and stressed the need for continuous engagement to identify new investment opportunities and develop commercially viable trade arrangements.
Highlighting changing global economic dynamics, the federal minister said rising international freight costs and disruptions to traditional trade routes had increased the importance of regional economic integration.
He observed that Pakistan and Turkmenistan were well positioned to benefit from emerging trade opportunities linking South Asia, Central Asia and the Middle East.
Jam Kamal proposed exploring direct flights between Islamabad and Ashgabat, along with chartered cargo services for perishable agricultural and food products.
He noted that the relatively short flying distance between the two capitals could provide a significant advantage for time-sensitive exports.
The Turkmen Minister acknowledged the importance of improved air connectivity and indicated that the proposal could be discussed further with relevant aviation authorities during the upcoming Intergovernmental Commission meeting.
