# Indian Air Force’s Rafale fleet down to 32, aviation publication says, backing Pakistan’s claim of downing four jets

---

The Indian Air Force (IAF) has 32 French-made Rafale fighter jets in service, down from 36 before the May 2025 conflict with Pakistan, according to estimates published by British aviation magazine Flight International.
The figures lend credence to Islamabad’s claim that it shot down several Indian Rafale jets during the conflict.
In its annual directory of air forces around the world, Flight International listed 32 Rafales in the IAF’s active inventory.
The Rafale, French for a gust of wind, is a twin-jet fighter aircraft designed and built by Dassault Aviation. The French group defines it as “omnirole, able to carry out missions including in-depth strikes, nuclear deterrence, reconnaissance and anti-ship strikes”.
The aircraft has seen combat in Afghanistan, Mali, Libya, Iraq, Syria and more recently, in the Pak-India theatre.
In 2016, India had entered a formal agreement to buy 36 Rafale fighter jets from France’s Dassault for a reported $8.8 billion. More recently, the neighbouring country ordered the purchase of additional 114 Rafale fighter jets.
The May 2025 conflict between Pakistan and India was sparked by an attack on tourists in occupied Kashmir, which New Delhi, without evidence, said was backed by Pakistan. Pakistan denied involvement, with the foreign ministry having questioned the credibility of India’s account of the events, saying it was “replete with fabrications”.
Both sides used fighter jets, missiles, artillery and drones during the four-day conflict, killing dozens of people. After tit-for-tat strikes on each other’s airbases, it took American intervention on May 10 for both sides to finally reach a ceasefire.
In the immediate aftermath of the conflict, Pakistan said it shot down at least six Indian fighter jets during the conflict, including the French-made Rafale. New Delhi acknowledged “some losses” during the conflict but denied losing six jets.
At the time, a high-ranking French intelligence official had also confirmed to CNN that one Rafale fighter jet operated by the IAF was downed by Pakistan.
The development marked the first time that one of the sophisticated French-made warplanes had been lost in combat. The French official told CNN that French authorities were looking into whether more than one Rafale jet was shot down by Pakistan in the aerial engagement.
In a press briefing in May this year, the Pakistan Air Force said that the “confirmed kills” during the conflict included four Rafales, one Su-30, one MiG-29, one Mirage 2000, and a multi-role unmanned aerial system.
US President Donald Trump has also stated numerous times that several aircraft were shot down during the conflict, with his latest tally at 10 in February.
Initially stating that five jets were shot down, Trump gradually raised that number to seven in October and then eight in November last year.
Published monthly by aviation media outlet FlightGlobal and founded in 1909, Flight International is a magazine focused on aerospace. It is the world’s oldest continuously published aviation news magazine.
