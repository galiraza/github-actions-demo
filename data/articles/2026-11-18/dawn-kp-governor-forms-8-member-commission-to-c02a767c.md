# KP governor forms 8-member commission to probe illegal placer gold mining

- Published: Wed, 18 Nov 2026 23:11:54 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2045455/kp-governor-forms-8-member-commission-to-probe-illegal-placer-gold-mining
- Collected: 2026-11-18 21:17:50 UTC

PESHAWAR: Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Governor Faisal Karim Kundi on Wednesday constituted an eight-member inquiry commission to probe illegal placer gold mining in the province.
The commission, according to a notification issued by the provincial home and tribal affairs department, will be headed by retired Justice Asadullah Khan Chamkani.
It will also include as members Barrister Mohammad Yaseen Raza, legal adviser/member Sher Akbar Khan, Deputy Inspector General of Police Wasif Rahman, Health Department Additional Secretary Engineer Ziarat Khan, and representatives of intelligence agencies.
According to the notification, the commission will inquire into the extent, history and nature of illegal placer gold mining in KP, particularly in Kohat, Nowshera, Karak and other affected districts. The inquiry includes the scale, duration and locations of illegal operations.
In addition to this, the commission will identify persons, groups and networks involved in illegal mining, including beneficiaries, and determine their “respective roles, financial gains and individual or institutional responsibility for facilitation, corruption, negligence, abuse of authority or failure to discharge legal duties”.
Similarly, it will also inquire into “allegations of patronage, facilitation and connivance by political figures, public representatives, government functionaries, law enforcement personnel or any other persons or institutions, including corruption, abuse of authority, deliberate inaction or protection provided to illegal mining operations”, the notification said.
The commission will also examine the financial dimensions of illegal mining, including the estimated quantity and value of gold illegally extracted, losses caused to the public exchequer, movement of proceeds, money laundering, smuggling and possible links with organised crime, terrorist groups or terrorist financing.
It will additionally examine the role and performance of concerned government departments, district administrations and the police in “preventing illegal mining, monitoring lease conditions, taking enforcement action, registering and investigating cases, prosecuting offenders and dealing with seized machinery, gold and other property”.
The commission has also been tasked to determine the reasons for the continuation or recurrence of illegal mining in spite of repeated complaints, government directions, enforcement operations and criminal proceedings, including negligence, deliberate failure to enforce the law, interference or facilitation by those responsible for enforcement.
In addition to this, it will also inquire into the circumstances surrounding the Kohat incident of Nov 11, including any links between illegal mining networks and the attack on police personnel, possible planning, financing, facilitation or support, and any relevant operational or security lapses, without prejudice to ongoing criminal investigations, the notification said.
Eighteen terrorists were killed while 10 cops were martyred on Nov 11 when a police team came under attack on its way back after completing an operation against elements involved in illegal gold mining.
The committee will also examine previous inquiries, reports, complaints and decisions, including those of the National Accountability Bureau (NAB), Provincial Cabinet Inquiry Committee (PCIC) and other competent authorities, assess the implementation of their findings and recommendations, and examine any fresh material or evidence warranting further inquiry.
The commission will assess the financial and environmental damage caused by illegal mining, including loss of government revenue and damage to riverbeds and adjoining areas, while identifying persons or entities against whom recovery, compensation or other legal action may be warranted.
It will also recommend specific action against those found responsible, including criminal investigation or prosecution, departmental proceedings, recovery of unlawful gains or public losses, and any other action warranted under the applicable laws.
It will also recommend immediate measures, wherever necessary, for preservation of evidence, protection of witnesses and records, securing public assets and preventing interference with the inquiry.
The commission will submit a comprehensive report containing its findings, supporting evidence and the responsibility of persons or institutions concerned, as well as specific recommendations for further action by the competent authorities.
“The commission may examine any person, record, transaction, lease, operation or incident relevant to its mandate, irrespective of the period involved or the position or status of the persons concerned,” the notification added.
It added that the inquiry would not be confined to any particular government, department, institution or category of persons.
According to the notification, the commission will be given access to all relevant material, including a confidential report received from the Prime Minister’s Office vide a letter dated Aug 25, the chief secretary’s directions of Sept 8, NAB and PCIC reports, relevant cabinet decisions, departmental files, lease and auction records, complaints, first information reports, investigation and prosecution records, machinery seizure and release records, and any other material relevant to the inquiry.
“The commission shall exercise the powers available under the West Pakistan Tribunals of Inquiry Ordinance, 1969, including summoning and examining witnesses on oath, requiring the production of records and documents, receiving evidence, calling for information from any person or authority, requisitioning public records and conducting proceedings in accordance with law,” the notification read.
According to the notification, the KP Mines and Minerals Development Department will provide secretarial, administrative and logistical support to the commission.
The commission will submit its report to the KP governor within 45 days of its notification.
The probe has been set up in the aftermath of an attack that led to the martyrdom of 10 police personnel in the Gumbat area of Kohat district on Nov 11 while they were returning from an operation against illegal placer gold mining along the Indus River.
The incident came a day after the Kohat deputy commissioner imposed a 60-day ban on parking excavators within blocks C and D of placer gold mining areas and adjoining locations. According to the order, these could easily be used for unauthorised gold extraction.
Condemning the attack, Kundi on Saturday announced a ban on gold mining across the province and the revocation of all mining leases as part of measures to curb illegal mining.
