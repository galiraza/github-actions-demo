# MQM-P demands nationwide referendum on new provinces next month

• Farooq Sattar urges inclusion of provisions for effective devolution of power in proposed 28th Amendment
• Muttahida slams Bilawal’s ‘bloodshed in Sindh’ statement
KARACHI: The Muttahida Qaumi Movement-Pakistan (MQM-P) has once again called for a nationwide referendum on the creation of new administrative units, proposing that the exercise be held before the start of 2027 to allow people to decide the future administrative structure of the country.
Speaking at a press conference at the party’s Bahadurabad headquarters on Tuesday, senior MQM-P leader and MNA Dr Farooq Sattar called for holding the referendum in December under Article 48(6) of the Constitution.
He also demanded that the true spirit of Article 140-A regarding the devolution of power to local government and the grassroots level be clearly defined and incorporated into the proposed 28th Constitutional Amendment.
He said that the creation of new administrative units should not be treated as a political controversy, arguing that public opinion must determine whether additional provinces or administrative units are needed.
“Who are the political leaders to decide what the people want? If the people of Pakistan want more provinces, they should have the constitutional right to express their will through a referendum,” he said, stressing that no political party should stand in the way of the people’s democratic right.
The MQM-P leader argued that the past “misuse” of referendums could not be made a pretext for denying people their right to express their opinion on an issue directly affecting their future.
Later in the evening, an MQM-P spokesperson strongly condemned Pakistan Peoples Party Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari’s apprehension that bloodshed would occur in urban Sindh over the creation of new provinces.
While talking to reporters at the Karachi Press Club, Mr Bhutto-Zardari expressed the fear that any “forced” attempt to carve up Sindh would lead to bloodshed.
In its reaction, the MQM-P asked Mr Bhutto-Zardari to explain that if provinces are created across the country, why would bloodshed occur only in Karachi and the urban areas of Sindh?
The spokesperson said that the creation of new provinces is a constitutional requirement, and the majority of people in urban areas support the creation of new provinces across the country, including Sindh.
Says party to resist eviction of residents of over 5,000 govt quarters
At the press conference, the MQM-P leader warned that his party would not abandon the residents of government quarters facing eviction in Karachi, alleging that more than 5,000 federal government quarters were at risk of being cleared, potentially rendering thousands of families homeless.
Flanked by MQM-P MNAs Dr Nighat Shakeel, Iftikhar Alam, Abdul Waseem, Faisal Rafiq and Jamal Ahmed, former MNA Salman Mujahid and other party leaders, Dr Sattar said the affected families included descendants of government employees who migrated from different parts of the undivided subcontinent at the invitation of Quaid-i-Azam Mohammad Ali Jinnah after the creation of Pakistan.
Dr Sattar said employees and their families had served the country for decades while residing in government quarters in Jahangir Road East and West, descendants of government employees who migrated from different parts of the undivided subcontinent at the invitation of Quaid-i-Azam Mohammad Ali Jinnah after the creation of Pakistan.
Dr Sattar said employees and their families had served the country for decades while residing in government quarters in Jahangir Road East and West, Martin Quarters, Clayton Quarters, Jamshed Quarters, Pakistan Quarters and F.C Area. The state, he argued, should have granted them ownership rights or relocated them to modern housing schemes at the same locations instead of threatening them with eviction.
Citing the issue’s historical and legal background, he said former prime minister Zulfikar Ali Bhutto had ordered in 1972 that residents should not be evicted until alternative accommodation was provided. General Ziaul Haq upheld the decision in 1979, while the Supreme Court, in a Feb 15, 1984 judgment, also protected the residents against eviction and fresh allotments.
He added that successive governments, including those led by Benazir Bhutto in 1989 and Nawaz Sharif in 1991, had reaffirmed the policy of protecting the residents and granting them ownership rights.
Recalling the 2018 eviction attempt, he said MQM-P leaders had resisted the move despite facing tear gas and baton charges. He said the SC on January 9, 2019, withdrew the eviction order and directed that the matter be resolved through consultations involving elected representatives, the Sindh governor and local stakeholders.
Dr Sattar accused certain officials at the Karachi Estate Office of acting contrary to the stated policy of Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif and Federal Housing Minister Riaz Pirzada. He alleged that the officials were misapplying the 2002 rental ceiling rules and using the Accountant General Pakistan Revenues to make compulsory deductions from the pensions and gratuities of retired employees.
He further alleged that houses belonging to families away on Umrah or religious pilgrimages were being broken into and occupied by land grabbers, who were subsequently constructing and selling illegal multi-storey buildings within weeks.
He urged the prime minister and the federal housing minister to take immediate action against officials involved in the alleged irregularities, warning that MQM-P would surround the Estate Office if eviction notices were not stopped.
“We will not leave any widow, orphan or government employee alone in this struggle,” he said, adding that the protection of affected families remained the party’s responsibility.
