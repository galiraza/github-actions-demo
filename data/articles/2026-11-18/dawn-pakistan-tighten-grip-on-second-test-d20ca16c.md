# Pakistan tighten grip on second Test as Sri Lanka stumble to 74-3

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Wed, 18 Nov 2026 17:37:58 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2045398/pakistan-tighten-grip-on-second-test-as-sri-lanka-stumble-to-74-3
- Collected: 2026-11-18 16:08:16 UTC

---

Pakistan strengthened their grip on the second Test on Wednesday, taking a commanding 227-run first-innings lead before reducing Sri Lanka to 74-3 at stumps on the second day at the Rawalpindi Cricket Stadium.
After wicket-keeper-batter Ghazi Ghori’s half-century and an explosive contribution from Razaullah helped Pakistan reach 325 all out, the hosts’ fast bowlers struck repeatedly to leave Sri Lanka facing a daunting task on day three.
The visitors, who were dismissed for 98 in their first innings, still trail by 153 runs with seven wickets remaining.
Pakistan’s innings had appeared in danger of ending with a considerably smaller advantage after Lahiru Kumara’s five-wicket haul kept the hosts in check. But Ghazi’s 54 off 90 balls, followed by Razaullah’s brisk 42 from 32 deliveries, ensured Pakistan added valuable runs in the lower order.
Resuming at 121-3, Pakistan reached 221-7 at lunch. Azan Awais, who had made 47, was dismissed without adding to his overnight score, while nightwatchman Saqib Khan contributed 37 from 78 balls. Tayyab Arif had earlier made 46 off 82 deliveries.
Kumara was Sri Lanka’s most effective bowler, removing Azan, Pakistan captain Babar Azam (eight), Saqib and Saud Shakeel (24) to put the visitors in a strong position. Asitha Fernando claimed three wickets, including those of Tayyab and Salman Ali Agha (21).
Ghazi then steadied the innings, reaching 54 with four boundaries before Milan Rathnayaka dismissed him. Aamer Jamal added 12, while Razaullah’s aggressive knock, which included four fours and three sixes, lifted Pakistan beyond the 300-run mark.
Kumara eventually bowled Razaullah to finish with five for 95. The final three wickets added 104 runs after lunch, taking Pakistan to 325 in 87.5 overs.
‘Scrappy performance’
Pakistan’s head coach Mike Hesson described the batting effort as scrappy but praised the contributions that helped the side build its lead.
“It was a real scrappy performance for the bat,” he said, singling out Kumara for his persistence and Ghazi for his half-century. Hesson also highlighted Saqib’s contribution as nightwatchman, saying he had batted for almost 100 minutes “at the toughest time of the day”.
Sri Lanka’s reply began poorly. Saqib struck in the third over when Lahiru Udara (seven) picked out Tayyab at extra cover. Razaullah then trapped Dinesh Chandimal leg-before for a duck, leaving the visitors at 10-2.
Kamindu Mendis briefly counter-attacked with three boundaries in his 16, but Aamer had him caught by Mohammad Taha at second slip to make it 39-3. Dhananjaya de Silva, unbeaten on 24, and Pasindu Sooriyabandara, on 18, survived the remaining overs before the close.
Hesson defended Pakistan’s approach to preparing livelier surfaces, saying the aim was to develop players capable of succeeding in different conditions.
“I wouldn’t call it a green top. I’d say you play on pitches like this all around the world,” he said. “We want to play on good cricket wickets where talented players shine through.”
Sri Lanka’s Kumara acknowledged that his side had conceded too many runs in the final session of Pakistan’s innings but remained hopeful of a response from the batters.
He said the team had confidence in its batting line-up and believed the players could perform better on the third day, adding that Pakistan’s batters had played well against the plans Sri Lanka had devised.
Hesson, meanwhile, cautioned against reading too much into the contest so far, noting that six Pakistan players had played either their first or second Test. “The fact that we’ve performed against an experienced Sri Lankan side so far is good signs moving forward,” he said.
Sri Lanka resume on Thursday needing a substantial partnership to reduce the deficit and keep the match alive.
