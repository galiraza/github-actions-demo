# Portion of Islamabad's Margalla Hills National Park excluded from protected area

- Published: Wed, 18 Nov 2026 11:42:35 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2045226/portion-of-islamabads-margalla-hills-national-park-excluded-from-protected-area
- Collected: 2026-11-18 08:10:50 UTC

---

• Under zoning overhaul, Bani Gala residents to get relief 
• Construction activities in Shah Allah Ditta and areas falling under Zone III remain barred, says official
ISLAMABAD: The Capital Development Authority’s plan for developing a major commercial zone in a protected area and the majority of buildings constructed within a two-kilometre radius of Rawal Dam got legal cover as the federal government has approved an amendment to zoning regulations and delineation for Rawal Lake, a part of Margalla Hills National Park.
After this decision, buildings constructed on Constitution Avenue, including the Supreme Court, Islamabad Club and others, are no longer part of Zone III.
Similarly, CDA’s commercial project on a new road and construction activities and projects to be launched by the Special Technology Zone Authority along Park Road on its allotted land will face no restrictions.
The CDA, through a joint venture with the Defence Housing Authority (DHA), is developing Margalla Enclave in the Kuri area, which falls in the permissible area.
However, for the said housing project, a 3.8km link road from Park Road to the housing scheme is being developed, with earthwork almost complete. But the road passes through Zone III, a protected zone where, under CDA regulations, no construction activity can take place.
For setting up a commercial area, the federal government had already granted the said road the status of an arterial/major road of Islamabad and directed the CDA to apply the regulations already notified for GT Road, Islamabad Expressway and other major roads.
Sources said that the federal government had now allowed the CDA to exclude a part of the national park from Zone III. Thus, there is now no legal obstacle to the said road and other projects, including the Islamabad Technopolis Project under the Special Technology Zone Authority (STZA). The STZA has 140 acres of land in this area, which falls in Zone III.
“The area encompassing Rawal Lake and bounded by Srinagar Highway/Murree Road in the north, Club/Murree Road in the west, Park Road in the south, proposed Murree Link Road in the east, and a 200-metre buffer along both sides of Korang River up to two kilometres upstream of Rawal Lake, and south-east of Korang Road,” read a notification issued by the federal government on November 10.
Sources in the Islamabad Wildlife Management Board, Ministry of Climate Change and Interior, confirmed to Dawn the said decision made by the federal cabinet. They said that stakeholders, including the Wildlife Board and CDA, were currently in the process of setting up new markers for the national park area in the said portion, including the Lake View area of Margalla Hills National Park.
Sources said that though the said decision would bring relief to residents of part of Bani Gala, there was still no relief for those living in Shah Allah Ditta and Bhara Kahu. “Changing zoning regulations should not have been taken in isolation for a specific area. The issues of those living in other parts of Zone III should have been considered,” said an official.
He said that residents of Zone III were barred from building even small houses on their own land. Interestingly, construction activities are completely banned in Zone III, mainly in Shah Allah Ditta and a portion of Bhara Kahu.
“With this new decision of the federal government, construction activities are now allowed in the erstwhile part of the National Park Lake View portion, while citizens will continue suffering in other parts of Zone III,” said an official, who requested not to be named.
He said that a CDA summary for including part of Bhara Kahu in the nearest zone and Shah Allah Ditta and adjoining areas in the nearest zone had been awaiting approval from the federal government for the last three years. “That summary should also be taken up by the federal cabinet,” he said.
