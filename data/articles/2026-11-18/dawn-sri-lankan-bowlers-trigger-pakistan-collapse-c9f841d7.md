# Sri Lankan bowlers trigger Pakistan collapse in second Test

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Wed, 18 Nov 2026 13:09:23 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2045398/sri-lankan-bowlers-trigger-pakistan-collapse-in-second-test
- Collected: 2026-11-18 08:10:50 UTC

---

Sri Lanka fought back strongly with the ball on the second morning of the second Test, claiming four wickets to restrict Pakistan to 221 for seven at lunch on Wednesday, although the hosts maintained a 123-run first-innings lead at the Rawalpindi Cricket Stadium.
Resuming their first innings at 121 for three, Pakistan appeared well placed to build on their advantage after bowling Sri Lanka out for a paltry 98 on the opening day. However, disciplined bowling from the visitors, particularly Lahiru Kumara, prevented the hosts from extending their dominance without a fight.
Pakistan added 100 runs in 31.4 overs during the morning session but lost four wickets in the process. Overnight batter Azan Awais, who was unbeaten on 47, failed to add significantly to his tally before falling for the same score as Sri Lanka found the breakthrough.
Kumara led the visitors’ fightback with an impressive spell, using pace and movement to trouble Pakistan’s middle order. Operating from round the wicket, he removed Awais before accounting for Saud Shakeel, who departed for 24.
The Sri Lankan pacer also dismissed nightwatchman Saqib Khan, who had shown considerable patience after resuming on two overnight. Saqib spent more than an hour at the crease and contributed a useful 37 before misjudging a dipping full toss from Kumara, having apparently anticipated a short delivery, and was trapped leg-before-wicket.
Salman Ali Agha offered some resistance with 21 but could not carry on, falling leg-before to Asitha Fernando late in the session.
At lunch, wicket-keeper Ghazi Ghori was unbeaten on eight, with Aamer Jamal on one, as Pakistan reached 221 for seven.
Kumara was the pick of Sri Lanka’s bowlers, claiming three wickets during the morning’s play, while Fernando provided valuable support with two.
Milan Rathnayake also maintained pressure through accurate and economical bowling, despite going wicketless.
The visitors’ response with the ball was particularly significant after a difficult opening day, when Pakistan’s pace attack had bundled them out for just 98.
The home side had capitalised on the conditions to secure a substantial advantage, but Sri Lanka’s morning effort ensured that Pakistan could not turn that position into an even more commanding one.
Pakistan, nevertheless, remain firmly in control with a 123-run lead and three wickets in hand.
