# Trump's Board of Peace faults Israel and Hamas for failure to end Gaza war

The body overseeing US President Donald Trump’s plan to end the Gaza war accused both Israel and Hamas of failing to meet their commitments to end hostilities, leaving the population of Gaza in dire conditions and the ceasefire in danger of collapse.
The 12-page Board of Peace report, seen by Reuters, is the body’s second to the United Nations Security Council since the US-backed ceasefire came into effect 13 months ago. It offers a sobering assessment of progress made since then.
The board, established and headed by Trump, is expected to present the report’s findings to a meeting of the council in New York at the end of November.
“The distance between what has been agreed and what is happening on the ground nonetheless remains the principal risk to the process,” the report said, warning that “a ceasefire that delivers nothing visible cannot be sustained indefinitely.”
In Gaza, witnesses said Israeli tanks advanced onto the main Salahudeen road, west of the Yellow Line that marks the boundary of the two-thirds of the Gaza Strip still under Israeli control despite a ceasefire that has lasted more than a year.
An Israeli airstrike near the army raid killed one person, Palestinian medics said. The medics said separate Israeli airstrikes had killed two other people earlier in the day in Deir al-Balah and Gaza City.
An Israeli military spokesperson claimed the military was carrying out an operation targeting “Hamas infrastructure” west of the Yellow Line in central Gaza and that it had struck a fighter in southern Gaza earlier on Wednesday. The spokesperson had no immediate comment on other strikes.
Peace plan stalled
While the ceasefire has halted major fighting, the wider plan, which calls for Hamas to disarm, Israeli troops to withdraw and reconstruction to begin, has effectively stalled since soon after it came into effect last year.
Hamas agreed in July to a Board of Peace roadmap to surrender its weapons and transfer governance of Gaza to a civilian transitional authority, but Israel rejected the roadmap, saying it will not withdraw until Hamas is dismantled.
Israel has carried out near-daily strikes on Gaza, while the report said Hamas fighters have also continued attacks on Israeli troops.
“Every day that Israel continues strikes against Hamas targets, it delays putting Hamas’ agreement to the roadmap to the test,” the report said.
“Meanwhile, Hamas militant activity continues, the commitment to cease all military activities has not been met in full by the parties, and the population faces another winter in tents and rubble.”
Senior Hamas official Basem Naim dismissed the report as “pure disinformation”, accusing the Board of Peace of providing cover to Israel to evade its commitments under the plan to end the war and to continue its aggression in Gaza.
The office of Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and a spokesperson did not immediately respond to a request for comment.
Israeli ‘absence of decisions’
Gaza’s population of more than 2 million Palestinians lives confined to around a third of the coastal territory, mostly in makeshift tents and damaged buildings, under the control of Hamas fighters. Israel occupies two-thirds of Gaza that has been largely razed and depopulated.
The report criticised Israel’s government for an “absence of decisions” on arrangements to train and deploy a civilian Palestinian police force to replace Hamas as Gaza’s law enforcement authority. It said this was impeding the full implementation of the plan.
It said restrictions imposed by Israel on supplies and equipment, alongside funding shortfalls, had hindered the ability of Gazans to prepare for and respond to seasonal flooding. Heavy rain inundated displacement camps last week across Gaza and more rain is expected later this month.
Hamas, for its part, was collecting taxes on goods entering Gaza that were impacting the availability of basic commodities, the report said.
The Board of Peace was established by Trump to oversee his ambitious plan to end the war that Israel launched in response to Hamas-led attacks from Gaza in October 2023.
Around 1,200 people were killed in Israel in the 2023 Hamas attacks, while the Israeli assault on Gaza has since killed 74,000 Palestinians. More than 1,600 Palestinians, including many civilians, and five Israeli soldiers have been killed in Gaza since the US-mediated ceasefire was struck in October 2025.
Trump’s plan received strong international backing, though the board itself has been met with scepticism from many of Washington’s Western allies.
Netanyahu’s right-wing coalition government remains in power in Israel in a caretaker capacity despite losing its majority in an inconclusive election last month.
On Wednesday, Defence Minister Israel Katz said Israel would not withdraw from Gaza until Hamas was dismantled and Gaza was demilitarised.
