# 14-province idea on the table, says planning minister

Published November 18, 2026
Planning Minister Ahsan Iqbal says he has heard of a plan for 14 provinces in Pakistan, with four in Punjab, four in Sindh, three in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and three in Balochistan.
There has also been discussion about giving Islamabad separate provincial status, he told The News.
“I have heard of four provinces in Punjab, four in Sindh, three in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and three in Balochistan,” Iqbal said. The minister said he had written to Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif in September, recommending the formation of a committee to examine the debate around creating additional provinces. However, no progress had been made on the matter so far. On Islamabad’s governance, Iqbal said the prime minister had tasked him with developing an alternative administrative model for the capital.
“After examining capital cities around the world, we presented a hybrid model of government between a local government and a provincial assembly,” he said, “Islamabad needs a legislative forum and a self-governance scheme.”
The model envisages a legislative forum for Islamabad, as legislation concerning the capital currently has to be taken up by parliament, comprising the National Assembly and Senate.
Under the proposed arrangement, the city could have a chief minister or mayor and an assembly of around 25 members, comprising 21 general seats, three reserved for women and one for minorities. An elected third tier of government, in the form of union councils, could also be introduced at a later stage, Iqbal said.
He added that he had also heard discussions about expanding Islamabad’s boundaries to incorporate adjoining areas of Rawalpindi division, potentially giving the capital the status of an independent province.
Iqbal also said the question of creating additional provinces would have to be resolved before the Senate elections scheduled for March 2027. The 96-member upper house currently gives equal representation to each of the four provinces.
“Otherwise, provinces are established after the Senate elections, the issue could arise that the new provinces may say they do not have proportionate representation in the Senate, and that too for six years,” he said.
Iqbal also dismissed speculation about a presidential system, saying he had not heard of any serious proposal to introduce one.
The minister further urged politicians to take the lead in advancing the debate on creating new provinces.
“Politicians should take the lead and move this debate forward, rather than have proposals emerge from here or there. Being evasive is not helping,” he said, “Pakistan needs administrative reforms because after eighteenth amendment provinces didn’t devolve an iota of function creating top heavy provincial structures.”
