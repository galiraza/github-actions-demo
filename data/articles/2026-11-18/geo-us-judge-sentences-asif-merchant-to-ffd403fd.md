# US judge sentences Asif Merchant to 27 years over alleged plot to kill Trump

Published November 18, 2026
A US federal judge has sentenced a Pakistani national to more than 27 years in prison for plotting to assassinate President Donald Trump and other prominent US politicians in 2024, in what US prosecutors described as an operation directed by Iran.
Asif Merchant, 48, was sentenced to 330 months by US District Judge Eric R Komitee after a federal jury convicted him in March of attempting to commit an act of terrorism transcending national boundaries and murder for hire, the US Department of Justice said on Tuesday.
Prosecutors said Merchant had attempted to recruit people in the United States to carry out the killings in retaliation for Washington's 2020 assassination of Iranian military commander Qassem Soleimani.
The potential targets included Trump, then-president Joe Biden and former US ambassador to the United Nations Nikki Haley, who were all presidential candidates during the 2024 election campaign, according to evidence presented at the trial.
Merchant testified that he had become involved with Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC), but said he acted under pressure because he feared for the safety of his family in Tehran. He said an Iranian handler had named the three potential targets but that he had not been given instructions to kill a specific person.
According to the US Justice Department, Merchant began working with the IRGC in late 2022 or early 2023 and received training in intelligence techniques, including counter-surveillance. He repeatedly travelled to Iran to meet his alleged handler before being sent to the United States in 2024 to recruit people for the assassination plan.
After arriving in the United States in April 2024, Merchant approached an acquaintance in New York whom he believed could help him arrange the killings. The acquaintance instead alerted the authorities and became a confidential informant.
Merchant subsequently met people he believed were hired killers, but who were undercover US law enforcement officers. He discussed killing a political figure at a rally, as well as stealing documents and arranging protests at political gatherings, according to the Justice Department.
He paid the purported hitmen $5,000 as an advance payment for the assassination plot, prosecutors said. The money had been obtained with assistance from someone overseas.
Merchant was arrested in Richmond, Texas, on July 12, 2024, as he was preparing to leave the United States. No attack was carried out.
His arrest came a day before an unrelated assassination attempt against Trump at a campaign rally in Butler, Pennsylvania. Reuters reported at the time that there was no indication Merchant was connected to that shooting.
Merchant's defence argued that he had acted reluctantly and under pressure from the Iranian operative, who he said had threatened his family in Iran. He testified that he believed US authorities would eventually stop the plan and that he might be able to cooperate with them.
Prosecutors challenged his account, pointing to anti-American and anti-Trump posts on his social media accounts following Soleimani's killing. They argued that his actions demonstrated a willingness to carry out the plot. The judge, while acknowledging Merchant's concern for his family, said he was also highly motivated to harm the United States, according to an Associated Press report.
The case had also prompted inquiries in Pakistan following Merchant's arrest in 2024. Geo News reported in August that sources in Pakistan's Counter Terrorism Department had found no criminal record for him in the country. The sources said authorities had examined his travel history and records of mobile phone SIMs and vehicles used by him.
At the time, the Foreign Office said Pakistan was in contact with US authorities and awaiting further details about the charges against Merchant.
Iran has denied targeting Trump or plotting attacks against US officials.
