# Aamer Jamal, Razaullah strike for Pakistan as Sri Lanka lose two wickets early on day three of second Test

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Thu, 19 Nov 2026 13:00:41 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2045665/aamer-jamal-razaullah-strike-for-pakistan-as-sri-lanka-lose-two-wickets-early-on-day-three-of-second-test
- Collected: 2026-11-19 08:22:15 UTC

---

Pakistan’s fast bowlers exploited a lively surface to claim two wickets in the opening session of the third day of the second Test against Sri Lanka, leaving the visitors at 166-5 at lunch but with Kusal Mendis and Sonal Dinusha offering stubborn resistance.
Sri Lanka, resuming their second innings at 74-3, added 92 runs in the morning session but lost the important wickets of Dhananjaya de Silva and Pasindu Sooriyabandara. They still trailed Pakistan by 60 runs.
Aamer Jamal provided the breakthrough in the first hour, removing de Silva for 37. The right-arm pacer struck the batter on the pads with a delivery that jagged back sharply, prompting an lbw decision which was upheld after de Silva reviewed.
De Silva had looked comfortable during his 47-ball innings, striking six boundaries, and his dismissal left Sri Lanka at 95-4.
Razaullah then ended Sooriyabandara’s determined resistance, dismissing the batter for 38 off 116 deliveries.
Having weathered a sustained examination from Pakistan’s seamers, Sooriyabandara fell while attempting to glance a delivery down the leg side, only managing a faint edge that wicket-keeper Ghazi Ghori held with a diving catch.
His innings, which included three boundaries, was particularly valuable on a surface offering considerable movement and uneven bounce.
Pakistan were without fast bowler Saqib Khan, who has been ruled out of further bowling in the match after sustaining a left hamstring strain on Wednesday. However, Aamer and Razaullah maintained pressure with disciplined lines and lengths, repeatedly beating the outside edges.
Mohammad Abbas also troubled the Sri Lankan batters, although he remained wicketless in the session despite several deliveries moving sharply off the pitch.
Kusal, meanwhile, showed composure in negotiating the difficult conditions. The Sri Lankan skipper was unbeaten on 32 off 58 balls, having struck five fours, while Dinusha was on 19 from 39 deliveries.
The pair added an unbroken 40 runs for the sixth wicket, with Dinusha also finding boundaries against Salman Ali Agha’s spin.
