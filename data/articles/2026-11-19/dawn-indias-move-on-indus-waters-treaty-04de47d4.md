# India’s move on Indus Waters Treaty is 'outlaw behaviour', Pakistan tells UN

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Thu, 19 Nov 2026 11:13:57 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2045644/indias-move-on-indus-waters-treaty-is-outlaw-behaviour-pakistan-tells-un
- Collected: 2026-11-19 08:22:15 UTC

UNITED NATIONS: Pakistan has described India’s unilateral decision to place the Indus Waters Treaty (IWT) in abeyance as “outlaw behaviour”, warning that the move violates international law, threatens the water security of more than 240 million people and risks escalating tensions between the two nuclear-armed neighbours.
Speaking at a UN Security Council meeting on natural resources and conflict prevention, Pakistan’s representative said India was seeking unilateral authority to decide when the treaty applied and what conditions should govern its implementation.
“That is not treaty law; it is outlaw behaviour,” First Secretary Ansar Shah said in Pakistan’s right of reply to remarks by the Indian representative.
He said weaponising the waters on which more than 240 million Pakistanis depended was not a “reassessment” of the treaty but an act of coercion that created conditions conducive to conflict.
Pakistan’s position was initially presented by Ambassador Usman Jadoon, Deputy Permanent Representative of Pakistan, at an Arria-formula meeting convened by Liberia on November 18 under the title “Natural Resources and Conflict: Strengthening Cooperation for Prevention and Peace”.
Jadoon said natural resources should serve sustainable development, shared prosperity and peace rather than become sources of exploitation and conflict. He warned that external interference, commercial interests, historical grievances, ethnic tensions, political exclusion and weak institutions could combine to turn resource wealth into a source of instability.
Expressing solidarity with African countries seeking sovereign control over their natural resources, he cited the Democratic Republic of Congo, where immense mineral wealth had sustained conflict instead of bringing prosperity to the population.
He outlined three priorities for preventing resource-related conflicts: addressing underlying political and governance problems; disrupting illicit financial flows through international cooperation; and ensuring that resource-rich developing countries benefited from investment, technology transfer, local processing and skills development.
Developing countries, he argued, should become equal partners in global supply chains rather than remain suppliers of raw materials.
Jadoon stressed that responsibility for disrupting illicit resource flows could not rest solely with producing countries. Transit and consuming states, multinational corporations and financial institutions also had to contribute through stronger traceability mechanisms, responsible supply chains and information-sharing.
Turning to shared natural resources, particularly water, he said cooperation among neighbouring states and peaceful dispute settlement were essential for regional stability. Citing UN Security Council Resolution 2788, he underlined the importance of peaceful means of resolving disputes envisaged under Article 33 of the UN Charter.
These principles, he said, were particularly relevant to transboundary water resources, where respect for international law and mutually agreed arrangements was indispensable.
Jadoon accused India of seeking to weaponise water against Pakistan by unilaterally placing the IWT in abeyance and threatening to stop river flows into the country.
He cited the August 31, award of the Court of Arbitration in the Indus Waters dispute, which found that the treaty remained fully in force and that India continued to be bound by its obligations. The court also rejected the grounds advanced by India to justify suspending or terminating the agreement.
He said no country should be allowed to use shared natural resources as instruments of coercion or political pressure in violation of international law and binding treaty obligations.
India defends its position
Responding to Pakistan’s intervention, India’s Permanent Representative, Ambassador Harish Parvathaneni, defended the use of existing international mechanisms for managing resource-related disputes, citing the Kimberley Process as an example of cooperation that had helped curb the trade in conflict diamonds while protecting legitimate commerce.
Pakistan rejects Indian allegations
Exercising his right of reply, Shah accused the Indian representative of resorting to deflection and denial rather than addressing the treaty dispute.
He said the 1960 IWT contained no provision allowing either party to place it unilaterally in abeyance. Responding to India’s argument that the world had changed since the treaty was signed, Shah pointed out that the UN Charter was even older.
“Does India consider that outdated as well?” he asked, accusing New Delhi of disregarding Security Council resolutions on Jammu and Kashmir.
Shah alleged that Indian forces had killed more than 100,000 Kashmiris since 1989 and continued to suppress their fundamental rights and deny them self-determination.
He also rejected what he called frivolous and inappropriate remarks by the Indian representative about Pakistan’s domestic affairs, describing Pakistan as a constitutional and democratic country where citizens were free to choose their leaders and exercise their fundamental rights.
Countering India’s accusations, he accused the ruling Hindutva establishment of promoting discrimination against minorities, suppressing free speech and inciting religious hatred.
Shah urged India to abandon its “hubris” and comply with international law and its obligations under treaties and Security Council resolutions.
The diplomatic exchange highlighted the widening disagreement between the two neighbours over the future of the treaty, which governs the sharing of the Indus river system.
Under the agreement, the eastern rivers — Ravi, Beas and Sutlej — were allocated primarily to India, while Pakistan received rights over the western rivers — Indus, Jhelum and Chenab — subject to specified uses by India.
