# MQM-P's Khalid Maqbool urges courts to take notice of Bilawal's 'bloodshed' remark

- Published: Thu, 19 Nov 2026 17:51:04 +0500

---

Muttahida Qaumi Movement-Pakistan (MQM-P) chief Dr Khalid Maqbool Siddiqui on Thursday urged the courts to take notice of PPP Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari’s remark, which the latter made during a speech that covered the creation of new provinces, among other matters.
“We talk about the garbage on Karachi’s streets, but if Sindh is divided, we will see blood flow through its streets,” Bilawal had said, calling for a “political reset” in the same address. Asked about it a day later, the PPP chief insisted he was not issuing a threat, but rather warning of the likely reaction to Sindh’s division.
The speech drew a strong reaction from leaders of PML-N, as well as its ally MQM-P, which has been calling for the creation of new administrative units in the country, particularly Karachi.
In a fresh rebuke of Bilawal on Thursday, Siddiqui particularly took exception to the Bhutto scion’s warning of bloodshed and said it warranted that the courts take suo motu notice.
Speaking at a press conference in Karachi, the MQM-P convenor said his party expected to be countered with arguments, not threats, in response to its own arguments.
“One should pause and think carefully before threatening us and the people of Sindh’s urban areas,” he said, alleging that presently, Pakistan had “feudalistic democracy” that was a far cry from democracy.
In “feudalistic democracy”, a political worker would not transition to becoming a leader, but an individual was made a leader on the basis of their background, he explained.
“And when the leadership emerges from dynastic and feudal backgrounds, their temperament is also feudalistic,” he commented.
Siddiqui asserted that MQM-P had been calling for the creation of new provinces across the country and never restricted their demand to just Sindh or Karachi.
Directly addressing Bilawal, he questioned: “If the demand was for the creation of provinces in all of Pakistan, why would there be bloodshed in just Karachi, Hyderabad, Mirpurkhas or Nawabshah? Are you threatening us?”
He alleged that PPP’s objection to the creation of new provinces in Sindh was due to “Karachi contributing 97 per cent to the province’s budget”.
Siddiqui then reiterated his query: “The urban areas of Sindh are waiting for an answer to the question why there would be bloodshed in these areas? And who will do it? Will you get Uzair Baloch to do it?”
Commenting on PPP’s governance in Sindh, Siddiqui highlighted issues of low literacy and lack of development in the province.
“Corruption happens everywhere in the world. It is called a by-product of development. But in Sindh, development is the by-product of corruption,” Siddiqui further alleged.
He went on to point out that the PPP was not just in power in Sindh but also had governance roles in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Gilgit-Baltistan and Balochistan.
“Were you able to become rulers there under democratic requirements? … We have asked Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif about this as well?” Siddiqui questioned.
Again, addressing Bilawal, Siddiqui said, “We respect you and we honour your mandate, but you’re using this kind of language because we had decided that we won’t fight.
“But we will still fight the constitutional and legal battle. We will still talk in the assemblies.”
He then again urged the courts and the federal government to take notice of Bilawal’s “threat” and asked the PPP scion to respond to the MQM-P through arguments because “threats do not work on us”.
Sindh govt to contact MQM-P, others on planned rallies
On the other hand, Sindh Senior Minister Sharjeel Inam Memon maintained that his PPP did not want any kind of tension, stressing that each person could hold a different opinion without “issuing edicts” on it.
Addressing a press conference later in the day, Memon clarified that Bilawal had “very clearly said” that his party does not incite violence or take up arms.
The minister stressed that the PPP chairman spoke considering the history of Sindh, which had witnessed communal clashes in the 1970s.
Memon said Bilawal specifically spoke of cities where different communities resided, hence heightening the risk of tensions.
Memon further announced that the provincial government has approved the procurement of 500 electric buses for Karachi, terming it “major good news”.
On plans of the MQM-P and the Sindh Taraqqi-pasand Party (STP) holding rallies in Karachi on Nov 29, Memon said the Sindh government would contact both parties and urge them not to go ahead with their gatherings.
MQM-P had earlier this month announced the rally on Sharea Faisal as part of the party’s campaign for new administrative units. Some Sindhi nationalist parties, including STP, have also announced their own gathering against new provinces on Sharea Faisal on the same day.
A day earlier, MQM-P leader Mustafa Kamal told a private news channel that his party would not hold the rally on Nov 29, but on some other date to avoid any clash.
According to Sharjeel, the nationalist party first announced the rally, following which MQM-P decided to hold their separate gathering.
“You know what happens if people with two differing opinions gather in one place on the same date,” the Sindh minister remarked, hinting at a possible law and order situation.
He stressed that the Sindh Assembly was the most powerful forum of public representatives in Sindh, adding that such decisions were to be made in the assembly, not on the streets.
“So, our request to all parties will be that you should not create such an environment,” the minister said.
