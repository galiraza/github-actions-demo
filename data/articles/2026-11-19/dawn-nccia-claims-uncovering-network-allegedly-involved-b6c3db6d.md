# NCCIA claims uncovering network allegedly involved in up to Rs1tr betting, money laundering

KARACHI: The National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency (NCCIA) on Thursday claimed to have uncovered an extensive alleged financial network involved in illegal online betting and gambling, fraudulent business operations and suspected money laundering, with an estimated transaction exposure of up to approximately Rs1 trillion.
Speaking at a press conference at his office, Karachi NCCIA Additional Director Shahzad Akbar said the investigation was initiated after suspicious financial activities were reported through the State Bank of Pakistan (SBP), which revealed massive transactions across commercial banks, microfinance banks and digital wallets.
Investigators identified an alleged organised network operating through online betting and gambling platforms, including 1XBet, Stake, X777, X555, 99c444, X111 and MelBet.
The network allegedly collected gambling-related payments through digital channels, including two renowned mobile wallets, before routing the funds through multiple bank accounts, registered companies, fictitious business establishments and third-party account holders.
“These arrangements were allegedly used to conceal the actual source, ownership and ultimate beneficiaries of the funds,” the NCCIA officer said.
The investigation also came across suspected fraudulent sub-merchant registrations, forged identity documents, fictitious businesses and accounts allegedly operated as rented or mule accounts.
Billions in suspicious transactions unearthed
According to NCCIA, a detailed financial analysis revealed exceptionally high transaction volumes in accounts allegedly associated with the network:
- Rs. 3.72 billion credited to one suspected settlement account within approximately 22 months
- Rs. 2.43bn credited to another company account.
- Rs. 1.97bn received through suspected rented or mule accounts.
These transactions were found to be grossly disproportionate to the declared business activities and financial capacity of the respective account holders, the cybercrime agency said.
The overall estimated transaction exposure may be up to approximately Rs1tr, highlighting the scale and complexity of the suspected financial network.
In a significant financial enforcement operation, Karachi’s NCCIA secured approximately Rs1bn in suspected illicit funds across multiple financial accounts within a single day, preventing their further withdrawal, transfer or potential dissipation.
“This action represents a major development in disrupting the financial infrastructure allegedly supporting the illegal betting and gambling network,” said the NCCIA additional director.
Banking officials, company directors under probe
The investigation has brought suspected banking officials, company directors, business operators, payment facilitators and account holders under scrutiny.
According to a first information report (FIR), certain banking officials allegedly facilitated the continued operation of suspicious accounts despite anti-money laundering alerts and other warning indicators.
“Their potential involvement and any institutional lapses are being examined through banking records, financial documentation and digital evidence,” Akbar said.
NCCIA Karachi conducted coordinated raids at multiple premises and arrested several individuals allegedly involved in the network.
The FIR, dated Nov 18, was registered under relevant provisions of the Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act (Peca), read with applicable provisions of the Pakistan Penal Code.
The FIR was filed against 40 individuals and business entities, including 32 chief executive officers of companies and five suspects arrested in connection with the alleged offences.
The NCCIA additional director claimed that most of those involved in online gambling by developing “different” apps were Chinese nationals.
Last week, the NCCIA called for stricter regulations governing company registration and bank account verification, warning that loopholes in the existing system are being exploited by illegal call centres, online betting operators and international fraud networks to launder money and defraud victims.
NCCIA Islamabad/Rawalpindi Additional Director Muhammad Shoaib Khan explained that fake companies were being registered through weak verification procedures, after which bank accounts opened in their names were linked to loan applications, betting and gaming portals to receive and transfer illicit funds.
