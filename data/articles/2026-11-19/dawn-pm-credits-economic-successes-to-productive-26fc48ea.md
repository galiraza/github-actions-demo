# PM credits economic successes to 'productive partnership' between political, military leadership

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Thu, 19 Nov 2026 17:20:44 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2045679/pm-credits-economic-successes-to-productive-partnership-between-political-military-leadership
- Collected: 2026-11-19 16:15:47 UTC

---

Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif on Thursday said Pakistan’s economic achievements were the product of a “productive partnership between the military and the political hierarchy”.
He was addressing a ceremony organised by the American Business Council of Pakistan.
The premier told the business community: “I can tell you without any fear of contradiction that this and other humble achievements couldn’t have been possible without this great team and great teamwork.
“And of course, I would say, again without any fear of contradiction, when was the last time you had struck a very productive partnership between military and political hierarchy? Never in the history of Pakistan.”
PM Shehbaz said he wished there had been such a partnership in the past, contending that Pakistan “would have completely transformed long ago” if that was the case.
“We would have touched the apex of glory through this teamwork, understanding, showing flexibility to each other in the national interest, to support the national interest, to serve the national interest,” he maintained.
The prime minister said he would refrain from taking credit for himself; rather, the credit goes to his team and the “other partners in this field” — Chief of Defence Forces and Chief of Army Staff Field Marshal Asim Munir and his team.
“We should make not only Pakistan’s economy stable, but make it vibrant,” he stated, adding that this required “sincerity of purpose untiring efforts, selfless service to the people and rising above your likes and dislikes”.
PM Shehbaz recalled that the country faced a serious threat of default in 2023. He then hailed his government’s efforts so far, highlighting various statistics showing macroeconomic stability.
“Despite the Gulf crisis, which has jolted even the most powerful economies around the globe, Pakistan is still passing through a period of stability,” he said, while acknowledging that the situation is “very fragile” in terms of energy requirements.
The premier asserted that despite the country importing more than 90 per cent of its energy requirements from abroad, no panic or long queues were seen at fuel stations.
“In fact, to the contrary, we have been able to carve out a subsidy programme to shelter the most vulnerable segments of our society,” PM Shehbaz said, referring to the Prime Minister’s Fuel Relief Scheme.
“It all came through hard work, untiring efforts, and pain borne by the common man of this country.”
Talking about the Middle East crisis, the premier said that Pakistan was “working tirelessly” to restore peace in the region and was talking to Tehran and Washington with the support of friendly and brotherly countries.
“Difficult as it is, I have no doubts in my mind that we will be able to arrest this situation. If peace is restored in the region, we are about to take off to an altitude never seen before,” he said.
During his address, the premier acknowledged that the members of the American Business Council of Pakistan had contributed “immensely” in promoting Pakistan’s economic interests.
“I have no doubt that you are great ambassadors for Pakistan,” he said.
The prime minister said that he was present today to listen to business community members and put in place a mechanism to address their genuine grievances. He said that the government was committed to removing impediments and bottlenecks in the way of investors.
“Transparency is the primary requirement of such a system. We are trying our best to be as transparent as we possible,” he said.
He went on to say that during the last budget, the government gave tax breaks worth Rs400 billion despite being under an International Monetary Fund (IMF) programme.
He said that the tax breaks were given to the exporters, manufacturing and corporate sectors and the salaried class to ensure that “this becomes a stimulus to promote and strengthen our economy”.
“Of course, you cannot solve this problem of seven decades overnight. I think we should stand up accept the challenge and move forward in unison. That is the only way to find our last place in the comity of nations,” he said.
The premier said that Pakistan had mineral deposits worth “trillions of dollars”. He said that for the past two to three years, work has been actively carried out in this sector.
“We have together taken the first step; God willing, we’ll keep marching in the right direction. And if we do not deviate, I can assure you, without any fear of contradiction, that we will reach our destination one day soon,” he said.
PM Shehbaz also talked about the auto industry, saying that they had received subsidies for years but had very little to show for it.
“And yet they do not want to face competition. This is the time for electric vehicles […] Is it in national interest to support them or the set of policies and modern technology which will a reduce our dependence on imported energy and control climate [disasters]?” he asked.
Concluding his remarks, the premier said that the government took “difficult decisions, exercised physical discipline and embarked upon a programme of structural reforms”.
“Our task now is to consolidate these gains and put Pakistan firmly on the path of sustained export-led growth. It has to be export-led growth,” he said.
