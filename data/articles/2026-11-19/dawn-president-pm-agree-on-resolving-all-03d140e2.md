# President, PM agree on resolving 'all matters' through dialogue amid rising political temperatures

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Thu, 19 Nov 2026 20:06:00 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2045691/president-pm-agree-on-resolving-all-matters-through-dialogue-amid-rising-political-temperatures
- Collected: 2026-11-19 16:15:47 UTC

---

President Asif Ali Zardari and Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif have decided that all matters will be resolved through dialogue, the Presidency said on Thursday after the two leaders met in Islamabad.
The meeting follows recent hard-hitting remarks by President Zardari’s son, PPP Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari, who questioned past decisions and actions taken by the ruling coalition — steered by PML-N and supported by PPP.
In a statement, the Presidency said the “current situation” was discussed in Thursday’s meeting.
Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi, whom Bilawal made pointed remarks about, was also present at the huddle.
The “current political situation was reviewed in detail”, while the Middle East conflict also came under discussion.
It was decided that the leadership will “sit down together on all matters and resolve them through consultation”, the Presidency said.
“The previous constitutional amendments were passed through mutual consultation among all allied parties,” the statement from President Zardari’s office said.
The recent friction between coalition partners PML-N and PPP comes amid a debate over the creation of new administrative units, which was sparked by Naqvi in July when he called for a “reset” in governance.
The PPP has consistently opposed carving out new provinces in Sindh, which it rules. The party has consistently invoked a unanimous resolution passed by the Sindh Assembly opposing the province’s division, although President Zardari has not commented on the matter publicly.
Bilawal’s remarks on Tuesday drew a strong reaction from leaders of the PML-N, as well as the Muttahida Qaumi Movement-Pakistan (MQM-P) — another coalition ally.
Despite months having passed since the debate began, Bilawal claimed that no one from the government had brought up the matter with him or President Zardari, even though some federal ministers have voiced their support for more provinces.
His remarks followed the Nov 14 meeting between President Zardari and PM Shehbaz. According to official sources, the two reached an understanding on sticking to the constitutional process for any new provinces.
Speaking at a ‘Meet the Press’ event in Karachi, the Bhutto scion expressed the fear that any “forced” attempt to carve up Sindh into new provinces would lead to bloodshed.
He also voiced his strong opposition to a referendum on creating new provinces — as initially suggested by Naqvi and endorsed by Information Minister Attaullah Tarar in response to Bilawal’s speech.
Bilawal even questioned whether the judiciary had the public’s confidence, particularly in the wake of the 27th Amendment, which he had strongly advocated for at the time of its passage just a year ago.
He said that the 27th Amendment was passed with a “brute majority” rather than a mutual consensus.
However, in a later interview, Bilawal clarified that he was not “disowning” the amendment, but rather advocating for greater consensus on such key legislation.
As he called for a “political and democratic reset” to meet the growing economic challenges, the PPP chairman suggested some steps the government could take to restore the opposition’s confidence so the Parliament could function properly.
