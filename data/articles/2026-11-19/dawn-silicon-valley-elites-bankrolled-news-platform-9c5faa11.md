# Silicon Valley elites 'bankrolled' news platform Visegrad24 publishing pro-Israel content: report

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Fri, 20 Nov 2026 01:04:03 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2045708/silicon-valley-elites-bankrolled-news-platform-visegrad24-publishing-pro-israel-content-report
- Collected: 2026-11-19 21:19:27 UTC

---

An extensive investigation by American magazine Wired revealed on Thursday how an “anti-migrant, pro-Israel propaganda machine” took form and gained traction via bankrolling by Silicon Valley elites.
The investigative report, published by Wired on Thursday morning, detailed the rise of the Polish right-wing news media platform Visegrad24 and how its founder, Stefan Tompson, deliberately engineered a pro-Israel editorial stance to make money from influential supporters, rapidly coming to “dominate” breaking-news discussion of the conflict in Gaza on social media platform X after the attack by Hamas on Oct 7, 2023.
The account, at the time three years old, worked “around the clock to pump out nearly 400 posts” on the attacks within three days after Oct 7: “a mix of terse news updates, soft tributes to Israeli victims, and — above all — rage-bait videos showing allegedly ‘pro-Hamas’ demonstrations in cities across the West”, the report said.
These racked up 370 million views, launching it into the spotlight overnight as “one of the most influential” pro-Israel accounts on X.
Dawn has not independently verified the information or internal correspondence mentioned in the Wired report.
Texts reviewed by Wired from Tompson to an anonymous colleague 15 days after the attack indicated that the Visegrad24 founder was already wary that Israel was angling for a wider regional conflict, writing, “The Israelis do seem to be doing some vicious things.”
Nevertheless, it revealed, “the founder reasoned a way through his qualms”, messaging the same colleague later the same day to detail his logic on arriving at an “editorial line” on Israel.
“As Tompson saw it, V24 had been faced with ‘four options’ once the war started,” Wired reported, quoting him as follows:
1. Say nothing;
2. Be objective — get hated by both sides;
3. Pro Muslim — there are 1.5 billion of them;
4. Pro Israel — There are fewer but also they have a lot of influence and money.
Tompson here admitted that his “goal is to fundraise off of this”, the report added, noting that he succeeded according to “an extensive trove of V24 financial records, internal communications, screenshots from group chats, and correspondence with donors”.
Wired added that the documents it obtained were responded to by Tompson “with a blanket denial and an email from a lawyer”, and that he did not agree to an interview with the magazine when they declined to send him the questions in advance.
“The documents […] reveal how the right kind of online influence, even when it’s strategically acquired by an entity just barely above the gutter of white nationalism, can command support from some of the most powerful people in the world,” the report read.
According to the report, messages to the colleague in November 2023 revealed that Tompson was added to two groups “that actively push back against founders who have supported Palestine”.
One of these, named Jewish Alliances, was described as a space to “bring together” those working to fight anti-Semitism, and the V24 founder was recommended by a general partner at Sequoia Capital, Shaun Maguire, who Wired claimed had helped to connect the Israel Defence Forces with Elon Musk’s Starlink satellite communication network.
The other group, an informal WhatsApp thread, consisted of “hundreds of the most powerful figures from Silicon Valley, Wall Street, and Hollywood” discussing how to build support for Israel after the Oct 7 attacks, according to a Puck newsletter. These group chats engaged in activities such as buying domains with pro-Palestinian phrases to “redirect to appropriate content”, such as longlivetheintifada.com and palestineisourhome.com.
According to Wired, “the two group chats appear to have paid off pretty handsomely for Visegrad24” in terms of fundraising, with Tompson revealing in messages to his colleague (with matching financial records) that donations were rolling in from major investment firms such as Ribbit Capital.
Notifications from the website GiveButter, which he used to solicit contributions, show other donations “linked to email addresses associated with” presidents, co-founders, CEOs and heavyweights at various other major companies, including SimpleVenue CEO Michael Sinensky, Warby Parker founder Neil Blumenthal, Wix president Nir Zohar, Lux Capital cofounder Josh Wolfe, and deep tech venture capitalist Matt Ocko.
The parties named either declined to comment, claimed to have no records of the donations, or declined to confirm while arguing that V24 did not count as a “far-right” group, according to the magazine.
Nevertheless, “over a million dollars” in donations poured in for the organisation, with the magazine reporting that “much of the money, including some of the largest sums, appears to have come in through ‘donor advised funds’ — ‘dark money’ philanthropic vehicles that allow donors to hide their identities, even from the recipients of their cash”.
These donations included $175,000 from DonorsTrust, “a fund closely aligned with the Trump administration’s priorities, often referred to as the ‘dark money ATM’ of the American right”, it said.
Wired also reported that Tompson encouraged his colleague to “play both sides” by taking opportunities to raise funds from Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates. “I do the Jewish side. You do Arabs,” he allegedly wrote.
According to the report, V24, founded in 2020, gained traction after its “intense volume” of coverage of the Russian invasion of Ukraine — as well as “international notoriety for spreading fake news”, with many of its claims being debunked.
It added that Tompson also denied that he was given money for the organisation by the Polish state, although Polish news outlets reported that the prime minister had tried to allocate “about $300,000” to the project in 2022 and “a bit under $600,000” in 2023, sending it to “a sports and fitness NGO” that appeared to be a “carve-out for a V24-affiliated project”.
Later, when Israel launched an attack on Iran in 2025, V24 took a stance of “enthusiasm” for restoring the leadership of Reza Pahlavi and even posted to “influence” US President Donald Trump to join the attack, it said.
The success of V24 saw Tompson interviewed by Michael Eisenberg in 2024, an American-born Israeli venture capitalist “often billed as an informal adviser to Israeli prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu”, it said.
