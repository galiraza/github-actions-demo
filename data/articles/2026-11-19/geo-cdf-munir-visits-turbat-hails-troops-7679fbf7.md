Published November 19, 2026
Chief of Army Staff (COAS) and Chief of Defence Forces (CDF) Field Marshal Asim Munir has commended the Pakistan Army's operational preparedness and professionalism amid challenging operational conditions, the Inter-Services Public Relations (ISPR) said on Thursday.
The army chief made the remarks during his visit to Turbat city, where he was given a comprehensive briefing on the prevailing security environment, operational preparedness and successful ongoing operations against externally sponsored terrorism, the military's media wing said in a statement.
The Corps Commander Balochistan received the army chief upon his arrival, after which he laid a floral wreath at the Shuhada Monument and offered fateha.
During his interaction with the troops, Field Marshal Munir lauded their unwavering resolve to defend the motherland.
The army chief reaffirmed the Pakistan Army's commitment to stand shoulder to shoulder with the people of Balochistan in their continued pursuit of peace, prosperity and sustainable development.
During the visit, the army chief was also apprised of the progress of various ongoing socio-economic development initiatives for the people of Balochistan.
CDF Munir's visit comes days after two Pakistan Army personnel, including a major, were martyred and eight terrorists were killed in Balochistan's Kolpur area.
According to the ISPR, the India-backed terrorists targeted military vehicles on patrol using armed quadcopters on November 14, resulting in the martyrdom of Major Omer Minhas Nasir and Havildar Muhammad Adnan Khan.
Pakistan has witnessed a surge in terror activities, particularly in its Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP) and Balochistan provinces, since 2021, when the Afghan Taliban came into power.
According to a report by the Centre for Research and Security Studies (CRSS), the two provinces remained the country's main hotspots for violence during the second quarter of 2026, with KP emerging as the worst-affected region.
The report stated that KP and Balochistan together accounted for nearly 96% of all violence-related fatalities recorded during the quarter. Of the total deaths, more than 61% (475) were reported in KP, while Balochistan accounted for 34% (265).
KP also recorded the highest number of violent incidents, with 151 cases, representing 57% of the nationwide total. Balochistan followed with 94 incidents, or 35%, underscoring the concentration of militant activity in the two provinces.
