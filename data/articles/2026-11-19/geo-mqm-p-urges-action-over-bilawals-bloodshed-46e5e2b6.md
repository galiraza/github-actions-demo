# MQM-P urges action over Bilawal's 'bloodshed' remarks amid debate on new provinces

Published November 19, 2026
Muttahida Qaumi Movement-Pakistan (MQM-P) Chairman Dr Khalid Maqbool Siddiqui has said the party has been asked to reconsider its November 29 rally in Karachi following concerns over the law and order situation.
"Law enforcement agencies contacted us and reviewed the law and order situation," Siddiqui said while holding a press conference in Karachi on Thursday. He added that a new date for the rally would be announced after consultations.
The party had announced plans to hold a rally on Karachi's Sharea Faisal on November 29 as part of its campaign for new administrative units.
Sindh Taraqi Pasand (STP) Chairman Dr Qadir Magsi had also announced a similar move in the port city on the same day against the creation of new provinces in Sindh.
Pakistan Peoples Party (PPP) Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari later voiced concerns that any "forced" attempt to carve up Sindh would lead to bloodshed.
Addressing an event in Karachi on November 17, the PPP chief said that he supported the creation of provinces in accordance with public wishes, the Constitution and the law, but would resist any move imposed through force.
The development comes amid intensifying debate over the creation of new provinces, with the PPP at odds with the Pakistan Muslim League-Nawaz (PML-N)-led federal government and its allies on the issue.
Speaking during today's presser, the MQM-P chairman said the issue of the new provinces is being discussed across Pakistan and has also come under debate in legislative assemblies.
He maintained that provinces, like districts and divisions, were administrative units and argued that new provinces should be created across the country in accordance with population.
"The demand being raised by urban areas of Sindh is now being discussed across the country," Siddiqui said.
He said that MQM-P had never demanded a separate Karachi province or a province exclusively for the Muhajir community.
The MQM-P chief also questioned why new provinces could be considered in Punjab but not in Sindh, referring to a resolution presented by the PPP in the Punjab Assembly.
Siddiqui argued that the 1973 Constitution provided for the creation of new provinces, adding that the authority to establish them rested with the federal government.
Criticism of PPP leadership
Taking aim at Bilawal, Siddiqui urged him to respond to the party's arguments through dialogue rather than threats.
"We present arguments and expect arguments in response. Is it threats that we are getting?" he said, asking the federal government and authorities to take notice of Bilawal's remarks.
"We will fight a constitutional and legal battle," he said, adding that the party is willing to sit down with its opponents and discuss the issue.
Siddiqui criticised what he described as feudal democracy in Pakistan, arguing that political power is concentrated among a limited number of families.
"Will a few hundred families represent the country?" he asked.
He also criticised the quota system, questioning why it had been introduced in Sindh and why similar arrangements were not being applied in other parts of the country.
Referring to poverty and development, Siddiqui said statistics showed that poverty had declined in three of Pakistan's four provinces, while Sindh had not witnessed a similar reduction.
