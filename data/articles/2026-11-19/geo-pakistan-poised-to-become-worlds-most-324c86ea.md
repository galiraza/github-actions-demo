# Pakistan poised to become world's most populous Muslim-majority country by 2070

Published November 19, 2026
Muslims are projected to overtake Christians as the world's largest religious group by 2035, with their global population expected to reach 2.57 billion against 2.52 billion Christians, according to a new study by the US-based Pew Research Centre.
The projections point to a significant shift in the world's religious composition over the coming decades, with both Muslim and Christian populations expected to grow, although the number of Muslims is forecast to increase at a considerably faster pace.
The study, titled "The Future of World Religions: Population Growth Projections, 2020-2070", estimates that the global Muslim population will increase by 80% between 2020 and 2070, compared with a 30% rise in the Christian population.
The report attributed the faster growth of the Muslim population primarily to higher fertility rates and a relatively younger population. Muslim women had an average of 3.1 children during the 2020-2025 period, compared with 2.5 among Christian women and 1.6 among women belonging to other religious groups.
According to the projections, the global Muslim population stood at 2.02 billion in 2020, compared with 2.27 billion Christians. By 2070, the number of Muslims is expected to rise to 3.64 billion, accounting for 36% of the world's population, while Christians are projected to reach nearly 2.96 billion, or about 29%.
The study also forecasts a major change in the geographical distribution of the world's Muslim population, with Pakistan expected to replace Indonesia as the country with the largest number of Muslims by 2070.
Pakistan's Muslim population is projected to nearly double from 227 million in 2020 to approximately 461 million in 2070, according to the report.
A similar shift is expected in the global distribution of Christians, with the Democratic Republic of the Congo projected to overtake the United States as the country with the largest Christian population by 2070.
The Christian population in the African country is expected to triple from 92 million in 2020 to 299 million in 2070, while the number of Christians in the United States is projected to fall from 217 million to 174 million over the same period.
The report further projects that more than half of the world's Christians, around 54%, will live in sub-Saharan Africa by 2070, reflecting the region's relatively young population and higher fertility rates.
Pew Research Centre said its projections examined changes in the size and geographical distribution of seven religious groups across 201 countries and territories between 2020 and 2070.
The estimates take into account differences in age profiles, fertility and mortality rates, migration patterns and religious switching.
The centre cautioned that the projections are based on the continuation of recent demographic trends and do not account for all possible developments, including wars, natural disasters, epidemics or changes in economic and social circumstances.
