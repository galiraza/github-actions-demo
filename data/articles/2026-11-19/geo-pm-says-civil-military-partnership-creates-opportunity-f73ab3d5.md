# PM says civil-military partnership creates opportunity for economic growth

Published November 19, 2026
Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif on Thursday said strong coordination between the political and military leadership had created an opportunity to advance Pakistan’s economic interests through collective efforts, stressing the importance of institutional cooperation for sustainable economic development.
Addressing a gathering of the Pakistan-American Business Council (PABC), the prime minister said export-led growth was the only sustainable way forward, stressing that the government was determined to consolidate economic stability gains and create an enabling environment for domestic and foreign investors.
Premier reaffirmed the government’s commitment to placing Pakistan firmly on the path of sustained export-led economic growth, calling for stronger public-private partnerships, investment facilitation and structural reforms to transform the country’s economy.
“It has to be export-led, nothing else, export-led growth,” he said, highlighting opportunities to expand bilateral trade and investment between Pakistan and the United States.
The premier said the government was exploring greater imports of American agricultural products, including soybeans, while seeking wider market access for Pakistani surgical instruments, sports goods and other manufactured products in the US market.
He assured American investors that the government was ready to address their genuine grievances and remove bureaucratic hurdles, red tape and procedural delays that discouraged investment.
“I am here to listen to you very attentively and, without delay, put in place a mechanism to address your genuine grievances,” he said, emphasising that transparency, efficiency and timely decision-making were essential to attracting investment.
The prime minister appreciated American companies for maintaining confidence in Pakistan’s economy and described them as important ambassadors of the country, acknowledging their contribution to investment, employment generation, exports and national revenue.
Highlighting the improvement in key economic indicators, he said Pakistan’s total foreign exchange reserves had reached around $27 billion, including holdings of commercial and central banks, compared with the difficult economic situation in 2023 when the country was facing a serious threat of default.
He said inflation, which had stood at around 32%, had declined to 11.5%, while the policy rate had been reduced from 23.5% to 12%.
The prime minister said these improvements had been achieved through hard work, fiscal discipline and collective efforts despite regional and global economic challenges, including the ongoing Gulf crisis.
He acknowledged the sacrifices made by people of Pakistan during the difficult economic transition, particularly vulnerable segments of society, and said the government had introduced targeted subsidies to provide relief to deserving people.
PM Shehbaz said the government had also provided tax relief worth Rs400 billion in the current budget to stimulate economic activity, adding that the International Monetary Fund (IMF) had expressed confidence in the country’s economic management.
He said he personally reviewed the implementation of key economic decisions with his team every week to ensure that policy commitments translated into practical results.
Emphasising the need to empower the private sector, the prime minister said governments did not conduct business but were responsible for facilitating entrepreneurs, encouraging investment and creating opportunities for businesses to expand.
He said the government had introduced reforms in the Export Development Fund and appointed private-sector entrepreneur Omar Saeed as its head to improve its performance. The Export-Import Bank of Pakistan had also been provided Rs4 billion to extend subsidised export financing to potential exporters.
He said the government was working to promote modern technologies, particularly electric vehicles, and had decided to encourage EVs while discouraging internal combustion engine (ICE) vehicles after extensive consultations with relevant ministries.
The prime minister said Pakistan’s automobile industry had received protection and incentives for decades but had failed to achieve the promised 80% localisation, remaining around 50%.
He stressed that promoting electric vehicles would help reduce dependence on imported fuel, conserve foreign exchange, improve industrial efficiency and support environmental protection.
Highlighting investment opportunities, he said Pakistan had enormous potential in agriculture, livestock, information technology, artificial intelligence, industry and minerals. He said practical work on mineral development had gained momentum during the past two to three years after decades of discussion.
He also described the country’s young population as a major national asset, stressing the need to equip millions of young people with vocational and technical skills through training programmes, laptops, iPads and stipends.
The prime minister termed agriculture, particularly livestock, a sector with tremendous potential to transform the economy, saying the government had separated the livestock department from the Ministry of Agriculture and established it as an independent unit. Private-sector experts and international consultants from Turkiye and Brazil were also being engaged to improve the sector.
He further highlighted government measures to eliminate institutional inefficiencies and corruption, including the dissolution of the Pakistan Public Works Department (PWD) and the replacement of the previous subsidised food distribution system with direct digital cash transfers to deserving families.
He said the government had disbursed Rs27 billion through digital transactions during the current year, enabling beneficiaries to receive assistance without standing in long queues.
Earlier, President of the Pakistan-American Business Council Adnan Asad highlighted the contribution of American companies operating in Pakistan, describing their performance as a reflection of continued investor confidence in the country’s market.
He said around 80 American companies were represented at the gathering, including 60 council members and 20 companies in the process of obtaining membership, adding that all the participating companies had posted profits and were satisfied with their operations in Pakistan.
Providing details of the council’s contribution, he said the cumulative revenue of the companies represented at the event stood at approximately Rs956 billion, while their capital investment during the past five years exceeded Rs74 billion.
He said these companies directly employed around 96,000 people and indirectly supported nearly two million jobs across the country.
Adnan Asad said the companies had reinvested Rs32 billion in Pakistan during the past two years, while their exports amounted to approximately $123 million. Their contribution to the national exchequer stood at around Rs185 billion, he added.
Highlighting their corporate social responsibility initiatives, he said the companies had collectively planted more than 500 million trees, supported 12 million patients through healthcare programmes and provided food assistance to 15 million people during flood-related relief activities.
He added that around 300,000 students were benefiting from scholarship programmes supported by the participating companies, while their combined spending on corporate social responsibility initiatives was close to Rs3 billion.
The prime minister welcomed the council’s contribution and reiterated that the government would continue working closely with the private sector to facilitate investment, expand exports, strengthen industrial competitiveness and put Pakistan on a sustainable path of economic development.
