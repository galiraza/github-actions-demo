Published November 19, 2026
ISLAMABAD: President Asif Ali Zardari and Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif on Thursday agreed to resolve all issues through dialogue and mutual consultation amid rising political temperatures over the new provinces debate.
The development came during a meeting between the president and the premier at the Aiwan-e-Sadr (President's House) in Islamabad.
In a post on X, the presidency said that the meeting reviewed the overall national situation, the current political landscape and developments in the Middle East.
“We will sit down together on all issues and resolve them through consultation,” read the statement.
The previous constitutional amendments were made through mutual consultation among all allied parties, it added.
Minister of State for Interior Talal Chaudhry has said that Bilawal is young and emotional, adding that his side did respond, but only to an appropriate extent, making no remarks like those made by the PPP leader.
Speaking on Geo News programme Aaj Shahzeb Khanzada Kay Saath, the minister said: “We have responded, because we also have to answer to our people.”
He said President Zardari and PM Shehbaz always want to move things forward through dialogue.
Questioning Bilawal’s call for talks and tolerance, Talal asked why he does not tolerate the stance of the interior minister and other ministers.
“Everything, from appointment to a grade-four post to becoming prime minister, has been decided through consultation here,” he said.
He said any legislation is first placed before allies in the committee and then brought to parliament.
“What concerns the public will be settled only through democratic means,” Talal said. He added that small parties cannot be kept out of the democratic process, even though the big parties are the main players.
“Such big decisions should not be taken without consensus, and they will not be,” he said.
Talal said administrative units are being discussed to improve governance, not because of anyone’s wish.
“This is about Pakistan, not about any one province,” he said.
He said nothing has been done outside the Constitution from the PDM government to date.
“All matters have been decided after extensive consultation with allies, especially the PPP,” the minister added.
The ongoing debate over the creation of new provinces took a fresh turn on Wednesday night as PPP Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari had taken a swipe at the Centre, saying that he would face opposition from the politicians as they lacked courage to hear the truth.
"It appears they [federal ministers] have decided to pick a fight with me for speaking the truth. The reaction [to his remarks] shows they are unwilling to tolerate criticism," Bilawal had said while responding to a series of statements by federal ministers earlier in that day.
The PPP chief had questioned how ordinary citizens could expect their voices to reach the government if politicians were unwilling to listen to him.
The federal ministers had strongly criticised Bilawal's opposition to the proposed division of Sindh, arguing that the creation of smaller provinces was necessary for better governance and that public opinion should prevail in the matter.
The PPP chairman, on Tuesday, had warned that if Sindh is divided by force, questions would be asked about the blood spilled on the streets in Karachi and Hyderabad.
Defence Minister Khawaja Asif had stressed that no political system could be changed without public support, while Federal Minister for Health Mustafa Kamal had described smaller provinces as inevitable, saying that the country could not function effectively without them.
Other ministers, including Talal Chaudhry, had also questioned Bilawal's stance, pointing to the PPP's position on the division of Punjab and arguing that democracy required effective governance, not merely political speeches and constitutional arguments.
Speaking about the debate, Bilawal had said he was offering suggestions as a major stakeholder to improve the situation and urged political leaders to exercise caution. He had said the discussion on new provinces had begun without consulting any minister, the prime minister or other political stakeholders.
“If you have initiated this debate, have the courage to hold it openly. It cannot be conducted through your own selected people, with everyone else expected to agree or remain silent,” he had said.
The PPP chairman had said the discussion should also focus on the country's fundamental problems rather than being confined to the creation of new provinces. He had questioned who had the authority to declare that the system had collapsed and decide that it needed to be reset.
“Does the PCB chairman have the authority to say that the system has collapsed? Has the responsibility for deciding on a reset been handed to the PCB?” he had asked.
“I was sounding the alarm, not issuing a threat. I was warning about the consequences if such a move was forced through,” he had said, adding that coercion could trigger unrest that would be difficult to contain.
Turning to constitutional reforms, Bilawal described the establishment of a constitutional court under the 27th Amendment as a historic achievement, saying he would not backtrack on the amendment. He clarified that he was not calling for the amendment or constitutional courts to be abolished.
“If we want to make a start, this is where we can begin,” he said, adding that the PPP had supported the government even when those seats were not in its hands. He said the party would not suffer a major setback if the seats were not returned.
Despite his reservations about the government's treatment of its coalition partners, Bilawal had said he continued to support the government and wanted PM Shehbaz to succeed. He said the public's main expectation from political leaders was a solution to economic problems.
He also questioned whether the PPP's political future depended on holding the offices of governor or Senate chairman.
Referring to PTI founder Imran Khan, Bilawal asked whether he could not be considered a political prisoner when he was both a politician and an inmate.
