Published November 19, 2026
LONDON: British-Pakistani politician and former Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaaf (PTI) government minister Sayed Zulfikar Abbas Bukhari, popularly known as Zulfi Bukhari, has been named among a new generation of influential global political figures who are changing the face of power and redefining political leadership across the world.
The London-based The Luxurist magazine has included Bukhari in its prestigious international list titled “The new generation of global politicians changing the face of power”.
Bukhari has been featured alongside New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani, newly elected US Senator Abdul El-Sayed, American Congresswoman Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, Ecuadorian President Daniel Noboa and Nepalese Prime Minister Balendra Shah by the magazine.
The magazine's selection highlights emerging and younger political figures who are challenging traditional political structures, embracing digital influence and connecting with new generations of voters.
In its profile of Bukhari, the publication praised his international political connections, business background, modern communication style and ability to engage with audiences across different cultures. It described Bukhari as occupying a distinctive position in Pakistani public life by combining politics, international business experience and a globally connected outlook.
It noted that his upbringing between London and Pakistan, along with his international exposure, had given him particular visibility among younger Pakistanis and the overseas Pakistani diaspora. The publication described Bukhari as a strategic thinker with a strong understanding of international affairs and an extensive network across business, media and public affairs.
“With his cosmopolitan background, contemporary communication style, ability to operate comfortably across cultures and an unmistakably charismatic, easy-on-the-eyes presence, Bukhari represents a new generation of globally connected Pakistani political figures,” the magazine wrote.
Bukhari, a close associate of former prime minister Imran Khan, served as Special Assistant to the Prime Minister on Overseas Pakistanis and Human Resource Development during the PTI government. He has remained associated with Khan and PTI and has maintained a prominent international political profile, particularly among overseas Pakistanis in Britain and other countries.
He left Pakistan shortly before Khan’s arrest. He now spends his time between London, Dubai and European countries, campaigning for the former PM. He faces several cases in Pakistan made after the ouster of Imran Khan’s government.
The magazine's recognition places Bukhari alongside several politicians who have attracted international attention through their political campaigns, public engagement and unconventional approaches to leadership. Among them is Zohran Mamdani, who became Mayor of New York City in January 2026 after a campaign focused on affordable housing, public transport and the rising cost of living. His political rise has attracted particular attention because of his grassroots campaigning and strong engagement with younger voters. Another prominent figure is Abdul El-Sayed, the Egyptian-American physician and public-health campaigner whom the magazine identifies as Michigan's newly elected US Senator. His political profile has centred on healthcare, economic opportunity and social justice.
The list also includes Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, one of America's best-known progressive politicians, who has built an international following through her advocacy on climate change, economic inequality, healthcare and workers' rights.
From South Asia, the magazine selected Tejasvi Surya, a prominent Indian parliamentarian representing Bengaluru South, and Balendra Shah, Nepal's former Kathmandu mayor and current Prime Minister, whose background as a rapper and engineer helped establish his unconventional political identity. Other politicians featured include Huang Jie, Taiwan's first openly LGBTQ+ national legislator; Daniel Noboa, President of Ecuador; Eduardo Leite, Governor of Brazil's Rio Grande do Sul state; and Eve Borg Bonello, one of Malta's youngest parliamentary figures.
The magazine said that although the politicians came from different political traditions and backgrounds, they shared an ability to communicate beyond conventional political channels and engage audiences on issues affecting everyday life.
It identified economic inequality, affordability, democratic participation, representation and accountability as some of the central issues shaping the next generation of global political leadership.
Bukhari's inclusion makes him the Pakistani representative in the magazine's selection of ten international political figures, placing him alongside politicians from the United States, India, Taiwan, Ecuador, Brazil, Malta and Nepal.
