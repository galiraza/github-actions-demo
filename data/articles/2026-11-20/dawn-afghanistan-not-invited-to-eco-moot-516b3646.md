# Afghanistan not invited to ECO moot as Islamabad does not recognise Kabul regime: FO

- Published: Fri, 20 Nov 2026 20:04:50 +0500

- Collected: 2026-11-20 15:53:36 UTC

---

The Foreign Office (FO) said on Friday that Afghanistan was not invited to participate in the Economic Cooperation Organisation (ECO) Council of Foreign Ministers meeting in Islamabad as Pakistan does not recognise the incumbent Afghan regime.
Addressing a weekly press briefing, FO spokesperson Sajjad Haider Khan said that Afghanistan’s membership had been suspended by the ECO Secretariat.
He added that ministerial-level participation in ECO meetings was the prerogative of the host country under the organisation’s procedures.
“Afghanistan was not invited to join the ECO meeting in Islamabad as Pakistan has not recognised the incumbent regime,” he said.
“Pakistan attaches great importance to strong regional cooperation, connectivity and economic integration,” the spokesperson said.
“This commitment also informs our approach towards Afghanistan, which we want to see as a peaceful, prosperous and stable country, fully integrated within the ECO framework and at peace with itself as well as with all its neighbours,” he added.
“At the same time, we believe that meaningful progress requires the Afghan authorities to address the legitimate security concerns of neighbouring countries, particularly the threat of terrorism emanating from Afghan soil, in the interest of sustainable regional peace, stability and development,” the spokesperson said.
Responding to questions about the ECO’s broader agenda, the spokesperson said the adoption of the Strategic Goals of Economic Cooperation 2035 represented an important step towards strengthening the organisation’s institutional and economic frameworks.
“The document builds on the achievements and guiding principles of the ECO Vision 2025 and provides a longer-term framework for regional integration, resilience, connectivity and sustainable prosperity,” he said.
He said the ECO had identified practical priorities, including trade facilitation, transport and transit connectivity, energy cooperation, digital transformation, industrial development, food security, minerals and emerging technologies.
“Pakistan has assumed the ECO chairmanship for the next two years under the theme ‘Reviving Regionalism for a Resilient ECO’. Our focus will remain on translating agreed objectives into implementable initiatives through regular ministerial meetings, effective coordination and stronger public- and private-sector participation,” the spokesperson said.
‘Makkah Alliance meeting to review progress, strengthen coordination’
Regarding the upcoming meeting of the Makkah defence pact between Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and Turkiye to be hosted by Islamabad later this month, Khan said the details would be shared after the meeting.
“This meeting follows the agreement reached among the three participating countries,” he said.
“It will provide an opportunity to review progress under the agreement, strengthen institutional coordination, as the alliance architecture is still evolving, and discuss matters of mutual interest.”
He also reiterated that the agreement was defensive in nature and not directed against another country, as well as expressing Pakistan’s continued full commitment to the agreement.
“Its aim is to strengthen collective defence and deterrence and ensure regional stability,” he said.
Islamabad MoU ‘impeded’
The spokesperson also addressed the implementation of the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) signed between the US and Iran in June, saying that it “unfortunately remains impeded”.
He stressed that Pakistan had “played its due role”, bringing the two sides together for negotiations and signing the MoU electronically, and drew attention to the ECO’s joint communique which appreciated the country’s mediatory role.
“We hope that the two sides find ways to resolve their issues based on the MoU’s ingredients,” Khan said.
“We feel that the foundation for a peaceful region has already been established, and the signing of the MoU […] provides the roadmap for resolution of the dispute between the two sides, and de-escalation at this particular time is needed,” he said.
