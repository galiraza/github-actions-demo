# Babar Azam stresses patience, continuity in backing young players

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Fri, 20 Nov 2026 17:09:49 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2045914/babar-azam-stresses-patience-continuity-in-backing-young-players
- Collected: 2026-11-20 15:53:36 UTC

---

Captain Babar Azam believes the country’s emerging Test cricketers need sustained opportunities and continuity to establish themselves at the highest level, cautioning that winning a single series does not necessarily mean the team has achieved its rebuilding objectives.
Speaking after Pakistan defeated Sri Lanka by eight wickets in the second Test on Friday to clinch the two-match series 1-0, Babar said the performances of the younger players had been encouraging and that the team management was keen to build on the opportunities provided to them following the series against England.
“After England, we had planned to play a new team and give opportunities to players, and the boys grabbed those opportunities. We have seen different performances from different players, both in batting and bowling, which is a very encouraging sign,” Babar said during the post-match press conference.
“We will try to continue this. The main thing is that they get proper opportunities.”
The Pakistan captain, however, was reluctant to declare the team’s rebuilding exercise a success on the back of the series victory, stressing that developing a competitive Test side was a gradual process requiring exposure to different conditions and opposition.
He said the younger players needed to be given sufficient time to understand the demands of the format, particularly when confronted with difficult situations against quality opposition.
“One series or one match does not mean you have achieved something. It takes time to become stable as a team. You have to play in different conditions and against different opponents and see how players react to different situations,” he said.
Babar added that the senior players had a responsibility to ensure newcomers felt confident and supported, particularly when results or individual performances did not go their way.
He said the dressing room was working towards creating an environment in which both senior and junior players could learn from their experiences without losing confidence.
“Giving confidence to players, as individuals and as a group, is very important. We are trying to give the new boys confidence and provide them with a good environment,” he said.
According to Babar, consistency could not be achieved overnight, with players needing to learn equally from successful performances and disappointing outings.
“You cannot win every day, just as you cannot lose every day. You learn from the things you do well, and you learn twice as much from the things you do badly. These things continue to happen in cricket,” he said.
The captain also pointed towards the importance of ensuring that promising youngsters did not become complacent after making an impression at international level.
He revealed that several members of the squad were expected to feature in four-day domestic cricket within a few days of the conclusion of the Test series, where they would be required to apply the lessons learnt in Rawalpindi.
“When a new player comes in, he needs proper opportunities. If he performs, it is important to keep backing him. But you also have to keep him focused and concentrated so that he does not start taking things for granted,” Babar said.
“They have learnt things here, and now they will go and apply them in first-class cricket and try to improve further.”
Asked about the decision to continue with the same group of players for Pakistan’s next home Test assignment against New Zealand, Babar said the matter would be discussed closer to the series, with conditions and the venue likely to influence selection.
He said Pakistan would assess the playing conditions before deciding on its combination rather than committing to a fixed squad at this stage.
Reflecting on the victory over Sri Lanka, Babar identified the visitors’ first-innings collapse as the defining moment of the match, with Pakistan dismissing them for just 98 in approximately 36 to 37 overs.
He felt the hosts could have capitalised more effectively on that advantage when they came out to bat, pointing out that although Pakistan posted 325, some of the partnerships did not develop into substantial stands.
“The first innings was the turning point for us. After that, when we started batting, our effort was to build long partnerships and score runs, but some of those partnerships came in short periods. We could have taken them further,” he said.
Babar admitted that Sri Lanka’s improved second-innings batting had briefly made the contest interesting, particularly when Kusal Mendis and the middle order put together partnerships after Pakistan had made early inroads.
The situation became more challenging for the hosts after Saqib Khan suffered a hamstring injury early in his second-innings spell, leaving Pakistan a fast bowler short.
Babar was particularly appreciative of the remaining pace attack, saying the additional workload had been absorbed through patience and sustained spells.
“When you lose a bowler, the workload on the others increases. The way our fast bowlers showed their effort and patience deserves credit. Aamer Jamal, Razaullah and Mohammad Abbas bowled long spells and kept the game under control,” he said.
He also praised the energy brought by the younger members of the side, particularly their contributions in the field, saying their enthusiasm had been evident throughout the series.
“When young players come into the team, there is excitement and energy. You see a different spark in them, whether it is batting, bowling or fielding. We were also excited when we first came into the team and wanted to grab our opportunities,” he said.
Babar singled out Saud Shakeel for his contributions, describing the left-hander as an important component of Pakistan’s Test batting order and someone the team could rely on to build substantial innings.
He acknowledged that opportunities had also come the way of other senior batters who had not always managed to convert them into big scores, but expressed hope that they would make better use of such chances in the future.
