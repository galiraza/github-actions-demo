# Bilawal wants PM Shehbaz to become first premier to complete 5-year term

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Fri, 20 Nov 2026 17:58:20 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2045911/bilawal-wants-pm-shehbaz-to-become-first-premier-to-complete-5-year-term
- Collected: 2026-11-20 15:53:36 UTC

---

PPP Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari said on Friday that he wanted Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif to become the first premier to complete a five-year term in office.
Since the country came into being in 1947, no elected prime minister has ever completed their five-year term.
Addressing an event in Karachi, Bilawal, citing the economic crisis resulting from the US-Israeli war on Iran, called for “empowering” PM Shehbaz so that he could present a way forward to tackle the crisis.
“My objective is that I want to strengthen the hand of the prime minister [..] usually politicians create difficulties or plot his fall and secure the seat for themselves. I will not do that […] I am saying that I want Shehbaz Sharif to be the first prime minister […] to complete a five-year term,” Bilawal said.
Bilawal noted that the prime minister had not been “given the chance” to focus on the economic issues so far.
“What can he do? Whenever he [PM Shehbaz] makes a plan, some federal minister objects and presents their own agenda to divert the topic at hand.”
The PPP chairman insisted he wanted the premier to complete his term as he foresaw difficulties ahead for the country.
“I am saying this because I know that there is no consideration for the people of Pakistan. Whether one province is created or a 1,000, the only worry the public has is having access to meals twice a day,” Bilawal said, recalling his party’s slogan of ‘roti, kapra, makan’ as primary concerns for the public.
Bilawal reiterated that the premier’s “hand should be strengthened” so he can draw attention to the economic issues facing the country. In that regard, he also underscored the need for a “national effort”.
“While I am praying for the success of the prime minister, I also believe that if we are to face the real issues of the country, one or two political parties alone cannot achieve this. It has to be a national effort,” Bilawal said.
He added that it did not matter whether the government “liked” Jamiat Ulema-i-Islam-Fazl (JUI-F) chief Maulana Fazlur Rehman or PTI founder Imran Khan, who Bilawal said had President Asif Ali Zardari and his sister Faryal Talpur “imprisoned.”
Bilawal asserted that he would not do politics “based on ego and personal vendettas” as he believed that it was not “more important than national interest”.
He called on the government to engage the opposition, particularly the PTI, in dealing with the country’s issues.
“My political differences with Khan sahib and his own mistakes aside, his party still has a majority representation in the opposition. And if we have to face these issues collectively then we will have to engage them, entice them and request them to play their role in [parliamentary] committees,” the PPP chairman said.
Bilawal added that the PPP would continue to work as per its “ideology and others may follow suit should they wish”.
“We cannot force someone, but it is my right to present my stance, and it is the prime minister’s right to decide whether he wants to take into account my suggestion,” he said.
Bilawal claimed that the PPP’s sole focus was to draw attention to the public’s grievances and expressed the hope to succeed in doing so.
The development comes amid renewed tensions between coalition partners, the PPP and the PML-N, triggered by a hard-hitting speech delivered earlier this week by Bilawal.
In the no-holds-barred address, Bilawal had warned against new provinces, constitutional tweaks, criticised the treatment of the opposition and past decisions taken by the ruling coalition.
Bilawal had also warned against “political revenge” in politics. “What a weird country this is; since 1947, whoever becomes the prime minister goes to jail, whoever gets out of jail goes to sit in the Prime Minister’s House,” he remarked.
The PPP chairman had referred to Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi’s recommendation of holding a referendum to decide on the creation of new administrative units and said: “In our history, a referendum has always been carried out for an undemocratic act and never a democratic one.”
He also remarked that the governance system had not collapsed, but was rather “made to collapse” — seemingly reacting to Naqvi’s statement in July that the current “system” of governance had “collapsed” and needed a “reset”.
In the aftermath, Defence Minister Khawaja Asif and other leaders from the ruling PML-N criticised the Bhutto scion for his remarks.
Despite this, Bilawal went on a media blitz on Wednesday, articulating the PPP’s grouse with the federal government and questioned many of its past decisions. This led to PM Shehbaz and Naqvi going to meet his father, President Asif Ali Zardari.
According to the brief official readout issued by the Presidency, the three men reviewed the current political situation, and resolved that all pending issues would be resolved through dialogue and discussion.
While leaders from both parties remained tight-lipped over what transpired during the meeting, observers termed it an attempt at ‘damage control’ or ‘mending fences’ after Bilawal adopted a decidedly aggressive tone against the ruling PML-N.
