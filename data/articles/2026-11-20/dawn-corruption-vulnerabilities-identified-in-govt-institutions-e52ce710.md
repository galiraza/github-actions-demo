# Corruption vulnerabilities identified in govt institutions in bid to undertake ‘self-accountability’, says law minister

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Fri, 20 Nov 2026 20:46:04 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2045925/corruption-vulnerabilities-identified-in-govt-institutions-in-bid-to-undertake-self-accountability-says-law-minister
- Collected: 2026-11-20 15:53:36 UTC

---

The federal government has identified weaknesses that could create opportunities for corruption in ten institutions through a comprehensive ‘National Risk Assessment of Corruption Vulnerabilities’ survey, Law Minister Azam Nazeer Tarar said on Friday.
In a televised video message, Tarar maintained that the report was not a “charge sheet”. Rather, it identified weaknesses that needed to be addressed through reforms to prevent corruption and promote “merit and transparency”.
Speaking about the assessment, Tarar said the Ministry of Law and Justice had coordinated with various agencies and institutions to prepare the report, which examined vulnerabilities across federal institutions using different indicators.
“Let me also say that no other government has made efforts like this until today,” the minister claimed, adding that the survey would also be shared with international agencies.
The minister detailed that ten institutions had been examined in depth because of the nature of their operations, particularly those involving finance-related matters, which, he said, presented opportunities for corruption.
Tarar also highlighted “positive” steps taken by Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif during his governance. He cited power generation projects and reforms in the electricity and energy sectors, saying the government, in collaboration with relevant departments, had undertaken a detailed and lengthy exercise to address various issues in the sector.
“As a result of these reforms, the government is expected to save Rs3,500 billion in the electricity and energy sectors,” he said.
He also recalled the privatisation of Pakistan International Airlines (PIA) last year, which Tarar said was conducted through an open auction and competitive bidding process after previous attempts failed to materialise. “You will see positive changes in that in the coming days,” he added.
The minister also referred to the government’s plans to right-size departments deemed unnecessary. As an example, Tarar highlighted the closure of Pakistan Public Works Department (PWD), which he said faced allegations of corruption and “wastage” of money.
“It was closed and a package finalised for its employees. Some legal amendments are pending in the Assembly,” he said, adding that the government saved billions of rupees through its closure. “Now this work will be executed through smaller institutions or provincial departments.
Shifting his attention back to the report, the law minister stated that the report had highlighted “checks and balances, misuse of authority” to stop leakages of a financial nature.
“This has been done for the guidance of the federal institutions. Consider it a precautionary step,” he added.
“25 institutions were selected, over 300 indicators were used, and the role of regulatory authorities was taken into consideration as well, and the need for planning reforms was felt,” he said, reiterating that the report was not a “charge sheet”.
