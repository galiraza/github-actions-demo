# Minister insists referendum on new provinces 'will happen'

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Fri, 20 Nov 2026 23:15:15 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2045936/minister-insists-referendum-on-new-provinces-will-happen
- Collected: 2026-11-20 20:59:10 UTC

---

Parliamentary Affairs Minister Tariq Fazal Chaudhry on Friday insisted that a referendum on the formation of new provinces “will happen”.
The remarks come after PPP Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari delivered a no-holds-barred speech on Tuesday that covered the question of new provinces.
Bilawal had expressed his opposition to the creation of provinces and the idea of holding a referendum for such a move in his speech. He cautioned that any “forced” attempt to divide provinces, particularly in Sindh, could lead to bloodshed.
However, the federal government maintains that a referendum is a legal and constitutional method of gauging public opinion on new provinces and that no political party should have reservations regarding it.
Speaking to the Geo News programme ‘Naya Pakistan’ on Friday, Chaudhry, when asked about the referendum, insisted that developing consensus on the matter should be a priority for the ruling coalition.
“National consensus is very important on the issue of new provinces,” the minister said, highlighting that the PTI-led opposition was also in favour of it.
Chaudhry said that the process of taking the public’s opinion cannot be stopped if a single party opposes it.
“The referendum will happen,” the minister insisted, adding that the PML-N would undertake “every last effort” to convince the PPP.
“A referendum is neither non-constitutional nor undemocratic; the public’s opinion can be sought on any important matter,” he remarked.
Asked about the timeline for the proposed move, Chaudhry said it was premature to talk about it. However, he added that “things will be clarified soon”.
Asked if PML-N supremo Nawaz Sharif was on board with the referendum, Chaudhry answered in the affirmative.
“One hundred per cent. The prime minister does not make any major decisions without his opinion,” he added.
On being asked if the PPP may join the opposition with the PTI and the Jamiat-Ulema-i-Islam-Fazl (JUI-F), Chaudhry maintained that the party included “mature and seasoned politicians”, expressing hope that it would continue in its role as a coalition partner.
“I believe matters will not reach a point where they might have to exit the coalition, as we will make sure to onboard them on the decision and vice versa,” he added.
In response to a question over Bilawal’s recent remarks and whether they were “justified”, he said that the PPP chairman had the right to present the party’s perspective on the formation of new provinces. However, he warned Bilawal against invoking other matters and targeting the PML-N.
“Attacking us [PML-N] or invoking other matters that have nothing to do with the debate related to the provinces was uncalled for.”
Chaudhry was also asked whether the government’s stance on the creation of provinces and the proposed referendum had been delivered to President Asif Ali Zardari in his meeting with Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif and Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi a day earlier.
To this, he said that various matters had been discussed during the meeting in Islamabad. However, the main topic was the recent verbal confrontation between the coalition partners, he added.
Chaudhry said that during the meeting, both President Zardari and PM Shehbaz decided to resolve all the differences through dialogue. He added that the matters between the allied parties had returned to “usual”.
“Most of the matters have backtracked,” the minister remarked.
The PML-N leader said the party leadership would decide when to bring the party’s perspective on new provinces to the media.
Chaudhry also said that before making the remarks publicly, Bilawal should have taken PM Shehbaz into confidence.
“It is clear that the PPP will not exit the coalition,” he reiterated, referring to Bilawal’s comments about the prime minister completing his five-year term in office.
Over the past few months, Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi has whipped up a political storm since he called for the formation of new administrative units in the country, saying the current “system” of governance had “collapsed” and needed a “reset”.
On November 12, Naqvi also floated the idea of holding a referendum to gauge public opinion on the issue, saying the exercise could settle the contentious debate once and for all.
However, Bilawal, in his hard-hitting speech on Tuesday, termed the referendum an “unconstitutional move”, vowing that his party would stand against any such measure.
Bilawal, as well as Sindh Chief Minister Murad Ali Shah and other senior party leaders, has repeatedly declared that the division of Sindh was neither legally nor politically acceptable to the party.
Soon after Bilawal’s remarks, Information Minister Attaullah Tarar defended the move, saying it was a legal and constitutional method of gauging public opinion.
Meanwhile, the JUI-F voiced its support for Bilawal’s opposition to a referendum, warning that it could be misused. The party termed Bilawal’s statements “realistic” and viewed them in high regard.
Against this backdrop, the Muttahida Qaumi Movement-Pakistan has also called for a nationwide referendum on the creation of new administrative units, proposing that the exercise be held before the start of 2027 to allow people to decide the future administrative structure of the country.
Asif hopes ‘resolution will soon be reached’
Defence Minister Khawaja Asif, speaking to Geo News show ‘Aaj Shahzeb Khanzada Kay Sath’, expressed hope that “some resolution will be reached” between the coalition partners on the issue of creating new provinces; however, he warned that certain politicians, without naming anyone, wanted to maintain “personal fiefdoms” over provinces.
“We have to drop this idea that a certain province in its current form is a personal fiefdom [..] I don’t want to take any names, but this is unacceptable,” he remarked, urging politicians to avoid pursuing personal agendas.
“I don’t want to sabotage any evolving situation, but the personal agendas of politicians have been brought to the surface,” he said, pointing out that the PPP had been pushing for a province in South Punjab for a long time.
Asif maintained that the creation of provinces was “not a political, but rather an administrative division for improved service delivery”.
However, Asif expressed confidence that a resolution will soon be found.
At the same time, referring to Bilawal’s comments warning of bloodshed in case of division of Sindh, Asif warned, “When you warn of bloodshed, you are attacking the state [..] they (PPP) should have clarity on this.”
On being asked if PM Shehbaz was making efforts towards the creation of fresh administrative units and if it meant that he had the “blessing” of Nawaz, Asif said, “Can anyone advance independently without Nawaz Sharif’s blessing [..] he is our leader [..] we want to pursue a solution where all stakeholders are on the same page.”
Asif also called on president Zardari to “intervene” so matters may be resolved.
