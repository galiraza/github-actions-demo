# On North Waziristan visit, CDF Munir vows to eliminate terrorism, preserve peace in KP

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Fri, 20 Nov 2026 12:04:07 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2045881/on-north-waziristan-visit-cdf-munir-vows-to-eliminate-terrorism-preserve-peace-in-kp
- Collected: 2026-11-20 08:01:54 UTC

---

Chief of Defence Forces (CDF) and Chief of the Army Staff Field Marshal Asim Munir reaffirmed on Friday the security force’s “unwavering resolve” to decisively eliminate terrorism and preserve peace and stability of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa in close coordination with law enforcement agencies (LEAs).
The CDF said this during a visit to Miranshah in KP’s North Waziristan, the military media wing said in a statement.
“He reaffirmed the unwavering resolve of the Security Forces, in close coordination with law enforcement agencies, to decisively eliminate the menace of terrorism and preserve the hard-earned peace and stability of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa,” the statement by the Inter-Services Public Relations (ISPR) quoted him as saying.
He also appreciated the “brave police force” in KP and expressed pride in its resilience and sacrifices against terrorism and Fitna-al-Khawarij — a term the state uses for the banned Tehreek-i-Taliban Pakistan.
During the visit, the CDF was given a comprehensive briefing on “the prevailing security situation, ongoing intelligence-based operations and measures being undertaken to consolidate the gains achieved through sustained counter terrorism efforts and ensure lasting peace and stability in the area”, the ISPR statement said.
It added that CDF Munir also interacted with officers and troops and appreciated their “high level of preparedness, professionalism and unwavering commitment to the defence and security of the motherland”.
Upon his arrival in Miranshah, the CDF was received by the Peshawar Corps commander and later laid a floral wreath at Yadgar-i-Shuhada to pay homage to the “supreme sacrifices of the shuhada (martyrs) of the Pakistan Army”, the statement said.
His visit to Miranshah comes a day after he visited Turbat in Balochistan, where, too, he was briefed on the prevailing security environment, operational preparedness and ongoing operations against externally sponsored terrorism.
Security forces continue to carry out counterterrorism action in KP and Balochistan, the two provinces that have been particularly affected by terrorism in Pakistan.
On Saturday, ISPR said four troops, including a major, were martyred while 24 terrorists were killed in IBOs in Balochistan and KP’s North Waziristan. Furthermore, last week, 74 terrorists were killed in IBOs in KP and Balochistan.
Meanwhile, the Pakistan Institute for Conflict and Security Studies (PICSS), an Islamabad-based independent think tank monitoring conflict and security developments, has reported encouraging trends in the two provinces in its monthly assessment for October.
Pakistan overall witnessed a significant decline in terrorism-related violence during October, with attacks falling by 37 per cent, deaths resulting from these attacks declining by 57pc, and injuries decreasing by 71pc compared to September, the report said.
It added that the improvement was particularly pronounced in Balochistan and mainland KP.
“Balochistan recorded a substantial reduction in militant violence, with attacks declining by 46pc, from 132 in September to 71 in October. Deaths resulting from militant attacks decreased from 136 to 44, a reduction of approximately 68pc, while injuries declined from 63 to 37, representing a decrease of 41pc,” the report said.
Regarding mainland KP, it said a “significant decline” was witnessed in terrorism.
PICSS recorded 40 attacks during October, compared with 58 in September, representing a reduction of 31pc. Meanwhile, deaths resulting from these attacks declined from 90 to 38, a decrease of approximately 58pc, while injuries fell from 165 to 19, representing a reduction of 88pc.
The merged districts of KP recorded 30 attacks during October, compared with 34 in September, and deaths resulting from these attacks decreased from 33 to 25, while injuries increased from 11 to 13, the report said.
In October, the federal government also imposed emergency and governor’s rule in KP, citing the “provincial government’s deliberate disengagement from the fight against terrorism”.
