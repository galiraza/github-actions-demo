# Pakistan-sponsored resolution on self-determination adopted by consensus at UN

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Fri, 20 Nov 2026 11:16:06 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2045879/pakistan-sponsored-resolution-on-self-determination-adopted-by-consensus-at-un
- Collected: 2026-11-20 08:01:54 UTC

---

UNITED NATIONS: The UN General Assembly’s Third Committee on Thursday adopted by consensus a Pakistan-sponsored resolution reaffirming the right to self-determination of peoples living under colonialism, foreign domination and alien occupation, with 62 countries co-sponsoring the initiative.
The resolution, titled “Universal Realisation of the Right of Peoples to Self-Determination”, was introduced by Pakistan’s Permanent Representative to the United Nations, Ambassador Syed Haider Shah. Pakistan has been sponsoring the resolution since 1981.
“The promise of self-determination remains unfulfilled for many peoples still living under foreign occupation,” Ambassador Shah said, drawing attention to the continuing denial of this right to the people of Palestine and Indian-occupied Jammu and Kashmir.
He said that in several situations, the legitimate aspirations of occupied peoples continued to be met with military oppression, excessive use of force, extrajudicial killings, arbitrary detentions, enforced disappearances, restrictions on fundamental freedoms and attempts at demographic engineering.
Such practices, he said, constituted grave violations of international law, undermined the credibility of the international legal order and challenged the principles of justice and human dignity.
Ambassador Shah noted that the exercise of self-determination had transformed the course of history, enabling millions to free themselves from foreign subjugation and establish sovereign states. The vast majority of UN member states, he observed, had themselves achieved independence through the exercise of this inalienable right.
He said the right to self-determination was a cornerstone of the UN Charter and firmly established in Article 1(2) of the Charter, Common Article 1 of the International Covenants on Civil and Political Rights and Economic, Social and Cultural Rights, as well as the Declaration on Principles of International Law and General Assembly Resolution 1514.
The ambassador said the resolution reaffirmed the universality and indivisibility of the right to self-determination, expressed solidarity with peoples deprived of their fundamental rights and freedoms and underscored the international community’s collective responsibility to uphold this principle.
He said the resolution’s consistent cross-regional support reflected its enduring relevance to situations involving foreign occupation and alien domination.
Ambassador Shah thanked the 62 countries that co-sponsored the resolution and urged other member states to join the initiative.
The adoption by consensus, without a vote, reflected broad support among UN member states for the principle of self-determination.
