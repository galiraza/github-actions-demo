LONDON: British-Pakistani Nasir Younas, the London entrepreneur who landed in the capital from Lahore as a teenager with £200 and very little English, has received the Freedom of the City of London at a ceremony in the Chamberlain's Court at Guildhall.
Nasir Younas was honoured for his services to business, the hospitality industry and philanthropy in the UK. Watched by his family, friends and leaders from business and public life, he read the Declaration of a Freeman, signed the Register of Freemen and was greeted as a Citizen of London.
One of the City of London's ancient traditions, the Freedom is believed to have begun in 1237 and originally allowed holders to carry out their trade in the city. Past holders of the Freedom include members of the Royal Family, among them Princess Elizabeth, Queen Elizabeth II, Queen Elizabeth The Queen Mother and Diana, Princess of Wales; the prime ministers Winston Churchill and Margaret Thatcher; world leaders including Jawaharlal Nehru and Nelson Mandela; the American presidents Theodore Roosevelt and Dwight D. Eisenhower; and Professor Stephen Hawking, Jimmy Choo, Morgan Freeman, J. K. Rowling, Sir Michael Caine and George Helon JP, along with leading figures from public life, business and the arts.
Nasir Younas said: "I landed in London in 2004 with £200 my father had borrowed, very little English and nobody to show me the way. To stand in Guildhall today and be greeted as a Citizen of London is the proudest moment of my working life. I have received this honour for my services to business, the hospitality industry and philanthropy, but London deserves much of the credit. This city educated me and built me.
"I came here meaning to earn some money and go home. London made me stay, and moving here turned out to be the first step towards everything I dreamed of."
Younas grew up in Ghoray Shah, near Lahore, the eldest son of a shopkeeper. From the age of 14 he opened his father's grocery shop at six every Sunday morning while his friends played cricket.
He arrived in London with £200 his father had borrowed to send him. His first job was in the basement of an Oxford Street restaurant, boiling rice from 11am to 10pm for £40 a day.
