# Oruc Reis arrival ushers in new era of Pak-Turkiye energy cooperation: DPM Dar

Published November 20, 2026
KARACHI: Deputy Prime Minister Ishaq Dar on Friday described the arrival of Turkish seismic research vessel Oruc Reis as a new chapter in Pakistan-Turkiye scientific and strategic cooperation, saying the two countries sought a long-term partnership in the energy sector.
Addressing a welcome ceremony at Karachi Port, he said Pakistan's coastline offered significant opportunities for offshore energy exploration, shipping and fisheries, but stressed the need for scientific research, skilled manpower and environmental protection.
DPM Dar said the vessel would combine Turkiye's scientific expertise with the capabilities of Pakistani energy companies to advance offshore exploration. The seismic data collected would help identify geological structures beneath the seabed and guide future exploration decisions.
He said seismic surveys could not guarantee commercially viable discoveries but were essential to identifying potential resources.
"Today, Pakistan is taking this step with confidence, responsibility and prayers," he said.
DPM Dar said Pakistan wanted to expand energy cooperation with Turkiye to cover offshore exploration, minerals and renewable energy. He also called for collaboration between Pakistani and Turkish geologists, engineers, universities and research institutions.
Referring to Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif's recent meeting with Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan in Antalya, he said the two leaders had agreed to accelerate cooperation in trade, investment, defence, energy and regional connectivity.
He added that the prime minister had directed relevant institutions to improve coordination and ensure strategically important projects did not face unnecessary delays.
"Our seas are an economic asset, but they are also a national trust. Development and environmental protection must go hand in hand," Dar said.
Earlier, Petroleum Minister Ali Pervaiz Malik said the vessel's arrival had turned bilateral energy agreements into practical cooperation.
Turkish Energy Minister Alparslan Bayraktar said Turkiye was ready to share its technical expertise and engineering capabilities with Pakistan.
He said Oruc Reis had reached Pakistan after a 19-day voyage and would conduct a three-dimensional (3D) seismic survey in Pakistani waters over approximately two months to assess oil and gas prospects off Sindh and Makran.
Turkish Petroleum Corporation (TPAO) would undertake the data acquisition, while the licensing process for five blocks had been completed, he added.
He said TPAO had established partnerships with Pakistani energy companies, including Oil and Gas Development Company Limited (OGDCL), Pakistan Petroleum Limited (PPL) and Mari Energies.
Several agreements on energy cooperation were also signed during the ceremony.
Turkiye's Oruc Reis seismic research vessel will begin surveying offshore fields in Pakistan for hydrocarbon resources, with drilling expected to start in 2027, Bayraktar said.
Last year, TPAO signed hydrocarbon exploration and production agreements with Pakistani companies, including Mari Energies, Fatima, OGDCL, PPL, Prime and GHDL, covering three offshore and two onshore fields.
In a post on X, Bayraktar said the vessel would conduct two- and three-dimensional seismic surveys in the Indus and Makran basins to assess their potential.
He described the mission as a "strong turning point" in bilateral energy cooperation, particularly amid turbulence in global oil and gas markets.
Last month, he said the planned operations off Pakistan had been delayed by around six months due to the war in Iran.
