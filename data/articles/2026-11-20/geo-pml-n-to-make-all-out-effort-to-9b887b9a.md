# PML-N to make 'all-out effort' to convince PPP for referendum on new provinces

---

Published November 20, 2026
Pakistan Muslim League-Nawaz (PML-N) leader Tariq Fazal Chaudhry on Friday said his party would make every effort to convince the Pakistan Peoples Party (PPP) to support holding a referendum on the creation of new provinces.
Speaking to Geo News, the federal minister said a referendum was a democratic option provided under the Constitution and would allow people to directly express whether they wanted new provinces. He maintained that the exercise was neither unconstitutional nor undemocratic.
Chaudhry said national consensus was important on the issue, adding that political parties were entitled to hold different positions. However, he said the process should not be stalled simply because one party opposed the creation of new provinces while others supported it.
He said the PPP should not object to a referendum, as it would establish public opinion before any decision on creating new provinces. He acknowledged that the PPP had reservations about the division of Sindh.
The PML-N leader also said the Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI) had responded positively to the proposal for new provinces, expressing support for their creation.
Chaudhry said the PML-N had not formally presented its position on new provinces to the media, adding that the party leadership would decide when to announce it. He said the time had not yet come to communicate a final position and that not every cabinet discussion could be made public.
Turning to the recent exchange of statements between the PML-N and PPP, he said Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari should have contacted Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif before speaking to the media. While the PPP was entitled to express its views, he said its criticism of the PML-N and remarks unrelated to the provinces debate were unnecessary.
He said leaders from both parties had made unnecessary statements over the past two to three days, stressing that differences should be resolved through dialogue, preferably behind closed doors. Coalition partners regularly discussed different issues, he added, saying there was no restriction on leaders meeting to ensure the smooth functioning of the alliance.
Commenting on the recent meeting between President Asif Ali Zardari and Prime Minister Shehbaz, Chaudhry said the two leaders had discussed the verbal exchanges between the coalition partners and several matters had returned to normal following their interaction. He said the prime minister had approached the president when the situation became tense.
The president and prime minister had agreed to resolve all issues through dialogue and mutual consultation during their meeting at Aiwan-e-Sadr. According to a statement shared by the Presidency on X, the meeting also reviewed the overall national situation, the political landscape and developments in the Middle East.
The Presidency said previous constitutional amendments had also been made through mutual consultation among allied parties, adding that the leaders agreed to sit together to resolve issues through consultation.
Chaudhry reiterated that the PPP would neither be allowed to leave the ruling coalition nor would it leave the alliance, expressing confidence that the two parties would continue working together.
The ongoing debate over the creation of new provinces took a fresh turn on Wednesday night as the PPP chairman had taken a swipe at the Centre, saying that he would face opposition from the politicians as they lacked courage to hear the truth.
"It appears they [federal ministers] have decided to pick a fight with me for speaking the truth. The reaction [to his remarks] shows they are unwilling to tolerate criticism," Bilawal had said while responding to a series of statements by federal ministers earlier that day.
The PPP chief had questioned how ordinary citizens could expect their voices to reach the government if politicians were unwilling to listen to him.
The federal ministers, however, had strongly criticised Bilawal's opposition to the proposed division of Sindh, arguing that the creation of smaller provinces was necessary for better governance and that public opinion should prevail in the matter.
The PPP chairman, on Tuesday, had warned that if Sindh is divided by force, questions would be asked about the blood spilled on the streets in Karachi and Hyderabad.
Defence Minister Khawaja Asif had stressed that no political system could be changed without public support, while Federal Minister for Health Mustafa Kamal had described smaller provinces as inevitable, saying that the country could not function effectively without them.
Other ministers, including Talal Chaudhry, had also questioned Bilawal's stance, pointing to the PPP's position on the division of Punjab and arguing that democracy required effective governance, not merely political speeches and constitutional arguments.
Speaking about the debate, Bilawal had said he was offering suggestions as a major stakeholder to improve the situation and urged political leaders to exercise caution. He had said the discussion on new provinces had begun without consulting any minister, the prime minister or other political stakeholders.
“If you have initiated this debate, have the courage to hold it openly. It cannot be conducted through your own selected people, with everyone else expected to agree or remain silent,” he had said.
The PPP chairman had said the discussion should also focus on the country's fundamental problems rather than being confined to the creation of new provinces. He had questioned who had the authority to declare that the system had collapsed and decide that it needed to be reset.
“Does the PCB chairman have the authority to say that the system has collapsed? Has the responsibility for deciding on a reset been handed to the PCB?” he had asked.
“I was sounding the alarm, not issuing a threat. I was warning about the consequences if such a move was forced through,” he had said, adding that coercion could trigger unrest that would be difficult to contain.
