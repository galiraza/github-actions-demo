# In New Delhi, Musadik hails opportunity for multilateral dialogue

- Published: Sat, 21 Nov 2026 07:11:27 +0500

KARACHI: After arriving in New Delhi to attend the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation’s (SCO) Civilisational Dialogue Forum, Minister for Climate Change Dr Musadik Malik hailed the occasion as a “tremendous opportunity” to have a multilateral dialogue.
This is the first visit to India by a Pakistani minister since Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari attended the SCO meeting in the Indian state of Goa in May 2023, and comes two years after India’s External Affairs Minister Subrahmanyam Jaishankar attended the bloc’s meeting in Islamabad.
Pakistan has assumed the rotating chairmanship of the SCO Council of Heads of State for the 2026-27 term and is scheduled to host the organisation’s summit in Islamabad in August 2027.
The gathering in New Delhi brings together representatives from SCO member states to discuss issues related to civilisational dialogue and cultural understanding, according to the Foreign Office.
“I think cultures and civilisations cut across boundaries, and it is fantastic that we are here and talking about our history, our geography, and our cultures and civilisations. It is a very welcoming sign,” Mr Malik told the ANI news agency.
Replying to a question regarding the improvement in Pakistan-India relationships, he said, “I think all of our relationships will improve. The region is in flux, as you know. The plates underneath geopolitics are shifting very fast.
Mr Malik is expected to formally address the forum today (Saturday).
In a separate media interaction, when asked about action against terrorism, the minister said that the issues we face are not limited solely to those between our two nations; they have become far broader.
“When the time comes to address the issue of terrorism… we must ensure transparency. Both sides must bring forward evidence. Our objective is to establish a transparent mechanism — a proposal we have put forward on several occasions. Such a mechanism would help restore trust and could lead to viable solutions,” he told ANI.
Highlighting how Pakistan suffered from terrorism, he acknowledged that India also experienced many such incidents.
“Our neighbourhood is a complicated one, and the challenges facing this entire neighbourhood need to be addressed… Rather than focusing on a single incident or bilateral discussions — where the tendency is often to point fingers at one another — it would be far better to address the problems of the entire region through a structured mechanism and based on concrete evidence.”
