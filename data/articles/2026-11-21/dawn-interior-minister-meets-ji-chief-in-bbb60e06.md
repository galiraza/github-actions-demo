# Interior minister meets JI chief in Lahore, discusses country's 'overall situation'

- Published: Sat, 21 Nov 2026 18:59:27 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2046126/interior-minister-meets-ji-chief-in-lahore-discusses-countrys-overall-situation
- Collected: 2026-11-21 20:00:18 UTC

---

Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi on Saturday met with Jamaat-i-Islami chief Hafiz Naeemur Rehman in Lahore, where both discussed the “overall situation” in the country as well as challenges facing the public.
In a statement, the interior ministry said Naqvi and Rehman also exchanged views on law and order measures during the “important” meeting. Both sides agreed to continue consultations in the future.
“Everyone must play their effective role in addressing the challenges facing the country,” the ministry quoted Naqvi as saying.
Meanwhile, the JI chief reiterated his party’s demand that providing relief to the public should be the government’s top priority.
According to a press release issued by JI, Rehman said the government should also take measures to improve law and order.
However, he added that people were not only facing law and order challenges but also bearing the brunt of poverty, inflation, and other economic difficulties, which required immediate government action.
Rehman highlighted that the “continuous increase” in the prices of flour, sugar, cooking oil, pulses, and other essential commodities had made life extremely difficult for ordinary citizens.
Weekly inflation was recorded at 10.41 per cent year-on-year in the week ending on Nov 19, driven largely by wheat flour prices, according to official data released by the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics.
The JI chief also called attention towards exorbitant electricity and gas bills, which have severely burdened middle- and lower-income people.
Rehman contended that salaried employees and the working class had become unable to meet household expenses on their limited incomes, adding that children’s education and healthcare were also increasingly becoming unaffordable for ordinary citizens.
The JI chief noted that increases in petrol and diesel prices, and consequently transport fares, were directly burdening the public.
While he acknowledged that the public has received some relief through a reduction in petrol prices, Rehman stated that JI’s struggle was aimed at the complete abolition of the petroleum levy and urged the government to take concrete measures in this regard.
The JI chief demanded that the government effectively control the prices of essential commodities, provide genuine relief in electricity and gas bills, and take strict action against hoarders and profiteers.
He maintained that the people needed practical relief rather than mere announcements, and that the government should shift the burden onto the privileged class instead of placing it on the public.
The JI has been demanding for over two months that the Centre abolish petroleum levies, as fuel prices have skyrocketed due to conflicts in the Middle East since March.
When Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif and Rehman met in October, they agreed to form a joint committee to recommend proposals for expanding the fuel relief scheme and for a phased reduction in the petroleum levy for the next budget.
Earlier this week, both sides completed negotiations at the technical committee level, finalising draft proposals on public relief measures, JI said.
