# Provinces cannot assume ‘unexercised’ federal powers: FCC

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Sat, 21 Nov 2026 04:32:44 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2046013/provinces-cannot-assume-unexercised-federal-powers-fcc
- Collected: 2026-11-21 07:57:02 UTC

---

ISLAMABAD: The Federal Constitutional Court (FCC) has ruled that parliament’s failure to exercise a fiscal power does not mean that the subject has been transferred to the provinces.
“An identified conflict with a competent federal law attracts the consequence prescribed under Article 142 of the Constitution, meaning that the federal law will prevail whereas the provincial law becomes void to the extent of that conflict,” observed FCC Chief Justice Aminuddin Khan in a 59-page judgement authored by him.
The ruling came as a two-judge FCC bench, consisting of the chief justice and Justice Aamer Farooq, issued its detailed judgement on a set of 568 challenges filed by different taxpayers against the provincial infrastructure development cess imposed by Sindh and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.
Following the verbal order issued a day earlier, the detailed FCC judgement explained that although the statutory schemes and procedural histories differed, the matters raised a common constitutional question for determination: whether the impugned levies, in their true nature and operation, fell within provincial legislative competence or encroached upon parliament’s exclusive authority over imports, exports and interprovincial trade.
Detailed order in provincial development cess case holds that federal law should prevail where it conflicts with provincial legislation
While allowing the civil appeals filed by the taxpayers, the FCC set aside the impugned judgements of the SHC and PHC and declared that the corresponding charging provisions, along with all dependent rules, notifications, assessments and recovery measures, were unconstitutional and without lawful authority.
“No demand, surcharge or penalty founded solely upon those charges shall be enforced,” the judgement said, adding that securities furnished solely for the invalid charges would be discharged upon verification and subject to any subsisting order governing their disposal.
However, the provinces remained free to enact constitutionally competent infrastructure or service charges on goods manufactured and consumed within their respective territories, the verdict ruled.
It explained that Article 160 of the Constitution and the applicable National Finance Commission arrangements distinguish between the power to levy a federal tax and entitlement to its proceeds.
“A province’s share does not authorise it to impose that tax independently. Equally, two competent levies are not unconstitutional merely because they affect the same goods. The defect in the present cases was legislative incompetence, not duplication; fiscal need could not supply the missing power,” the judgement said.
The FCC observed that the impugned judgements had given controlling weight to the infrastructure purpose, movement of goods and asserted compensatory character of the cess without first establishing provincial legislative competence.
None of these considerations provided an independent provincial basis for the challenged import, export and external-transit charges, it said.
The FCC further observed that the abolition of the Concurrent Legislative List through the 18th Amendment had expanded provincial competence but had not transferred subjects expressly retained in the federal domain.
Provincial autonomy must, therefore, be given full effect within its constitutional sphere while preserving parliament’s exclusive authority over matters reserved to it, the judgement said.
During the hearing, the taxpayers had contended that the levies encroached upon parliament’s exclusive legislative domain and could not be sustained as fees or compensatory charges merely because the goods used provincial infrastructure.
They had also challenged the collection arrangements, discriminatory treatment, restrictions on trade and retrospective validation.
The provinces, meanwhile, invoked their residual legislative powers, infrastructure responsibilities and services provided to commercial users, distinguishing the cess from customs duties.
