# Rizwan, Shan, Saim among notable omissions as PCB awards contracts to 38 players

- Published: Sat, 21 Nov 2026 18:17:25 +0500

Former captain Mohammad Rizwan, Shan Masood and Saim Ayub were among the notable omissions as the Pakistan Cricket Board (PCB) on Saturday announced central contracts for 38 men’s cricketers under a revamped, format-based structure that places greater emphasis on specialised roles and the development of emerging talent.
The contracts, effective from Nov 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027, mark a departure from the traditional A-to-D grading system, replacing salary-based rankings with five tracks based on players’ format commitments.
The new list includes eight more players than the previous cycle, while 19 cricketers have been placed in the newly introduced development category.
Besides Rizwan, Shan and Saim, the notable omissions include Hasan Ali, Abdullah Shafique, Mohammad Haris, Mohammad Nawaz, Noman Ali, Hussain Talat, Khurram Shahzad, Abbas Afridi and Mohammad Wasim Jnr. The players had been without central contracts since their previous agreements expired in June.
Announcing the list at Gaddafi Stadium, member of the selection committee and director of high performance Aaqib Javed said the restructuring followed consultations involving selectors and the coaching staff to identify players expected to feature in particular formats over the coming months.
“The idea was to award central contracts to players who are currently playing across all three formats, as well as those whom the selectors and head coach believe are likely to feature in a particular format by the end of June next year,” Aaqib said.
Head coach Mike Hesson said the model was intended to give players greater clarity about their roles and allow the management to plan their preparation accordingly. He noted that several Pakistan players were still in the development phase after recently breaking into international cricket.
The five tracks comprise AB for Test and ODI players, A for Test specialists, BC for ODI and T20I cricketers, C for T20I players and D for developing talent. Babar Azam and Salman Ali Agha are the only two players contracted for both Tests and ODIs, while no player has received a contract covering all three formats.
Aaqib clarified that the categories would not restrict players from appearing in other formats or participating in franchise leagues.
Players on Test-only contracts, however, would be encouraged to prioritise four-day cricket if taking up additional playing commitments. He cited Babar’s participation in the International League T20 as an example of a commitment unaffected by the new arrangement.
He also stressed that central contracts did not guarantee selection, saying players outside the list, including Rizwan, remained eligible for national duty.
The PCB has increased players’ financial remuneration, although Aaqib did not disclose the percentage. Existing incentives include doubled match fees for victories against higher-ranked opponents and half the Test match fee for contracted players appearing in domestic four-day cricket. Fitness testing and domestic participation will also form part of players’ contractual obligations.
The development category includes several players who have recently made an impression in first-class cricket and earned opportunities in the national side.
Tayyab Arif, Mohammad Taha, Ghazi Ghori and Saqib Khan, for instance, featured in Pakistan’s recent Test series against Sri Lanka, which the hosts won 1-0 on Friday. Saqib claimed nine wickets across the two matches, while Ghazi scored a half-century in the second Test.
Hesson said Pakistan were also working towards narrowing their selection pool to around 20 players through multi-team matches in Multan and upcoming domestic competitions, including the ODI Cup.
On Shaheen Shah Afridi’s absence from the Test-specialist category, Hesson welcomed the fast bowler’s return to consistent first-class cricket and said discussions had taken place about his red-ball involvement. Shaheen has instead been contracted for ODIs and T20Is.
Players awarded central contracts

Test: Azan Awais, Mohammad Abbas, Sajid Khan, Saud Shakeel.
ODI and T20I: Abrar Ahmed, Arafat Minhas, Fakhar Zaman, Faheem Ashraf, Haris Rauf, Shaheen Shah Afridi.
