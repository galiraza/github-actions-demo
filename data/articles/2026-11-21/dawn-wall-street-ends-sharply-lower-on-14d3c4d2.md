# Wall Street ends sharply lower on AI selloff, S&P 500, Dow in correction

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Sat, 21 Nov 2026 11:35:56 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2046101/wall-street-ends-sharply-lower-on-ai-selloff-sampp-500-dow-in-correction
- Collected: 2026-11-21 07:57:02 UTC

---

US stocks extended their rout on Friday, and the S&P 500 and the Dow both confirmed corrections after Anthropic’s momentous debut fell flat, exacerbating worries about the AI trade that has driven this year’s stock market rally.
All three major US equity indexes ended sharply lower. The Dow and the S&P 500 are now more than 10 per cent below their record closing highs, confirming both indexes have entered a correction.
The bellwether S&P 500 notched its seventh consecutive drop, its longest losing streak since February 2020.
The S&P 500 and the Dow logged their largest weekly percentage losses since April 2025, when US President Donald Trump announced his market-rattling “liberation day” tariff package.
The Nasdaq’s weekly drop was its largest since March 2020. The tech-heavy index has dipped into negative territory for the year.
“This is clearly a challenging day for capital markets, and as we head into a Friday close, it appears to be accelerating to a certain degree,” said Bill Northey, senior investment director at US Bank Wealth Management in Billings, Montana.
“But as we step back and evaluate fundamentals and sentiment, sentiment is clearly winning the day today.”
“Sentiment is always the more powerful force in the immediate space, whereas fundamentals tend to win out over time,” Northey added, referring to economic strength and solid corporate earnings, among other things.
Anthropic’s supersized debut as a publicly held company, which was intended to raise as much as $2 trillion in market cap, failed to catch fire on Thursday, closing 17.1pc above its $83 offer price as enthusiasm for the stock faded throughout the session.
In its second day as a publicly held company, Anthropic’s shares fell 9.1pc; Nvidia’s shares tumbled 13.8pc.
The Anthropic IPO, combined with Nvidia’s quarterly earnings report, failed to dispel fears over risks associated with artificial intelligence and the massive expenditures on the nascent technology.
“Nvidia reported what might have been one of the greatest quarters in corporate earnings history; they did everything right,” said Chuck Carlson, chief executive officer at Horizon Investment Services in Hammond, Indiana.
“A lot of people, myself included, were looking to the Anthropic debut to some extent as a clearing event, and it wasn’t.”
The Philadelphia SE Semiconductor Index, which has powered much of the broader stock market’s gains in recent months, was up as much as 106.6pc in mid-June. That lustre has faded, with the index’s year-to-date advance having shrunk to 18.7pc.
Among hyperscalers, Amazon fell 3.9pc, while Meta dropped 5.9pc.
Neocloud firm CoreWeave fell 12.1pc and its peer Nebius slid 16.1pc.
The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 949.73 points, or 1.93pc, to 48,147.33; the S&P 50 lost 265.41 points, or 3.70pc, to 6,910.42 and the Nasdaq Composite lost 1,261.47 points, or 5.28pc, to 22,617.73.
The CBOE Market Volatility Index, often referred to as Wall Street’s “fear gauge,” rose to its highest close since April 7, 2025.
