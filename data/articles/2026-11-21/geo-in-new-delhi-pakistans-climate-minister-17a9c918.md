# In New Delhi, Pakistan's climate minister urges collective efforts for improvement in region

Published November 21, 2026
Minister for Climate Change and Environmental Coordination Dr Musadik Malik has expressed hope for an improvement in the regional situation through collective engagement by relevant countries.
The minister made the remarks following his arrival in India's New Delhi for the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO) Civilisational Dialogue Forum.
The visit marks the first trip to India by a Pakistani federal minister since May 2023, when then foreign minister Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari visited Goa for a meeting of the SCO Council of Foreign Ministers.
Speaking to the media, Malik said that there were several centres of instability in the region, expressing hope that countries sitting together would help improve the situation.
Pakistan will support all efforts aimed at promoting peace and stability in the region, he said, adding that Islamabad had made every effort to play its role in bringing stability to the region.
"Whoever makes efforts to advance peace and security, we will stand with them," he said.
The minister arrived in New Delhi on Friday to lead Pakistan’s delegation at the forum, according to the Pakistan High Commission in India.
The forum will discuss the shared civilisational heritage and cultural ties among member states, with a focus on promoting regional heritage and strengthening cultural cooperation.
Malik's arrival in India let loose a barrage of criticism from Congress spokesperson Supriya Shrinate, Congress leaders Surendra Rajput and Pramod Tiwari, Samajwadi Party’s Fakhrul Hasan Chand, and NCP (SP)’s Clyde Crasto, according to The News.
They accused the ruling BJP government of inconsistency and "double standards" by inviting Pakistan. The Indian government, however, strongly defended Malik's presence and said he was in New Delhi on its invitation.
Defending Malik's visit, the Indian official explained that invitations were extended to all SCO member states in accordance with established multilateral practice, with India originally sending an invitation to Pakistan's heritage minister before Islamabad nominated Malik.
Asked about India's concern over cross-border terrorism, Malik responded, "I think tomorrow, once I give my statement, we’ll have a lot of conversations..."
On being asked about civilisational linkages between India and Pakistan, he said, "I think dialogues are between distinct entities. Singularity doesn’t require a dialogue. Singularity only requires a monologue. Dialogue is about diversity. I think between India, Pakistan, Russia, China and Central Asian states, if you go to the excavations, you would find that dialogue taking place just in the fore. I think it's a very welcome dialogue."
At the Delhi meet, the climate minister called for more dialogue under the SCO umbrella.
"We are having a dialogue across civilisations and cultures. I think we need more and more such engagements. Dialogue across civilisations is particularly important at a time when the entire region is undergoing changes and geopolitics are in flux. It is a very welcome development," he said.
