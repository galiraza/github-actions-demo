# Karachi finds a new way to remember its icons

Published November 21, 2026
KARACHI: The homes of some of Karachi’s most celebrated personalities are now being identified with commemorative blue plaques as part of a new initiative launched by the district administration in the port city’s Central District.
Under the Commemorative Blue Plaque Initiative, plaques have initially been installed at the former residences of renowned qawwal Amjad Sabri, legendary entertainer Umer Sharif and celebrated artist Syed Sadequain Ahmed Naqvi.
Speaking to Geo Digital, Talha Sabri, brother of the late Amjad Sabri, said representatives of the district administration visited their home to inform the family about the installation of the plaque.
According to Talha, the officials told the family that they had been sent by the deputy commissioner to install a commemorative plaque at Amjad Sabri’s residence.
When he saw the plaque, he noticed that it carried his brother’s name, date of birth and date of martyrdom. He said he was pleased to see the city’s celebrated artists and personalities being remembered in this way.
He added that such initiatives were important to ensure that younger generations also learn about the artists and cultural figures who contributed to Karachi’s identity.
Talha also requested the district administration to install a plaque in honour of his father, renowned qawwal Ghulam Farid Sabri, alongside the one commemorating his brother.
The plaque installed at Amjad Sabri’s residence describes the house as the former home of a renowned qawwal.
Talha said he had spent his childhood and youth in Liaquatabad, an area with which his family shares countless memories.
“We grew up here, watching our father and brother rise to fame. Even today, if you ask someone to take you to Amjad Sabri’s house, they will bring you right to its doorstep. The sad part is that the people whose names gave this house its recognition are no longer with us,” he said.
Responding to a question about selection for the blue plaques, Central District Deputy Commissioner Taha Saleem told Geo Digital that a committee had been formed to select the personalities whose residences would receive commemorative plaques.
He said the administration wanted to ensure that the selection process was not solely a bureaucratic decision.
“We did not want the selection of personalities to become a bureaucratic choice, so we formed a committee comprising journalists Syed Waliullah and Afia Salam, historian Aqeel Abbas Jafri, Mirza Iqbal Baig and Arts Council Karachi member Shakeel Khan. These individuals understand Karachi’s issues, culture and literature, so the selection of personalities for the plaques is being made through the committee’s recommendations,” he said.
According to the deputy commissioner, plaques have initially been installed at the residences of Sabri, Sharif and Sadequain.
He added that the administration had not yet determined the total number of personalities who would be included in the initiative, as more names were still under consideration. The project, he said, would continue.
When asked what the Blue Plaque initiative is and where it began, he said the tradition of installing commemorative blue plaques to identify buildings associated with notable historical figures began in London, United Kingdom, in 1863.
The idea was to preserve the memory of prominent personalities by marking the places where they had lived or worked. The first commemorative plaques under the initiative were installed in 1867.
Nearly 160 years later, Karachi is adopting a similar tradition, beginning with Central District. The district administration hopes that the initiative will eventually be extended to other parts of the city.
