# Focus on public issues, avoid new fault lines: PPP

ISLAMABAD: Seeking to ease tensions between her party and PML-N amid the ongoing debate on need for new provinces, PPP Vice President Senator Sherry Rehman on Saturday urged the government to focus on people’s problems rather than create new political fault lines.
Provocative statements by ministers should be avoided, she said, adding that PPP Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari wanted politics to address people’s problems rather than create new divisions.
“Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari is looking to prevent bloodshed, not shed it,” she said.
Her statement came before Deputy Prime Minister Ishaq Dar and National Assembly Speaker Sardar Ayaz Sadiq met President Asif Ali Zardari at President House to discuss the overall political situation. Besides Senator Rehman, PPP leader Raja Pervez Ashraf was also present.
The meeting followed an apparent easing of tensions between President Zardari and Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif who also discussed the matter with Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi after the PPP chairman’s remarks at a ‘Meet the Press’ event at the Karachi Press Club. He had opposed the creation of new provinces, warning that dividing Sindh would lead to bloodshed.
He also criticised the coalition over several issues, including the treatment of JUI-F chief Maulana Fazlur Rehman, PTI founder Imran Khan’s incarceration and the 27th Amendment. He later clarified that he was raising an alarm, not issuing a threat, and complained that the PPP was not being consulted.
The remarks drew sharp reactions from PML-N ministers Khawaja Asif and Tallal Chaudhry, while MQM-P chief Khalid Maqbool Siddiqui urged the courts to take notice. Mustafa Kamal also criticised Mr Bhutto-Zardari.
President Zardari subsequently met Prime Minister Shehbaz and Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi, with the meeting calling for dialogue and restraint in public statements.
A separate PPP meeting reaffirmed the party’s opposition to Sindh’s division. A meeting between Mr Bhutto-Zardari and the prime minister is also expected in the coming days.
Senator Rehman also warned of a difficult winter, with gas and resource shortages and extreme weather in many areas, urging politicians not to add to people’s troubles by creating new fault lines.
Constitutional process
She also drew a constitutional line on demands by some ministers for a referendum on new provinces. “They [the ministers] should know that the Constitution will still refer the matter to parliament. There is no walking away from the votes of the assemblies.
A referendum does not override the Constitution, nor does it change any ground reality where any given province has spoken with its majority,” she said.
The PPP’s message was that it wanted the truce to hold but would oppose the creation of new provinces outside the constitutional parliamentary process.
Whether the easing of tensions lasts will depend on whether the parties pursue consultation and consensus through parliament or revive the referendum debate.
