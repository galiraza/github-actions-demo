# More than 2,300 Indian Sikh pilgrims arrive in Pakistan to mark Guru Nanak's birthday

- Published: Mon, 23 Nov 2026 00:13:48 +0500

---

LAHORE: Over 2,300 Indian Sikh pilgrims arrived in Pakistan through the Wagah Border on Sunday to attend celebrations marking the 557th birth anniversary of Baba Guru Nanak Dev Ji, the founder of Sikhism.
The visiting pilgrims were received by Evacuee Trust Property Board (ETPB) Chairman Qamar Zaman, Secretary Nasir Mushtaq, Pakistan Sikh Gurdwara Prabandhak Committee (PSGPC) President and Punjab Minorities Minister Sardar Ramesh Singh Arora, the committee’s secretary general Satwant Kaur and other officials at the Wagah border, where the jatha leaders were presented with bouquets.
According to ETPB spokesperson Amir Hashmi, 2,339 Sikh pilgrims arrived from India here on Sunday to take part in the festivities in Nankana Sahib.
The Pakistan High Commission in New Delhi had reportedly issued around 3,100 visas to Indian Sikhs.
Special counters were set up at the Wagah Border to facilitate immigration and baggage screening, while Rangers were deployed to ensure security.
The ETPB had also established currency exchange facilities at the border, while its teams promptly issued identity cards and bus tickets to the pilgrims to ensure a smooth onward journey, he added.
“The pilgrims subsequently left for Nankana Sahib in three separate convoys under tight security to attend the main ceremony on Nov 24 marking Baba Guru Nanak’s birth anniversary at Gurdwara Janam Asthan, his birthplace,” Hashmi said.
He added that over 20,000 Sikhs — from Pakistan and across the globe — are expected to take part in the formal festivities, which begin today and continue until Nov 24.
Federal Minister for Religious Affairs Sardar Muhammad Yousuf, along with other federal and provincial ministers, will attend the event as special guests.
The event participants will perform religious rituals, including Bhog and Akhand Path Sahib, followed by a traditional palanquin procession, known as Nagar Kirtan.
Speaking on the occasion, ETPB Chairman Zaman said the board was making every effort to ensure that the visiting pilgrims faced no inconvenience during their stay in Pakistan.
He said the Indian Sikh pilgrims would return home with fond memories of love, peace and brotherhood.
Minister Arora urged the Indian government to immediately reopen the Kartarpur Corridor, which provides Sikh pilgrims with access to Gurdwara Darbar Sahib in Kartarpur, one of the holiest sites in Sikhism.
India suspended movement through the Kartarpur Corridor in May 2025 amid the four-day conflict between the countries.
Kaldeep Singh Gurgaj, a leader of the Akal Takht authority from India, said the Sikh community shared a deep bond of reverence with Pakistan, where its members were always treated with respect and honour.
“Our gurdwaras in Pakistan are safe and preserved in their original state,” he said.
Parmjeet Singh, a leader of a Delhi Gurdwara Management Committee delegation, said Pakistan held special significance for Sikhs as the land associated with their guru.
“Pakistan is the land of our guru, and we love it very much,” he said.
After attending the main ceremony, the Sikh pilgrims will visit Gurdwara Sacha Sauda in Farooqabad on Nov 25 before proceeding to Hassan Abdal, where they will stay overnight at Gurdwara Panja Sahib.
They will travel to Gurdwara Darbar Sahib in Kartarpur, Narowal, on Nov 27.
The visiting devotees will then spend Nov 29 and 30 visiting various historic gurdwaras in Lahore before returning to India through the Wagah border by road on Dec 1.
Sikh pilgrims frequently visit Pakistan to observe events of religious significance. In June, around 600 Sikhs arrived from India to observe the death anniversary of Guru Arjan Dev Ji, known as Jore Mela; before that, about 2,800 visas were issued to pilgrims to celebrate the Baisakhi festival in April.
