# Naqvi hits back at minister for repeated criticism: report

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Sun, 22 Nov 2026 21:03:48 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2046332/naqvi-hits-back-at-minister-for-repeated-criticism-report
- Collected: 2026-11-22 20:15:36 UTC

---

Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi has hit back at a federal minister for what he said was repeated criticism against him, Geo News reported on Sunday.
The news website reported that Naqvi, without naming the minister, claimed the latter had been commenting on him on television almost every day to “gain attention”.
The report described the remarks as part of an informal conversation with Geo News and further quoted Naqvi as saying that he had been patiently listening to the minister’s statements for the past two-and-a-half years — the tenure of the incumbent government since the 2024 elections.
“The day I comment on this minister, he will not be able to show his face,” Naqvi warned, according to the outlet.
Naqvi also defended the businesspeople present at an economic forum who he said were criticised by the minister. He said the attendees were respectable citizens who had served as presidents and office-bearers of chambers of commerce at the federal and city levels, according to the report.
While the report did not quote him as mentioning any specific details in this connection, Naqvi’s remarks at the Pakistan Economic Summit in Islamabad in July had been making headlines in recent months. Naqvi had whipped up a political storm by calling for creating new administrative units in the country to “reset” the current “system” of governance while speaking at the forum, which was attended by businessmen.
In his remarks to Geo News, Naqvi stated that the businessmen were also among the country’s highest taxpayers.
“If these businessmen are so bad, why do you take billions of rupees from them in the name of party funds at every election?” the interior minister was quoted as saying.
Naqvi’s remarks have been reported a day after Inter-Provincial Coordination Minister Rana Sanaullah questioned the objectives of some of the businesspeople at that July forum.
In an interview on Geo News programme ‘Jirga’ aired on Saturday, Sanaullah said the “matter got worse in such a way that the debate on provinces began from an economic forum” and Naqvi started the discussion.
Sanaullah, also Naqvi’s predecessor, contended that there were “some objectionable businessmen sitting among the audience of that economic forum”.
“It has been their background that in each tenure, they keep on telling the incoming ruler, especially the army chief, that ‘sir, the whole world is looking at you and only you are the saviour,’” Sanaullah said.
The PML-N leader clarified that he was not referring to the entire business community, but rather “a few people who keep committing crime in business, and here as well”.
On differing stances among federal cabinet members on the issue of new provinces, Sanaullah contended that politicians could express their opinions, as “regimental discipline” could not be brought into the cabinet.
Sanaullah stressed that the debate on provinces was “not wrong”, but he contended that the “debate was being entangled with other matters; saying that the system has collapsed; after that some remarks were made about the prime minister, and similarly about politicians contesting elections in so and so manner”.
The PML-N leader also criticised PPP Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari for further complicating the situation by referring to various matters in his Nov 17 speech, rather than merely voicing his objection to carving out new administrative units out of Sindh.
Sanaullah further termed both Bilawal and Naqvi as “sons” of President Asif Ali Zardari, adding that they were “under his discipline”.
Asked about the independent senator being “under President Zardari’s discipline”, Sanaullah reiterated, “Yes, definitely. He has not disowned him yet. He himself calls him (Naqvi) his son; it is not that we are saying so.”
Referring to a recent meeting between President Zardari and PM Shehbaz Sharif, during which Naqvi was also present, Sanaullah said a “ceasefire was decided” there.
He claimed that, according to information he had, a meeting was held at President House recently, which was attended by President Zardari, Bilawal and Naqvi, and it was decided to reduce tensions.
Replying to a query about the independent senator, Sanaullah remarked that Naqvi was a senator of both PPP and PML-N, as leaders from both parties contributed to the votes for his election.
In September as well, Sanaullah had said that the “debate should have been started in Parliament”, adding that issues of such magnitude should not be reduced to speeches, social media, seminars or business forums.
The PML-N leader has also taken jibes at the interior minister on a few other occasions since the ruling coalition came to power.
On the other hand, Sindh Local Government Minister Nasir Hussain Shah also took exception to Sanaullah’s remarks, asserting that Bilawal was the only son of President Zardari.
“Mohsin Naqvi sahib is worthy of respect in his place, but nothing about him compares to the chairman (Bilawal),” he told reporters.
Shah maintained there was “no such thing” as suggested by the PML-N minister, declaring, “There is only one Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari, and only his words will prevail here.”
