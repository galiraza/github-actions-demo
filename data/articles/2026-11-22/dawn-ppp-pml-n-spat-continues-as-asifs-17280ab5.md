# PPP, PML-N spat continues as Asif's jibe at Bilawal draws 'red line' warning

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Sun, 22 Nov 2026 17:01:51 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2046317/ppp-pml-n-spat-continues-as-asifs-jibe-at-bilawal-draws-red-line-warning
- Collected: 2026-11-22 15:00:36 UTC

---

In the latest from the ongoing wrangling between the ruling PML-N and its ally in the Centre, the PPP, leaders from the latter have said Defence Minister Khawaja Asif’s remarks directed at Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari were “unacceptable” and crossed the party’s “red line”.
The reaction from PPP Senator Sherry Rehman and MNA Sharmila Faruqui on Sunday came on a Nov 19 post by Asif, in which he shared two video clips of PPP Chairperson Bilawal.
In the first clip, Bilawal is seen criticising the PTI and its founder, Imran Khan, and in the second one, from his recent address at the Karachi Press Club (KPC), he terms Imran a “political prisoner”.
“A person becomes the prime minister and is later imprisoned. So I can either accept that every prime minister in the country’s history has been a thief, or I can accept that all prime ministers in our history have been victims of political revenge,” he adds.
Commenting on the two clips, Asif posted on X: “We believed that the new generation would be better and more principled than ours. Did not know they would turn out to be masters of dishonesty and change colours so remarkably.”
Reacting to his remarks, Rehman posted on X: “Unacceptable flow of comments from the federal defence minister about the PPP organisation and our leadership, Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari. He has crossed a red line.”
Similarly, Faruqui said Asif’s “derogatory language”, particularly his “irresponsible remarks” about Bilawal, were unacceptable for the PPP.
“This is a red line for us.”
Directly addressing Asif, she went on to say that while everyone had the right to disagree politically, there was a difference between derision and disagreement.
“As a senior parliamentarian and a federal minister, you are expected to exercise political judgement, seriousness and decorum. Political differences are addressed through reasoned argument, not personal attacks. Remember, words reflect not only a person’s character but also the dignity of their office.”
The PPP and the PML-N have been trading barbs after Bilawal speech at the KPC on Nov 17. On the occasion, the Bhutto scion addressed the ongoing debate on the creation of new provinces, expressed support for several opposition demands and questioned past decisions and actions taken by the ruling coalition. He also warned of bloodshed in Sindh over the creation of new provinces and called for a “political reset”.
His remarks drew a strong reaction from senior leaders of the PML-N, Jamaat-i-Islami (JI) and Muttahida Qaumi Movement-Pakistan, who accused him of “political hypocrisy”.
Asif continued his criticism of Bilawal on Saturday, accusing him of being the “biggest beneficiary and a critic of the system at the same time”.
“Rank opportunism,” he commented.
Earlier, he brought the ownership of Bilawal House in Lahore into the political argument.
In a post on X, the defence minister questioned the ownership and use of Bilawal House, asking: “Can someone disclose the ownership of this property? Who owns it, and who lives there? If this palace-like fortress is being used by anyone, who are they?”
The PML-N senior leader further questioned whether those occupying properties without having legal title to them could “morally claim any privileged position”, adding that the 250 million people of Pakistan deserved to know the financial and property details of those who considered high status and privilege their entitlement.
Responding to him, PPP senior leader Nadeem Afzal Chan posted a video showing party workers gathering at the Bilawal House in Lahore, saying the property was the home of every PPP worker. “Show us people entering any other big house in the same way. Uncle… answer the questions, whether you like it or not,” Chan said in his X post, directly addressing the defence minister.
Separately, the PPP accused the defence minister of attempting to provoke the party into launching personal attacks against the PML-N leadership in retaliation. In a statement posted on X, the PPP said Bilawal had directed party leaders to ignore the defence minister’s remarks and concentrate on issue-based politics.
The statement, however, warned that the PPP was also familiar with the political controversies surrounding the PML-N leadership, referring to the Panama Papers, Avenfield properties and Al-Azizia Steel Mills cases.
However, even as the heated exchange of words continued between the two sides, President Asif Ali Zardari and Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif had decided that all matters would be resolved through dialogue, the Presidency said on Thursday after the two leaders met in Islamabad.
The two again met on Saturday to discuss the overall political situation.
After the meeting, Rehman urged the government to focus on people’s problems rather than creating new political fault lines.
Provocative statements by ministers should be avoided, she said, adding that Bilawal wanted politics to address people’s problems rather than create new divisions.
“Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari is looking to prevent bloodshed, not shed it,” she said.
