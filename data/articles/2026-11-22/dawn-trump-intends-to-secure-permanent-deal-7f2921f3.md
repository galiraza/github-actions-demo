# Trump intends to secure ‘permanent deal’ with Iran

---

• Araghchi says Tehran seeks ‘honourable, dignified’ agreement; says China, Russia oppose change in nuclear doctrine 
• Trump envoys discuss mediation with Qatari officials 
• Two tankers catch fire in Strait of Hormuz
WASHINGTON: US President Donald Trump has said he intends to secure a “permanent” deal with Iran, insisting that no future American president should be able to allow Tehran to obtain a nuclear weapon, according to an Anadolu Agency report.
Speaking in a phone interview with Fox News on Saturday, Trump said his approach was shaped by what he described as “Iran’s repeated failure” to honour agreements. “We make a deal, and then they break the deal the next day,” he said, arguing that any new agreement must prevent Iran from developing nuclear weapons regardless of who occupies the White House in the coming years.
Reuters quoted the US president as telling Fox News that he’s “going to make a deal that is permanent”.
He added that he would not allow a future administration to reverse course and permit Iran to acquire a nuclear weapon.
Trump also addressed suggestions that Washington was pursuing regime change in Tehran, saying his administration had already dealt with successive groups of Iranian leadership.
“We’re dealing with the third group, so you could call that the ultimate regime change,” he said, adding that the current figures were “probably … more reasonable” but remained difficult to negotiate with.
Guarantees sought
On the other hand, Iran’s Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi told the state-owned Iran newspaper that messages continued to be exchanged between Tehran and Washington through mediators, and that Tehran was seeking “an agreement that would ensure there was no further war”.
He also said Iran wanted guarantees that Washington was serious about negotiations before proceeding further, adding that Iran had discussed with mediators the form and nature of confidence-building measures.
He also said China and Russia oppose a change in Tehran’s nuclear doctrine, amid domestic calls for a shift. He told the state-run Iran Daily that the issue was “broad, serious” and had its own “supporters and opponents”.
“Russia and China are also opposed” to a change in Iran’s doctrine, he added.
The remarks came as diplomatic efforts by Qatar continued to try to end a conflict that has destabilised the Middle East, disrupted shipping, driven up global energy prices and drawn in countries across the region.
The foreign minister said Iran’s red lines included keeping its highly enriched uranium in the country, though he said a “fair solution” was still possible.
He said Iran remains open to diplomacy with the United States but would not accept an agreement that compromises its dignity or undermines the victory it has achieved on the battlefield, IRNA reports.
Araghchi said Tehran was seeking a “good, honourable and dignified agreement,” stressing that any deal must safeguard Iran’s national interests and prevent a renewed conflict.
“We are seeking an agreement that would ensure there is no more war afterwards.”
US President Donald Trump’s envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner earlier met senior Qatari officials in Berlin on Thursday to discuss mediation efforts between the US and Iran, Axios reports according to Reuters, citing two sources with knowledge of the meeting.
Two tankers caught fire
Meanwhile, two tankers caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz, at the heart of the conflict between Iran and the US. “A tanker transiting outbound in the Strait of Hormuz has been hit on the stern by an unknown projectile. There are no reported casualties at this time,” the British maritime agency UKMTO stated.
Also, Iran said it shot down a US drone over Qeshm Island in the strait, according to Iranian state TV.
Araghchi has said reopening the Strait of Hormuz is “not difficult”, but requires the US to accept “reasonable conditions” under international law and acknowledge its “mistakes” and previous commitments, Al Jazeera reports.
