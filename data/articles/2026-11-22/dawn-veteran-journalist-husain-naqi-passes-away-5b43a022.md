# Veteran journalist Husain Naqi passes away in Lahore

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Sun, 22 Nov 2026 17:54:05 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2046310/veteran-journalist-husain-naqi-passes-away-in-lahore
- Collected: 2026-11-22 15:00:36 UTC

---

LAHORE: Veteran journalist and human rights activist Husain Naqi passed away in Lahore on Sunday.
He was a former editor of The News and had also been associated with the Human Rights Commission of Pakistan (HRCP).
HRCP official Muhammad Ilyas told Dawn that Naqi died of a cardiac arrest.
“He attended a book launch ceremony at the HRCP the previous day. Later, he suffered a heart attack around 6pm on Saturday. He was admitted to a private hospital where he died early in the morning, around 5 am,” Ilyas said, adding that he had left behind three sons.
Naqi was buried in Model Town Extension in Lahore.
Meanwhile, condolences over his passing continued to pour in.
State broadcaster Radio Pakistan reported that Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif expressed “deep grief and sorrow over” Naqi’s passing and said it marked the “end of a bright chapter in journalism and democracy”.
The premier remembered Naqi as a “principled journalist who fearlessly raised voice for freedom of expression, democratic values and human rights throughout his life”.
The prime minister expressed his “heartfelt condolences to the bereaved family, the journalistic community and human rights organisations. He also prayed for the departed soul”, the Radio Pakistan report said.
President Asif Ali Zardari also extended his condolences to the bereaved family, expressing “deep sorrow”, a statement issued by the Presidency said.
The president paid tribute to Naqi’s “services to journalism, freedom of expression and human rights”, the statement said.
It further quoted the president as saying: “Husain Naqi played a prominent role in promoting journalism and democratic values. His contributions will always be remembered.”
The HRCP also released a statement, saying it “deeply mourns” Naqi’s passing.
“Over a career spanning more than 70 years, from student leader to newspaper editor to treasurer of HRCP, neither multiple incarcerations nor vindictive acts by the authorities ever weakened his resolve to speak truth to power. Naqi sahib served HRCP in different capacities for three decades and was an active council member at the time of his passing. One of the strongest voices for people’s rights and democracy in Pakistan has gone silent,” the statement said.
PPP Senator Sherry Rehman posted on X that she was “truly grieved to hear about the sad demise of the veteran journalist and rights campaigner, the iconic Husain Naqi”.
“His passing signals the end of an era. Naqi sahib always spoke unflinching truth to power. Many of us cub journalists learnt at his desk, walked in rallies under his leadership. He led so many groups and networks in the hardest days of dictatorship.
“We often called him the rebel angel of our times, yet he never stoked anger. He sought the right to dissent with respect. He will be missed. May his soul rest in peace and power,” she said.
PTI Secretary General Salman Akram Raja began his codolence on X by stating: “Alas! Naqi sahib is no longer among us.”
He said his acquaintance and admiration for Naqi spanned decades.
Naming I.A. Rehman, Asma Jahangir and Amin Mughal along with Naqi, Raja said, “The most precious gems who dreamed of and championed the dignity of human beings and the rising of voices of the poor in Pakistan are leaving us.
“Their dreams have yet to be realised. Their dreams are the dreams of 250 million people.”
Journalist Absa Komal said Naqi spent his entire life advocating for freedom of speech.
“I met him a few months ago at a conference in Karachi, and he still had the same spirit and commitment to the cause. A remarkable life,” she added.
Journalist Raza Rumi was more succinct in his message that said: “Rest in peace Naqi sahib.”
Former senator Mushahid Hussain Sayed said Naqi was the “last Icon of ‘Old Guard’ progressive journalists” who always spoke truth to power.
Who was Husain Naqi?
Naqi was born in Lucknow, India before the Independence in 1947. In his autobiography Mujh Se Jo Ho Saka, Naqi said he was perhaps born a year before his officially recorded birthdate in 1936.
His great-grandfather had moved to Lucknow from a village in what is now Haryana, in the then-undivided Punjab. It was a well-heeled and well-educated family that flourished in Lucknow over the next few generations.
However, Naqi subtly confronted the conservatism of his traditional family from a very young age. Outside, he was not subtle. He began his student activism in secondary school and started taking on the minor establishments of power at a fairly young age.
After early schooling and college education in Lucknow, Naqi moved to Karachi in the 1950s, where he developed into a student leader.
As a student in Karachi, Naqi played a key role in several demonstrations and protests. During the 1956 Suez Canal crisis when Egypt was attacked by Israel, France and Britain, Naqi was part of the executive committee of the National Students Federation (NSF). At the time, the NSF organised a march to the British High Commission in Karachi to protest against the attack and was faced with police batons and tear gas.
As president of Karachi University’s student union, Naqi made a deal with the university’s vice-chancellor for Naqi to be rusticated for three years instead of three Baloch students who had been rusticated in response to an incident at the hostel.
Naqi’s activism during his student days was not just centred around protests but also had to do with improving conditions in the institution. He fought for adding a new campus with better facilities, providing safe transport for girls and ensuring that exams were held on time and not postponed.
He was a strong advocate for student unions.
In an interview published in 2011, he said, “it was the banning of student unions that destroyed campus peace. In our time, student unions were a positive influence. We organised cultural activities. We tolerated diversity. Representatives with various political leanings were able to work together despite their ideological differences”.
After a few turbulent years in Karachi, Naqi permanently settled down in Lahore.
As a journalist, Naqi found his first mentor in Javed Bokhari, with whom he worked for a couple of years. “In those days I used to literally live at the PPI office,” he had said of the time.
He ended up working as the bureau chief of PPI. However, he eventually had to leave the position following a reporting incident in which he refused to accept a false news of desecration of the Holy Quran.
Nonetheless, he continued to write for various newspapers, including the Viewpoint, Holiday, Outlook, Frontier Post, Frontier Guardian, Daily Star, Punjab Times and Punjab Punch.
Naqi consistently campaigned for the preservation and promotion of the Punjabi language in Pakistan, starting the first-ever daily newspaper in the Punjabi language — Sajjan.
Naqi had also been imprisoned a couple of times because of his outspokenness. In the 2011 interview, he recalled those instances with a sense of humour. “I was lucky to invariably have someone favourably disposed towards me at the jail premises, who made sure that I was not mistreated.”
