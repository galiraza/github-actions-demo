# Lahore again tops global pollution ranking with AQI above 500

Published November 22, 2026
Lahore has once again topped the list of the world's most polluted major cities, recording a particulate matter reading of 538 between 9am and 10am on Sunday, according to the figures from the Swiss air quality monitoring platform IQAir.
The Swiss air quality platform categorises readings above 300 as "hazardous", indicating extremely polluted air that poses serious health risks to residents.
India's Delhi ranked second among the world's major cities, with a particulate matter reading of 207.
Bahawalpur in Pakistan's Punjab reported a high reading of 463, while Sargodha recorded 382.
Multan registered a particulate matter reading of 255, according to IQAir.
The toxic haze has left Punjab's capital city shrouded for days, reducing visibility and causing widespread health complaints, including throat irritation, breathing difficulties and itchy eyes.
Over recent years, Pakistan and India have each accused the other of worsening the air.
Earlier this month, Punjab Senior Minister Marriyum Aurangzeb linked the worsening smog across the province to large-scale fireworks during Diwali celebrations in India.
In a post on X, she said that polluted winds entering through the eastern corridor from Indian cities contributed to the increase in the provincial capital's AQI.
Silwat Saeed, chief of the Environment Protection and Climate Change Department in Punjab, previously said that the challenge was huge for both countries and that Pakistan has raised it with officials from India at international forums.
Every winter, much of South Asia experiences a surge in pollution as cold air traps fine particles near the surface. Emissions from transport, factories, construction dust and crop residue burning all contribute to the dense smog that lingers for weeks.
Health experts warn that prolonged exposure to such high pollution levels can lead to severe health issues, including stroke, heart disease, lung cancer and chronic respiratory illnesses — a growing concern for millions across South Asia.
