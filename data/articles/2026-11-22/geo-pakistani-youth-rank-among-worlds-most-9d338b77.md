LONDON: Pakistani students rank among the world's most optimistic about the future of the environment, placing seventh globally in a new survey examining young people's outlook across several aspects of life.
The study, conducted by Oxford summer school provider Oxford Scholastica Academy, assessed students' optimism about their personal futures, the economy, the environment, politics and society as a whole. While the survey covered 100 countries, responses from students in 41 countries were sufficient for the rankings. It received a total of 1,433 responses.
Pakistan ranked 14th among the 41 countries, with an overall score of 3.94, placing it in the top third of the rankings. It narrowly edged out India (3.93) and Bangladesh (3.92).
Pakistani students recorded an environmental optimism score of 3.73, the seventh-highest among the countries surveyed, ahead of Canada (3.67), the United States (3.58) and the United Kingdom (3.06).
They also expressed confidence in their personal futures (4.55) and the future of society (4.05), with Pakistan ranking 13th in the latter category, well ahead of the US (24th). Political optimism was the weakest area, at 3.27, although Pakistan still scored higher than Germany (3.21), the UK (3.04) and France (2.38).
Sri Lanka topped the overall rankings, with students recording the highest environmental optimism score in the study (4.62) and strong confidence in their personal futures (4.75). Despite significant economic turbulence in recent years, Sri Lankan students also remained optimistic about the future of their society, scoring 4.25.
Kenya ranked second, with an overall score of 4.31. Its students recorded a score of 4.43 for optimism about the future of society, jointly the highest alongside Cyprus, and the highest political optimism score among all countries surveyed (3.96).
Nepal ranked third, recording a perfect score of 5.00 for personal optimism, the only country to achieve this. It also posted the highest economic optimism score (4.80), suggesting that students remain confident about their individual prospects despite the country's lower-income status.
Kazakhstan came fourth, with students recording strong economic confidence (4.56) and the third-highest political optimism score in the study (3.76).
Nigeria scored 4.08 overall, while Indonesia recorded 4.07. Nigerian students expressed strong environmental optimism (3.92), while their Indonesian counterparts reported high economic confidence (4.41).
Among Western countries, Canada ranked highest, placing sixth overall with a score of 4.08. It was followed by the US (3.99), Germany (3.73), the UK (3.61) and France (3.22).
Kuwait ranked last, with an overall score of 2.92. Its students recorded the lowest political optimism score in the study (1.80), as well as the lowest score for confidence in the future of society (2.40).
Rwanda was the second-least optimistic country, scoring 3.20 overall, with particularly low scores for the future of society and environmental optimism, both at 2.83.
Turkiye ranked third from the bottom, with an overall score of 3.22. Its students recorded the fifth-lowest political optimism score (2.58) and the fourth-lowest environmental score (2.62).
France and Poland also ranked among the least optimistic countries. French students recorded the lowest environmental optimism score in the study (2.25), alongside a political optimism score of 2.38. Poland recorded a political optimism score of 2.30, the second-lowest after Kuwait.
The UK ranked 33rd out of 41 countries, with an overall score of 3.61. British students scored 3.04 for political optimism and 3.06 for environmental optimism, placing the country in the bottom third in both categories.
Commenting on the findings, Lavinia Abell, co-director of Oxford Scholastica Academy, said: “In every aspect of society, there are things to be anxious and pessimistic about. This could be the rise in AI, war, the rising cost of living and much more. This will be especially worrisome for young people, facing challenges as they enter the world of work. What this study highlights is the attitudes of each nationality towards these uncertainties.
“What stands out is that students in countries like Sri Lanka and Kenya, which have faced significant economic and political challenges, are far more optimistic than their peers in wealthier nations like the UK and France. It suggests that optimism among young people is not simply a function of material comfort."
