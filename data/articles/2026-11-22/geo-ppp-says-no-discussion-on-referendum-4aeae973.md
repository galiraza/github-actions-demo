# PPP says no discussion on referendum for new provinces at leadership level

Published November 22, 2026
Pakistan Peoples Party (PPP) Senator Sherry Rehman on Sunday said no discussion had taken place at the party’s senior leadership level on holding a referendum for the creation of new provinces, rejecting reports that coalition partners would be persuaded to support the proposal.
Speaking on Geo News programme "Naya Pakistan", she said she had attended a high-level leadership meeting but had not heard any discussion about a referendum.
“I have not heard anything about a referendum at the senior level in my presence,” she said, adding that there had also been no discussion about convincing coalition partners on the matter.
The remarks came after Pakistan Muslim League-Nawaz (PML-N) leader Tariq Fazal Chaudhry on Friday said his party would try to convince the PPP to support a referendum to gauge public opinion before any decision on creating new provinces.
He said the PPP would neither be allowed to leave the ruling coalition nor would it leave, expressing confidence that the two parties would build a consensus.
Rehman said such proposals should first be brought forward for consultation at the highest level. She stressed that decisions taken by the Sindh Assembly or any other provincial assembly could not be overruled.
The senator said the Sindh Assembly had passed resolutions against the division of the province on several occasions, while the Punjab Assembly was entitled to support the creation of new provinces if it wished to do so.
She said senior ministers should avoid making such statements at a time when the issue required careful consideration.
Rehman said the PPP had its own position on the matter and that its response was in accordance with the Constitution. She said President Asif Ali Zardari had helped steer the country through difficult times.
She said the PPP took important matters to its Central Executive Committee (CEC), recalling that the party had spent a year working on the 18th Amendment to ensure that all stakeholders were taken along.
Whenever constitutional amendments were discussed, PPP Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari and the party were at the forefront of efforts to build consensus, she said.
Rehman added that the party first seeks agreement among its coalition partners before trying to bring the opposition on board.
The senator said the PPP’s role in parliament was evident and that a committee had been formed on Bilawal’s instructions.
She also called for committees on electoral reforms, saying the Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf had been invited to participate in the process.
Rehman said the PPP had not targeted anyone with criticism in parliament, adding that the party’s political red line remained firmly in place.
Khawaja Asif expects new provinces issue to take shape by New Year
Defence Minister Khawaja Asif on Sunday reiterated his expectation that the issue of creating new provinces would be resolved soon, saying the matter was unlikely to remain pending for much longer.
His remarks follow his earlier statement that consultations were progressing towards consensus and that the proposed new system would take shape by the New Year.
Speaking in an interview with a private television channel, Asif said a mutually acceptable solution would emerge soon and that the process would yield positive results.
He said the Constitution provided for a referendum and also laid down the procedure for constitutional amendments. A referendum could be held at the beginning of the new year, although he could not give a definitive timeline.
The defence minister added that any documents relating to the creation of new provinces would be prepared jointly, while contacts with other stakeholders on the referendum were continuing.
He said he wanted to see more provinces created across the country, although it was not yet clear how many there would be.
Asif also argued that the debate could have been avoided if the 18th Amendment had been implemented in its entirety, claiming that its spirit had been taken out.
The debate over new provinces has intensified in recent days, triggering an exchange of statements between leaders of the ruling PML-N and the PPP.
