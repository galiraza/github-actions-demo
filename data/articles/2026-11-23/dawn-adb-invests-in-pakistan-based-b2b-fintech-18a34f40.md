# ADB invests in Pakistan-based B2B fintech company Neem

ISLAMABAD: The Asian Development Bank (ADB) on Monday said it invested an undisclosed amount in Neem, a Pakistan-based B2B fintech company that helps businesses receive, manage, and pay out money digitally.
In a statement, the Manila-based lending agency said its investment will flow through ADB Ventures, the venture capital arm of the ADB.
The seed investment will support Neem’s product development, help expand its team, and accelerate the onboarding of enterprise and merchant clients across Pakistan, the ADB said.
Pakistan has made strong progress in digitising consumer payments, with the State Bank of Pakistan (SBP)’s instant payment system, Raast, and mobile wallets bringing millions of people onto digital rails. Business payments, however, have not kept pace.
According to SBP, an estimated 71 per cent of retail transaction value is still settled in cash, and companies looking to go digital must integrate separately with banks, wallets, payroll providers and reconciliation tools, a process that can be slow, costly, and hard to scale.
Neem aims to close this gap by giving businesses a single platform to manage how money comes in, how it is tracked, and how it is paid out. Its API-first system combines collections, disbursements, a programmable ledger and finance workflows, and connects directly with a company’s existing operating systems to automate settlement and reconciliation in real time.
Neem currently serves more than 70 enterprise clients across logistics, utilities, insurance, manufacturing and retail.
“Pakistan is laying a strong foundation for digital payments, and the next step is ensuring that businesses of all sizes can fully benefit from this progress,” said Emma Fan, ADB Country Director for Pakistan.
“Neem is helping enterprises move away from cash and fragmented systems toward payment solutions that are faster, more transparent and easier to manage.”
The impact extends well beyond the enterprises Neem serves directly. As businesses digitise their payments, the suppliers, small merchants and gig workers they pay are brought into the formal financial system.
Neem’s solutions are built to meet the payment needs of businesses of every size, including women-owned businesses and micro-entrepreneurs, so access to modern financial infrastructure is not limited to large enterprises.
As part of its lending portfolio, Neem also gives employees responsible access to wages they have already earned. The offering focuses on blue-collar workers in industries that employ large workforces across Pakistan and helps reduce reliance on informal borrowing.
ADB Ventures invests in early-stage technology companies building solutions that address the most pressing challenges across Asia and the Pacific, with a focus on sectors including financial technology, agricultural technology, climate resilience and inclusive growth.
