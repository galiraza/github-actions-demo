# Bilawal calls for restoring supremacy of Parliament and trust in judiciary, electoral system

- Published: Mon, 23 Nov 2026 20:26:34 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2046543/bilawal-calls-for-restoring-supremacy-of-parliament-and-trust-in-judiciary-electoral-system
- Collected: 2026-11-23 18:56:11 UTC

---

PPP Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari on Monday called for restoring the supremacy of Parliament as well as trust in the judiciary and the electoral system.
He made the remarks while addressing the inauguration ceremony to mark the launch of the Sindh Youth Card in Karachi.
“To restore the trust of Pakistan’s youth, if any reset is to take place, it must be a democratic reset,” the party leader said.
“If a democratic reset happens, then the people’s stake will remain, and through that reset, we must restore the supremacy of our Parliament. We must restore confidence in our judiciary, and the election system we have, we must restore trust in it now.”
Bilawal’s remarks come as the debate over a “system reset” in the country has intensified over the past few months. In July, Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi stirred a political storm by calling for the creation of new administrative units, arguing that the current system of governance had “collapsed” and needed a “reset”.
Last week, in a hard-hitting speech, the PPP chairman asserted that the governance system had not collapsed, but was rather “made to collapse”.
“Every child in this country hates corruption. The youth [of Pakistan] want to see a country where there is no corruption. They want it, and we also want to join together and build such a Pakistan,” he said on Monday.
According to Bilawal, to achieve that objective, two things needed to be done: the first was to use modern digital technology, whether it was the Youth Card or Kissan Card, to build systems that shut down avenues for corruption.
“Along with that, we have to build a system that functions without discrimination,” he added.
“Instead of taking revenge, [the system] works to fix our situation.”
Bilawal stressed that the restoration of trust would have to be a “joint effort” — to restore the opposition’s trust in the government and vice versa, and the public’s trust in the country’s system, its parliament, judiciary and media.
He said if such conditions were created, Pakistan could achieve unity for the first time, enabling it to confront economic hardships, terrorism and climate change-related disasters.
“I know that a Pakistan that is united, [will] have the ability to face any crisis, any disaster, any catastrophe.”
“At the same time, I fear that if we keep fighting among ourselves and remain divided further, and [spread hatred] in society instead of hope, then we will not have the capacity to face any crisis,” Bilawal added.
“Then whatever happens to Pakistan, whatever negative consequences occur, the youth will hold us accountable, asking, ‘You were there, what did you do to save us from this trouble?’ And at least I want when that question is asked, I can proudly, with my head held high, say that my intentions were good,” he said.
The Bhutto scion claimed he had poured his blood and sweat into resolving the nation’s issues.
“I made this effort so conditions would be created in this country enabling us to serve you,” he said.
“And if I succeed, then it will be for the benefit of Pakistanis, for the benefit of our country. Even if I fail, history will remember that a young man stood up and tried.”
At the outset of his speech, Bilawal said he had been a part of Pakistan’s politics for 20 years, during which he maintained ties with all political parties and politicians.
“Given the circumstances we face today, and the position we are in, if I myself am disillusioned, one can only imagine how the people of Pakistan, especially the youth, must feel,” he said.
The inauguration ceremony was attended by Chief Minister Murad Ali Shah, Sindh Sports Minister Sardar Mohammad Bux Khan Mahar, and other PPP leaders.
Speaking at the event, CM Murad said the Sindh Youth Card would establish an “effective means of direct engagement” with young people and create a database.
He said that the card had been initially launched for 100,000 young people, with the programme’s scope to be expanded over time.
“With Sindh’s youth population estimated at approximately 13 to 14 million, further measures are being taken, including youth development centres and public-private partnerships, to secure young people’s futures while providing them with better opportunities for growth and a supportive environment,” the CM said.
