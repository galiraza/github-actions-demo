# Govt moves to overhaul ‘weak’ audit system

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Mon, 23 Nov 2026 07:32:55 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2046399/govt-moves-to-overhaul-weak-audit-system
- Collected: 2026-11-23 08:33:01 UTC

---

• FBR task force flags weaknesses in existing review framework 
• Cabinet approves SECP Amendment Bill 2026 after consultation 
• Proposed legislation aims to strengthen independent audit oversight
ISLAMABAD: The government has decided to overhaul the country’s entire audit system after the Prime Minister’s Federal Board of Revenue (FBR) Task Force described the existing framework as weak, saying it discourages local and foreign investment and limits access to capital markets.
Documents seen by Dawn suggest the government plans to introduce legislative amendments in parliament to reform the existing audit system.
The proposed amendments will be introduced in the light of recommendations made by the Prime Minister’s FBR Task Force, which has already raised serious concerns over weaknesses in the existing audit framework, according to the document.
In this regard, the government will introduce legislation, including the Securities and Exchange Commission of Pakistan (SECP) (Amendment) Bill, 2026. A similar bill was passed by the National Assembly in 2021 after scrutiny and recommendations by the NA Standing Committee on Finance. However, it was withdrawn from the Senate in 2022 for resubmission. The federal cabinet has now approved the proposed bill again after extensive consultations and reviews by three ministerial-level committees.
The task force has also recommended amendments to the Audit Oversight Board (AOB) law to improve tax collection.
The process of reforming the AOB was already underway, but market-related concerns continued to highlight weaknesses in audits. Pakistan’s existing AOB law did not comply with international standards, an official briefing document said.
“Investors and lenders rely on audited accounts, but weak audits discourage local and foreign investment and access to capital markets.
“The International Organisation of Securities Commissions (IOSCO), the securities market standard-setter, declared Pakistan non-compliant with independent audit oversight. The European Union (EU) also withdrew Pakistan’s equivalence status for independent audit oversight,” it added.
The report cited Unity Foods and SE Foods as examples of companies affected by weak audits and prospectus disclosures. It also highlighted concerns over Hascol’s audited accounts.
The report said developed and developing countries had amended their laws and shifted from self-regulatory oversight by accountancy bodies to independent audit oversight. Even smaller jurisdictions, including Sri Lanka, Mauritius and Botswana, are compliant members of the International Forum of Independent Audit Regulators (IFIAR), it added.
It has been learnt that the proposed system will end the dominance of private auditors.
The document said the federal cabinet had approved the draft legislation following a comprehensive review by the FBR Task Force, IFIAR, SECP and AOB, along with extensive consultations with stakeholders. The proposed legislation is expected to strengthen Pakistan’s international recognition and establish an effective audit oversight system.
The salient features of the SECP Amendment Bill, 2026, are as follows:
The independence of the nominating committee responsible for appointing AOB board members will be strengthened by including the FBR chairman in place of the president of the Institute of Chartered Accountants of Pakistan (ICAP).
AOB board members will not be permitted to have any association with audit firms.
The AOB board will be responsible for policy matters, while management, operations and adjudication will be handled in line with governance principles and the board’s regulatory functions.
The AOB will conduct regular, risk-based inspections of auditors of Public Interest Companies (PICs) to assess compliance with professional auditing standards.
Accountancy bodies will continue to conduct regular inspections and Quality Control Reviews (QCRs) of non-PIC audits under the oversight of the AOB.
The AOB will directly investigate complaints and references from the SECP, State Bank of Pakistan (SBP) and FBR after approval by its board or a designated committee.
Standard action will be taken against individuals who refuse to provide required information, while the AOB will also be empowered to file complaints concerning criminal offences involving falsification.
