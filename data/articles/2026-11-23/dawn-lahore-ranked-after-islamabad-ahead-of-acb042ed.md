# Lahore ranked after Islamabad, ahead of major cities in safety perception index

LAHORE: Lahore has scored ahead of several major international cities, including New York, Paris and New Delhi but lower than Islamabad in the recent Numbeo’s 2026 Mid-Year Crime and Safety Index with safety and crime indexes at 63.8 and 36.2 respectively.
A report in this regard was presented to Punjab Inspector General of Police (IGP) Abdul Kareem during his visit to the Lahore Police Lines, Qilla Gujjar Singh here on Sunday, where he held a high-level meeting on the crime situation in the city.
Capital City Police Officer (CCPO) Bilal Siddique Kamyana, DIGs Faisal Kamran, Zeshan Raza, all the SSPs, SPs and other senior police officers were present. A senior official told this reporter that the meeting discussed various other key issues including criteria of posting the station house officers (SHOs), deteriorating response time of police on helpline 15 calls, shortage of Dolphin Force bikes and mishandling of some cases, such as a police raid at the residence of Attorney General for Pakistan (AGP) Mansoor Usman Awan.
During the session, he said, the DIG operations presented Numbeo’s report which has placed Lahore in the “Low” to “Moderate” crime range based on user-submitted perception surveys.
“Lahore scores lower on crime than Karachi (Crime Index 57.8–57.9) and Rawalpindi (51.8), while Islamabad scores lower on crime (30.8–31.1)”, said Mr Kamran while quoting the report. He added that the provincial capital also scored lower on crime than many major Western cities.
“These figures are based on visitors’ perception on this website,” he said, adding that a total of 380 cities of the world were included in the surveys.

The CCPO told the meeting that Lahore was the only city in Punjab without any no-go area, major land-grabbing mafia, gangsters or hooligans as the police had eradicated them to bring peace in the city. He told the meeting that the crime situation of the city had improved significantly during the last four years.During the meeting, the official said, the IGP expressed displeasure over the deteriorating response time of the police on 15 calls, saying that it had dropped to 22pc across the province. He declared it a matter of serious concern and said that as per reports, the callers were being asked to reach police stations to file their reports.
The official said that DIG operations clarified that the response time of the police in Lahore was recorded 66pc. However, Abdul Kareem declared the response time of the Lahore police at 15 ‘still insufficient’ as Lahore police possessed significantly more resources than those in other cities.
The official said that Mr Kamran cited shortage of bikes for Dolphin Force as a major reason for low response time. He further elaborated that the Dolphin Force were patrolling on bikes of various police stations as a makeshift arrangement.
Mr Kareem revealed that Punjab Chief Minister Maryam Nawaz had approved procurement of new bikes for the provincial police at a cost of Rs300 millions with Lahore Police getting as many as 300-400 bikes by December.
According to the insider, the IGP said that inspectors were complaining that they were not being considered for SHO posts in the provincial capital. He said that they were worried that a majority of junior officials, sub-inspectors, were serving as SHOs.
In response to these complaints, the DIG operations said that two-thirds of SHOs were sub-inspectors and it was meant to produce a pool of trained officials for sensitive jobs.
About the complaints of missing women, the DIG investigation told the IGP that out of thousands of such cases, less than 100 were yet to be traced.
