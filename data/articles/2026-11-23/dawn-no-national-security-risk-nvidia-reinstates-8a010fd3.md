# 'No national security risk': Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO after affair scandal

- Published: Mon, 23 Nov 2026 21:55:16 +0500

- Collected: 2026-11-23 18:56:11 UTC

Nvidia on Monday reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive after input from senior US government officials left its board confident he posed no national security risk, less than two weeks after the co-founder resigned due to an alleged spy scandal.
The board voted unanimously to bring Huang back as president, chief executive and board member, effective immediately, after a series of meetings left directors satisfied with his “candour and ability to lead,” the company said in a statement.
Nvidia did not name the government officials it consulted or say what they told the board.
Nvidia shares surged on the news, jumping more than 25 per cent in premarket trading.
The decision came three days after US President Donald Trump publicly urged Nvidia to take Huang back, calling him “one of the truly most brilliant minds in the world” and saying he would “love to see Jensen be given a second chance”.
Trump described Huang as “a good man” who “made some bad decisions,” and said the company should rehire him “if they were smart.” Huang had offered to return, but the board initially refused.
Board chair Harvey Jones had said on an earnings call last week that directors had unanimously accepted his resignation and did “not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO”.
“I take this responsibility with the greatest humility and renewed commitment,” Huang said, thanking the board, staff, shareholders and his family.
“I can’t wait to get back to work.”
Huang, 63, stepped down on Nov 12 following reports that he had a relationship with Alice Huang — no relation — a former executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed, a company that bought billions of dollars worth of Nvidia.
US intelligence services reportedly concluded she passed information to Beijing, which has denied the allegations.
Board chair Harvey Jones said Monday that directors now believe Huang “is the right person to lead Nvidia” and that his return serves the interests of shareholders, employees, partners and customers.
Jones, a former Synopsys chief executive who was named Nvidia’s first-ever board chair during the crisis, will stay in the role. Chief financial officer Colette Kress, who served as interim CEO, will continue as CFO.
Nvidia said a committee of independent directors set up earlier this month to investigate whether Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort will see its probe through to the end. Huang supports the investigation and has committed to cooperating, the company said.
Trump, who had previously called on the AI giant to reinstate Huang, congratulated him in a post on Truth Social.
“The Nvidia board of directors has made the correct and proper decision to return Jensen Huang to the position of CEO. The country needs Jensen’s leadership at Nvidia in order to ensure the success and prosperity of not only the company, but the American economy,” he wrote.
The US president clarified that the US government had “no reason” to believe that Huang’s reinstatement posed risks to national security, adding that the US attorney general had confirmed that the government did not possess evidence against Huang.
Trump further said that he had gotten to know Huang, describing him as a “great patriot and national treasure”. Trump directed that Nvidia should continue its internal investigation into compliance with US export controls.
Trump told CNBC on Monday that Huang “didn’t know about” advanced Nvidia chips going to China.
The US president described him as a “genius” and a “brilliant guy”, adding: “Losing the brainpower of somebody like Jensen — to me, that would be a far bigger threat than anything we’re talking about.“
“As far as the woman is concerned, every time you have a Chinese woman that’s good-looking or beautiful, they say she’s a spy. Now with Swalwell, she probably was a spy — but with Jensen, I don’t believe that’s true,” Trump said.
Jack Gold of J. Gold Associates called the reinstatement “a big mistake”, noting that the investigation into whether Huang was compromised by the alleged spy was still under way.
“This action further amplifies the notion that big ‘tech bros’ don’t need to play by the rules like the rest of us do,” Gold said, adding that the board’s decision appeared “driven more by financial influences than ethical practices”.
US Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer vowed to investigate potential wrongdoing surrounding Huang’s reinstatement, accusing Trump of putting personal financial interests ahead of national security.
“Democrats will wield their full power to investigate any wrongdoing by the Trump Administration, Jensen Huang, other Nvidia executives, and anyone selling out our national security for financial gain,” said the veteran New Yorker, who is seen as likely to remain in his position when Democrats take control of Congress in January.
Alice Huang had contact with China spy agency: NYT
Alice Huang, the alleged spy at the centre of the Nvidia scandal, was in touch with an official from China’s top spy agency during an affair with the chip giant’s former chief executive, the New York Times reported on Sunday.
She was in contact with an officer of China’s Ministry of State Security from the spring of 2025 until the fall of this year, the Times reported, citing people with knowledge of the intelligence.
According to the Times, Alice Huang’s contacts with the Chinese spy agency correspond to the affair as well as a period when Megaspeed was acquiring large amounts of advanced Nvidia chips.
The Times also reported that US intelligence agencies have gathered evidence that Alice Huang’s business partner Kang Wei — who the newspaper said was often introduced as her husband — worked with the same Chinese spy agency.
He is to be questioned by US lawmakers on December 1 over national security concerns.
