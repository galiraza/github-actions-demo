# Peshawar to host opening ceremony, inaugural match for 12th edition of PSL: PCB chief

- Published: Mon, 23 Nov 2026 20:58:54 +0500

PESHAWAR: Pakistan Cricket Board (PCB) Chairman Mohsin Naqvi said on Monday that Peshawar will host the opening ceremony and the inaugural match for the 12th edition of the HBL Pakistan Super League (PSL) scheduled for next year.
Naqvi made the announcement while addressing a press conference alongside Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Governor Faisal Karim Kundi in Peshawar.
“The biggest announcement today, for which I congratulate both the governor and Peshawar Zalmi, is that after 12 years, PSL will hold its opening ceremony and first match in Peshawar,” the PCB chief said.
He added that multiple matches will be held in the provincial capital, with the schedule to be finalised later by PSL.
The 12th edition of the cricket tournament will be held from March 18 to May 2, 2027.
The announcement by Naqvi came shortly after Kundi — whose administration now runs the province under governor’s rule — said the name of Arbab Niaz Stadium would be restored, reversing the PTI government’s decision to rename it to Imran Khan Stadium.
In its press release, PCB stated: “The announcement marks a defining moment for cricket in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and for the thousands of supporters who have followed the league, and their team, from afar since the very first season in 2016.”
The board noted that fans in Peshawar “have filled stands, packed streets and carried their colours across the country for more than a decade, without once watching an HBL PSL match in their own city”.
“That changes with HBL PSL 12. Peshawar’s supporters will finally experience the league’s electric atmosphere at home, with the world’s best T20 talent on show in front of a crowd that has earned its moment,” the statement declared.
“This is a moment the league, and above all the fans of Peshawar, have been looking forward to for 12 years,” PSL Chief Executive Officer Salman Naseer was quoted as saying.
“Peshawar’s love for cricket is unmatched, and HBL PSL 12 will finally give that passion the stage it deserves. Adding a sixth venue is a major step in the growth of the league, and we look forward to delivering a memorable experience for every fan who walks into the stadium.”
Speaking to the media, the PCB chairman noted that Peshawar Zalmi and its owner Javed Afridi had also been wishing for the tournament to come to the city.
Naqvi, also the interior minister, stated that matters related to law and order in the province were “improving very rapidly”.
He recalled that Governor Kundi would call for “reviving cricket in KP” whenever the two met, even before the imposition of governor’s rule and emergency in the province on Oct 11.
“This province has produced amazing stars for the cricket team, and I strongly believe that with some promotion and streamlining, […] along with joint efforts, we will see positive results from here in months, not years,” the PCB chief said.
He also announced holding a week-long cricket tournament between the U-19 team, Pakistan Shaheens and the national team.
Peshawar was supposed to host a PSL match on March 28 in the previous edition, but matches were restricted to Karachi and Lahore as part of austerity measures after the US-Iran war broke out.
Asked about that, Naqvi maintained that “best efforts” were made so a match could be staged in Peshawar, but it could not happen due to “purely administrative issues and some concerns”.
He clarified there was no “political reason” for not holding the match, adding that Peshawar was an option for one of the venues but it was not announced as such.
Responding to a query, Naqvi said that there were plans underway to build an academy for tribal districts, with the specifics of its location and construction timeline to be finalised in the next two weeks.
“Those districts are also very important for us,” he affirmed. The merged districts are among the 12 districts where special emergency measures were imposed on Oct 11 as the areas were deemed to face intense security challenges.
Speaking on district-level cricket grounds, Naqvi said the PCB planned to work in collaboration with the government to maintain grounds at “whatever level possible”.
‘Preserving Peshawar’s history’: Kundi on reversing stadium name change
Announcing his administration’s decision to restore Arbab Niaz Stadium’s name, Kundi said on X: “In 13 years, despite KP’s immense sporting talent, the PTI could not build a single new cricket stadium, even though the Pakistan cricket team boasts a significant number of Pathan players. Yet, they were quick to rename the one we already had.”
He contended that the decision was about “preserving Peshawar’s history, respecting its heritage and honouring those who have genuinely contributed to the province’s sporting legacy through their work and achievements”.
The stadium was named after Arbab Niaz, a former federal minister for sports. In Feb 2025, the PTI government in KP, led by then-chief minister Ali Amin Gandapur, renamed it after PTI founder and ex-premier Imran Khan.
Speaking on the matter during Monday’s press conference, Kundi termed the renaming of the stadium as a “huge injustice to the people of Peshawar”.
The governor highlighted that Niaz had the cricket ground built in the 1980s, stating that putting one’s plaques on past projects completed by others was inappropriate.
“If they had built it themselves and named it after him, that would have been fine,” the PPP leader said, adding that the name would be restored within a few days.
Speaking about provincial matters, Kundi reaffirmed that countering terrorism remained the top priority.
He stated that 70 per cent of a Rs160 billion package announced by the federal government will be allotted to the 12 designated districts, while the remaining 30 per cent will go to the rest of KP.
He also highlighted his administration’s crackdown on encroachments and illegal placer gold mining, and a cleanliness campaign.
