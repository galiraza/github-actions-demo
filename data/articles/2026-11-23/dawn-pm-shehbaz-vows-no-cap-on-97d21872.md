# PM Shehbaz vows no cap on homes for poor, sets Rs1,000bn disbursement target under Apna Ghar scheme

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Mon, 23 Nov 2026 21:15:34 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2046567/pm-shehbaz-vows-no-cap-on-homes-for-poor-sets-rs1000bn-disbursement-target-under-apna-ghar-scheme
- Collected: 2026-11-23 18:56:11 UTC

---

Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif on Monday set a target of Rs1,000 billion in disbursements under the Prime Minister Apna Ghar Programme over the next six months.
He declared that there was no limit on the number of homes the government would build for the poor, vowing to construct millions if needed.
Addressing a ceremony held to mark the milestone of over Rs100bn in loans reaching citizens under the scheme, PM Shehbaz said it was a matter of deep concern that while a few enjoy every comfort, a vast number of people are deprived of shelter, education, healthcare and dignified employment.
This, he said, was not the Pakistan envisioned by Quaid-i-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah.
The premier said that housing schemes in the past had mostly served the elite, who did not need bank loans, while the real need was among labourers, widows, orphans and low-income families across Pakistan, including Azad Jammu and Kashmir and Gilgit-Baltistan.
“If the state cannot provide even a roof to such families, it has no right to rule.”
The prime minister recalled that the programme was launched in April 2026.
“In about six and a half months, banks have disbursed Rs112bn against loan sanctions of about Rs500 billion,” PM Shehbaz said, calling this a good start. However, he maintained that the journey ahead is long and must be covered at “lightning speed”.
He directed the finance and housing ministers, the State Bank of Pakistan (SBP) governor, and banks to aim for Rs1,000bn in disbursements in the next six months.
“Any hurdles and legal impediments will be removed jointly, without exposing banks to undue risk,” he assured the banks.
The premier noted that banks had initially been reluctant to enter this segment because of comfortable returns on treasury bills and government deposits.
He praised those that came forward and said he was told the scheme was free of corruption and nepotism.
The ceremony also featured the presentation of awards to the top five high-performing banks: United Bank Limited Deputy CEO Abdul Aleem Qureshi, Habib Bank Limited President Muhammad Nassir Salim, Bank Alfalah President Atif Bajwa, Meezan Bank Retail Acting CEO/COO Ziaul Hasan, and Mobilink Microfinance Bank Chief Business Officer Attaur Rehman.
Announcing recognition for the top five high-performing banks, he said its leaders would be given blue passports, with a notification to be issued the same day.
He said it would be for each bank’s owners or CEOs to decide who would hold them. He added that the recognition also carries responsibility, as any delay in genuine loan approvals would now be attributed to them.
“An unencumbered and clear title property is the main requirement for a loan, and that other documentation should be handled by banks by helping applicants rather than turning them away,” the prime minister stated.
He said some banks had claimed to be unaware of the scheme, which he described as unacceptable, and urged them to set up dedicated cells.
Responding to the suggestions of the Association of Builders and Developers (Abad) chairman, PM Shehbaz said he welcomed a proposed tripartite coordination involving Abad, the SBP governor, the builders and banks, and directed the finance minister to hold a meeting with the housing minister and housing secretary.
“We want results,” he said, adding he was ready to support the effort if it truly helped build homes.
He thanked Housing and Works Minister Riaz Hussain Pirzada, Finance Minister Muhammad Aurangzeb, Economic Affairs Minister Ahad Khan Cheema, and SBP Governor Jameel Ahmad for their efforts, and appreciated Information Minister Attaullah Tarar for the publicity.
Abad Chairman Hanif Kohari appreciated the prime minister for turning the housing initiative into a practical programme, and said it had created opportunities for the construction sector.
“Abad, representing about 1,500 members nationwide, is working with the government and banks,” Kohari said. He expressed confidence that the target of 500,000 homes would be met through continued cooperation.
The programme beneficiaries also shared their experiences, including Musarrat Qamar (AJK), Dr Asad Ali (Quetta), Anam Akhtar (Rawalpindi), Jameela Bibi (Rahim Yar Khan), and Karachi couple Babar Zaib and Tayyaba Qazi, along with others from Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. They said the financing helped them complete or buy homes after years in rented accommodation.
