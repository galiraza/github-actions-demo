# Rana Sanaullah hits back at Naqvi, says he mishandled debate on new provinces

Published November 23, 2026
Adviser to the Prime Minister on Political Affairs Rana Sanaullah has hit back at Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi, accusing him of mishandling the debate over the creation of new provinces and turning it into a controversy.
Speaking on Geo News programme "Aaj Shahzeb Khanzada Kay Saath" on Monday, Sanaullah said Naqvi should acknowledge his failure to handle the matter instead of reacting angrily to criticism.
Responding to Naqvi's recent warning to an "unnamed minister", Sanaullah said he was not someone who could be intimidated by threats.
He added that he would not have to hide his face, responding to Naqvi’s remark that the minister he was referring to would be unable to show his face if he responded publicly.
Sanaullah said that Naqvi complicated the matter through irrelevant statements, saying it would now reach parliament as a controversial issue.
Naqvi had earlier issued a stern warning to an unnamed minister, saying he had patiently listened to the latter’s criticism for the past two-and-a-half years.
“The day I comment on this minister, he will not be able to show his face,” he had said while speaking to Geo News.
The interior minister also defended businessmen who had been criticised by the minister, describing them as respectable citizens and among the country's highest taxpayers.
He questioned why political parties accepted billions of rupees in party funds from them if they considered them problematic.
The debate over the creation of new provinces has intensified in recent days, with political leaders from the ruling coalition expressing differing views on the proposal.
The issue has also triggered an exchange of statements between PML-N and Pakistan Peoples Party (PPP) leaders, with discussions focusing on the need for political consensus and the constitutional process for creating new provinces.
Meanwhile, Sanaullah recalled that when Naqvi first raised the matter on July 30, he had immediately said that parliament was the appropriate forum for discussion.
"If the matter had been brought to parliament on the first day, none of these complications would have arisen," he said.
He also questioned why President Asif Ali Zardari and PPP Chairman Bilawal Bhutto Zardari were saying that nobody had consulted them on the issue despite four months of discussions.
"If President Zardari and Bilawal Bhutto are saying that nobody spoke to them about this matter, then what were you doing during those four months?" he asked.
According to Sanaullah, the interior minister was leading the discussion and should accept responsibility for the current situation.
However, he expressed optimism that a way forward on the referendum could still be achieved through dialogue.
He suggested that the government should hold talks with the PPP in parliament and provide assurances that the Constitution would be upheld in the matter.
