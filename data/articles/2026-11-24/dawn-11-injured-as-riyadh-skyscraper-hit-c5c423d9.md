# 11 injured as Riyadh skyscraper hit second time in less than a week

Riyadh’s tallest skyscraper was struck for the second time in days on Tuesday, injuring 11 people, as the coalition fighting Houthis denounced a “terrorist attack”.
The building, which was also attacked last Thursday, is one of several high-profile targets hit since hostilities with the Houthis restarted in July.
Saudi Arabia’s Civil Defence reported Tuesday’s injuries and posted a picture of the landmark tower housing the headquarters of the Saudi Public Investment Fund (PIF).
The image showed some damage to the building about halfway up the facade, similar to the impact from a strike on the same tower on Nov 19, which caused damage but no injuries.
Reuters could not immediately determine when the image was taken or the extent of the further damage from the latest strike.
Saudi Civil Defence said the 11 people injured included two Saudi citizens, four Egyptian nationals, three Nepalese nationals and two Indian nationals.
“Relevant authorities responded to the incident immediately, implementing all established protocols and measures for handling such situations, and taking the necessary steps to ensure the safety of lives and property,” the Arabic statement said.
“One of the towers in the financial centre was targeted in a cowardly terrorist attack,” coalition spokesperson Turki al-Malki said in a social media post, without naming the building.
Standing 385 metres high, the PIF tower is the most prominent landmark of King Abdullah Financial District (KAFD), a showcase for Saudi Arabia’s economic ambitions.
A witness told AFP that a vehicle fitted with a speaker had warned pedestrians in the area to take cover on Tuesday. No warning was sent to mobile phones about any threats to the capital.
Afterwards, police cars blocked some entrances to the gleaming financial district. With imposing skyscrapers, luxury shops and green spaces, the district also hosts the headquarters of the Saudi stock exchange along with multinationals such as Goldman Sachs.
An attack on the PIF is symbolic as the fund is driving efforts to diversify the oil-reliant Saudi economy — a project that has been complicated by the Middle East war and Houthi strikes.
A tower belonging to state oil giant Aramco in KAFD was hit earlier this month, while Riyadh’s international airport was repeatedly struck in October, with one attack killing 14 people.
The Houthis, who control much of western Yemen including the capital Sanaa, have been firing at Saudi airports, oil infrastructure and office buildings since Yemen’s frozen civil war reignited in July in a knock-on from the US-Iran war.
The attacks have caused the cancellation of most international flights to Riyadh, and forced Saudi Arabia to postpone or cancel major international events, including soccer’s AFC Asian Cup.
Islamabad has condemned Houthi attacks repeatedly, including on international platforms, and termed them a grave violation of the kingdom’s sovereignty.
Pakistan and Riyadh have signed two defence pacts aimed at collective deterrence, the Strategic Mutual Defence Agreement between the two and the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement that also includes Turkiye.
Under the Makkah Defence Alliance, Pakistani, Saudi and Turkish leaders met in Riyadh in October and agreed to deploy military forces and capabilities rapidly in the kingdom.
Pakistan is providing “whatever support is needed” to defend Saudi Arabia amid the Houthi attacks, the military’s spokesperson Lieutenant General Ahmed Sharif Chaudhry said later that month.
‘26 killed in Yemen attack’
On the ground in Yemen, the war has caused a major humanitarian crisis, with hundreds of thousands of people displaced from their homes since July.
The Houthis have seized swathes of territory, forcing forces aligned with the internationally recognised government to stage a major retreat this month.
The Saudi-led military coalition has been striking Houthi targets in the impoverished country.
On Tuesday, local officials said a coalition airstrike on buses and trucks killed 26 people in Lahj province. On the other hand, government forces blamed the Houthis for the attack.
As the conflict shows no sign of slowing, Britain sanctioned the Houthis and four of its top officials “for deliberately attempting to undermine stability” on Tuesday.
The measures include an asset freeze against the group and an asset freeze and travel ban on four figures, including security and intelligence head Abdul Hakim Hashem Ali al-Khaiwani, and interior affairs official Abdulkarim Amir al-Din Hussein al-Houthi.
The Saudi-led coalition intervened in Yemen in early 2015, after the Houthis had captured the capital Sanaa and sent the internationally recognised government fleeing to Aden.
The war left hundreds of thousands dead and triggered a humanitarian catastrophe before an uneasy ceasefire went into effect in 2022.
