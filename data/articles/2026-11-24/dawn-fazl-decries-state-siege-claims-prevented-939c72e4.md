# Fazl decries ‘state siege’, claims prevented from attending Lahore event

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Tue, 24 Nov 2026 21:50:13 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2046756/fazl-decries-state-siege-claims-prevented-from-attending-lahore-event
- Collected: 2026-11-24 18:35:42 UTC

---

LAHORE: Police began removing barriers around Maulana Fazlur Rehman’s place of stay in Lahore on Tuesday evening, with the Jamiat Ulema-i-Islam-Fazl (JUI-F) chief claiming he had been put under an hours-long “state siege” to restrict his movement.
In a post at 10:40am, JUI-F said Fazl reached Lahore on Monday night and was scheduled to address lawyers today at a seminar at the invitation of the Lahore High Court Bar Association (LHCBA). However, due to police deployment, he was restricted from leaving his accommodation, JUI-F said.
JUI-F spokesperson Hafiz Ghazanfar Aziz said police began removing the hurdles by 7pm and informed Fazl that he was free to leave. However, the senior politician decided to stay in the city for the night.
Aziz condemned the alleged government action, calling it an attack on civil liberties and constitutional rights.
A city police officer, who spoke on condition of anonymity, also confirmed that police began “removing the barriers” by evening.
Meanwhile, a plea challenging the alleged “siege” of Fazl’s place of stay was filed in the Lahore High Court (LHC). On the other hand, the district administration also placed a three-day ban on public gatherings and “unlawful assemblies”.
In an earlier video message, the JUI-F chief alleged that he was under a “state siege” and that roadblocks and checkpoints across Lahore were preventing him from attending a multi-party seminar organised by the LHCBA.
Fazl thanked the bar association, its president and members for inviting him to address the seminar, but regretted that “state-imposed restrictions” had prevented him from attending in person.
He said the entire city had been “choked” by roadblocks and checkpoints, adding that peaceful citizens were being stopped from entering an environment of “intellectual discussion”.
“God willing, these sieges will break, these obstacles will be removed. Oppression never achieves permanence, but patience has always enjoyed permanence throughout history,” he said.
Fazl said his party had always worked for peace and had put its workers’ lives at risk for the country. He added that JUI-F did not need “a certificate of patriotism” from the rulers or any state institution.
“Neither from the rulers nor from any state institution have we come to obtain a certificate of loyalty to the homeland. In fact, I believe that perhaps we should be the ones issuing this certificate to people now. Their loyalties are questionable, whereas our loyalties are above all suspicion,” he said.
Claiming arrests of party workers and leaders in different areas, the JUI-F chief said his party would go ahead with its planned public conference in Sargodha on Nov 26.
He alleged that businesses were being shut down, shops and flour mills sealed, and party office-bearers arrested in Bhakkar, Mianwali, Khushab, Sargodha and its tehsils.
The JUI-F chief urged the public to make the gathering successful and demonstrate their commitment to freedom, constitutional supremacy, the rule of law and democracy.
“We are fighting the case of the public. We are fighting the case of Pakistan. We are fighting the case of this state,” he said.
Fazl alleged that the state had intensified its actions against JUI-F after the party exposed its role in matters concerning mineral resources in tribal areas.
He said his party was fighting for the rights of people across all provinces, including Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Balochistan, Sindh and Punjab, as well as Gwadar.
“These are the property of the people, not the property of the federation, nor the property of any single powerful institution, and we will not allow it to be looted by anyone,” he said.
Fazl said his party would remain steadfast in its position and take the matter to its “logical conclusion”. He reiterated his gratitude to the LHCBA and regretted that restrictions had prevented him from addressing its members directly.
Opposition leaders Mehmood Khan Achakzai and Allama Raja Nasir Abbas, PTI MNA Asad Qaiser, and veteran lawyer Ali Ahmed Kurd were among the participants of the LHCBA gathering, videos showed.
Plea filed in LHC against alleged ‘siege’
The plea against the alleged “siege” of Fazl’s accommodation was filed before the LHC by JUI-F lawyer Advocate Abrar Hussain.
It has yet to be fixed.
In his petition, the lawyer alleged that around 3,000 police personnel had been deployed outside Fazl’s residence, effectively confining him to his house.
The petitioner said he had sought a copy of the legal order authorising the detention or confinement of Fazl by police officials, but authorities did not share any such document.
He further alleged that police had also denied him permission to meet the JUI-F chief.
The petitioner requested the court to direct the police to lift the “siege” of Fazl’s residence.
In a statement, the party’s spokesperson warned that arrests of JUI-F workers in different cities on Monday, followed by the alleged deployment of a heavy police contingent outside the party chief’s residence, would have political consequences for the government.
He said arrests, roadblocks, the sealing of businesses and the registration of FIRs could not deter the party from its struggle. The JUI-F, he added, would continue its efforts for the supremacy of the Constitution and the protection of people’s rights.
Section 144 imposed in Lahore
Separately, Section 144 was imposed in Lahore for three days, from Nov 24 to 26, according to an order issued by the deputy commissioner, a copy of which is available with Dawn.
The order cited reports from law enforcement and intelligence agencies about a “serious and imminent threat” to public peace, security and the safety of life and property across Punjab, including Lahore, from militant and terrorist organisations and hostile intelligence agencies.
It said assemblies, rallies, gatherings, sit-ins, processions, demonstrations and public meetings could become targets for terrorist attacks, adding that the possibility of an untoward incident could not be ruled out.
The order also expressed apprehension that certain elements could engage in activities likely to lead to unlawful gatherings, disturb public order and pose security risks, potentially resulting in the loss of life and property or damage to critical infrastructure.
Under the restrictions, a complete ban has been imposed on the assembly of five or more people, processions and gatherings in any public place, street, road, highway, motorway or open space. The order also prohibits inciting people to participate in unlawful assemblies.
However, the restrictions will not apply to marriage ceremonies, funeral prayers, burial gatherings, prayers in mosques and churches, official duties of government and semi-government organisations, and courts of law, the order stated.
Fazl-govt rift
Tuesday’s developments came amid tensions between Fazl and the federal government, which have been ongoing since mid-October.
Fazl had criticised the Centre for imposing governor’s rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and making the then-PTI provincial government non-functional. The JUI-F chief also accused the Centre of reneging on its commitments despite his efforts to mediate between the PML-N and PTI governments.
Tensions escalated when Fazl accused the Centre of depriving the provinces of their rights, resulting in the veteran politician and key federal ministers exchanging harsh remarks against each other.
In recent weeks, PPP Chairman Bilawal-Bhutto Zardari — a key ally of the ruling PML-N — has voiced his support for Fazl and condemned the “government’s actions” against him.
Last week, police and Federal Constabulary (FC) personnel deployed to protect Fazl and other members of his family were withdrawn from the residences they guarded in Islamabad and Dera Ismail Khan.
JUI-F spokesman Aslam Ghouri had confirmed the withdrawal, alleging that the Maulana’s Islamabad residence had been cordoned off by capital police and that security personnel had harassed visitors. He also claimed that the party’s Central Secretariat in Islamabad had been besieged by police.
An Islamabad police official, however, said only one police guard had been withdrawn.
The JUI-F chief’s son has also come under scrutiny from the anti-graft watchdog, with the National Accountability Bureau (NAB) summoning former federal communications minister Asad Mahmood over alleged irregularities in 4,252 appointments at Pakistan Post during his 2022-23 tenure. Mahmood skipped two consecutive summonses issued for Nov 10 and 14, according to a NAB source.
The JUI-F staged protests in parts of Sindh and Balochistan on Nov 7 against a “coordinated character assassination campaign” targeting Fazl.
Party leaders and workers condemned the alleged drone surveillance of his residence and the circulation of footage on social media, demanding an impartial investigation and action against those responsible.
