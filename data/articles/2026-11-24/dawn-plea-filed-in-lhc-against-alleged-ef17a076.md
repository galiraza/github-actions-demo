# Plea filed in LHC against alleged 'siege' of Fazl's residence in Lahore

LAHORE: A plea challenging the alleged “siege” of Jamiat Ulema-i-Islam-Fazl (JUI-F) chief Maulana Fazlur Rehman’s residence in Punjab’s provincial capital was filed in the Lahore High Court on Tuesday.
The petition, filed by JUI-F lawyer Advocate Abrar Hussain, has yet to be fixed.
In his petition, the lawyer alleged that around 3,000 police personnel had been deployed outside Fazl’s residence, effectively confining him to his house.
The petitioner said he had sought a copy of the legal order authorising the detention or confinement of Fazl by police officials, but authorities did not share any such document.
He further alleged that police had also denied him permission to meet the JUI-F chief.
The petitioner requested the court to direct the police to lift the “siege” of Fazl’s residence.
Separately, the JUI-F posted on X that Fazl had reached Lahore on Monday night. The party said he was scheduled to address lawyers today at the invitation of the Lahore High Court Bar Association. But personnel had been deployed outside his house and he had been restricted from leaving his residence, the party alleged.
The plea by the JUI-F lawyer has been filed amid tensions between Fazl and the federal government.
Tensions between the JUI-F chief and the federal government have escalated since mid-October, particularly after Fazl criticised the Centre for imposing governor’s rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and making the then-PTI provincial government non-functional. The JUI-F chief also accused the Centre of reneging on its commitments despite his efforts to mediate between the PML-N and PTI governments.
Last week, police and Federal Constabulary (FC) personnel deployed to protect Fazl and other members of his family were withdrawn from the residences they guarded in Islamabad and Dera Ismail Khan.
JUI-F spokesman Aslam Ghouri had confirmed the withdrawal, alleging that the Maulana’s Islamabad residence had been cordoned off by capital police and that security personnel had harassed visitors. He also claimed that the party’s Central Secretariat in Islamabad had been besieged by police.
An Islamabad police official, however, said only one police guard had been withdrawn.
The JUI-F chief’s son has also come under scrutiny from the anti-graft watchdog, with the National Accountability Bureau (NAB) summoning former federal communications minister Asad Mahmood over alleged irregularities in 4,252 appointments at Pakistan Post during his 2022-23 tenure. Mahmood skipped two consecutive summonses issued for Nov 10 and 14, according to a NAB source.
The JUI-F staged protests in parts of Sindh and Balochistan on Nov 7 against a “coordinated character assassination campaign” targeting Fazl.
Party leaders and workers condemned the alleged drone surveillance of his residence and the circulation of footage on social media, demanding an impartial investigation and action against those responsible.
Section 144 imposed in Lahore
Meanwhile, Section 144 was imposed in Lahore for three days, from Nov 24 to 26, according to an order issued by the deputy commissioner, a copy of which is available with Dawn.
The order cited reports from law enforcement and intelligence agencies about a “serious and imminent threat” to public peace, security and the safety of life and property across Punjab, including Lahore, from militant and terrorist organisations and hostile intelligence agencies.
It said assemblies, rallies, gatherings, sit-ins, processions, demonstrations and public meetings could become targets for terrorist attacks, adding that the possibility of an untoward incident could not be ruled out.
The order also expressed apprehension that certain elements could engage in activities likely to lead to unlawful gatherings, disturb public order and pose security risks, potentially resulting in the loss of life and property or damage to critical infrastructure.
Under the restrictions, a complete ban has been imposed on the assembly of five or more people, processions and gatherings in any public place, street, road, highway, motorway or open space. The order also prohibits inciting people to participate in unlawful assemblies.
However, the restrictions will not apply to marriage ceremonies, funeral prayers, burial gatherings, prayers in mosques and churches, official duties of government and semi-government organisations, and courts of law, the order stated.
