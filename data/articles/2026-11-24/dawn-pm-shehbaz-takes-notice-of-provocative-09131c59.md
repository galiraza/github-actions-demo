# PM Shehbaz takes notice of provocative statements by cabinet members, urges them to settle differences amicably

ISLAMABAD: Amid rising political tensions, Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif on Tuesday took notice of provocative statements by the federal cabinet members against each other and directed them to resolve their differences through consultation.
The remarks come amid a war of words between coalition partners over the past few days regarding a proposed referendum on the creation of new provinces, with a federal cabinet member publicly expressing his grievances against a colleague.
On Monday, PML-N Senator Rana Sanaullah said Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi had “complicated” the matter of creating new provinces by making an “irrelevant speech at an irrelevant forum”.
In his remarks, Sanaullah, who also holds the portfolio of inter-provincial coordination, stood by his earlier criticism of the interior minister’s choice to start the debate on provinces while addressing the Pakistan Economic Summit in Islamabad in July.
Naqvi had whipped up a political storm by calling for creating new administrative units in the country to “reset” the current “system” of governance. Following Sanaullah’s earlier remarks, Naqvi, without naming Sanaullah had warned last week, “The day I comment on this minister, he will not be able to show his face.”
In a statement issued by the PM Office on Tuesday, the prime minister took notice of recent statements by ministers and said the federal government’s entire focus was on public welfare projects.
“The government is continuing its journey from economic stability towards sustainable economic growth,” the prime minister said.
The prime minister said the federal government has always promoted the politics of development and progress.
“On this occasion, I direct all federal cabinet ministers to refrain from making political statements against one another,” the prime minister added.
“Matters among federal cabinet members should be resolved through mutual understanding and consensus,” the prime minister said.
He said statements by federal ministers against one another create a negative impression and divert attention from the politics of development and progress.
PM Shehbaz said federal cabinet ministers were performing well and should remain focused on public service, development and progress.
He said representatives of provincial governments should also refrain from making such statements and promote constructive dialogue.
“Statements against one another must cease completely. It is our collective responsibility to play an effective role in ensuring political stability,” the premier said.
Speaking to Geo News on Monday, Sanaullah had said it was his right as a colleague and as a political worker to express his opinion on Naqvi’s work.
The PM’s adviser contended that his colleague’s remarks at the business forum “complicated this issue, and today the matter… has become extremely controversial”.
“Now, if it comes to parliament, it will arrive in a controversial state,” he observed, affirming that his party will make all possible efforts to build a consensus.
The development came against the backdrop of a proposed referendum over the creation of new provinces. The issue gained renewed attention after PPP Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari opposed the creation of new provinces and the proposed referendum in a speech on Tuesday, warning that any “forced” attempt to divide provinces, particularly Sindh, could lead to bloodshed.
The federal government, however, maintains that a referendum is a legal and constitutional means of gauging public opinion on the creation of new provinces. Minister for Parliamentary Affairs Tariq Fazal Chaudhry also insisted last week that a referendum on the matter “will happen”.
On November 12, Naqvi had floated the idea of holding a referendum to gauge public opinion on the issue, saying the exercise could settle the contentious debate once and for all.
