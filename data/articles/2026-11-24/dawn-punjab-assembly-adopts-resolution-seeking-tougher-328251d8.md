# Punjab Assembly adopts resolution seeking tougher punishment for blasphemy

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Tue, 24 Nov 2026 20:25:59 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2046787/punjab-assembly-adopts-resolution-seeking-tougher-punishment-for-blasphemy
- Collected: 2026-11-24 18:35:42 UTC

---

LAHORE: The Punjab Assembly on Tuesday unanimously passed a resolution demanding legislation to make the punishment harsher under Section 298-A (use of derogatory remarks, etc, in respect of holy personages) of the Pakistan Penal Code (PPC).
Currently, Section 298-A provides “imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to three years”, or fine, or both as punishment for “whoever by words, either spoken or written, or by visible representation, or by any imputation, innuendo or insinuation, directly or indirectly, defiles the sacred name of any wife (Ummul Mumineen) or members of the family (Ahl-i-bait) of the Holy Prophet (peace be upon him), or any of the righteous Caliphs (Khulafa-i-Rashideen) or companions (Sahaaba) of the Holy Prophet”.
The resolution, moved in the House by opposition MPA Rana Aurangzaib, contended that the existing penalty was “insufficient” for such an act which “hurts religious sentiments”.
The House urged the federal government to move on the matter on a “priority basis” and enact legislation needed to increase the penalty under Section 298-A.
It also called on the federal government to ensure effective lawmaking and implementation to uphold respect for religious sanctities and to maintain social peace and harmony.
In August 2023, Parliament passed the Criminal Law (Amendment) Bill 2023 to increase the punishment under Section 298-A from three years of imprisonment to at least 10 years in jail.
However, then-president Arif Alvi returned the bill to Parliament.
The Human Rights Commission of Pakistan had noted that the bill would have made it “more difficult for the accused to obtain statutory bail, which will now be granted at the discretion of the court rather than as a legal right”.
It observed that “in general, blasphemy cases are often lodged by the police under pressure from far-right clerics or complainants with personal vendettas or to stem the risk of mob violence following mere allegations of blasphemy”.
