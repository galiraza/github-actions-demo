# JUI-F moves LHC against 'house arrest' of Maulana Fazl in Lahore

Published November 24, 2026
LAHORE: A petition has been filed in the Lahore High Court (LHC) on Tuesday seeking the recovery of Jamiat Ulema-e-Islam-Fazl (JUI-F) Chief Maulana Fazlur Rehman after police allegedly restricted his movement and stationed personnel outside his residence in Lahore's Chuhng area.
The plea was filed by JUI-F worker and lawyer Advocate Abrar Hussain, who named the Punjab police chief, Lahore deputy commissioner and other officials as respondents.
In a statement, the JUI-F said that Fazl arrived in Lahore on Monday night and was due to address a lawyers' session on Tuesday at the invitation of the LHC Bar Association, but the Punjab Police have stopped him from leaving his residence.
Police, meanwhile, said personnel have been deployed outside Fazl's residence in Lahore's Chuhng area and that the JUI-F chief had been asked to limit his movement.
In the plea, the petitioner contended that police cordoned off Fazl's residence and restricted his movement, effectively placing him under house arrest.
The petitioner maintained that the JUI-F chief was scheduled to attend an all-parties conference (APC) organised by the Lahore High Court Bar Association.
He requested the court to order Fazl's recovery and also sought an immediate hearing of the petition.
Meanwhile, JUI-F Senator Kamran Murtaza has condemned the "restrictions imposed by police".
"Maulana Fazl is the head of a major political party in the country. Preventing him from leaving his residence runs against fundamental civil liberties and democratic traditions," he said.
"Political differences aside, there is no justification for obstructing the free movement of any leader," he added.
Tensions between Fazl and the federal government have increased since mid-October, particularly after he criticised the Centre's decision to impose governor's rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.
The seasoned politician also accused the Centre of failing to honour its commitments despite his efforts to mediate between the PML-N and PTI-led KP government.
Last week, police and Federal Constabulary (FC) personnel assigned to provide security to Fazl and his family were withdrawn from their residences in Islamabad and Dera Ismail Khan.
Meanwhile, Fazl's son, former federal communications minister Asad Mahmood, has been summoned by the National Accountability Bureau (NAB) over alleged irregularities in appointments at the Pakistan Post during his 2022-23 tenure.
However, according to NAB sources, Asad did not appear before the anti-graft watchdog despite receiving two consecutive summonses for November 10 and 14.
