# Pakistan's first astronaut Zeeshan Ali receives send-off ahead of historic space mission

Pakistan's first astronaut, Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, received a ceremonial send-off from Pakistani and Chinese delegates early Wednesday as he prepared to embark on his historic journey into space aboard China's Shenzhou-24 spacecraft.
Federal Minister for Planning, Development and Special Initiatives Ahsan Iqbal was also among those attending the farewell ceremony, held ahead of the astronauts' departure for the mission.
Ali is set to become Pakistan's first astronaut in space and the first foreign astronaut to visit China's Tiangong space station, marking a major milestone for Pakistan's space programme and its cooperation with Beijing.
He will join Chinese astronauts Tang Hongbo and Wu Yi on the three-member mission, serving as a payload specialist. Tang will command the mission, while Wu will serve as flight engineer.
The spacecraft is scheduled to lift off from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Centre in northwest China at 5:18am Pakistan time.
Ali is expected to spend approximately one week aboard the Tiangong space station, where he will participate in scientific experiments under the Pakistan-China space research programme.
His duties will include installing experimental samples and research equipment, as well as carrying out scheduled experiments.
The scientific work will build on four Pakistani biotechnology experiments selected by Chinese institutions from more than 300 proposals submitted to the Space and Upper Atmosphere Research Commission (SUPARCO) by universities across the country.
The research focuses on areas relevant to food security and climate change, according to details previously shared by Suparco officials with Reuters.
Ali's flight follows a selection and training process launched after Pakistan and China signed an agreement in February 2025 on selecting and training Pakistani astronauts for missions to the Chinese space station.
Following three rounds of screening and assessment, Ali and Khurram Dawood were selected as astronaut candidates and completed their training in China. Ali was subsequently confirmed for the flight, with Dawood designated as his backup.
The mission marks the first joint crewed spaceflight involving Chinese and foreign astronauts in China's human spaceflight programme. It is also expected to open new avenues for Pakistan's participation in human spaceflight and scientific research.
President Asif Ali Zardari has congratulated the nation on Pakistan's participation in China's Shenzhou-24 mission, describing it as a "landmark achievement" and a moment of immense national pride.
In a message issued by his office, the president expressed hope that the mission would open new horizons for Pakistan's space programme and that the scientific experiments conducted during the flight would contribute to research and knowledge.
He said Pakistan's participation in human spaceflight was not merely about reaching space but also about harnessing its opportunities for research, learning and innovation.
Quoting national poet Allama Iqbal's verse, "Sitaron se aagay jahan aur bhi hain", Zardari said the milestone should inspire the country's younger generation to pursue knowledge, innovation and scientific discovery with confidence and ambition.
Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif has described Pakistan's participation in the Shenzhou-24 mission as a historic moment belonging to the entire nation, saying the country is looking towards the skies with "anticipation, pride and deep gratitude".
In a post on X, the prime minister paid tribute to the Space and Upper Atmosphere Research Commission (SUPARCO), academic institutions, scientists, the armed forces and other national institutions for their contributions to the achievement.
He also thanked Chinese President Xi Jinping, saying his leadership and commitment to Pakistan-China friendship had taken the two countries' All-Weather Strategic Cooperative Partnership to unprecedented heights.
Sharif described the mission as a testament to the "exceptional trust, mutual respect and enduring brotherhood" between the two countries and wished the crew a safe and successful flight.
"Pakistan-China friendship is surpassing the heights of the Himalayas, in its journey to outer space," he wrote.
