# PM urges ministers to avoid spat amid Sanaullah-Naqvi row over provinces

Published November 24, 2026
Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif on Tuesday took notice of recent statements by ministers and government representatives amid an intensifying debate over the creation of new provinces, directing members of the federal cabinet to refrain from making political remarks against one another.
In a statement issued by the Prime Minister Office (PMO), the premier said the federal government's entire focus was on public welfare projects and that Pakistan was continuing its journey from economic stability towards sustainable growth.
"By the grace of Allah, Pakistan has also achieved several important successes at the international level and on the diplomatic front," he said, adding that the federal government had always promoted the politics of development and progress.
PM Shehbaz directed all ministers to avoid political statements against their colleagues, stressing that matters within the cabinet should be resolved through mutual understanding and consensus.
"Statements by federal ministers against one another create a negative impression and divert attention from the politics of development and progress," he said.
The prime minister said members of the federal cabinet were performing well and should remain focused solely on public service and development.
He also urged representatives of provincial governments to refrain from such statements and promote positive dialogue.
"Statements against one another must stop completely. It is our collective responsibility to play our full role in ensuring political stability," he said.
The prime minister's intervention comes amid an increasingly heated exchange among ruling coalition leaders over proposals to create new provinces, with recent remarks by Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi and Adviser to the Prime Minister on Political Affairs Rana Sanaullah adding to the controversy.
A day earlier, Sanaullah criticised Naqvi for what he described as mishandling the debate, while the interior minister had issued a warning to an "unnamed minister" over repeated criticism.
The debate began after broader disagreements among federal ministers and Pakistan Peoples Party (PPP) leaders over the proposed division of provinces, particularly Sindh.
PPP Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari's warning that any "forced" attempt to divide Sindh would lead to bloodshed drew strong criticism from federal ministers, who argued that smaller provinces were necessary for better governance and that public opinion should prevail.
Defence Minister Khawaja Asif stressed that no political system could be changed without public support, while Federal Minister for Health Mustafa Kamal described smaller provinces as inevitable, saying the country could not function effectively without them.
Other ministers, including Talal Chaudhry, also questioned Bilawal's stance, pointing to the PPP's position on the division of Punjab. They argued that democracy required effective governance rather than merely political speeches and constitutional arguments.
Responding to the criticism on Wednesday night, Bilawal said federal ministers appeared to have decided to pick a fight with him for speaking the truth.
