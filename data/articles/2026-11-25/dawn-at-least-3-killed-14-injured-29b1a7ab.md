# At least 3 killed, 14 injured after under-construction building collapses near Karachi's Baloch Colony

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Wed, 25 Nov 2026 22:11:38 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2046989/at-least-3-killed-14-injured-after-under-construction-building-collapses-near-karachis-baloch-colony
- Collected: 2026-11-25 17:45:43 UTC

---

KARACHI: At least three people were killed and 14 injured on Wednesday when an under-construction building collapsed in Karachi Administration Housing Society near Baloch Colony, according to police and rescue services officials.
Rescue 1122 spokesperson Hassaan Ul Haseeb Khan told Dawn that a ground-plus-five-storey building with one penthouse was constructed on 600 square yards and it collapsed when over 20 workers were present at the site.
He added that rescue teams responded immediately, launching an operation with modern equipment and machinery.
He said three bodies and 14 injured persons were recovered from the debris using around one dozen rescue vehicles.
Meanwhile, police surgeon Dr Summaiya Syed confirmed three bodies were brought to Jinnah Postgraduate Medical Centre (JPMC), adding that another 14 injured were brought to the hospital.
During the initial probe, it emerged that the Sindh Building Control Authority (SBCA) had approved only a ground-plus-one-storey building, East Deputy Commissioner Nasrullah Abbasi told Dawn.
He held that the owner violated the approved plans, adding that when the SBCA ordered construction to halt, the owner approached the court and obtained a stay order against the authority.
Subsequently, the SBCA did not take any further action. He said the building was at the final stage of construction and it had not been ascertained what caused it to collapse.
Meanwhile, East senior superintendent of police (SSP) Zubair Nazir Shaikh told Dawn that three suspects, including a contractor and a family member of the building’s owner, were arrested. He also said the exact cause of the building collapse would be ascertained after proper investigation by the SBCA officials.
Karachi Commissioner Syed Hassan Naqvi told Dawn that the “government will take strict action against those who are responsible”.
Separately, the commissioner’s spokesperson, Sattar Javed, said in a statement that 12 under-construction buildings in different blocks of the Karachi Admin society had been sealed.
Earlier, Sindh Inspector General of Police Javed Alam Odho had directed police officials to reach the incident site immediately and assist in rescue operations, according to a statement from the police spokesperson.
He directed officials to ensure close coordination with Rescue 1122, the fire brigade and other relevant agencies, and take necessary measures to secure the site, remove obstacles to rescue efforts and maintain traffic flow.
The IG also ordered that all available resources be used to rescue people potentially trapped under the rubble and that assistance be provided to ensure the injured receive immediate medical attention.
He directed the traffic police to clear roads, maintain an uninterrupted flow of traffic and make arrangements for alternative routes.
Relevant police officials were also instructed to personally supervise rescue operations and provide regular updates on the situation, the statement said.
SBCA planned to demolish building: Mayor Wahab
Speaking to the media at the site, Karachi Mayor Murtaza Wahab confirmed that the SBCA had planned to demolish the building.
However, the authority had been unable to do so due to a stay order, he said.
“If there had been no stay order, this building would have been demolished,” he said, underscoring the need to “exercise responsibility”.
“The portion mafia and corrupt elements have caused many losses to this city,” he said.
Wahab also noted that there was “political influence” behind the construction. However, he regretted that when the administration tried to take action, a stay order came from the courts.
The Karachi mayor said that he would discuss the matter with the Sindh chief minister and the local government minister.
“Strict action must be taken to prevent this in future,” he said.
Sindh CM directs SBCA to examine possible negligence, vows strict action
Sindh Chief Minister Murad Ali Shah took notice of the incident and directed the relevant authorities to reach the site and expedite rescue operations, said a statement from the Chief Minister House.
He also visited the site to review the ongoing rescue operation, where he was briefed by Local Government Secretary Waseem Shamshad, DC East Nasrullah Abbasi and Rescue 1122 personnel on the progress of rescue efforts and the arrangements for providing medical treatment to the injured.
Speaking to the media, CM Murad said the exact number of people inside the building at the time of the collapse remained unclear due to multiple contractors working at the site.
According to information received from the affected families, around 10 people were reported missing, although rescue officials were still verifying the final number, he added.
According to the chief minister, the building had reportedly been approved for only two floors, including the ground floor, but six floors had been constructed.
Expressing serious concern over the illegal construction, he questioned the role of the SBCA and other relevant departments, stressing that such large-scale violations could not have taken place without the possible involvement or negligence of government officials.
CM Murad said the building owner, consultant, contractor and any government officials found involved would be held accountable for the incident, which he termed not merely an accident but a case of criminal negligence. He vowed that those responsible for the loss of lives would face strict punishment and that justice would be ensured for the affected families.
He warned that negligence by the SBCA or district administration would not be tolerated, adding that officials found responsible would face strict legal action, including imprisonment.
The chief minister also directed authorities to immediately inspect nearby buildings to ensure public safety. He was briefed on the medical facilities being provided to the injured, instructing hospital administrations to remain on high alert and ensure that no negligence occurred in their treatment.
He reaffirmed that the rescue operation remained a priority and that all possible measures would be taken to recover missing persons and bring those responsible for the tragedy to justice.
CM Murad directed the relevant authorities to utilise all available resources to rescue anyone potentially trapped beneath the rubble. He also urged media personnel and citizens to maintain a safe distance so that rescue teams could carry out their operations without obstruction.
In his earlier statement, CM Murad had directed Rescue 1122, the district administration, police and other relevant agencies to coordinate and complete the rescue operation.
He directed the SBCA to examine the building’s approved plans, construction standards and any possible negligence.
The administration was also ordered to determine the cause of the collapse and identify those responsible in case of negligence or illegal construction.
The chief minister also directed the Karachi commissioner and relevant agencies to continuously monitor the rescue operation and submit a detailed report on the building collapse.
President, PM express condolences
President Asif Ali Zardari expressed sorrow at the incident and called for a comprehensive investigation to determine the cause of the building collapse, according to a statement issued by his office.
He added that the investigation should examine construction standards, building plans, and oversight provided by relevant authorities. He directed the Sindh government to take action against those responsible.
President Zardari directed all available resources to be utilised to recover individuals trapped under the rubble and accelerate the rescue operation. He also directed the best possible medical care for the injured.
He further instructed that a safety assessment should be carried out on the surrounding buildings.
“There is no room for any lapse in the discharge of duties by relevant institutions regarding the construction and safety of buildings,” the statement said.
Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif also expressed sorrow at the building collapse and offered condolences to the bereaved family, Radio Pakistan reported.
He directed the authorities to ensure priority medical treatment for the injured, the report added.
Roof and building collapses are common across Pakistan, mainly because of poor safety standards and the use of shoddy construction materials. But Karachi, home to more than 20 million, is especially notorious for poor construction, illegal extensions, ageing infrastructure, overcrowding and lax enforcement of building regulations.
In September, five people were killed and two others injured when a wall of the dilapidated building of a government school collapsed on them in Orangi Town.
Earlier this year, at least 15 people, including women and children, were killed, and 18 others were injured after a gas explosion caused part of a residential building to collapse in Karachi’s Soldier Bazaar area.
In July 2025, at least 27 people died, and 10 others were injured after a five-storey building collapsed in the Lyari area of the metropolis. The building had already been declared uninhabitable by authorities due to its dilapidated structure.
