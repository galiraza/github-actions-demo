# KP’s Karak reports 'first polio case in 6 years', taking countrywide 2026 tally to 10

---

ISLAMABAD: The National Emergency Operations Centre for Polio Eradication (NEOC) has confirmed the country’s 10th paralytic polio case of the year, with the latest infection from Karak district in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.
According to an official of the polio programme, the affected child is a 14-month-old girl from the district’s Shnawa Gudi Khel union council.
The case is the first reported from Karak in six years, and the eighth of the year from KP, according to NEOC.
“The case is genetically linked to poliovirus detected in southern KP, where poliovirus circulation has remained intense this year despite an overall transmission reduction in the country,” the official said.
The centre underscored the urgency of consistent vaccination across the country to keep all children protected from lifelong paralysis.
According to the official, Bannu district — which borders Karak — has reported wild poliovirus type 1 (WPV1) in sewage samples for the past eight months, which means that continued virus circulation remains a major risk to children’s wellbeing.
The country’s ninth polio case of the year was reported earlier this month, in KP’s Khyber district. Of the 10 cases reported this year, eight have been reported from KP and two from Sindh.
Pakistan is one of the last two countries in the world, alongside Afghanistan, where polio remains endemic.
Polio has no cure and can cause lifelong paralysis or even death. It is therefore essential that every child under five receives two drops of the oral polio vaccine during each vaccination campaign.
The vaccine is prequalified by the World Health Organisation (WHO) and has been safely used in 195 countries, including all Muslim-majority countries.
With the help of the vaccine and the efforts of hundreds of thousands of polio workers, Pakistan has reduced polio cases by 99.8pc since the launch of its national eradication programme in 1994. The number of cases has fallen from an estimated 20,000 to 31 in 2025.
The third sub-national polio vaccination campaign of 2026, targeting 97 high-risk districts across the country, was launched in October by the Pakistan Polio Eradication Initiative (PEI). The drive was part of intensified efforts to stop poliovirus transmission and protect children from lifelong paralysis.
