# NCCIA claims busted call centre at Karachi's Sharea Faisal defrauding elderly US citizens

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Wed, 25 Nov 2026 19:53:55 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2047010/nccia-claims-busted-call-centre-at-karachis-sharea-faisal-defrauding-elderly-us-citizens
- Collected: 2026-11-25 17:45:43 UTC

---

The National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency (NCCIA) said on Wednesday that it had busted a call centre in Karachi for fraud, arresting five people and recovering Rs150 million in cash as well as about Rs60m in cryptocurrency.
According to a press release issued by the agency, the call centre allegedly defrauded elderly United States citizens with claims surrounding medical equipment and health insurance.
The network allegedly operated under the names ‘Global Resource Group’, ‘Phoenix Marketing Solution’ and ‘The Call Solution’ between 2020 and 2025 from offices at Anam Empire on Sharea Faisal, the NCCIA alleged.
It added that the network moved about Rs1.2bn to Pakistan in “proceeds of crime”.
A first information report (FIR) was registered against 10 suspects, the statement said, adding that five had been arrested. The other five, including a bank branch manager, were absconding and efforts to recover them were underway.
The FIR invoked Sections 3 (unauthorised access to information system or data), 4 (unauthorised copying or transmission of data), 13 (electronic forgery), 14 (electronic fraud), 16 (unauthorised use of identity information) and 26 (spoofing) of the Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act (Peca), read with sections 34 (common intention) and 109 (abetment) of the Pakistan Penal Code (PPC).
The probe was also widened to cover money laundering under the Anti-Money Laundering Act, 2010. The agency said the assets bought with the money were currently being identified, along with other accomplices and cross-border facilitators.
“According to the investigation, the accused made unsolicited calls to elderly patients in the United States. They misrepresented the eligibility, coverage and insurance benefits of durable medical equipment (DME) products”, which gave them access to the personal and medical information of their victims, the NCCIA said.
It added that the data was passed to a US-based medical billing partner, which submitted unlawful claims to US health insurers including Medicare, Humana and UnitedHealthcare. The billing partner kept “its commission and expenses” while the rest of the money was shared among the suspects.
The money was brought to Pakistan through “illegal hawala/hundi channels and merchant facilities” and received in cash and through dummy bank accounts belonging to fictitious companies and firms, the NCCIA said, noting that 23 such accounts had been identified.
“Approximately Rs1.2bn was debited and credited through these accounts in a short period,” it said.
“The money was then allegedly spent on movable and immovable property and digital assets to hide its illegal origin.”
Rs150m in cash was recovered, alongside a currency-counting machine, four luxury vehicles estimated to cost Rs48m, about Rs60m in cryptocurrency identified in a Binance account, and digital evidence including 11 mobile phones, tablets and laptops; a passport and bank records of the fictitious firms.
Earlier this month, the NCCIA claimed to have conducted a major operation against an alleged international fraud network and arrested 273 people from a call centre located on Sharea Faisal. In a separate raid in October, it claimed to have arrested over 40 suspects in a raid on another “illegal call centre” located on the same road.
In September, more than 70 people were arrested in two raids on as many call centres in PECHS for allegedly targeting US citizens through fraudulent schemes involving offers for new credit cards and low-interest financial services.
