Pakistan, China agree on government-to-government arrangements for fuel storage, terminal facilities
ISLAMABAD: Pakistan and China agreed on Wednesday to pursue government-to-government (G2G) arrangements for Chinese investment in Pakistan’s petrol and liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) storage and terminal facilities, according to a Petroleum Division statement.
Petroleum Minister Ali Pervaiz Malik and Pakistan State Oil (PSO) Chief Executive Officer Jawwad Ahmed Cheema met Chinese ambassador to Pakistan, Jiang Zaidong, and his commercial team, said the statement.
The two sides explored transformative investment opportunities and agreed on concrete steps to deepen bilateral cooperation across Pakistan’s energy and mining sectors.
Both sides identified two G2G priorities for early action: an LPG plant with associated storage and terminal facilities to expand domestic supply, improve access to cleaner fuel and ease peak-season shortages; and the import of motor gasoline (MoGas), including sourcing, storage and logistics, to strengthen fuel security and ensure a reliable supply at competitive prices.
Both initiatives are expected to deliver early results, with tangible benefits for consumers and the wider economy, the statement said.
To ensure tangible progress, both sides also agreed to constitute a joint commercial team comprising representatives from Pakistan and China.
The team will evaluate identified opportunities, engage prospective companies, assess investment and partnership models, and develop a structured roadmap for mutually beneficial projects across upstream, offshore and downstream operations, including the G2G LPG plant and MoGas import arrangements, as well as minerals and mining.
The minister also encouraged the Chinese side to advance investment across upstream, offshore, minerals, mining and downstream energy infrastructure, building on President Xi’s priorities announced at Apec 2026 in Shenzhen.
Moreover, Malik congratulated Pakistan Air Force Wing Commander Muhammad Zeeshan Ali on his historic participation in China’s Shenzhou-24 space mission from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center.
The minister commended the “landmark” achievement that made Wing Commander Muhammad Zeeshan Ali the first Pakistani in orbit and the first foreign visitor to China’s Tiangong space station, describing it as a “proud moment” for Pakistan and a testament to the enduring friendship and growing scientific and technological cooperation between Pakistan and China.
The engagement comes days after China hosted the 33rd Apec Economic Leaders’ Meeting in Shenzhen on November 18 and 19, 2026, held under the theme “Building an Asia-Pacific Community to Prosper Together” with openness, innovation and cooperation as its priorities.
The Pakistan-China energy partnership reflects that same vision: open markets that welcome new investors, innovation through Chinese technology and industrial expertise, and practical cooperation that connects Pakistan’s resources with the wider region’s growth.
The meeting covered a broad spectrum of collaboration, including upstream oil and gas exploration, offshore development, mineral exploration, mining and the establishment of strategic energy infrastructure.
Particular emphasis was placed on unlocking investment potential in Balochistan and on emerging offshore opportunities, including the development of integrated offshore hubs.
The discussions also covered the entry and expansion of Chinese downstream companies in Pakistan through strategic partnerships with PSO.
Opportunities in petroleum storage, terminals and wider downstream infrastructure were discussed, positioning PSO as the key commercial partner for Chinese investment in Pakistan’s downstream energy ecosystem.
The engagement will also bring together leading national energy companies, including OGDCL, and Chinese upstream and downstream enterprises to build commercially viable partnerships across the energy value chain, according to a Petroleum Division statement.
The minister highlighted the government’s resolve to transform Pakistan’s investment landscape through progressive deregulation of the petroleum sector, investor-friendly policies, streamlined import and export procedures, and improved security arrangements.
He assured the Chinese delegation of the government’s commitment to facilitating investors, protecting their investments and providing an enabling environment for long-term business operations.
“Pakistan offers immense untapped potential across its energy and natural resources sectors. Our priority is to translate this potential into concrete investments, industrial growth and sustainable economic opportunities,” the minister was quoted as saying.
Malik further added: “We are committed to creating a transparent, facilitative and secure investment environment, while opening our energy market to greater participation and competition. China’s technological strength, industrial expertise and investment capacity, combined with Pakistan’s resource potential, present an exceptional opportunity to build partnerships that deliver lasting value for both nations.”
The minister added that the country wants to move “decisively” from discussions to implementation.
The discussions also encompassed strengthening bilateral trade through more efficient import and export policies, streamlined procedures and enhanced institutional and commercial coordination to facilitate investment, technology transfer and cross-border business.
Ambassador Jiang Zaidong reaffirmed China’s continued support for Pakistan and assured full cooperation and coordination through the Chinese Embassy and its commercial team.
He welcomed the proposed engagement framework and expressed China’s keen interest in exploring investment opportunities and strengthening business-to-business linkages between the two countries.
Building on the strength of an all-weather friendship, both countries are poised to turn shared ambitions into lasting economic gains, the statement concluded.
