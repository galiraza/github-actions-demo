# PIA passengers in UK left distressed as 2 flights cancelled due to abrupt Norse Atlantic suspension

Pakistan International Airlines (PIA) said on Wednesday it will move its UK operations to its own Boeing 777 fleet from Nov 26 after wet-lease partner Norse Atlantic Airways abruptly suspended operations, forcing the cancellation of two flights from London and Manchester.
The development came after Norse Atlantic, a long-haul budget carrier, said its financial situation is “critical” and it requires new financing to meet short-term liquidity needs and continue operating.
Hit by soaring fuel costs caused by disruptions linked to the US-Israeli war against Iran, Norse launched a formal process for a potential sale or merger in July and has said it is in talks with airlines about leasing some Boeing 787 aircraft.
In a statement, PIA said flights PK786 from London Heathrow to Islamabad, scheduled for 7:55pm GMT, and PK710 from Manchester to Lahore, scheduled for 7pm GMT, were cancelled.
“Notice of this operational suspension was communicated to PIA without any prior warning just as passengers were preparing to board their respective flights in London and Manchester,” the airline’s spokesperson said.
“PIA shares in the deep frustration and disappointment of every affected traveller and sincerely apologises for the distress, inconvenience, and disruption caused.”
The statement added that to protect passengers from “any further third-party disruptions and safeguard schedule integrity, PIA is transitioning its UK flight operations directly onto its own dedicated Boeing 777 fleet effective tomorrow” (Thursday).
In a later post on X, PIA said its “teams are actively contacting the passengers to re-accommodate on next flights”, referring to those impacted by the two cancellations.
“It is deeply regrettable that our wet-lease partner suspended operations without advance notice at the very moment boarding was set to commence,” PIA stressed in its earlier statement.
It said it was pursuing the matter with Norse Atlantic Airways at the “highest contractual and executive levels”.
On its part, PIA said it took “decisive action to deploy our own aircraft from tomorrow so our customers can travel with complete confidence in PIA’s service continuity”.
Contingencies have already been put in place and PIA’s schedule will operate normally across all network points, the statement affirmed.
“PIA remains profoundly grateful to its passengers for their patience, cooperation, and understanding during this unexpected situation.”
Passengers seeking immediate assistance regarding rebooking or flight schedules were advised to contact PIA’s 24/7 call centre at +92-21-111-786-786, or reach out to its UK station desks at London Heathrow (Terminal 4) and Manchester Airport, or visit www.piac.com.pk.
PIA was privatised in December last year through an auction. A consortium led by Arif Habib Corporation Limited had won the auction for 75 per cent shares of PIA in December last year, making a winning bid of Rs135 billion. The Arif Habib-led consortium had vowed to transform the loss-making airline through operational restructuring, fleet expansion, and improved customer service.
Norse Atlantic says financial situation ‘critical’
In October, PIA signed an ACMI (aircraft, crew, maintenance, and insurance) lease agreement with Norse Atlantic Airways to acquire two state-of-the-art Boeing 787-9 Dreamliners, one of which arrived in Islamabad on Nov 1.
A PIA spokesperson at the time said the addition of the Boeing 787-9 Dreamliner would mark the start of the expansion of the airline’s fleet as it aims to meet rising demand.
Following Norse Atlantic’s announcement on Wednesday about its financial situation, its shares plummeted by 76 per cent at 1:03pm GMT. Most of the airline’s market value has already been wiped out this year.
The Norwegian company is now in talks to secure short-term financing, it said in a statement.
“However, there can be no assurances that such discussions will result in a viable solution,” Norse said.
It did not say how long it could still operate without new financing and did not immediately respond to a Reuters request for further details.
The carrier, which does not hedge its jet fuel needs, cut capacity as costs skyrocketed following the outbreak of the US-Iran war in late February. Many other airlines are faced with the same problem, filing for bankruptcy or initiating restructuring proceedings to survive.
“Long-haul, low-cost is most exposed to high fuel costs as demand is subject to a high level of price elasticity. So maybe not a surprise,” aviation analyst James Halstead told Reuters in response to the Norse announcement.
In a separate development on Wednesday, Norway’s Civil Aviation Authority imposed a fine of 8,000 Norwegian crowns ($814) per business day on the airline, citing its failure to pay financial compensation owed to passengers.
Norse’s plight echoes the wider strain on Europe’s smaller, financially weaker carriers.
Analysts warned during the peak summer season that small airlines could struggle to generate enough cash to get through the winter, with some predicting trouble as early as February, when carriers traditionally run short of funds.
