# Tarar urges social media companies to establish offices in Pakistan

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Wed, 25 Nov 2026 18:59:32 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2047005/tarar-urges-social-media-companies-to-establish-offices-in-pakistan
- Collected: 2026-11-25 17:45:43 UTC

---

Federal Information Minister Attaullah Tarar on Wednesday urged social media companies to establish their offices in Pakistan and expand their presence in the country.
He made the remarks while addressing a seminar organised by the Sustainable Policy Development Institute in Islamabad.
“Social media platforms are most welcome. We want their business to grow in Pakistan,” he said.
The minister said that the country needed their presence as it had a vibrant youth population that created “positive content”.
“We have the potential to create good and positive content and these social media companies can benefit from that content.
“They should open their offices in Pakistan,” Tarar urged.
At the same time, the minister said that social media was “financially incentivising terrorism, harassment against women and child abuse”.
“Anything that goes viral is directly linked to financial incentives. Social media platforms are paying you according to your [number of] views,” he said.
“There is an algorithmic injustice,” he declared.
“Social media has failed to protect the dignity and privacy of citizens,” the minister went on to say.
He said that the government had been engaging with the social media platforms on various matters. However, the government could not push social media companies to adhere to the laws of the country, Tarar said.
He said that whenever the government spoke to these companies, they said that they were very eager to open their offices in Pakistan and enhance their presence.
“We are ready to work with all social media platforms to help them formulate their policy according to local peculiarities,” he maintained. Tarar argued that it could not be a one-size-fits-all fix as every country had its own dynamics and issues.
The remarks come a day after Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi asserted that freedom of speech should not become a shield for those who cross “red lines”, adding that terrorism, hate speech, incitement to violence and sexual exploitation of children were the four non-negotiable red lines for the state.
“This is not about technology; this is about people. We are not saying that free speech must be curtailed. Journalists must report, citizens must question and dissent must be heard, including criticism of the government. But freedom of speech can never become a shield for those who cross these red lines,” the interior minister said.
