# Trump says he will meet 'smart guy' Abdul El-Sayed on universal healthcare

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Wed, 25 Nov 2026 20:42:19 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2047020/trump-says-he-will-meet-smart-guy-abdul-el-sayed-on-universal-healthcare
- Collected: 2026-11-25 17:45:43 UTC

---

United States President Donald Trump said on Tuesday he would meet with Michigan Senator-elect Abdul El-Sayed to discuss universal healthcare, signalling a possible opening for cooperation after a combative election campaign.
“I know I’ll be meeting with him,” Trump told the New York Post in an Oval Office interview, referring to El-Sayed’s proposal to rebrand Medicare for All as “Trumpcare”.
“Maybe there’s some place in the middle that we could go,” Trump added, suggesting room for compromise on healthcare reform.
Trump described El-Sayed as “a smart guy” and said he had done “a very good job” promoting the idea.
El-Sayed made universal healthcare a central issue in his campaign and sought to appeal directly to Trump by proposing that the plan carry the president’s name.
Earlier this month, El-Sayed posted an image of a gold “Trumpcare” card on US social media company X, urging the president to guarantee healthcare coverage “from cradle to grave”.
Trump called the branding idea “very smart” and said he saw room to work with the incoming Democratic majorities in Congress, though he expected tensions at first.
“I’m just going to veto everything there is, and then at some point I think they’re going to want to negotiate,” he said. “I will, and we can do health care. We can do a lot of great things together.”
Trump repeatedly attacked El-Sayed during the campaign, while the Michigan Democrat was a vocal critic of the Trump administration. El-Sayed is set to become the first Muslim US senator.
Republicans ran a scorched-earth campaign to paint Democrats as communists and branded El-Sayed a terrorist who would endanger Americans.
But the straight-talking 42-year-old El-Sayed pledged to help cash-strapped voters navigate America’s fractured, for-profit health care system.
During the race, El-Sayed drew huge campaign crowds across the state, even in deeply conservative Republican areas.
El-Sayed also vowed to get “money out of politics” and address the harsh economic woes that have voters yearning for a political newcomer to shake up the status quo.
