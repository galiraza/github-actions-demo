# At UNSC, Pakistan seeks AI guardrails to curb arms race

Published November 25, 2026
UNITED NATIONS: Pakistan has urged the UN Security Council to establish strict global guardrails and regulatory frameworks to prevent artificial intelligence and other emerging technologies from fueling a destabilising arms race — warning that nuclear launch decisions must strictly remain under human control.
“We must ensure that rapid emergence of technologies does not supercharge the ongoing arms race impacting global peace and security,” Ambassador Syed Haider Shah, permanent representative of Pakistan to the UN, told a high-level meeting of the 15-member Council, held under Latvian presidency.
Speaking in a debate on emerging technologies and conflict resolution, he stressed the urgent need for military restraint and confidence-building measures to mitigate the dangers of miscalculation and compressed decision-making timelines.
“In the military domain, we need measures of restraint and confidence-building to address the risks of miscalculation and compressed decision-making,” the Pakistani envoy said.
On human control over nuclear weapons, Ambassador Haider Shah said, “This is most critical in the nuclear domain, where decisions on the use of nuclear weapons must remain under human control, and AI systems must never be used to target nuclear command, control and communications systems.”
In this regard, he highlighted that integrating AI into military operations compresses the window for human judgment during crisis, significantly raising the risk of unintended escalation.
Alongside keeping human beings at the center of nuclear launch authority, the Pakistani envoy called for an absolute prohibition against deploying AI capabilities to target nuclear command, control, and communications (NC3) systems.
Pakistan, he said, believes that all relevant issues concerning the development and deployment of technologies as well as their broader implications should be taken up comprehensively in representative and inclusive forums with universal participation under the UN auspices.
At the outset, United Nations Secretary-General Antonio Guterres said that AI has entered the battlefield in troubling ways, noting that “today’s wars have become proving grounds for AI-enabled targeting.”
From intelligence gathering to target selection, he said algorithms are increasingly shaping life-and-death decisions.
“As long as nuclear weapons exist, the decision to use them must rest with human beings alone,” the UN chief stressed.
AI cannot be allowed to erode the fundamental principle of human control over the use of force and the decision to kill on the battleground.
“When civilians are killed, or human rights are violated, the answer must never be ‘the algorithm did it,’” Guterres said, adding that “killer robots must have no place in our future”.
He reiterated his call to urgently launch negotiations for a legally binding instrument on lethal autonomous weapons systems.
AI has turbocharged the spread of falsehoods and it has set off an uncontrolled race — driven by commercial interests and geopolitical rivalry, he said.
The rush for critical minerals, combined with the massive energy and water consumption of AI data centres, is already inflaming tensions in communities and between countries.
“This Council has a special duty to ensure that competition over emerging technologies does not destabilise international peace and security,” the UN chief said, stressing that “the countries with the greatest AI capabilities carry the greatest responsibility”.
He called for confidence-building measures now — including direct channels of communication to prevent miscalculation and escalation to avoid a crisis.
“Moreover, the concentration of power in a handful of countries and companies should worry us all,” the secretary-general added.
