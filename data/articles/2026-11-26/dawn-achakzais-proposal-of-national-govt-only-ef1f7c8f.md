# Achakzai's proposal of national govt only solution to political, constitutional crisis: TTAP

ISLAMABAD: The Tehreek-i-Tahafuz-i-Ayin-i-Pakistan (TTAP) on Thursday maintained that forming a national government is the only solution to the country’s ongoing political and constitutional crisis.
Earlier this week, TTAP Chairman and National Assembly Opposition Leader Mahmood Khan Achakzai had called for the formation of a national government and comprehensive constitutional and political reforms to steer the country out of the mounting crises.
However, the main opposition party, the PTI, has distanced itself from Achakzai’s proposal. A joint meeting of its parliamentary groups discussed the matter, and a majority of the members opposed the idea, according to one participant.
Speaking to Dawn on Thursday, TTAP spokesperson Akhunzada Hussain Ahmed Yousafzai argued that a national government would pave the way for the release of PTI founder and former prime minister Imran Khan, who has been in prison since Aug 5, 2023.
However, Yousafzai made it clear that the proposed arrangement could not be imposed or negotiated behind closed doors.
He stressed that all political forces, particularly PTI and its founder, must be part of consultations before any decision on the formation of a national government.
However, he warned that excluding any political force would undermine the democratic legitimacy of a possible national government.
“The political uncertainty cannot be overcome through political victimisation or unilateral decisions, but requires serious and meaningful dialogue among all political parties, democratic stakeholders and the establishment,” the TTAP spokesperson stated.
“The proposal for a national government is neither directed against any political party nor intended to provide political advantage to any particular group,” he affirmed, describing it as an effort to restore public confidence in democratic institutions and end political polarisation.
Under the proposed framework, the first priority would be restoration of a democratic environment in which “fundamental rights, constitutional freedoms, freedom of expression and political activities” are protected, Yousafzai said.
He added that such an arrangement would allow all the parties to participate in peaceful political activities without discrimination.
The TTAP spokesperson also called for making the Election Commission of Pakistan (ECP) independent under a mutually agreed, credible and non-partisan chief election commissioner who enjoyed the confidence of all major political parties.
Yousafzai said the proposed national government should ensure free, fair and transparent general elections across the country in accordance with the Constitution so that the people’s actual mandate was reflected in the composition of the elected government.
He also called for a multi-party conference to be convened before the formation of the proposed government.
The meeting, he said, should focus exclusively on reaching a consensus over the mechanism, framework and necessary arrangements for holding credible elections and establishing a genuinely representative government.
The TTAP spokesperson cautioned that the national government must not become an alternative to the people’s mandate or a permanent political arrangement. Its sole purpose should be to facilitate elections and transfer power to representatives chosen by the electorate, he emphasised.
“Pakistan needs neither an imposed political arrangement nor a government established through political expediency,” he said, adding that the authority to govern must flow exclusively from the free and fair vote of the people.
He said the ultimate objective should be a country governed under one Constitution, an independent electoral system and a government that genuinely represented the will of the people.
