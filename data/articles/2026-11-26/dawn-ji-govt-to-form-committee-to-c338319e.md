# JI, govt to form committee to further deliberate, implement recommendations on public relief

- Published: Thu, 26 Nov 2026 21:45:32 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2047234/ji-govt-to-form-committee-to-further-deliberate-implement-recommendations-on-public-relief
- Collected: 2026-11-26 17:29:52 UTC

The federal government and Jamaat-i-Islami (JI) on Thursday agreed to form a committee to further deliberate on and implement the latter’s recommendation to improve the economy, hoping for “positive outcomes”.
On Nov 11, the government and the JI had agreed on the “basic outlines” of a proposed agreement as the two sides held their 10th round of negotiations on petroleum levy relief.
A final decision was expected after a meeting between Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif and JI chief Hafiz Naeemur Rehman, which took place on Thursday, a month after their previous one.
The Rehman-led JI delegation, including Vice Emir Liaqat Baloch and other leaders, called on PM Shehbaz at the PM House in Islamabad.
Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar, Petroleum Minister Ali Pervaiz Malik, Information Minister Attaullah Tarar, PM’s political adviser Rana Sanaullah and other government officials were also present.
“We have considered the recommendations by the JI chief by all means,” PM Shehbaz said while addressing the meeting.
He noted that all consultations between the two sides were held with “sincerity and honesty”.
“For the betterment of the economy, it has been decided that a committee will be formed to further deliberate on and implement the recommendations by the Jamaat-i-Islami,” the premier stated.
He expressed hope that the committee would result in “positive outcomes” and affirmed that the recommendations would be implemented “speedily, with sincerity and honesty”.
The prime minister thanked the JI leadership and appreciated the party’s efforts to seek relief for the common citizen as fuel prices had skyrocketed due to the Middle East crisis.
He also praised JI for maintaining contact with the government and discussing measures to provide maximum possible relief.
PM Shehbaz recalled the government’s efforts to provide relief to the public, including allocating over Rs128 billion from the development budget soon after the US-Iran war erupted.
Moreover, the government in September launched the Prime Minister’s Fuel Relief Scheme to give targeted relief to two-wheelers and vehicles up to 800cc, the premier noted.
He underscored that 20 million people had benefitted from the scheme.
Speaking on the occasion, the JI chief said his party believed “there has been a positive development” in the talks on public relief.
Appreciating the government for holding consultations over the public’s problems, Rehman emphasised that the JI would keep making efforts for further relief and engage with the government on issues facing the people.
He contended that the JI had introduced a culture of peaceful resistance and still had the right to stage demonstrations to demand further relief for the public.
Rehman noted that his party delegation had held more than 10 meetings with the government team.
He appreciated the accessibility of the government’s fuel relief scheme, but also termed it insufficient and called for drastic measures to mitigate rising inflation.
The JI has been demanding for about three months that the Centre abolish petroleum levies, as fuel prices have skyrocketed due to conflicts in the Middle East since March.
The Centre and JI agreed to form committees in early September — when the latter had planned a nationwide strike — to recommend measures to reduce the levy.
On Sept 20, the party launched a protest march towards Islamabad, warning that if the government failed to abolish the petroleum levy, the protest could turn into one to oust the government.
On Sept 22, the JI emir said his party, whose convoy had reached Multan, was postponing its long march at the request of PM Shehbaz, who had assured the other side of a meeting to discuss the matter.
When PM Shehbaz and Rehman finally met about a month later, they agreed to form a joint committee to recommend proposals for expanding the fuel relief scheme and for a phased reduction in the petroleum levy for the next budget.
The committees then held multiple rounds of discussions on practical measures to provide relief to low-income citizens from petroleum prices.
