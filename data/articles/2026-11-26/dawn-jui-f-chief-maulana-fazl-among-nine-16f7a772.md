# JUI-F chief Maulana Fazl among nine party leaders barred from Sargodha district for 30 days

---

LAHORE: Jamiat Ulema-i-Islam (JUI-F) chief Maulana Fazlur Rehman is among nine party leaders barred from entering, residing or remaining in Sargodha district for 30 days, according to a notification issued by the deputy commissioner.
The development came as the party was scheduled to hold a rally in Sargodha on Nov 26 (Thursday).
The order, a copy of which is available with Dawn, states that the restriction was imposed under Section 7(1) of the West Pakistan Maintenance of Public Order Ordinance, 1960, read with the Punjab Maintenance of Public Order (Amendment) Ordinance, 2008, and would remain in force for 30 days from Nov 25.
Besides Maulana Fazlur Rehman, whose district is listed as Dera Ismail Khan, the notification names eight other party leaders: Maulana Naseer Ahmad Abrar, Maulana Amjad Khan and Prof Ghazanfar Aziz, all from Lahore; Maulana Safi Ullah from Bhakkar; Maulana Muhammad Noor Khan Hans from Multan; Maulana Muhammad Zakria Shah from Faisalabad; Maulana Muhammad Yahya Abbasi from Muzaffargarh; and Maulana Jamal Abdul Nasir from Dera Ghazi Khan.
According to the notification, the deputy commissioner reviewed a report submitted by the Sargodha district police officer and expressed concerns that the individuals had a history of delivering provocative and objectionable speeches that could threaten public order.
It described them as “firebrand speakers” whose speeches were allegedly provocative and inflammatory, adding that they were likely to assemble crowds unlawfully and incite public sentiment against the state and its institutions.
The order further stated that their presence could create a law and order situation and pose a potential threat to peace and security.
Meanwhile, several senior JUI-F leaders were detained in Sargodha and served notices for allegedly violating Section 144, police said. Several local JUI-F leaders were also detained under the Maintenance of Public Order (MPO) Ordinance in Khushab district.
Separately, the JUI-F chief, in a video message, said that despite the road restrictions and blockade, he was leaving for Sargodha.
He demanded that the government immediately remove all restrictions, release detained party leaders and workers and ensure that constitutional rights and civil liberties be protected.
JUI-F Punjab spokesperson, Hafiz Ghazanfar Aziz, said the government was continuing its “high-handed tactics” against the party.
Aziz claimed that a large number of party workers had been stranded on the road and that arrests were being made across Punjab, with cases registered against JUI-F activists.
Fazl-govt rift
On Tuesday, JUI-F chief Maulana Fazlur Rehman claimed he had been put under an hours-long “state siege” to restrict his movement.
The JUI-F chief was scheduled to participate in a multiparty conference hosted by the Lahore High Court Bar Association on Tuesday. However, he could not leave his residence in a private housing society on Multan Road due to the deployment of a heavy police contingent.
The development came after tensions persisted between JUI-F and the Centre. Fazl had criticised the Centre for imposing governor’s rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and making the then-PTI provincial government non-functional. The JUI-F chief also accused the Centre of reneging on its commitments despite his efforts to mediate between the PML-N and PTI governments.
Tensions escalated when Fazl accused the Centre of depriving the provinces of their rights, resulting in the veteran politician and key federal ministers exchanging harsh remarks.
In recent weeks, PPP Chairman Bilawal-Bhutto Zardari — a key ally of the ruling PML-N — has voiced his support for Fazl and condemned the “government’s actions” against him.
Last week, police and Federal Constabulary (FC) personnel deployed to protect Fazl and other members of his family were withdrawn from the residences they guarded in Islamabad and Dera Ismail Khan.
A JUI-F spokesman had confirmed the withdrawal, alleging that the Maulana’s Islamabad residence had been cordoned off by capital police and that security personnel had harassed visitors. He also claimed that the party’s Central Secretariat in Islamabad had been besieged by police.
An Islamabad police official, however, said only one police guard had been withdrawn.
The JUI-F chief’s son has also come under scrutiny from the anti-graft watchdog, with the National Accountability Bureau (NAB) summoning former federal communications minister Asad Mahmood over alleged irregularities in 4,252 appointments at Pakistan Post during his 2022-23 tenure. Mahmood skipped two consecutive summonses issued for Nov 10 and 14, according to a NAB source.
The JUI-F staged protests in parts of Sindh and Balochistan on Nov 7 against a “coordinated character assassination campaign” targeting Fazl.
Party leaders and workers condemned the alleged drone surveillance of his residence and the circulation of footage on social media, demanding an impartial investigation and action against those responsible.
