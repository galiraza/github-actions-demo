# MNA among senior JUI-F leaders detained in Sargodha: police

- Published: Thu, 26 Nov 2026 12:21:45 +0500

---

Several senior Jamiat Ulema-i-Islam-Fazl (JUI-F) leaders were detained in Sargodha late on Wednesday, police said, prompting the party to demand their “immediate release”.
The developments come amid tensions between Fazl and the federal government, which have been ongoing since mid-October.
Sargodha Regional Police Officer (RPO) Sohail Sukhera told Dawn that Maulana Abdul Ghafoor Haideri, a member of the National Assembly, Malik Awais and Maulana Amjad Khan were detained at a house in Sargodha and served notices for violating Section 144 imposed across the division.
He added that several other party leaders had also been arrested.
Responding to a question about Maulana Haideri’s arrest, Sukhera said the leaders had not been arrested but that “their movement had been restricted in the division”.
In a post on X last night, JUI-F claimed that Punjab police had “arrested” MNA Haideri, Maulana Khan and Awais.
“The government should come to its senses; if they are not released immediately, the Punjab government will be held responsible for the consequences,” the post quoted party spokesperson Aslam Ghouri as saying.
He questioned why political gatherings were banned when Punjab Chief Minister Maryam Nawaz herself was holding rallies.
“Do opposition parties not have the right to hold such gatherings?” he asked.
“Maulana Abdul Ghafoor Haideri is a member of the National Assembly; was his arrest carried out with the permission of the NA Speaker?” the spokesperson said.
Ghouri demanded the immediate release of the leaders, adding that “arrests made without warrants were a slap in the face of democracy”.
He further maintained that homes of JUI-F office bearers were being raided.
Leaders detained under MPO in Khushab
Meanwhile, several local JUI-F leaders were detained under the Maintenance of Public Order (MPO) Ordinance in Punjab’s Khushab district late on Wednesday, police said.
A first information report (FIR), a copy of which is available with Dawn, was registered under Sections 188 (disobedience to an order duly promulgated by a public servant), 290 (punishment for public nuisance), 341 (punishment for wrongful restraint), 147 (punishment for rioting) and 149 (every member of an unlawful assembly guilty of an offence committed in prosecution of a common object) of the Pakistan Penal Code, read with Section 16 (dissemination of rumours) of the MPO.
The FIR named Maulana Abdullah Ahmed, Qazi Umair Ahmed, Muhammad Abdur Rehman Saud, Muhammad Dilawar Khan, Muhammad Ammar, Hasnain Muawiya, Muhammad Ismail Khan, Muhammad Umar, Ibrahim Khan, Muhammad Qasim Afzal and Muhammad Awais Ahmed. It also mentioned nine to 10 unidentified individuals who were carrying sticks and managed to flee. The named individuals were reportedly carrying JUI-F flags.
According to the FIR, police officials were patrolling Purana Chowk in Khushab to inspect security arrangements when they found Maulana Abdullah Ahmed violating Section 144 by gathering people for a rally outside Madrasa Taleem-ul-Islam and “inciting them through inflammatory speeches”.
Following this, the police immediately conducted a raid.
“A large number of people gathered outside the Madrasa, blocking the street and public thoroughfare,” it stated, adding that they were brought under control.
It stated that the Khushab deputy commissioner had imposed a complete ban on gatherings, rallies, processions and protests under Section 144 on November 23. The nominated individuals had violated the order and “incited the crowd by delivering inflammatory speeches”.
Fazl-govt rift
On Tuesday, JUI-F chief Maulana Fazlur Rehman claimed he had been put under an hours-long “state siege” to restrict his movement.
The JUI-F chief was scheduled to participate in a multiparty conference hosted by the Lahore High Court Bar Association on Tuesday. However, he could not leave his residence in a private housing society on Multan Road due to the deployment of a heavy police contingent.
The development came after tensions persisted between JUI-F and the Centre. Fazl had criticised the Centre for imposing governor’s rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and making the then-PTI provincial government non-functional. The JUI-F chief also accused the Centre of reneging on its commitments despite his efforts to mediate between the PML-N and PTI governments.
Tensions escalated when Fazl accused the Centre of depriving the provinces of their rights, resulting in the veteran politician and key federal ministers exchanging harsh remarks.
In recent weeks, PPP Chairman Bilawal-Bhutto Zardari — a key ally of the ruling PML-N — has voiced his support for Fazl and condemned the “government’s actions” against him.
Last week, police and Federal Constabulary (FC) personnel deployed to protect Fazl and other members of his family were withdrawn from the residences they guarded in Islamabad and Dera Ismail Khan.
JUI-F spokesman Ghouri had confirmed the withdrawal, alleging that the Maulana’s Islamabad residence had been cordoned off by capital police and that security personnel had harassed visitors. He also claimed that the party’s Central Secretariat in Islamabad had been besieged by police.
An Islamabad police official, however, said only one police guard had been withdrawn.
The JUI-F chief’s son has also come under scrutiny from the anti-graft watchdog, with the National Accountability Bureau (NAB) summoning former federal communications minister Asad Mahmood over alleged irregularities in 4,252 appointments at Pakistan Post during his 2022-23 tenure. Mahmood skipped two consecutive summonses issued for Nov 10 and 14, according to a NAB source.
The JUI-F staged protests in parts of Sindh and Balochistan on Nov 7 against a “coordinated character assassination campaign” targeting Fazl.
Party leaders and workers condemned the alleged drone surveillance of his residence and the circulation of footage on social media, demanding an impartial investigation and action against those responsible.
