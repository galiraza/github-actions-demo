# PIA restores UK flight operations after Norse Atlantic disruption

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Thu, 26 Nov 2026 16:52:27 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2047217/pia-restores-uk-flight-operations-after-norse-atlantic-disruption
- Collected: 2026-11-26 17:29:52 UTC

---

Pakistan International Airlines (PIA) said on Thursday that it had restored normal flight operations to and from the United Kingdom, a day after two flights were cancelled by wet-lease partner Norse Atlantic as passengers prepared to board.
Norse Atlantic abruptly suspended operations on Wednesday, saying its financial situation was “critical” and it needed new financing to meet short-term liquidity needs and continue operating.
In a press release on Thursday, PIA said: “Flight operations between Pakistan and the United Kingdom have been restored following yesterday’s cancellations by our wet-lease partner.”
The airline said its teams assisted all passengers in both London and Manchester and that passengers affected by the flight cancellations had been re-accommodated on subsequent flights “in accordance with their individual travel preferences”.
“Passengers who opted to alter or defer their travel plans due to the disruption have been facilitated with full, penalty-free refunds,” it added.
The statement said that in line with the contingency plan activated last night, the airline had “transitioned its UK operations directly onto its own widebody Boeing 777 fleet”.
“Today’s scheduled UK services and operations are running on schedule,” the airline added.
In yesterday’s statement, PIA said flights PK786 from London Heathrow to Islamabad, scheduled for 7:55pm GMT, and PK710 from Manchester to Lahore, scheduled for 7pm GMT, were cancelled.
“Notice of this operational suspension was communicated to PIA without any prior warning just as passengers were preparing to board their respective flights in London and Manchester,” the airline’s spokesperson said.
It termed the abrupt suspension of Norse Atlantic’s operations without notice “deeply regrettable” and said it was pursuing the matter with the carrier at the “highest contractual and executive levels”.
PIA was privatised in December last year through an auction. A consortium led by Arif Habib Corporation Limited had won the auction for a 75 per cent stake in the airline, making a winning bid of Rs135 billion. The Arif Habib-led consortium had vowed to transform the loss-making airline through operational restructuring, fleet expansion, and improved customer service.
In October, the airline signed an ACMI (aircraft, crew, maintenance, and insurance) lease agreement with Norse Atlantic Airways to acquire two state-of-the-art Boeing 787-9 Dreamliners, one of which arrived in Islamabad on Nov 1.
A PIA spokesperson at the time said the addition of the Boeing 787-9 Dreamliner would mark the start of the expansion of the airline’s fleet as it aims to meet rising demand.
Following Norse Atlantic’s announcement on Wednesday about its financial situation, its shares plummeted by 76 per cent at 1:03pm GMT. Most of the airline’s market value has already been wiped out this year.
The Norwegian company is now in talks to secure short-term financing, it said in a statement.
“However, there can be no assurances that such discussions will result in a viable solution,” Norse said. It did not say how long it could still operate without new financing.
The carrier, which does not hedge its jet fuel needs, cut capacity as costs skyrocketed following the outbreak of the US-Iran war in late February. Many other airlines are faced with the same problem, filing for bankruptcy or initiating restructuring proceedings to survive.
