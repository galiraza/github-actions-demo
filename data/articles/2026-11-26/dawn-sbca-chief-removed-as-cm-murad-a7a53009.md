# SBCA chief removed as CM Murad orders sweeping action over building collapse in Karachi's Baloch Colony

- Published: Thu, 26 Nov 2026 20:24:19 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2047189/sbca-chief-removed-as-cm-murad-orders-sweeping-action-over-building-collapse-in-karachis-baloch-colony
- Collected: 2026-11-26 17:29:52 UTC

---

KARACHI: Sindh Local Government Secretary Waseem Shamshad, who also served as the Sindh Building Control Authority (SBCA) director general, was removed from both the posts on Thursday on Chief Minister Murad Ali Shah’s directives following the collapse of an under-construction building in Karachi’s Baloch Colony the previous day.
The CM ordered sweeping action over the incident while chairing a high-level meeting, further issuing directives for the removal of East Deputy Commissioner Nasrullah Abbasi, as well as Senior Superintendent of Police (SSP) East Zubair Sheikh over “negligence”, a statement issued by the CM Office said.
Regarding Shamshad, the CM further directed that his services be surrendered to the federal government. Moreover, a recommendation should be made to the federal government to suspend Shamshad and order a formal inquiry against him, the CM instructed.
Following CM Murad’s directives, the Sindh government notified Shamshad’s transfer.
The Services, General Administration and Coordination Department’s notification, seen by Dawn, said the BS-21 officer had been transferred “with immediate effect” and directed to report to the same department.
Meanwhile, the chairperson of the Inspection, Enquiries and Implementation Team Department, Bilal Memon, was posted as the Sindh local government secretary with immediate effect and until further orders, another notification by the Services, General Administration and Coordination Department said.
Apart from the removal of the three officials, CM Murad further issued directives for the suspension of 16 SBCA officials and the initiation of an inquiry against them.
He directed the police to “immediately” arrest the building’s owner, Salman Tahir, and the builder, Zeeshan.
Moreover, he instructed that the building’s architect and structural engineer be included in the incident’s investigation to determine whether the construction complied with approved plans, building regulations and safety standards.
He further directed that a “comprehensive and transparent” probe be carried out and strict action be taken against any other SBCA employee found responsible.
The statement quoted him as saying that any negligence resulting in the loss of lives or causing suffering to people would not be tolerated.
He said public safety must remain the “foremost responsibility” of every government department and warned that negligence in enforcing building regulations would lead to strict action, the statement added.
Sindh Local Government Minister Syed Nasir Hussain Shah, Sindh Home Minister Ziaul Hasan Lanjar, Karachi Mayor Murtaza Wahab, Sindh Chief Secretary Asif Hyder Shah, Karachi Commissioner Hassan Naqvi and other senior officials attended the meeting.
Separately, CM Murad said during a press conference in Karachi that authorities had halted the construction of 123 buildings that were being built under stay orders.
At least 123 buildings in this city were being constructed under stay orders,” he reiterated.
He added that he had asked the provincial law minister to speak to the chief justice and the court had been requested to decide the cases of those 123 buildings in 15 days.
He said the building that collapsed in Baloch Colony was also being constructed under a stay order.
The chief minister said that the government had identified 16 to 17 SBCA officers and ordered their suspension over misconduct. He added that he had further instructed that a legal case be filed against those officials so that they could be terminated under law and an example could be set.
CM Murad said that he had also ordered the removal of the SBCA chief. He stated that the government had requested the federal government to suspend him.
“We are going to have a reset in the Sindh Building Control Authority,” he announced.
Death toll rises to 4
On Wednesday, a ground-plus-five-storey building with one penthouse, covering 600 square yards, collapsed in Karachi’s Baloch Colony — at a time when over 20 workers were reported to be present at the site.
Officials and witnesses said that the building collapsed when work to dig a borehole was under way. On the day of the collapse, authorities had confirmed three deaths and injuries to 14 others.
The search operation, which was halted on Wednesday night, was resumed on Thursday, and search teams had pulled out another body by evening.
Rescue 1122 spokesperson Hassaanul Haseeb Khan told Dawn that the deceased was a middle-aged man, and the death toll had risen to four following the recovery of his body.
The body was recovered hours after Khan told Dawn in the afternoon that an information desk had been set up in collaboration with the East district administration and the families of 17 missing individuals had approached it.
According to Khan, the rescue authorities were taking extra care in the removal of the heavy debris from the area to find possible survivors. He said they had persuaded a private house owner to demolish the wall of his house to get another point of access to the collapsed building. Rescue workers also needed to enter the collapsed building, using sensors and vibration to find bodies before initiating debris removal operations.
Khan said the operation was a delicate one and the rescue service was avoiding using heavy machinery to prevent the possible loss of human life in case they were still alive under the debris. Initially, he said, the debris was being removed with help of heavy machinery, before a rescue operation was launched to find possible survivors.
On Wednesday, the now-removed East DC told Dawn it had transpired during the initial probe that the SBCA had given the approval for constructing the ground-plus-one-storey building to its owner.
However, the owner allegedly violated the approved plan and constructed six storeys. When the SBCA tried to halt the construction, the owner approached the court and got an order against the SBCA, he said. But the SBCA did not take any action against him.
Meanwhile, the relatives of the missing persons on Thursday afternoon protested against the alleged slow pace of the rescue operation, blocking Sharea Faisal near Baloch Colony Bridge.
According to them, despite a day passing, they had received no more news about their missing loved ones and the officials were not providing them with any clarity.
According to a traffic police statement, both tracks of the main artery were blocked near Baloch Colony Bridge. Traffic was diverted on alternative routes for over half an hour, after which the protesters dispersed peacefully.
Earlier, in a fit of anger and sorrow, the protesters broke security barriers and entered cordoned areas, where contingents of the police prevented them from moving further.
The relatives told the media that they were not satisfied with the rescue operation. They said that instead of removing the collapsed building material with shovels, heavy crane machinery should be used to save the missing people from the debris.
14 ‘illegal’ buildings in KAEHS to be demolished
The KDA’s Additional Deputy DG Farhan Kaiser visited the site and told the media that their first priority was to remove the debris. He said that under directions of the Sindh CM and LG minister, “illegal buildings” were being sealed across the metropolis.
He said that so far, 14 buildings had been sealed in the KAEHS after the building collapse, and would be demolished in a second phase of the operation.
Kaiser added that it was the responsibility of the residents to report illegal buildings into their respective areas, and vowed to take action against the builders and owners of these buildings.
Members of the Chief Minister Inspection Team (CMIT) Bilal Memon and Anwar Shar also visited the scene and collected samples of the material used in the construction of the building for investigation purposes.
Rehabilitation secretary Rafiq Mustafa Shaikh, SBCA DG Abid Saleem, Rescue 1122 DG Brig. (retd) Wajid Sibghatullah Mahar and Rescue 1122 COO Dr. Abid Jalaluddin Shaikh also visited the site and supervised the rescue operation.
FIR registered
A first information report (FIR) of the incident was registered at the Tipu Sultan police station on Wednesday, on the complaint of an official of the Sindh Local Government Department.
The FIR, seen by Dawn, invoked Sections 302 (intentional murder), 119 (public servant concealing design to commit offence), 427 (mischief causing damage), 337-H(1) (hurt caused by rash or negligent act), 109 (abetment) and 34 (acts done by several persons in furtherance of common intention) of the Pakistan Penal Code.
The building’s four owners — including Salman Tahir whose arrest the Sindh CM has ordered — its builder, architect, structural engineer and its contractors have been nominated in the FIR.
According to the FIR, the complainant alleged that poor-quality material was being used in the construction of the building. He claimed that the suspects, in collusion with the officials of various government departments, had been involved in constructing additional structures and not complying with the building’s approved plan.
This was why the building collapsed around 1pm, the complainant alleged.
Police have arrested four of the suspects, including one of the owners, Saad Tahir, and three contractors, who were produced before the judicial magistrate (south) today.
The judge granted police five-day physical remand of the suspects.
