# Bilawal says referendum on new provinces would be 'most controversial' in history

Published November 26, 2026
Pakistan Peoples Party (PPP) Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari has opposed holding a referendum on the creation of new provinces, saying it would be the "most controversial" in the country's history and that Parliament could take up the matter instead.
Speaking at a press conference in Multan on Thursday, Bilawal said he was seeking the views of people within his party on the referendum proposal but believed it would not be accepted.
"We can create a province through Parliament without wasting time on a referendum," he said, adding that holding a referendum on the issue would only make it controversial.
The PPP chairman said a democratic, constitutional and parliamentary approach could help build political consensus on the matter.
He referred to a resolution concerning the creation of a province passed by the Punjab Assembly under the PML-N government, while noting that the Sindh Assembly had repeatedly rejected resolutions seeking the division of Sindh.
Bilawal said the PPP had a consistent position on the issue across provinces, adding that he would support decisions taken by both the Punjab and Sindh assemblies.
He also reiterated his opposition to dividing Punjab, saying he had never advocated carving the province into four parts.
Moving on to the broader political situation, Bilawal said Pakistan needed a "reset" or "reboot" through democratic means to restore public confidence in the system and enable the country to deal with its challenges.
"If we genuinely want the system to serve the people, we will have to take steps quickly," he said, calling on the government and opposition to discuss ways to restore the system.
He said parliament must be made effective and that all national decisions should be taken within the legislature, adding that decisions made outside parliament could not be considered final.
Bilawal said political parties must determine why the system had collapsed before attempting to restore it. He argued that the 1973 Constitution had been made to collapse repeatedly, rather than collapsing on its own.
He described the 18th Amendment as particularly significant, saying no other constitutional amendment had the same strength, and maintained that the 1973 Constitution could help the country overcome its difficulties.
The PPP chairman also called on opposition parties to consider joining the parliamentary committee on electoral reforms, saying elections would remain controversial as long as political parties continued to raise objections to them.
He noted that both the ruling parties and the opposition had raised objections to the previous election, with both sides describing it as rigged.
Bilawal said the opposition also had a role in the current state of parliament, which he described as weak.
He said he had made efforts to build consensus on the 26th Constitutional Amendment, while describing the passage of the 27th Amendment with a two-thirds majority as a major achievement.
He added that greater consensus gave constitutional amendments more strength, while clarifying that he was not suggesting the existing document should be discarded.
