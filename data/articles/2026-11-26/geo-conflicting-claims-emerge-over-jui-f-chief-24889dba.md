Published November 27, 2026
LAHORE: Conflicting claims emerged over the reported arrest of Jamiat Ulema-e-Islam-Fazl (JUI-F) chief Maulana Fazlur Rehman near Thokar Niaz Baig, with a party official claiming he had been detained while Lahore police denied the arrest.
JUI-F Deputy Secretary Information for Lahore Hafiz Zain claimed that party chief Fazl had been arrested near Thokar Niaz Baig, alleging that Lahore Capital City Police Officer (CCPO) Bilal Siddique Kamyana and DIG Operations Faisal Kamran had detained him.
However, the DIG Operations rejected the claim, saying Fazl had not been arrested as he had returned to his residence along with his convoy.
The police official also said traffic on Multan Road and the motorway was flowing smoothly.
The conflicting statements came amid tensions surrounding the JUI-F protests and restrictions on the party's planned activities in Punjab as per the party officials' statements.
The JUI-F had earlier alleged restrictions on Fazlur Rehman's movement in Lahore, prompting the party to approach the Lahore High Court over his alleged confinement. Police, however, denied arresting or formally placing him under house arrest.
In the plea filed before the LHC, the petitioner contended that police cordoned off Fazl's residence and restricted his movement, effectively placing him under house arrest.
The petitioner maintained that the JUI-F chief was scheduled to attend an all-parties conference (APC) organised by the Lahore High Court Bar Association.
The petitioner requested the court to order Fazl's recovery and also sought an immediate hearing of the petition.
Tensions between Fazl and the federal government have increased since mid-October, particularly after he criticised the Centre's decision to impose governor's rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.
The seasoned politician also accused the Centre of failing to honour its commitments despite his efforts to mediate between the PML-N and PTI-led KP government.
Meantime, police and Federal Constabulary (FC) personnel assigned to provide security to Fazl and his family were withdrawn from their residences in Islamabad and Dera Ismail Khan, last week.
Moreover, Fazl's son, former federal communications minister Asad Mahmood, has been summoned by the National Accountability Bureau (NAB) over alleged irregularities in appointments at the Pakistan Post during his 2022-23 tenure.
However, according to NAB sources, Asad did not appear before the anti-graft watchdog despite receiving two consecutive summonses for November 10 and 14.
