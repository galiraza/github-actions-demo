# Death toll in Karachi under-construction building collapse climbs to four

Published November 26, 2026
KARACHI: Rescue teams recovered another body from the rubble of a collapsed under-construction six-storey building in Karachi's Admin Society near Baloch Colony, taking the death toll to four.
Jinnah Postgraduate Medical Centre (JPMC) spokesperson said that three bodies and 14 injured people were brought to the hospital.
According to the spokesperson, 13 of the injured were discharged after receiving medical treatment, while one person remains under treatment at the hospital.
Police registered a case over the building collapse, invoking charges related to murder, negligence and carelessness, among other offences.
Ten people have been nominated in the case, four of whom have been arrested, police said.
Provincial emergency services had said in a statement on Wednesday they had "retrieved three bodies and 14 injured people from the rubble", after AFP journalists saw huge piles of wreckage and twisted metal at the site of the collapse in Karachi's Baloch Colony area.
At the scene in the densely populated area of eastern Karachi, the mound of rubble reached several floors high.
Crowds of people gathered and dozens of rescue personnel were working to move bricks and debris, and recover people believed trapped beneath the smashed piles of concrete.
At an information desk set up nearby, people showed pictures of those missing on their phones to officials.
Building collapses are frequent in Pakistan, largely due to inadequate safety standards and poor construction materials in the South Asian nation of more than 240 million people.
Karachi, a sprawling metropolis of more than 20 million people, is plagued by shoddy construction, unauthorised building extensions, deteriorating infrastructure, overcrowding and weak enforcement of building codes.
In February, a building collapse caused by a gas explosion in the megacity killed 16 people and in July last year, 27 people were killed after a five-storey building collapsed in Karachi's impoverished area of Lyari.
The latest incident triggered a major reshuffle in the province's administrative structure, with the Sindh government ordering the removal of Sindh Building Control Authority (SBCA) Director General Waseem Shamshad.
The decisions were taken during a high-level meeting chaired by Sindh Chief Minister Murad Ali Shah at the CM House to review the matters related to the incident.
The Sindh chief minister also suspended East Deputy Commissioner Nasrullah Abbasi, East Senior Superintendent of Police (SSP) Zubair Sheikh and other SBCA officials over alleged negligence.
He also directed the suspension of 16 relevant SBCA officials and ordered an inquiry to identify those responsible.
Shamshad was also removed as the Local Government Secretary, with Bilal Memon appointed as his successor.
Separately, Abid Saleem was appointed director general of the SBCA, replacing Shamshad.
