# Sindh suspends SBCA chief, other officials over 'negligence' in Karachi building collapse

Published November 26, 2026
KARACHI: A day after the deadly building collapse near Baloch Colony, the Sindh government on Thursday ordered the suspension of Sindh Building Control Authority Director General Waseem Shamshad — while recommending that the Centre suspend him as local government secretary — as well as the East deputy commissioner, SSP East and other SBCA officials over alleged negligence.
The decisions were taken during a high-level meeting chaired by Sindh Chief Minister Murad Ali Shah at the Chief Minister’s House to review the matters related to the incident, according to a statement issued by the CM’s spokesperson.
At least three people died, and another 14 were injured on Wednesday after a six-storey building under construction collapsed in Karachi's Admin Society near Baloch Colony, while rescue teams continue searching the rubble for possibly trapped people.
Officials at the Jinnah Postgraduate Medical Centre (JPMC) said 14 injured people had been brought to the hospital, nine of whom were discharged after receiving treatment.
The SBCA said that the building was constructed on a 600-square-yard plot and commercial construction was allegedly carried out on a residential plot.
Chairing the meeting today, the provincial chief executive expressed anger over the building collapse and said he would not tolerate any incident resulting in loss of life or harm to people.
Following a briefing on the incident and consultations with Sindh Local Government Minister Nasir Hussain Shah, the chief minister also recommended that the federal government immediately suspend Local Government Secretary Shamshad and conduct an inquiry against him.
The chief minister further ordered the suspension of East Deputy Commissioner Nasrullah Abbasi and East Senior Superintendent of Police (SSP) Zubair Sheikh, citing negligence.
He also directed the suspension of 16 relevant SBCA officials and ordered an inquiry to identify those responsible. The chief minister said disciplinary action should be taken against any concerned authority employee found involved in the incident.
Murad also directed the immediate arrest of the building's owner and builder. He instructed the chief secretary to include the building's architect and engineer in the investigation.
Police have registered a case over the building collapse at the Tipu Sultan police station on behalf of the state.
According to police, the case includes charges relating to murder, negligence and carelessness, among other provisions.
10 people have been nominated in the case, four of whom have been arrested, police said.
The Sindh CM visited the site later Wednesday evening, where he received a briefing from officials on the incident and the ongoing response.
During his visit, he questioned officials about how the illegal construction had been allowed to proceed.
Officials said stay orders had been obtained for the construction activities. However, the chief minister questioned the circumstances surrounding the building's construction.
"This is murder. Take action against those responsible," CM Murad told the officials.
He said initial information suggested that permission had been granted for a ground-plus-one structure, but six floors had subsequently been constructed.
He further maintained that such extensive illegal construction would not have been possible without the involvement or connivance of government officials.
