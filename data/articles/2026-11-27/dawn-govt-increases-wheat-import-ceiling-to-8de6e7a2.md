# Govt increases wheat import ceiling to curb price increases

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Fri, 27 Nov 2026 23:51:24 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2047509/govt-increases-wheat-import-ceiling-to-curb-price-increases
- Collected: 2026-11-27 21:53:10 UTC

---

ISLAMABAD: The government on Friday decided to increase the wheat import ceiling by 150 per cent to 2.5 million tonnes to check continuously rising prices of wheat and wheat flour in the market.
The decision was taken at a hurriedly called and virtually conducted meeting of the Economic Coordination Committee (ECC) of the Cabinet late at night, presided over by Finance Minister Muhammad Aurangzeb.
The meeting was called in view of continuous increases in flour prices by 5-6pc on a weekly basis amid supply constraints.
The ECC “approved in principle the enhancement of the wheat import ceiling to up to 2.5 million metric tonnes (MMT) for Food Year 2026-27, to ensure adequate wheat availability, stabilise market prices and meet provincial requirements until the forthcoming harvesting season”, according to a statement issued after the meeting by the finance ministry.
The decision was taken after detailed consideration of a summary submitted by the Ministry of National Food Security and Research, seeking the enhancement of the previously approved wheat import ceiling from one million tonnes to 2.5 MMT. The decision will be placed before the federal cabinet for ratification.
The committee was informed that the provincial governments had initially assessed their wheat import requirements at 750,000 metric tonnes, which were then revised downward to 550,000 metric tonnes by the provinces themselves, according to the ministry.
“However, in view of the recent increase in wheat and flour prices and the need for enhanced releases from public stocks, the provinces have reassessed their requirements, raising the cumulative import demand to approximately 1.7 MMT,” the ministry statement said.
This includes 950,000 metric tonnes for Punjab, 450,000 metric tonnes for Sindh, subject to confirmation, and 300,000 metric tonnes for Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. Balochistan’s requirements are to be met through available Pakistan Agricultural Storage and Services Corporation stocks.
The ECC agreed that enhancing the import ceiling to 2.5 MMT would provide enough flexibility to meet additional provincial requirements or shortfalls in available stocks, without requiring repeated approvals.
The actual quantities to be imported would be determined in accordance with confirmed provincial requirements and prevailing market conditions.
“The committee approved that the entire cost of imported wheat, including procurement, freight, insurance, transportation, port charges, taxes, duties and other associated expenses, would be borne by the respective provincial governments under the existing financial arrangements,” the ministry said, adding that the provinces would confirm their requirements in writing and ensure advance payments or appropriate banking arrangements in accordance with the agreed mechanism.
To facilitate the timely arrival and delivery of imported wheat, the ECC also approved priority berthing arrangements for wheat-carrying vessels at the relevant ports, in coordination with the maritime ministry and concerned port authorities, to minimise delays and additional costs, the statement said.
Regarding implementation of the arrangements, the committee agreed that while the Steering Committee headed by the Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar would continue to exercise overall oversight of the wheat import programme, the food security ministry would be responsible for its day-to-day monitoring, implementation and execution.
It also agreed that any operational or implementation issues requiring intervention would be brought to the attention of the Prime Minister’s Office on a real-time basis for prompt resolution.
While the ECC approved the enhanced wheat import quantities, the finance minister emphasised the importance of timely assessment of wheat requirements, effective management of available stocks and close coordination between the federal and provincial governments to maintain stability in wheat supplies and prices.
Aurangzeb said that wheat availability and price stability needed to be addressed together, with particular focus on ensuring adequate releases from public stocks and responding promptly to changing market conditions.
“He stressed that a coordinated and forward-looking approach was essential to safeguarding consumers from inflationary pressures while ensuring the efficient use of national resources,” the statement added.
The minister stressed the government’s commitment to maintaining sufficient wheat supplies and protecting consumers from unjustified price increases. He emphasised that the decision was intended to provide flexibility to meet emerging requirements, while sending a “clear message” to the market regarding the government’s resolve to maintain price stability, the statement said.
Aurangzeb stressed the need for a collective and coordinated response by all concerned authorities to “discourage speculative practices, prevent market manipulation and ensure that the benefits of adequate wheat availability reach consumers”, calling for close monitoring and timely implementation of the decisions taken by the ECC.
Last month, Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar expressed concern at the rising wheat prices and directed federal and provincial governments to take all necessary measures, including increasing market supply, to bring prices under control.
In October, Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif had set the indicative wheat support price for 2027 at Rs4,500 per maund (40kg), an increase of Rs1,000 from the rate fixed last year. The decision was aimed at supporting farmers, protecting their economic interests and boosting domestic wheat production.
