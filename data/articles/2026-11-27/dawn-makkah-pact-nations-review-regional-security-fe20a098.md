# Makkah pact nations review regional security, condemn Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia

A meeting of the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement’s top decision-making body on Friday reviewed the regional security situation and condemned “persistent and heinous” Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia.
A meeting of the Strategic Political and Defence Committee (SPDC) was held in the federal capital, where authorities announced a public holiday and imposed special movement restrictions in view of the high-level gathering. A local holiday was also announced in Rawalpindi.
The SPDC comprises the foreign and defence ministers and top military officials of the three countries. Friday’s meeting lasted approximately two-and-a-half hours.
Addressing the media after the meeting, Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar thanked the participating officials.
He said that the SPDC comprised the foreign ministers, defence ministers, chiefs of defence forces and general staff of all three countries.
Dar said that he himself, Defence Minister Khawaja Asif and Chief of Defence Forces and Chief of Army Staff Asim Munir took part in the meeting.
He said that Saudi Arabia’s delegation comprised Defence Minister Prince Khalid bin Salman bin Abdulaziz Al Saud, Foreign Minister Prince Faisal bin Farhan and Chief of General Staff of the Saudi Armed Forces Gen Fayyadh bin Hamed Al-Ruwaili.
Meanwhile, Turkiye’s delegation consisted of Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan Defence Minister Yasar Guler and Chief of General Staff General Selcuk Bayraktaroglu.
“I would like to take this opportunity to thank our distinguished guests for their presence here in Pakistan,” he said.
“The SPDC meeting reviewed the evolving regional security situation and reaffirmed the determination of the three countries to continue working collectively towards peace, security and stability,” he said.
Dar said that the three countries reiterated their commitment to the pact, including collective defence, mutual solidarity and respect for sovereignty and territorial integrity.
“The three sides also reviewed progress towards implementing the decisions taken at the previous meeting of the Makkah Alliance for Defence and expressed satisfaction at the pace of progress towards the establishment and operationalisation of the alliance secretariat in Saudi Arabia,” Dar added.
He said the SPDC agreed to continue consultations to enhance cooperation and coordination between the member states in areas of mutual interest, and to support efforts towards de-escalation of regional tensions and encourage peaceful dispute resolution.
The states condemned the “persistent and heinous” attacks by Yemen’s Houthis on civilians and civilian infrastructure in Saudi Arabia, reaffirming their “collective resolve” to continue efforts in safeguarding Saudi Arabia’s security, sovereignty and territorial integrity in accordance with the “collective defence provisions” in the pact, Dar said.
He further stated that the three countries also pledged to strengthen cooperation in defence industries, joint technological development, training and capacity building “with a view to enhancing the collective capabilities and cohesion of their armed forces in order to address mutual security concerns and contribute to peace and security in the region.”
Dar added that the meeting not only symbolised the bond of “longstanding friendship and solidarity” among the states but also “represents Pakistan’s continued efforts towards regional and international peace and security”.
“The meeting also shows Pakistan’s growing and robust diplomatic footprint at the international level,” he concluded.
What was on the agenda?
According to a Pakistani official, the Islamabad meeting was expected to focus on finalising standard operating procedures, establishing an implementation mechanism and defining coordination between the three countries’ political, defence and military institutions, a Pakistani official said.
Defence Minister Khawaja Asif said on Thursday that the foreign and defence ministers of Saudi Arabia, Turkiye and Pakistan would participate in the meeting and the “continuous turmoil south of Saudi Arabia will obviously be one agenda point in the meeting”.
“It is very important,” he remarked.
Saudi Arabia has faced repeated attacks by Yemen’s Ansar Allah movement, commonly known as the Houthis, targeting cities, oil facilities and shipping since fighting in Yemen’s civil war intensified in July.
“Any threat to Saudi Arabia is a threat to Pakistan and Turkiye according to the pact, which is absolutely clear,” Asif said.
Dar, Fidan agree to maintain ‘close coordination’ under pact
On the sidelines of the meeting, DPM Dar met with his Turkish counterpart, where the two expressed satisfaction at the “growing momentum” in bilateral cooperation in trade, investment, defence and energy, the Foreign Office said in a post on X.
FO added that the two leaders reaffirmed their commitment to each other on matters of mutual interest.
Dar and Fidan also agreed to “translate their strong political ties into tangible economic dividends and maintain close coordination under the Makkah Agreement for Joint Defence to promote dialogue and de-escalation”.
Hakan, for his part, thanked Pakistan for “warm hospitality” and the country’s “active diplomacy”.
The alliance
The three countries signed the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement on Aug 7. The pact provides that an armed attack on any one member will be considered an attack on all, obliging the other two to extend assistance through available means, including military support.
The SPDC, which brings together the foreign and defence ministers and chiefs of defence staff of the three countries, is the alliance’s principal decision-making forum.
A permanent secretariat has been established in Riyadh to coordinate its work, with Pakistan’s retired Lt Gen Nauman Mahmood appointed as its first secretary general for a three-year term. A working committee comprising officials from member countries is also functioning.
Turkiye’s ratification of the agreement has completed the accession process for all three member states, but the alliance’s institutional and operational framework remains at what Asif described as a “nascent stage”.
“Initially, we need to have frequent meetings to steer what is being formulated, in a way that in the next couple of months we have everything in place so that we can move forward,” the defence minister said.
The alliance has yet to publicly define its doctrine, the composition of forces, command arrangements or procedures governing a collective military response.
An emergency SPDC meeting in Riyadh on Oct 5 had called for moving towards practical implementation of collective defence commitments, including arrangements for the rapid deployment of agreed forces and capabilities, subject to national laws.
Wider regional tensions
The alliance’s formation also comes against the backdrop of wider regional tensions involving Iran, the United States and Israel.
While the three members share an interest in strengthening regional deterrence, their differing strategic priorities make it important to define the alliance’s scope without allowing it to become an instrument for wider military confrontation.
Asif declined to comment on how the alliance would respond to a direct Iranian attack on Saudi Arabia, but identified Israel as a possible threat to other countries in the region.
“The possibility of some conflict, or aggression from the Israeli side exists, so we need to close our ranks, the countries of this region,” he said.
Managing relations with Iran will be particularly important for Pakistan, which has sought to preserve diplomatic engagement with Tehran while deepening defence cooperation with Saudi Arabia and Turkiye.
A meeting in Tehran involving representatives of the three countries and Iranian officials, held on Pakistan’s initiative, reflected efforts to keep channels of communication open between the two sides.
Pakistan brokered a June accord that led to a brief halt in fighting between the US and Iran and laid out a roadmap for reopening the Strait of Hormuz and negotiations on Tehran’s nuclear programme.
However, the truce collapsed, with Washington and Tehran accusing each other of violating the agreement, prompting months of shuttle diplomacy led principally by Qatar and Pakistan.
Asif said Pakistan had played “a very, very constructive role” between Washington and Tehran, but the outcome of ongoing mediation was “still fragile” and peace was “not yet embedded in a way people were expecting”.
Recent Pakistani military deployments, including JF-17 aircraft, drones and air defence systems such as the HQ-9, were made under bilateral cooperation predating the alliance, a security source said, adding that these existing arrangements would now become part of the trilateral framework.
Meanwhile, Turkiye has stressed the defensive character of the agreement and remained cautious about involvement in combat operations in Yemen.
