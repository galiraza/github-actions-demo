# 2 injured as building collapses in Karachi due to ‘gas explosion’

- Collected: 2026-11-28 15:56:43 UTC

KARACHI: Two persons were injured with burns when a building collapsed in an explosion ostensibly caused by gas near Mehmoodabad on Saturday evening, Rescue 1122 officials said.
A statement issued by the rescue service said that its urban search and rescue team was dispatched to the scene of the collapse upon receiving the information, accompanied by a disaster response vehicle and an ambulance.
Rescue 1122 spokesperson Hassaanul Haseeb Khan told Dawn that the two-storey house collapsed ostensibly due to a cylinder explosion near Mehmoodabad’s Ambala Bakery.
Five members of a family were caught under the collapsed building. With the help of residents, three men were able to be pulled out from under it, while a woman and man remained under the debris, Khan said.
However, the rescue workers immediately responded and recovered both the remaining family members from the rubble, transporting them to the Burns Centre at Civil Hospital Karachi for treatment, he said.
An Edhi Foundation spokesperson said in a statement that the woman, 55, had sustained critical burn wounds.
According to Khan, the explosion had triggered a fire, which was being controlled, while the house was completely destroyed in the impact of the explosion. He added that there was no one remaining under the debris.
Campaign announced against sale of unsafe gas cylinders
Karachi Commissioner Syed Hassan Naqvi, in a statement, commended Rescue 1122 and the district administration for the safe rescue of the individuals following the explosion.
He lauded the performance of the rescue team, newly-appointed East Deputy Commissioner Kashif Nabi Khuhro and the assistant commissioner for their prompt relief operations, stating that they demonstrated “a high sense of responsibility, professional expertise, and dedication to duty, playing a pivotal role in saving human lives and ensuring timely emergency response”.
The commissioner also directed all deputy commissioners to take priority action to curb the sale and use of sub-standard and unsafe gas cylinders across the city. He underscored the need for strict action against individuals involved in the illegal trade of these cylinders to “ensure the safety of citizens’ lives and property”.
To this end, he announced the launch of a city-wide campaign against the sale of these “unsafe and sub-standard” cylinders from Sunday, instructing that all such cylinders across the city should be confiscated.
Roof and building collapses are common across Pakistan, mainly because of poor safety standards and the use of shoddy construction materials. But Karachi, home to more than 20 million, is especially notorious for poor construction, illegal extensions, ageing infrastructure, overcrowding and lax enforcement of building regulations.
On Wednesday, the collapse of a six-storey building killed three people and injured 14 near Baloch Colony, with the death toll steadily rising to at least 13 over the next few days as rescue services continue to recover bodies from the debris. Four people currently remain missing and the rescue operation remains under way.
In September, five people were killed and two others injured when a wall of the dilapidated building of a government school collapsed on them in Orangi Town.
Earlier this year, at least 15 people, including women and children, were killed, and 18 others were injured after a gas explosion caused part of a residential building to collapse in Karachi’s Soldier Bazaar area.
