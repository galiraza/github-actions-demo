# AJK PM extends olive branch to protesters in Rawalakot

• Gillani says govt ready to discuss all demands within constitutional, legal framework 
• Urges protesters to dissociate themselves from armed groups 
• Speaker calls for elections to fill seven vacant assembly seats
MUZAFFARABAD: Azad Jammu and Kashmir (AJK) Prime Minister Iftikhar Gillani on Friday offered to discuss the demands of protesters in Rawalakot within the constitutional and legal framework, but urged those involved in the unrest to dissociate themselves from armed groups.
Addressing a PML-N public meeting at Lal Chowk, the prime minister said the AJK government was prepared to take every possible measure, in the light of a recent resolution of the assembly, to restore peace and stability in the region.
“We are ready to discuss every demand that falls within the ambit of the Constitution and law. Disassociate yourselves from armed groups; dialogue can only take place within the constitutional and legal framework,” he said.
Mr Gillani said some people publicly distanced themselves from armed groups but continued to provide them space, which was detrimental to efforts to restore peace.
Referring to some of the protesters’ demands, the prime minister said the government was planning to establish catheterisation laboratories (cath labs) and stroke centres in different parts of AJK. The government was also pursuing road improvement projects and the Kashmir Express electric bus service, besides planning employment and self-employment opportunities for young people, he said.
“Our aim is not to make a flurry of announcements but to translate plans into practical measures and deliver results,” he added.
100-day action plan
Referring to the 100-day action plan, Mr Gillani said his government had drawn up a strategy extending to five years, adding that decisions concerning the state’s future and prosperity required careful planning.
He said four governments had changed during the previous tenure, disrupting development activities and affecting
the resolution of public problems. His administration would focus on ending political uncertainty and improving governance, he added.
The prime minister said the plan covered healthcare, education, employment, tourism, transport, digital governance and urban development, with several initiatives to be implemented during the first 100 days and others continuing beyond that period.
He said recruitment in government departments would be carried out through third-party agencies on merit, while interest-free loans would be offered to young people to encourage entrepreneurship and self-employment. Scholarships at leading universities would also be provided to deserving students.
Tourism promotion, public-private partnerships for government rest houses and facilities for local businesses would help generate investment and employment, he said. Digitalisation of government services through the AJK One Portal was intended to reduce bureaucratic hurdles and spare citizens repeated visits to public offices.
Mr Gillani said the government would also introduce housing loans for deserving people, improve sanitation in all 10 district headquarters and undertake beautification projects in Muzaffarabad.
He said the coming 100 days would provide an opportunity for people to assess the government’s performance following the Nov 18 visit to Muzaffarabad by PML-N supremo Nawaz Sharif, Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, Deputy Prime Minister Ishaq Dar and Punjab Chief Minister Maryam Nawaz.
“Building roads and public infrastructure alone is not enough; the state also needs to address its underlying problems. Society must restore moral values and mutual respect. Differences of opinion do not justify humiliating one another,” he said.
Call for dialogue
Earlier, Legislative Assembly Speaker Chaudhry Tariq Farooq said the government had assumed office to serve the people and promote development and public welfare.
He said the Kashmir freedom movement remained the party’s fundamental objective and that it would defend Pakistan’s interests in AJK while preserving the distinct identity of Jammu and Kashmir.
Referring to a recently adopted Assembly resolution, he said it reflected the common position of the people of AJK, the PML-N and all political parties represented in the legislature.
Mr Farooq said the people of Poonch should regain their right to representation through elections on the seven Assembly seats that remained unfilled, thereby completing the strength of the legislature.
Energy and Water Resources Minister Saqib Majeed Raja said unrest and uncertainty in AJK since 2023 had affected the region’s administrative system, economy and everyday life.
He urged society to assess what it had gained and lost during the past three years, saying the support and solidarity extended to Kashmiris by the people of Pakistan were a valuable asset that should not be squandered amid political differences.
He said lasting solutions to public grievances lay in dialogue, democratic processes and effective performance by elected representatives rather than protests, confrontation and vandalism.
People should give their elected representatives an opportunity to resolve their problems, he said, adding that voters had the power to reject them in the next elections if they failed to meet public expectations.
Several other PML-N leaders also spoke on the occasion.
