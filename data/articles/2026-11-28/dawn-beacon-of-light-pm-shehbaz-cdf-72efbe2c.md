# ‘Beacon of light’: PM Shehbaz, CDF Munir speak with astronaut Zeeshan Ali aboard China’s space station

Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif and Chief of Defence Forces and Chief of the Army Staff Field Marshal Syed Asim Munir held a phone call on Saturday with Pakistani astronaut Zeeshan Ali, who is aboard the Shenzhou-24 mission.
On Wednesday, Ali became the first Pakistani in orbit and also the first foreign astronaut to board China’s Tiangong space station, marking a historic milestone in Pakistan’s space ambitions and Beijing’s efforts to expand its space diplomacy.
On Saturday, the premier and the military chief spoke with Ali from the headquarters of the Space and Upper Atmosphere Research Commission (Suparco) in Islamabad.
Speaking first during the call, which was aired on media channels, PM Shehbaz noted he was accompanied by CDF Munir, Deputy PM and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar, and China’s Ambassador Jiang Zaidong.
“Zeeshan, today is a great day for all of us sitting here and 240 million people of Pakistan, and I extend my warmest felicitations to you on becoming the first Pakistani astronaut to orbit the Earth,” the premier told the astronaut, asking him to also convey his warm greetings to the Chinese crewmates.
Reciting a couplet of Allama Iqbal, he said: “My dear Wing Commander Zeeshan, your success has ignited a renewed spirit of ambition in a generation eager to push boundaries, explore new frontiers, and take our nation to greater heights through hard work, sincerity of purpose, untiring efforts, and unwavering determination.”
Speaking next, CDF Munir told Ali: “We are really proud of you and proud of the armed forces that one of us has gone into space and achieved this great accomplishment.”
The field marshal said Ali’s presence aboard China’s space station was a “proud testament to our time-tested strategic partnership”.
“Truly, our friendship with China is beyond compare. And we always say higher than the Himalayas, deeper than the oceans, sweeter than the honey, and now boundless as the universe,” CDF Munir remarked.
Responding to them, Ali said it was a great honour for him to “represent Pakistan 400 kilometres above the Earth, orbiting at 7.9 kilometres per second; to represent Pakistan not only as the first astronaut to orbit the Earth, but [also as] the first international astronaut to enter the Chinese space station”.
“I am truly grateful for your kind words and the good wishes of the entire nation. Everything is going very well over here and according to the plan,” the astronaut added.
The payload specialist affirmed he was doing his best to perform his “duties and the scientific research work as per the expectations of the entire nation”.
Speaking again, PM Shehbaz remarked that while Islamabad’s ties with Beijing were already described as “higher than the Himalayas and deeper than the ocean”, they have now “soared beyond the skies”.
“This landmark mission comes as our two great countries celebrate 75 years of our diplomatic relations; what could be a more befitting tribute for this enduring friendship?” the prime minister said.
He extended his gratitude to China’s President Xi Jinping, noting his “leadership and steadfast commitment to Pakistan-China friendship have taken our all-weather strategic cooperative partnership to unprecedented heights”.
“Our iron brother China has given Pakistan the honour of being the first foreign partner in its space programme. This unparalleled gesture shall forever remain etched in the hearts and minds of the people of Pakistan,” the premier highlighted, saying “Thank you, China” in Chinese.
‘Excel in science and technology,’ Ali tells youth
In remarks addressed to the Pakistani youth, Ali urged them to “promote education and to excel in science and technology”.
“I hope that the next generation of astronauts, engineers, and scientists will travel even farther into space and explore further for the betterment of humanity.”
The astronaut then also thanked President Xi for sending a “congratulatory note exclusively for us at the Tiangong space station for the successful launch of this joint flight mission of Shenzhou-24”.
PM Shehbaz hailed Ali’s “extremely sincere and enthusiastic words”, asserting that his message would serve as a beacon of light for Pakistan’s youth and “breathe a new spirit into the promotion of science and technology among them”.
The prime minister then extended his congratulations to the Suparco chairman and his team, as well as the China Manned Space Agency (CMSA) and its members.
PM Shehbaz further said Ali represented the “beginning of Pakistan’s manned missions into space”.
“The knowledge you bring back will open a very important chapter in Pakistan’s pursuit of excellence in science and technology and other related fields.”
The premier also noted emphasised Pakistan’s belief that the “outer space should serve peaceful purposes and the collective well-being of the entire humanity around the globe”.
“The experiments you are conducting alongside your talented Chinese brothers are not just for Pakistan; they will enrich the collective scientific knowledge of the international community,” he emphasised.
The prime minister wished the astronaut best of health throughout his stay in space and a safe and sound return home.
“Please convey to your Chinese brother astronauts the warmest wishes of the people of Pakistan for their good health as well as the success of this wonderful mission. The entire Pakistani nation is eagerly awaiting your safe return home after your great success story.”
Experiments planned onboard Tiangong
The 40-year-old pilot, who is the first Pakistani in orbit, is working as a payload specialist and is set to stay at the station for 5 to 10 days. He also learnt Chinese songs and poetry, practised calligraphy and played table tennis.
Reaching the Tiangong space station on Wednesday, the Shenzhou-24 crew — comprising Chinese commander Tang Hongbo, flight engineer Wu Yi and Ali — was greeted with hugs by the three astronauts of the Shenzhou-23 mission already aboard the station.
“As the first foreign crew member to board the China Space Station, I am incredibly excited. China Space Station here I come!” said Ali in Chinese, according to Chinese state broadcaster CCTV.
The spacecraft had docked with Tiangong three hours and 38 minutes after a Long March-2F rocket carrying it lifted off from China’s Jiuquan Satellite Launch Centre at 8:18am Beijing time (5:18am Pakistan time) under a pink sky.
The spacecraft separated from the carrier rocket about 10 minutes after liftoff and entered orbit, the Chinese space agency said. According to Suparco, docking took place at 11:56am Beijing time (8:56am Pakistan time).
During the mission, Pakistani scientific payloads will be deployed in the Wentian and Mengtian laboratories aboard the Tiangong station, according to Suparco.
The experiments include cultivating microalgae as a potential source of food and oxygen for future space missions and studying the effects of spaceflight on quinoa seeds.
The CMSA said the crew would conduct around 90 experiments during the mission. Its objectives also include an orbital rotation with the Shenzhou-23 crew, routine operations and maintenance of the station, and further space science research involving various payloads.
‘Carrying hopes of 250m Pakistanis’
In April, Zeeshan Ali and Khurram Daud were chosen from Pakistan to start training in China.
Suparco and CMSA signed a cooperation agreement in February 2025, under which two Pakistanis were to train in China, with one joining a mission on the Tiangong space station operated by Beijing.
Eventually, Ali was chosen to join the Chinese mission.
Speaking at a press briefing in Jiayuguan on Tuesday, Ali said he was “deeply grateful” to the Chinese government for helping “us realise our dream of going into space”.
He said he arrived at the Astronaut Centre of China a few months ago, carrying with him the hopes of 250 million Pakistani people.
Ali said he believed that “what comes after this flight matters more than the flight itself”.
“This mission is not a finish line; it is a launch pad. And I hope the next chapter of China-Pakistan space cooperation will grow from one astronaut, one flight to multiple joint flights, to the Moon, Mars and beyond.”
On Wednesday, PM Shehbaz congratulated the nation on Ali’s departure for the Tiangong space station, saying Pakistan had begun its journey “beyond the stars”.
He described the mission as a journey not just for one astronaut but for 240m Pakistanis, particularly the younger generation and scientists.
