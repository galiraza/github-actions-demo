# Bilawal recalls past differences between PPP and PML-N, says elders learned from their 'mistakes'

- Collected: 2026-11-28 15:56:43 UTC

---

PPP Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari on Saturday recalled past differences between his party and the PML-N over the discontinuation of projects initiated by each other, saying leaders from both sides had “learned from their mistakes”.
He made the remarks while addressing the foundation-laying ceremony of a Sindh Institute of Urology and Transplantation (SIUT) hospital in Rahim Yar Khan.
The Bhutto scion has been on a visit to Punjab for the past few days, touring multiple cities in the province’s south. Amid the ongoing debate on new provinces, he has stressed that the proposed south Punjab province had secured a political consensus through democratic means, with the Senate and the provincial assembly backing the idea.
Speaking on Saturday, Bilawal thanked Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif for allowing federal funds to be channelled to the Sindh government at his request. He said the provincial government subsequently worked with SIUT to develop the project plan, thanking its founder Dr Adib Rizvi.
The PPP chairman emphasised the importance of ensuring continuity in development projects, irrespective of which government was in power, saying this was essential to serving the public.
Bilawal thanked Punjab Chief Minister Maryam Nawaz of the PML-N — which PPP supports in the Centre — for not creating hurdles in the project.
“If this project is moving forward, one reason is that the Punjab chief minister created no hurdles in it,” he said.
Bilawal stated that he wanted to continue the “same tradition” set by Maryam Nawaz of not obstructing projects initiated by other parties for the public good.
“We should also learn from the mistakes our elders made, and which they themselves have learned from. This was an issue between the PPP and the PML-N. Our elders initially made some mistakes, but later learned from them,” he said.
He recalled that in the past, the PPP would form a government only to be “ousted” a few months later, after which the PML-N would come to power and projects initiated by the PPP would come to a halt.
Similarly, Bilawal said, former prime minister Nawaz Sharif would be removed from office, allowing the PPP to return to power, whereupon it would focus on its own projects instead of continuing initiatives started by the previous PML-N government.
And the cycle would repeat, he added, noting that the public’s problems would continue to increase.
The Bhutto scion then recalled the 2006 Charter of Democracy that his mother Benazir signed with Nawaz, wherein they decided to accept each other’s mandate.
Bilawal went on to note that afterwards, PPP and PML-N did not conspire to bring down the other’s government nor halt the other party’s projects. He maintained that this was the only way to serve the public.
Bilawal urges avoiding ‘greatest injustice to the people’
Reiterating his stance that ordinary citizens wanted politicians to focus on issues such as rising inflation and unemployment, Bilawal stressed the need to ease the public’s financial hardships amid the ongoing economic crisis triggered by the conflict in the Middle East.
He again underscored the need to set aside political differences, “abandon the politics of political revenge”, and unite to solve people’s issues, as that is what the public expected.
The PPP chairman stressed that the government should make all decisions with mutual consensus and democratic and constitutional means.
“There are 24 hours in a day, and if in those 24 hours, you think ‘I have to target my political opponent, someone associated with another political party, that we must seek revenge against them’, then we will be doing the greatest injustice to the people of Pakistan.”
Responding to a query, Bilawal said he did not want to speak on politics as that would divert the attention away from the hospital project.
At the outset of his address, the PPP chairman contended that his efforts had always been aimed at providing world-class healthcare facilities to people at their doorstep.
He said the SIUT hospital will comprise two phases, with efforts to ensure that construction for the second phase begins as soon as the first is completed.
Explaining the contours of the hospital’s construction stages, Bilawal said the first phase would have 50 beds, with facilities for dialysis, lithotripsy and laboratory tests, including ultrasound and X-ray.
SIUT is a major tertiary care institution known for providing quality healthcare free of cost. In the Sindh government’s budget for this financial year, an estimated Rs250 million each were proposed for SIUT hospitals in Rahim Yar Khan, Gujjar Khan, Sargodha, Dera Ismail Khan and Kot Addu.
