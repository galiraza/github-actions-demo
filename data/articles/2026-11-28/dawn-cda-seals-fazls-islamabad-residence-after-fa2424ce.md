# CDA seals Fazl’s Islamabad residence after 'foreign' owner's CNIC cancelled

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Sat, 28 Nov 2026 12:11:53 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2047652/cda-seals-fazls-islamabad-residence-after-foreign-owners-cnic-cancelled
- Collected: 2026-11-28 08:39:38 UTC

ISLAMABAD: The Capital Development Authority (CDA) on Saturday sealed the Islamabad residence of Jamiat Ulema-i-Islam-Fazl (JUI-F) chief Maulana Fazlur Rehman after the identity card of the property’s legal owner was cancelled.
The move comes amid continuing tensions between the JUI-F and the federal government.
A notice affixed on the property gate on Thursday said the building has been sealed by CDA’s Building & Housing Control “on account of cancellation of ownership”.
CDA’s Building & Housing Control swung into action a day after the deputy director for CDA’s Estate Management-I ordered sealing of the property due to the change of title being cancelled.
In an order dated Nov 27, the deputy director for CDA’s Estate Management-I noted that the owner, Amanullah, son of Muhammad Younus, had been “identified as a foreign national and his Pakistani Computerised National Identity Card (CNIC) had accordingly been cancelled”.
“In exercise of the powers delegated by the Authority to the member (Estate) and after consideration of the available record and observance of the prescribed process, the change of title granted in favour of Mr Aman Ullah s/o Muhammad Younas, […] has been CANCELLED with immediate effect,” read the order, available with Dawn.
It directed the director general of the CDA’s Building & Housing Control Department to seal the property, adding that he could seek assistance from CDA formations and the Islamabad Capital Territory (ICT) administration, if required.
“Consequential legal action shall be taken by Directorate of Estate Management-1,” it added.
The CDA order noted that the focal person for Islamabad Capital Territory (ICT) Illegal Foreigners’ Repatriation Plan (IFRP), in a letter dated Nov 25, also confirmed the cancellation of Amanullah’s Pakistani CNIC “following verification by Nadra and requested initiation of appropriate legal proceedings in respect of immovable properties reportedly acquired by him in Islamabad”.
CDA highlighted that Amanullah had been given an opportunity of hearing as a show-cause notice was issued to him on Nov 25 to appear personally before the CDA member for Estate on Nov 26 and explain why the change of title in his favour should not be cancelled.
The CDA said Amanullah could not be found at the “subject property for personal service of the notice, whereupon the notice was duly pasted at the premises of the subject property” to communicate the proceedings to him.
However, Amanullah allegedly failed to appear before the CDA Estate member on Nov 26, nor “did he avail himself of the opportunity of hearing to rebut the allegations or produce any valid identity document or prior written permission of the competent government” for acquiring the property.
CDA had also issued notice against the same property on Oct 30 for placing a generator outside and for alleged illegal/unauthorised construction.
‘Violation of prescribed transfer requirements’
According to the CDA order, the matter regarding Amanullah’s nationality and identity credentials was brought to its notice through “official communications received from various government agencies”, including the Office of the District Magistrate, the focal person for IFRP, ICT and the National Database and Registration Authority (Nadra).
It noted that as per a Nadra letter, Amanullah had originally obtained his CNIC on the basis of his parents’ CNIC, whereas “discrepancies had been observed in the family record, including the existence of two CNICs of his late father, Muhammad Younas”.
The order said that subsequent verification by Nadra established that the identity document furnished by Amanullah “for the purpose of obtaining change of title in respect of the subject property was not a valid Pakistani CNIC and had been cancelled by the competent authority, thereby rendering the mandatory identity requirement for processing the change of title unfulfilled”.
It stated that under Chapter 6 of the CDA Property Manual, which requires producing a valid CNIC, furnishing an invalid identity document constituted a “material violation of the prescribed transfer requirements and vitiates the administrative process through which the change of title was obtained”.
The CDA also cited Section 3 of the Foreigners Act 1946, which empowers the government to regulate the acquisition of property by foreigners, stating that a 1984 order under it provided that no foreigner shall “acquire land or any interest in land or landed property in Pakistan except with the previous permission in writing of the federal government or of the provincial government”.
“No such permission has been produced or placed on the record of the Authority in respect of the subject property,” the order maintained.
Consequently, the CDA’s Estate Wing initiated proceedings to cancel the change of title granted in Amanullah’s favour, the document added.
Fazl-govt rift
Fazl had criticised the Centre for imposing governor’s rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and making the then-PTI provincial government non-functional. The JUI-F chief also accused the Centre of reneging on its commitments despite his efforts to mediate between the PML-N and PTI governments.
Tensions escalated when Fazl accused the Centre of depriving the provinces of their rights, resulting in the veteran politician and key federal ministers exchanging harsh remarks.
In recent weeks, PPP Chairman Bilawal-Bhutto Zardari — a key ally of the ruling PML-N — has voiced his support for Fazl and condemned the “government’s actions” against him.
Last week, police and Federal Constabulary (FC) personnel deployed to protect Fazl and other members of his family were withdrawn from the residences they guarded in Islamabad and Dera Ismail Khan.
A JUI-F spokesman had confirmed the withdrawal, alleging that Fazl’s Islamabad residence had been cordoned off by capital police and that security personnel had harassed visitors. He also claimed that the party’s Central Secretariat in Islamabad had been besieged by police.
An Islamabad police official, however, said only one police guard had been withdrawn.
The JUI-F chief’s son has also come under scrutiny from the anti-graft watchdog, with the National Accountability Bureau (NAB) summoning former federal communications minister Asad Mahmood over alleged irregularities in 4,252 appointments at Pakistan Post during his 2022-23 tenure. Mahmood skipped two consecutive summonses issued for Nov 10 and 14, according to a NAB source.
The JUI-F staged protests in parts of Sindh and Balochistan on Nov 7 against a “coordinated character assassination campaign” targeting Fazl.
Party leaders and workers condemned the alleged drone surveillance of his residence and the circulation of footage on social media, demanding an impartial investigation and action against those responsible.
On Tuesday, Fazl claimed he had been put under an hours-long “state siege” to prevent him from attending a multiparty conference hosted by the Lahore High Court Bar Association.
On Thursday, authorities barred the JUI-F chief and eight other leaders of his party from entering, residing or remaining in Sargodha — where the JUI-F planned to hold a rally that day — for 30 days. Despite the restriction, Fazl said in a video message that he was setting out for Sargodha.
The next day, hours after Fazl claimed that he was under arrest, Lahore Capital City Police Officer Bilal Siddique Kamyana denied that the politician had been taken into custody.
The JUI-F also claimed that several party activists and workers had been arrested and booked across Punjab and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa as the party staged protests at various locations on Thursday and Friday.
