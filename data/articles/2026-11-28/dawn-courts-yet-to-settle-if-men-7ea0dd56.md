# Courts yet to settle if men can be prosecuted over ‘marital rape’: report

ISLAMABAD: Pakistan removed the marital rape exception from its criminal law two decades ago, but the courts have yet to settle whether a husband can be prosecuted for raping his wife, according to a new report by the South Asian Movement for Accessing Justice (SAMAJ), a coalition of 26 organisations convened by Equality Now.
The report, Challenging the Marital Rape Exception in South Asia, led and authored by lawyer Sahar Bandial, reviewed the law in eight countries.
It noted that the Protection of Women (Criminal Law Amendment) Act, 2006 redefined rape under Section 375 of the Pakistan Penal Code without reference to marital status, so marital rape can in principle attract the same penalty as any other rape. Yet no reported superior court judgement has confirmed this.
Cases described in the media as marital rape convictions have mostly been brought under Section 377, which covers “unnatural offences”.

In August 2026, a special court in Lahore sentenced a man to ten years under Section 376 for raping his wife — a clear marital rape conviction, the report says, though only at trial level.
By contrast, a 2025 Lahore High Court ruling held that forced sex within an ongoing marriage was not rape, effectively reading the exception back into the law.
In November 2026, the same court dismissed a petition seeking directions for legislation on marital rape. Domestic violence laws offer only civil remedies.
Regionally, the Maldives is the only country to fully criminalise marital rape on par with other rape, following a 2021 amendment. Nepal and Bhutan criminalise it with far lighter penalties. India and Sri Lanka recognise it only where spouses are separated; Bangladesh exempts husbands whose wives are over 13, and Afghanistan has no applicable law.
The World Health Organisation estimates cited in the report put lifetime intimate partner violence in Pakistan at 24.8pc, below Afghanistan (50.9pc), Bangladesh (48.9pc) and India (29.9pc), though the figures do not isolate marital rape.
The report urges legislators to make consent within marriage explicit in law.
Child marriages
Meanwhile, in a statement on the International Day for the Elimination of Child, Early and Forced Marriage, Senator Sherry Rehman called for stronger legal protections, effective enforcement and sustained efforts to address the social and economic factors driving child marriages.
The PPP leader stressed that the practice deprived girls of their childhood, education, dignity and fundamental human rights.
“One in six women in Pakistan was married as a child. Pakistan ranks sixth globally among countries with the highest number of child marriages, while more than 19 million women and girls in the country were married before the age of 18. Nearly 4.8m girls were married before the age of 15.
“Globally, around 650m women and girls have been married in childhood. These are not just statistics; they represent millions of lives shaped by lost opportunities, interrupted education and diminished choices,” she said in a statement issued here.
She noted that child marriages had become particularly prevalent in flood-affected areas, where families facing displacement, poverty, loss of livelihoods and insecurity increasingly viewed early marriage as a survival mechanism.
“When disasters push families deeper into poverty and insecurity, girls are often the first to bear the cost. Marriage cannot be a coping mechanism for economic hardship, displacement or climate-induced vulnerability.
“It further exposes girls to risks and deprives them of the opportunity to rebuild their lives through education and empowerment,” she said.
Senator Rehman said the legislation had strengthened the legal framework for accountability and protection, but laws alone could not deliver justice without effective implementation, institutional commitment and public awareness.
“Every girl has the right to learn, to make decisions about her own future, to grow up without fear and to live a safe and dignified life. We cannot protect our girls through legislation alone unless we confront the inequalities and economic pressures that make child marriage possible,” she said.
