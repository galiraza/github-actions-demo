# Death toll in Baloch Colony building collapse rises to 13 as rescuers recover four more bodies, locate fifth

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Sat, 28 Nov 2026 18:29:29 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2047647/death-toll-in-baloch-colony-building-collapse-rises-to-13-as-rescuers-recover-four-more-bodies-locate-fifth
- Collected: 2026-11-28 15:56:43 UTC

---

KARACHI: Rescue workers on Saturday recovered four more bodies from the rubble of a six-storey building that collapsed near Karachi’s Baloch Colony area and were in the process of recovering a fifth, taking the death toll to 13.
On Wednesday, three people were killed and 14 others injured when the under-construction building — reportedly being constructed illegally on a residential plot — collapsed. Another body was recovered on Thursday, taking the death toll to four. On Friday, four more bodies were recovered, taking the toll to eight.
Rescue 1122 spokesperson Hassaanul Haseeb Khan said that during the operation, one body was recovered at around 4:20am and two more were extricated from the debris around 8:30am. The fourth body was recovered in the afternoon.
A later rescue statement said that another body — the 13th in total — had been located in the debris and efforts to recover it were under way. Four bodies now remain missing.
Khan added that the rescue teams had now initiated “fast track” operations from all four sides. He said searching and debris removal operations had also been expedited.
He hoped that the operation would likely be completed on Saturday night and the team would extricate the remaining bodies from the debris.
Videos shared by Rescue 1122 from the site showed members of the Urban Search and Rescue team using heavy-duty bolt cutters to cut through rebar and recover bodies, while clearing rubble with various tools.
Commissioner’s office submits report to CM
The Karachi Commissioner’s Office has submitted a report to the chief minister on action taken against illegal and unauthorised buildings in the city, stating that 516 buildings have been sealed as part of an ongoing crackdown, as of Nov 27.
According to the report, action was taken against 262 buildings under construction in various districts. Of these, 158 were sealed, while notices were issued to 12 buildings. Construction work was found to have been halted at one site during an inspection.
In Korangi district, 41 of the 48 buildings were sealed, while notices were issued to seven. In Keamari, 12 of the 19 buildings were sealed and notices were issued to five, while construction work was found to have stopped at one site. The report also recorded 12 buildings in West, 21 in Malir, 64 in Central, 61 in South and 37 in East. Of these, eight buildings in Malir, five in Central, 55 in South and all 37 in East were sealed.
Karachi Commissioner Syed Hassan Naqvi has directed deputy commissioners to continue action against illegal and unauthorised constructions and ensure that those violating building regulations are dealt with in accordance with the law.
Man rescued alive after 54 hours
A day earlier, emergency services rescued a man alive from beneath the rubble, two days after the building collapsed.
Rescue 1122 said that 28-year-old Muhammad Adil was rescued alive from the rubble. It said that the search operation for any other individuals feared trapped was underway.
Khan termed the rescue a “breakthrough”. He added that rescue operations were ongoing to search for other survivors from the debris.
He said the rescued man told them that two other persons who might be alive were in touch with him. However, Khan said the rescuers conducted a search in the identified area but could not locate them.
Khan added that heavy machinery was also being used to remove debris, but that whenever a human body was spotted in the debris, the team stopped the machine operations and initiated manual work with extra care to recover the body.
Sindh Chief Minister Murad Ali Shah expressed gratitude that the man trapped under the rubble survived for 54 hours, describing it as a “miracle”, according to a statement issued from his office.
According to the statement, the chief minister directed rescue agencies and the Karachi commissioner to accelerate the ongoing rescue operation and deploy all available resources.
Murad also praised rescue workers for their tireless efforts and directed officials to ensure the survivor receives the best possible medical treatment.
Sindh Local Government Secretary Waseem Shamshad, who also served as director general of the Sindh Building Control Authority (SBCA), was removed from both posts on Thursday on the directives of CM Murad following the incident.
The CM ordered sweeping action over the incident while chairing a high-level meeting, further issuing directives for the removal of East Deputy Commissioner Nasrullah Abbasi, as well as Senior Superintendent of Police (SSP) East Zubair Sheikh, over “negligence”, a statement issued by the CM Office said.
In addition to the removal of the three officials, CM Murad further issued directives for the suspension of 16 SBCA officials and the initiation of an inquiry against them.
He directed the police to “immediately” arrest the building’s owner, Salman Tahir, and the builder, Zeeshan.
Moreover, he instructed that the building’s architect and structural engineer be included in the incident’s investigation to determine whether the construction complied with approved plans, building regulations and safety standards.
14 ‘illegal’ buildings in KAEHS to be demolished
The KDA’s Additional Deputy DG Farhan Kaiser visited the site on Thursday and told the media that their first priority was to remove the debris. He said that under the directions of the Sindh CM and LG minister, “illegal buildings” were being sealed across the metropolis.
He said that so far, 14 buildings had been sealed in the KAEHS after the building collapse and would be demolished in a second phase of the operation.
Kaiser added that it was the responsibility of the residents to report illegal buildings in their respective areas, and vowed to take action against the builders and owners of these buildings.
Members of the Chief Minister Inspection Team (CMIT), Bilal Memon and Anwar Shar, visited the scene and collected samples of the material used in the construction of the building for investigation purposes.
Rehabilitation secretary Rafiq Mustafa Shaikh, SBCA DG Abid Saleem, Rescue 1122 DG Brig. (retd) Wajid Sibghatullah Mahar and Rescue 1122 COO Dr Abid Jalaluddin Shaikh also visited the site and supervised the rescue operation.
FIR registered
A first information report (FIR) of the incident was registered at the Tipu Sultan police station on Wednesday, on the complaint of an official of the Sindh Local Government Department.
The FIR, seen by Dawn, invoked Sections 302 (intentional murder), 119 (public servant concealing design to commit offence), 427 (mischief causing damage), 337-H(1) (hurt caused by rash or negligent act), 109 (abetment) and 34 (acts done by several persons in furtherance of common intention) of the Pakistan Penal Code.
The building’s four owners — including Salman Tahir whose arrest the Sindh CM has ordered — its builder, architect, structural engineer and its contractors have been nominated in the FIR.
According to the FIR, the complainant alleged that poor-quality material was being used in the construction of the building. He claimed that the suspects, in collusion with the officials of various government departments, had been involved in constructing additional structures and not complying with the building’s approved plan.
This was why the building collapsed around 1pm, the complainant alleged.
Police have arrested four of the suspects, including one of the owners, Saad Tahir, and three contractors, who were produced before the judicial magistrate (south) today.
The judge granted police a five-day physical remand of the suspects.
