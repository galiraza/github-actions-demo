# Lahore ranked world's most polluted major city; Sargodha, Peshawar also record hazardous air

- Published: Sat, 28 Nov 2026 13:20:23 +0500

- Collected: 2026-11-28 08:39:38 UTC

---

LAHORE: Lahore was ranked the world’s most polluted “major city” on Saturday morning as its air quality index (AQI) reached 400, while Peshawar and Sargodha also recorded AQIs above 400, according to monitoring firm IQAir.
The Swiss air quality monitoring organisation placed Lahore at the top of its ranking for global major cities for the 9am to 10am period on Nov 28, with Kabul second at an AQI of 304 and Delhi third at 238.
The ranking at that time showed other cities in the region reporting high pollution levels, with Dubai at an AQI of 168, Kathmandu 166, Kolkata 161 and Karachi 160. Cairo, Chengdu and Kampala also featured among the 10 most polluted major cities.
Lahore’s AQI of 400 fell in the hazardous category, according to IQAir’s scale, which ranks AQI above 300 as such. Exposure to such pollution can pose serious risks, particularly to children, older people and those with respiratory or cardiovascular conditions.
The deteriorated air quality comes as smog continues to affect several parts of Punjab, with pollution levels remaining elevated across major urban centres.
By 1pm, Lahore’s AQI had declined to 293, falling in the “very unhealthy” category.
The concentration of PM2.5 — particulate matter so small they can be absorbed into the bloodstream upon inhalation — was “43.6 times the World Health Organisation annual PM2.5 guideline value”, IQAir noted.
Within Lahore, pollution levels varied considerably between localities, with several monitoring stations recording readings substantially higher than the citywide level.
For 9-10am, Allama Iqbal Town recorded an AQI of 680, the highest among the listed monitoring stations, followed by ARM at 557 and ARC at 461. Bedia Thethar Road recorded 416, while A Block, Model Town, reported 392.
Barki Road, among the relatively less polluted locations in the city, still recorded an AQI of 208, indicating “very unhealthy” air quality.
The pollution crisis extended beyond Lahore, with several cities across Punjab and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa reporting very unhealthy to hazardous air quality.
An IQAir ranking of Pakistani cities, for 9-10am, placed Peshawar at the top with an AQI of 414, followed by Sargodha at 404, Gujranwala at 388 and Faisalabad at 384. Lahore ranked fifth nationally with an AQI of 363, followed by Multan at 347, Rahim Yar Khan at 328, Sialkot at 301, Bahawalpur at 280 and Muzaffargarh at 259.
By 1pm, Sargodha had overtaken Peshawar — which now had 327 AQI — with an AQI of 412, and Lahore had declined to 293.
Residents were advised to limit outdoor exposure, remain indoors where possible and use N95 masks if outdoor activities cannot be avoided.
Vehicular emissions, industrial pollution and crop residue burning are among the major sources contributing to smog, while winter weather conditions can restrict dispersal of pollutants and allow them to accumulate near the ground.
With winter setting in, the recurring smog crisis has raised concerns over public health and the effectiveness of measures aimed at reducing pollution across Punjab.
