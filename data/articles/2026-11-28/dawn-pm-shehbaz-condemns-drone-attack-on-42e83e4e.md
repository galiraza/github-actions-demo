# PM Shehbaz condemns drone attack on Kuwait that injured Pakistani national

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Sat, 28 Nov 2026 19:28:43 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2047684/pm-shehbaz-condemns-drone-attack-on-kuwait-that-injured-pakistani-national
- Collected: 2026-11-28 15:56:43 UTC

---

Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif on Saturday strongly condemned a recent drone attack on Kuwait that killed two, expressing Pakistan’s solidarity with the Gulf country.
A drone attack on Thursday killed two Italian technicians working on an air defence system, in the first fatal strike in the Gulf state for months. The attack also injured a Kuwaiti national and a Pakistani national, according to Kuwait’s ministry of foreign affairs.
In a post on X on Saturday, PM Shehbaz said: “strongly condemn the recent drone attack on Kuwait. Pakistan stands in full solidarity with the leadership and people of brotherly Kuwait.
“We reaffirm our firm support for Kuwait’s sovereignty, security and territorial integrity and underscore that these must be fully respected,” the prime minister said.
“I extend my heartfelt condolences to the families who lost their lives and pray for the swift recovery of those injured, including a Pakistani,” he said.
PM Shehbaz said the Pakistani embassy in Kuwait was in close contact with the Kuwaiti authorities and was providing “all necessary assistance” to the injured Pakistani citizen.
Meanwhile, Radio Pakistan reported that on the premier’s directives, Pakistan’s ambassador to Kuwait, Dr Zafar Iqbal, visited the hospital to inquire about the health of the injured Pakistani national.
“The ambassador received a detailed briefing from the doctors regarding the injured national’s treatment. According to the doctors, the injured Pakistani national is in stable condition and is expected to make a speedy recovery,” the report said.
The deaths, confirmed by both Italy and Kuwait, came as Iran has ratcheted up attacks on Kuwait and Bahrain in the last week while it continues to fight the United States for control of the contested Strait of Hormuz.
The conflict — which began with US-Israeli strikes on Iran in late February — has seen the Gulf states caught in the crossfire, with US military facilities and energy installations on their soil targeted.
Last week, Iran said it had targeted US drone and missile sites in Kuwait overnight after the US struck Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz, marking Tehran’s first attack on the US-allied state since September.
Kuwait’s defence ministry said before the incident that its army had detected a number of drones within its airspace, in what it described as an Iranian attack.
Iran’s military has denied that it was behind the deadly attack on Kuwait.
On Friday, Kuwait’s foreign ministry said it had summoned Iran’s ambassador over “continued Iranian attacks” in the wake of the strike.
The attack also drew condemnation from UN chief Antonio Guterres, who called on “all parties to exercise restraint”.
The secretary general expressed solidarity with Kuwait and offered his condolences to the families of the victims, his spokesman Stephane Dujarric said. He also expressed his deep concern over the escalation of tensions in the region.
