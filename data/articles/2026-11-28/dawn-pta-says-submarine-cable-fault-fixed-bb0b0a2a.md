# PTA says submarine cable fault fixed, internet services return to normal

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Sat, 28 Nov 2026 14:37:06 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2047668/pta-says-submarine-cable-fault-fixed-internet-services-return-to-normal
- Collected: 2026-11-28 15:56:43 UTC

---

The Pakistan Telecommunication Authority (PTA) on Saturday announced the restoration of the SEA-ME-WE 5 (SMW5) international submarine cable system following the repair of a fault, saying internet services across the country had returned to normal.
On November 18, PTA said that a fault had been reported in the SEA-ME-WE 5 (SMW5) international submarine cable system, approximately 11 kilometres from Pakistan’s coast.
In a statement today, the regulator said the cable was now fully operational and the fault had been rectified through coordination between Transworld Associates (TWA) and the international SMW5 consortium.
It said technical teams had worked round the clock to repair the fault and restore connectivity. Meanwhile, temporary rerouting of internet traffic through alternative international links had helped minimise disruptions during the outage.
The PTA confirmed that internet services had returned to normal operating capacity following the restoration of the cable.
Submarine cables are a critical part of international telecommunications infrastructure, carrying data between countries and continents. Faults in these systems can affect internet speeds and connectivity, particularly when traffic has to be redirected through alternative routes.
Currently, seven submarine cables provide internet connectivity to Pakistan: Asia-Africa-Europe 1 (AAE-1), South East Asia-Middle East-West Europe 4 (SMW4) and India-Middle East-Western Europe (IMEWE), operated by PTCL; and South-East Asia-Middle East-Western Europe 6 (SEA-ME-WE 6), South East Asia-Middle East-West Europe 5 (SMW5) and Transworld Associates 1 (TWA-1), operated by Transworld Associates.
Cyber Internet Services operates the seventh cable named Pakistan and East Africa Connecting Europe (PEACE).
In July this year, a fault in the same SMW5 cable system had caused widespread connectivity issues in Pakistan. The PTA subsequently announced that the fault had been repaired on July 3, following its detection on July 1.
