# Tandoor owners threaten to stop selling roti from Dec 4 in Punjab

- Source: Dawn
- Published: Sat, 28 Nov 2026 09:22:38 +0500
- Link: https://www.dawn.com/news/2047585/tandoor-owners-threaten-to-stop-selling-roti-from-dec-4-in-punjab
- Collected: 2026-11-28 08:39:38 UTC

---

LAHORE: Protesting over the increasing actions of the Punjab Enforcement & Regulatory Authority (Pera) Force over using subsidised wheat flour, tandoor owners have announced that they will stop selling rotis from Dec 4 across the city.
The announcement came after the Pera Force started sealing tandoor shops, having wheat flour bags procured on subsidised rates, claiming that tandoors were not authorised to procure subsidised flour. Following the action, tandoor owners had staged a protest demonstration at the DC Office a couple of days ago to demand an end to Pera actions and provide wheat flour on subsidised rates so they could sell rotis at notified rates.
“All was going well and we were selling sada roti for Rs17 following the provision of a 10kg flour bag at Rs1,100. But all of a sudden, the government increased and fixed the 10kg flour bag price at Rs1,275. The Pera Force also started sealing tandoors, having flour bags purchased on subsidised rate. So far, eight tandoors have been sealed,” Mutahidda Nan Roti Association President Aftab Gill told Dawn on Friday.
“While the LPG prices are already on the higher side and gas loadshedding is also ongoing, we are unable to sell roti for Rs17. Therefore, we have decided to better stop baking flatbread with effect from Dec 4,” he argued.
He said the Dec 4 deadline was given just to give the government time to think and consider the demands of the association, comprising as many as 15,000 tandoors across Lahore city district.
“We need 15,000 flour bags (80kg each) daily for all tandoors. If the government gives us the bags on subsidised rates, we can sell roti for Rs17, failing which, we will have no option but to stop baking flatbread,” he warned.
Meanwhile, the association, through a written application to the Lahore DC, has also sought notifying the roti rate at Rs30, keeping in view the increasing rates of wheat flour.
The DC was not available for comments.
COMMISSIONER: Lahore Commissioner Muhammad Jahanzaib Awan on Friday visited several sites in the Walled City to review the progress of ongoing development and conservation projects, including work at Mori Gate, Taxali Gate, Sheranwala Gate and Pani Wala Talab.
During the visit, the commissioner reviewed sewerage, restoration and construction work being carried out under various development schemes.
Officials and engineers from the Walled City of Lahore Authority, Communication and Works Department, Nespak and project monitoring teams briefed him on the progress of the projects.
Mr Awan inspected the outer drain at Mori Gate and reviewed the preservation and restoration of historic buildings.
He also visited Government Victoria Girls Higher Secondary School inside Mori Gate, where he was briefed on the proposed conservation work. He also reviewed progress on the underground parking and market project at the Taxali Gate.
The commissioner was further briefed on the conservation and upgradation plans for Pani Wala Talab being undertaken by the Water and Sanitation Agency (Wasa).
Officials said the reservoir, established in 1883 and supported by 518 pillars, continued to serve as an important source of water supply for the city.
During the visit, Mr Awan also reviewed larva surveillance activities being carried out by anti-dengue teams in the Walled City. He interacted with residents and distributed pamphlets as part of the dengue awareness campaign.
